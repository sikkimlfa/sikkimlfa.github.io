Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025: A Complete Overview

August 4, 2026 · 6 min · Directorate of Local Fund Audit

The Government of Sikkim’s Finance Department officially issued Notification No. 03/FIN/ADM through Extraordinary Gazette No. 303. Enacted under clause (3) of Article 166 of the Constitution of India, the Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025 establish clear procedures, financial ceilings, entitlements, and operational guidelines governing all official state vehicles.

1. Key Definitions & Operational Framework#

The rules apply to all government vehicles—including two-wheelers, passenger cars, utility pick-ups, and heavy vehicles—across state departments.

  • Cumulative Expenditure: Represents the progressive total expenditure incurred on repair and maintenance over the life of the vehicle.

  • Financial Year: Defined as a 12-month period starting from the original purchase date of the vehicle.

  • Head of Department (HOD): A government servant in the rank of Secretary or above who heads the department.

  • Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO): An officer designated by the Finance Department to draw bills and handle state payments.

2. Vehicle Entitlements by Officer Level#

Rule 10 outlines strict rules for purchasing and allocating vehicles based on official designated ranks:

Level of Officers Vehicle Entitlement
Group A Level 22 and above / Corresponding All India Service (AIS) Officers Scorpio N ZS MT or any other SUV approved by the Govt.
Group A Level 20–21 / Corresponding AIS Officers Scorpio Classic S11 or any other SUV approved by the Govt.
Group A Level 19 / Corresponding AIS Officers Bolero NEO/N10 or any other vehicle approved by the Govt.
Gazetted Touring Officers below Group A Level 19 Bolero B6 (2WD) or approved vehicle (requires prior approval of the Chief Secretary)

New or replacement vehicles can only be procured with the prior concurrence of the Finance Department (and Home Department where required), along with sanction from the Chief Minister via the Chief Secretary.

3. Financial Limits & Sanctioning Authorities#

Minor & Private Garage Repairs#

Head of Departments (HOD) and Head of Offices (HOO) can authorize repair work in private garages approved by the Sikkim Nationalised Transport (SNT) Division without prior technical bill verification, provided cost limits are respected:

  • Two Wheelers: Maximum ₹5,000 per instance; capped at ₹10,000 per annum.
  • All Other Vehicles: Maximum ₹15,000 per instance; capped at ₹50,000 per annum.

Special Accessories & VVIP/VIP Vehicles#

  • Safety Accessories: HODs may sanction up to ₹10,000 once during a vehicle’s lifetime for functional accessories like rain guards or mudguards. Decorative items (alloy wheels, custom searchlights, media players) are prohibited.
  • VVIP / VIP Vehicles: Special fittings adhere strictly to SNT technical specifications. HODs have full powers to sanction up to ₹50,000 over the lifetime of the vehicle.

4. Routine Servicing, Accessories & Tyre Replacements#

Routine maintenance items (servicing, tyres, seat covers, and mats) do not count toward the cumulative repair ceiling but must be entered into the vehicle’s official history sheet.

Servicing Cost Restrictions#

Servicing costs (including lubricants) are restricted per session:

  • Two Wheelers: ₹2,000
  • Gypsy / Car / Van / Others: ₹10,000
  • Bolero / Utility / Pickup: ₹15,000
  • Scorpio / SUVs / Heavy Vehicles: ₹20,000
  • Innova / Fortuner: ₹25,000

Servicing frequency is capped at once every 3 months / 8,000 km for touring vehicles, or once every 6 months / 8,000 km for local duty vehicles.

Tyre & Tube Replacement Thresholds#

Up to four tyres/tubes can be replaced once the following mileage thresholds are reached:

  • Utility / Pickup: 17,000 km
  • Light Motor Vehicles / SUVs / Two Wheelers: 20,000 km
  • Heavy Vehicles: 15,000 km

Interior Furnishing Costs#

  • Mats: Maximum ₹6,000 for SUVs (3 pcs) or ₹4,500 for others (replacement permitted only after 2 years).
  • Seat Covers: Complete seat covers (including stitching) are capped at ₹10,000 (replacement allowed only after 3 years).

5. Monthly Vehicle User Charges#

Officers or functionaries using government vehicles for both official and private purposes pay a monthly deduction directly from their salaries:

Purchase Cost of Vehicle Monthly User Charge
Up to ₹10,00,000/- ₹1,000/-
₹10,00,001/- to ₹15,00,000/- ₹1,500/-
Above ₹15,00,000/- ₹2,000/-
  • Shared Usage: Shared vehicles split the user charge across all assigned officers.
  • Programme Vehicles: Officers using programme vehicles pay 50% of the standard rate.
  • Exemptions: Vehicles assigned to the Governor, Chief Minister, Chief Justice, High Court Judges, along with ambulances, hearses, buses, trucks, fire tenders, and police pilot/escort vehicles are exempt.

6. Repairs Following Accidents & Work Guarantees#

  • Accident Repairs: If repair costs exceed ₹60,000 following an accident, the proposal requires SNT technical examination alongside Home and Finance Department concurrences. An FIR, police cause report, and departmental inquiry report must accompany the claim. Approved accident costs do not count toward the annual vehicle repair limit.
  • Personal/Unauthorized Use: Accident repair costs resulting from personal use or unauthorized drivers will not be borne by the government.
  • Repair Guarantees: Garages must provide a mandatory 1-year guarantee for major engine repairs, minor engine repairs, and gearbox repairs.

7. Vehicle Condemnation & Public Auction Procedures#

A vehicle can be condemned only after receiving a technical fitness certificate from the SNT Division stating that it is no longer fit for economical use.

Lifespan Criteria for Condemnation#

  • Standard Motor Vehicles (<18 HP): 1.50 lakh km run or 10 years of service (whichever is completed later).
  • Fire Service Vehicles: 5,000 hours of static operation or 10 years in service (24 km run is treated as equivalent to 1 hour stationary operation).

Disposal Process#

Condemned vehicles are disposed of through public auctions managed by the Transport Department. Mandatory public auction notices must be published across print and digital media with at least one month’s advance notice. All auction proceeds are credited directly back into the official Government Revenue Head.
