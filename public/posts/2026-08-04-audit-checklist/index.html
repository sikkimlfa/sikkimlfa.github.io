The Ultimate Guide to Local Body Audits: Comprehensive Requisition Checklists, Financial Formats, and Compliance Frameworks for GPUs, ZPs, and Municipalities

August 4, 2026 · 13 min · Directorate of Local Fund Audit

Conducting a statutory or internal audit of local self-government institutions—such as Gram Panchayat Units (GPUs), Zilla Panchayats (ZPs), and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) or Municipalities—requires a structured, systematic approach. Auditing these bodies involves verifying not only cash transactions and bank balances but also statutory compliance, public procurement guidelines, revenue realisations, and asset registers.

This comprehensive guide compiles every essential checklist, fillable requisition format, financial reporting template, and technical explanation needed to prepare for or execute an audit of local governing bodies.

1. Statutory Framework and Audit Objectives#

Local bodies operate under specific state acts (such as the Sikkim Panchayat Act 1993, the Sikkim Municipalities Act 2007, and the Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act 2012) alongside Central Finance Commission guidelines. The primary objective of the audit is to ensure:

  • Financial Propriety: Verifying that expenditure is backed by proper sanctions, budget provisions, and valid vouchers.
  • Revenue Optimization: Ensuring local taxes, user fees, and non-tax revenues are accurately assessed, collected, and deposited.
  • Asset Accountability: Confirming that public infrastructure, dead stock, tools, and equipment are correctly catalogued and physically verified.
  • Compliance and Governance: Scrutinising scheme implementation (e.g., MGNREGA, 15th FC), social audits, Gram Sabha minutes, and RTI proactive disclosures.

Institutional & Primary Financial Records#

  • Cash Books: Updated and balanced Main Cash Book and Subsidiary Cash Books.
  • Bank Statements & Passbooks: Complete statements for all active accounts (15th FC, SFC, OSR, MGNREGA).
  • Bank Reconciliation Statements (BRS): Monthly BRS prepared and reconciled for every account.
  • Annual Financial Statements: Receipt and Payment Accounts, Income and Expenditure Accounts, and Balance Sheets.
  • Budget Estimates: Approved Annual Budget and any supplementary estimates.
  • Vouchers & Receipts: Chronologically organized original paid vouchers with bills, receipts, and challans.

Registers & Internal Controls#

  • Imprest/Advance Registers: Tracking permanent advances and personal/impersonal pending advances.
  • Stock & Asset Registers: Dead Stock Register, Consumable Stores Register, and Tools & Plant (T&P) Ledger.
  • Establishment Records: Salary Audit Registers, Service Books, Attendance Registers, and Honorarium Registers.
  • Log Books: Vehicle Log Books, history sheets, and Petrol, Oil & Lubricants (POL) usage logs.

Procurement, Works, & Schemes#

  • Works Register: Master catalog of all ongoing and completed projects.
  • Individual Work Files: Project reports, Administrative Approvals (AA), Technical Sanctions (TS), and detailed estimates.
  • Measurement Books (MBs): Updated MBs with check-measurement entries by authorized engineers.
  • Procurement Files: Tender notices, quotations, comparative statements, and purchase orders for stationery, furniture, and equipment.

Governance & Compliance#

  • Meeting Records: Gram Sabha, Ward Sabha, or Municipal Board Meeting Minutes Registers.
  • Previous Audits: Prior Audit Reports along with the Action Taken Report (ATR) on past objections.
  • RTI Compliance: Proactive disclosure records as mandated under Section 4 of the RTI Act.

6. Understanding the Demand, Collection, and Balance (DCB) Framework#

The Demand, Collection, and Balance (DCB) statement is the ultimate audit tool for evaluating the financial self-sufficiency and revenue performance of a local authority.

Core Components of DCB#

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        TOTAL DEMAND (A + B)                            │
└───────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                    │                                │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
        │   ARREAR DEMAND (A)   │        │   CURRENT DEMAND (B)  │
        │  (Uncollected from    │        │  (Freshly levied for  │
        │    previous years)    │        │    current year)      │
        └───────────────────────┘        └───────────────────────┘
                    │                                │
                    └────────────────┬───────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
                        ┌───────────────────────────┐
                        │    TOTAL COLLECTION (C)   │
                        │  (Actual cash deposited)  │
                        └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                                     │
                                     ▼
                        ┌───────────────────────────┐
                        │  UNCOLLECTED BALANCE (D)  │
                        │    Formula: (A + B) - C   │
                        └───────────────────────────┘
  1. Demand: The total revenue legally due to the institution.
  • Arrear Demand (A): Uncollected taxes/fees carried forward from previous financial years.
  • Current Demand (B): Fresh tax assessments, lease fees, or license fees levied for the current year.
  1. Collection (C): Actual revenue realized and deposited into the official bank account. Auditors cross-verify collections against the Daily Collection Register (DCR) and bank deposit slips.
  2. Balance (D): The remaining uncollected balance ($D = (A + B) - C$). A large balance indicates weak collection mechanisms, potential revenue leaks, or unaddressed billing disputes.
