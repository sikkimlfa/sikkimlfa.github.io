Key Takeaways from the National Conference on Strengthening Panchayat Audit Systems

August 5, 2026 · 6 min · Directorate of Local Fund Audit

Key Takeaways from the National Conference on Strengthening Panchayat Audit Systems#

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR), in collaboration with the Comptroller and Auditor General (C&AG), conducted a national-level deliberation to improve the audit ecosystem of Rural Local Bodies (RLBs). The discussions focused on improving accountability, transparency, digital audit systems, Action Taken Reports (ATRs), and strengthening the institutional capacity of Local Fund Audit Departments.

This article summarizes the major deliberations, recommendations, and action points that emerged from the conference.

Why the Conference Was Important#

The implementation of the Fifteenth Finance Commission (XV FC) grants has significantly increased financial transfers to Panchayats. With higher public expenditure comes greater responsibility for ensuring that funds are used effectively and transparently.

The conference focused on:

  • Improving the quality of Panchayat audits.
  • Strengthening Action Taken Report (ATR) mechanisms.
  • Expanding the use of AuditOnline.
  • Integrating digital governance platforms.
  • Building institutional capacity for Local Fund Auditors.

Major Observations by the Secretary, Ministry of Panchayati Raj#

The Secretary highlighted several strategic reforms required for improving the audit framework.

1. District-Level Consolidation of Audit Reports#

Instead of viewing audit reports individually, district-level consolidated reports should be prepared to identify recurring irregularities, systemic weaknesses, preventive measures, and corrective actions. These reports can serve as valuable references for policy analysis and future planning.

2. Standard Reporting Framework#

The Secretary suggested that C&AG and State Accountants General should jointly develop a common framework for preparing district audit reports. Such standardization would improve comparability, monitoring, and policy interventions.

3. Capacity Building#

Preparation of quality audit reports depends largely upon competent auditors. Therefore, emphasis was placed on:

  • Regular training
  • Digital literacy
  • AuditOnline usage
  • Analytical skills
  • Report writing

4. Encouraging Open Discussion#

Participants were encouraged to freely express concerns, share practical challenges, and propose innovative solutions. The presentations submitted by States were accepted as part of the conference proceedings.

Presentation by Joint Secretary, MoPR#

The Joint Secretary presented the current status of XV Finance Commission grants, AuditOnline implementation, and the completion of Panchayat audits across States.

Major Suggestions Included:

  • Faster disposal of audit observations
  • Stronger monitoring of ATRs
  • Supervisory role for Block Panchayats
  • District monitoring committees
  • Legislative committees similar to the Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Observations by the Deputy Comptroller & Auditor General#

The Deputy C&AG emphasized that auditing is much more than checking accounts. It consists of two equally important components: Preventive Accountability and Corrective Accountability.

Preventive Measures#

Integration of eGramSwaraj and Government e-Marketplace (GeM) would automatically improve compliance with General Financial Rules (GFR), procurement rules, and financial discipline, thereby reducing audit objections before they occur.

Corrective Measures#

State Local Fund Audit Departments should establish effective mechanisms for monitoring audit observations, tracking Action Taken Reports, and ensuring timely compliance.

Role of Gram Sabhas#

One important recommendation was to place audit observations before Gram Sabhas.

Benefits Include:

  • Community monitoring
  • Greater transparency
  • Public accountability
  • Improved follow-up

This may require amendments to State Acts or Rules.

District-Level Monitoring#

The Deputy C&AG supported district monitoring systems, district audit analysis, and structured follow-up mechanisms. He informed participants that nearly 50 district-level Panchayat audit reports had already been prepared and shared with respective State Panchayat Departments.

Future Vision for AuditOnline#

The Additional Secretary, MoPR outlined the future roadmap for AuditOnline.

Integration with eGramSwaraj#

Future versions of AuditOnline will automatically fetch GPDP, receipts, expenditure, and implementation status directly from eGramSwaraj, eliminating duplicate data entry and improving audit efficiency.

Artificial Intelligence in Auditing#

MoPR plans to introduce Artificial Intelligence to assist auditors by:

  • Generating draft audit observations
  • Identifying financial anomalies
  • Preparing preliminary audit reports
  • Highlighting risk areas

AI will support auditors rather than replace professional judgment.

Strengthening Panchayat Capacity#

Technology alone cannot improve governance. Panchayats require trained staff, adequate manpower, digital infrastructure, and technical support. States were requested to raise these issues with Chief Secretaries and Finance Secretaries.

Preparing for the Sixteenth Finance Commission#

The Additional Secretary cautioned that future Finance Commission grants are likely to include stricter conditions relating to transparency, auditing, online reporting, and accountability. States should prepare themselves by adopting digital governance, online accounting, and automated service delivery.

Common Themes Emerging from States#

Although each State presented its own experiences, several common themes emerged.

AuditOnline Adoption#

Many States have successfully implemented AuditOnline (e.g., Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Kerala), while others are still transitioning from offline systems.

Manpower Shortage#

Several States reported inadequate audit staff (e.g., Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Manipur, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh). Many have started empanelling Chartered Accountants to bridge the gap.

Support for Gram Sabha Oversight#

Several States supported placing ATRs before Gram Sabhas, including Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Assam, Karnataka, Uttarakhand, and Tripura. This was viewed as an effective accountability mechanism.

Need for Better Integration#

States suggested stronger integration between AuditOnline, eGramSwaraj, and State Audit Software to reduce duplication and improve audit quality.

Strengthening State Finance Commissions#

Several States informed that constitution of new State Finance Commissions was underway (e.g., Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Jharkhand, West Bengal).

Training Requirements#

Training needs were highlighted across multiple States, particularly for Local Fund Auditors, newly recruited auditors, and Panchayat officials in areas like AuditOnline, digital accounting, audit procedures, and financial analysis.

Selected State Highlights#

  • Andhra Pradesh: Achieved 100% AuditOnline targets; supports Gram Sabha review of ATRs; resolving mapping issues for newly created Mandals.
  • Tamil Nadu: Conducts six Gram Sabha meetings annually; displays receipts and expenditure publicly; uses five-year perspective planning linked to annual plans.
  • Kerala: Maintains a strong Local Fund Audit Committee; District Planning Committees may monitor ATRs; parallel performance audits are conducted.
  • Haryana: Suggested that auditors should have direct access to GPDP, revised development plans, and grant utilization details to detect deviations from approved plans.
  • Rajasthan: Already operates district monitoring committees, state-level review committees, and integrated State audit software; recommended integration with AuditOnline.
  • Sikkim: AG’s Office assists Panchayat audits; consolidated reports are submitted to the State Public Accounts Committee; presently no statutory provision for placing ATRs before Gram Sabhas.

Action Points Finalized#

The conference concluded with several time-bound action points:

  1. States: All States were required to submit existing audit observation process flows, timelines for disposal, ATR workflow suggestions, and improvement recommendations for AuditOnline and eGramSwaraj to MoPR.
  2. Ministry of Panchayati Raj: MoPR committed to modifying AuditOnline, improving eGramSwaraj, consulting States and C&AG, and strengthening the ATR module.
  3. Finance Commission Linkage: Future recommendations for release of grants will increasingly consider audit completion, ATR compliance, digital reporting, and financial transparency.

Key Recommendations#

The conference identified several priority reforms:

  • Prepare district-level consolidated audit reports.
  • Standardize audit reporting across States.
  • Strengthen Local Fund Audit Departments.
  • Improve monitoring of Action Taken Reports.
  • Present audit findings before Gram Sabhas.
  • Integrate AuditOnline with eGramSwaraj.
  • Use Artificial Intelligence for audit assistance.
  • Increase manpower and training for auditors.
  • Improve digital infrastructure in Panchayats.
  • Build stronger legislative oversight mechanisms.

Conclusion#

The conference marked a significant step toward transforming Panchayat auditing from a compliance exercise into a comprehensive accountability framework. The discussions emphasized that effective auditing requires more than financial verification—it demands timely corrective action, preventive controls, digital integration, skilled human resources, and active public participation.

As AuditOnline evolves and integrates with platforms like eGramSwaraj, the future of local governance will increasingly depend on data-driven audits, transparent financial management, and stronger institutional oversight. If successfully implemented, these reforms have the potential to significantly improve service delivery, financial accountability, and public trust in Rural Local Bodies across India.
