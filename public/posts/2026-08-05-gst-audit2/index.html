Building a Practical GST Compliance Audit Framework: From Raw Records to Professional Audit Reports

August 5, 2026 · 6 min · Directorate of Local Fund Audit

Building a Practical GST Compliance Audit Framework#

Over the course of this project, the goal was not simply to prepare another GST audit report. The objective was to build a repeatable audit methodology that can be used by government auditors, internal auditors, chartered accountants, and finance professionals for conducting GST compliance audits efficiently.

The work evolved from preparing simple audit observations into developing a structured audit framework, reusable templates, reconciliation tools, reporting formats, and workflow automation.

This article summarizes the complete journey.

Why This Project Started#

Many GST audits suffer from common problems:

  • Audit observations are inconsistent.
  • Documentation is incomplete.
  • Every auditor follows a different reporting style.
  • Manual reconciliation consumes most of the audit time.
  • Important red flags remain unnoticed.

The idea was to create a standardized audit workflow that reduces manual effort while improving audit quality.

Understanding the Audit Process#

The audit was designed around one simple principle:

Everything must reconcile.

Every figure appearing in GST Returns should reconcile with:

  • Books of Accounts
  • Purchase Register
  • Sales Register
  • Bank Statements
  • E-Way Bills
  • Financial Statements

Whenever reconciliation fails, an audit observation is created.

Learning GST Audit in 24 Hours#

Instead of memorizing the CGST Act, the focus shifted toward high-impact practical auditing.

The audit process was divided into four major stages.

Stage 1 — Collect Data#

Essential documents include:

  • GSTR-1
  • GSTR-3B
  • GSTR-2A
  • GSTR-2B
  • GSTR-9
  • Purchase Register
  • Sales Register
  • Trial Balance
  • Ledger
  • Bank Statements
  • E-Way Bills

Without these documents, meaningful verification cannot begin.

Stage 2 — Reconciliation#

Four reconciliations provide maximum audit value:

  • Sales: Books → Sales Register → GSTR-1 → GSTR-3B
  • Purchases: Purchase Register → Books → GSTR-2B → ITC Claimed
  • Banking: Bank Deposits → Sales → GST Returns
  • Transportation: Purchase Invoices → Vehicle Numbers → E-Way Bills → Distance → Supervision Charges

Templates Developed#

During the project several reusable templates were created.

GST Audit Checklist#

The checklist covers:

  • Registration
  • GST Returns
  • ITC
  • Purchase Register
  • Sales Register
  • E-Way Bills
  • Credit Notes
  • HSN Codes
  • Reverse Charge
  • Bank Reconciliation
  • Annual Returns

Instead of relying on memory, auditors can simply follow the checklist.

GST Audit Report Format#

The report structure contains:

  1. Executive Summary
  2. Audit Objectives
  3. Scope
  4. Methodology
  5. Detailed Audit Observations
  6. Risk Assessment
  7. Recommendations
  8. Conclusion

This provides a uniform reporting format.

Client Information Sheet#

Basic information captured includes:

  • GSTIN
  • Legal Name
  • Trade Name
  • Constitution
  • Registration Date
  • Business Nature
  • Jurisdiction
  • HSN Codes

This becomes the first page of every audit.

Excel Tools#

Several Excel templates were designed.

GST Reconciliation Workbook#

Separate sheets were created for:

  • GSTR-1
  • GSTR-3B
  • GSTR-2B
  • Books Sales
  • Books Purchase

This structure allows Power Query to merge and compare records automatically.

Dashboard#

The dashboard displays:

  • Sales Difference
  • Purchase Difference
  • ITC Difference
  • Tax Difference
  • Status (Match / Mismatch)

This instantly highlights risky areas.

Power Query Automation#

Instead of manually comparing thousands of invoices, Power Query can:

  • Import GST data
  • Merge tables
  • Compare invoices
  • Flag differences
  • Generate exception reports

Typical workflow: Books Sales → Merge → GSTR-1 → Difference Column → Mismatch Report

The same process applies to purchases and ITC.

Professional Audit Observations#

One major improvement during the project was rewriting observations into professional audit language.

Instead of merely describing the issue, every observation now contains:

  • Observation
  • Audit Criteria
  • Risk
  • Implication
  • Recommendation

This approach aligns with standard audit reporting practices.

Major Audit Observations#

The Balaji Enterprises audit produced several significant findings.

1. Non-maintenance of Purchase Register#

The entity produced GSTR-2A instead of the purchase register maintained in its books.

This violates:

  • Section 35 of CGST Act
  • Rule 56 of CGST Rules

Impact:

  • ITC cannot be verified.
  • Purchases remain unsubstantiated.

2. Inadmissible Input Tax Credit#

ITC was claimed on:

  • iPhone
  • Personal Tour

Section 17(5)(g) specifically disallows ITC on personal consumption.

Recommended action:

Reverse ITC with applicable interest.

3. Missing Purchase Documentation#

Portal records indicated purchases from multiple suppliers.

However, only a small portion of invoices were produced.

This prevents verification of:

  • ITC
  • Purchase authenticity
  • Financial accuracy

4. Price Equalization Discounts#

The entity claimed price equalization through ledger entries.

No supporting credit notes were produced.

Without documentary evidence, discounts cannot be verified.

5. Sale Below Purchase Cost#

Cement was purchased at approximately ₹290 per bag.

Sale price averaged approximately ₹292.

Such thin margins deserve examination because they may indicate:

  • Undervaluation
  • Price manipulation
  • Artificial ITC accumulation

6. Sales Register vs GSTR-1#

Differences existed between:

  • Taxable Value
  • CGST
  • SGST
  • IGST

These mismatches require reconciliation before the financial statements can be considered reliable.

7. Missing HSN Codes#

Only a limited number of HSN codes were declared.

However, audit found additional hardware items being traded.

Potential consequences include:

  • Wrong tax rate
  • Incorrect invoice classification
  • GST reporting inconsistencies

Recommendation:

Update GST registration and ensure invoices reflect correct HSN codes.

8. Missing Sales Invoices#

Complete sales invoices were not produced.

This prevented:

  • Verification of turnover
  • Sample testing
  • Matching with GST returns

9. GSTR-9 Not Filed#

Annual return for one financial year remained unverified.

This increases compliance risk.

10. Income Tax Return Not Produced#

The entity claimed exemption.

No documentary evidence was produced.

11. Vehicle Number Repetition#

Identical vehicle numbers appeared across multiple invoices.

Further verification is required to rule out documentation errors.

12. E-Way Bill Issues#

Observed issues included:

  • Missing E-Way Bills
  • Underreported distance
  • Non-generation below threshold

These require closer scrutiny.

13. Supervision Charges#

Goods transported between Siliguri and Gangtok frequently reflected only 10 km instead of the expected route distance.

If confirmed, this may affect computation of supervision charges.

The report recommends verification with supporting transport records before determining liability.

14. Bank Reconciliation#

Bank deposits substantially exceeded GST turnover in certain years.

Possible reasons include:

  • Unrecorded sales
  • Timing differences
  • Non-GST receipts
  • Classification issues

This area requires detailed reconciliation before any conclusion is drawn.

Risk Assessment#

Each observation was categorized based on severity.

Risk Examples
High Wrong ITC, Sales Suppression, Missing Registers
Medium HSN Issues, GSTR-9, Missing Credit Notes
Low Documentation Improvements

This helps management prioritize corrective action.

Documents Requested#

The audit also tracks document availability.

Examples include:

  • Purchase Register
  • Sales Register
  • Credit Notes
  • Balance Sheet
  • Profit & Loss Account
  • Annual Report
  • GST Returns
  • E-Way Bills

Maintaining a requisition tracker prevents repeated requests.

Final Recommendations#

The project resulted in a practical audit methodology.

Key recommendations include:

  • Maintain proper statutory books.
  • Reconcile GST returns every month.
  • Verify ITC only after GSTR-2B matching.
  • Preserve supporting documents for discounts.
  • Update HSN classifications regularly.
  • Perform periodic internal GST reviews.
  • Strengthen documentation before statutory audit.

Tools Created During This Project#

The following resources were developed:

  • GST Audit Checklist
  • GST Audit Report Template
  • Excel Reconciliation Workbook
  • Power Query Reconciliation Workflow
  • Dashboard Template
  • Client Data Collection Format
  • Observation Templates
  • Meeting Record Format
  • Risk Assessment Matrix
  • Recommendation Tracker

These templates can be adapted for future GST audits with minimal modification.

Lessons Learned#

The biggest lesson from this project is that GST auditing is less about memorizing legal provisions and more about systematic verification.

A disciplined workflow built around reconciliation, documentation, and evidence allows auditors to identify most compliance issues efficiently.

Standardized templates, automation with Excel and Power Query, and well-structured audit observations significantly reduce audit effort while improving consistency and report quality.

For audit teams, investing time in reusable tools pays dividends across every future engagement.
