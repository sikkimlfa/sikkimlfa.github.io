The cooperative sector in Sikkim has evolved into a vital pillar for rural development, agricultural credit, and economic empowerment. Governed by the **Sikkim Co-operative Societies Act, 1978** and the **Rules of 1981**, the Cooperation Department of the Government of Sikkim oversees a network of primary cooperatives, marketing bodies, and apex credit institutions. At the grassroots level of this system sits the **Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS)**.

---

## 1. Schemes and Funds Under the Sikkim Cooperation Department

The Department of Cooperation provides structural, financial, and managerial support to cooperative societies across Sikkim through state budgetary provisions and central collaborations.

### Direct State Financial Assistance

* **Transport and Marketing Subsidies:** Offered to agricultural and marketing cooperatives to lower the cost of moving produce to market hubs.
* **Managerial Subsidies:** Tapering financial grants provided to newly established or developing societies to offset initial administrative overheads.
* **Pre-establishment Assistance:** Financial aid for organizational and preparatory expenses prior to official commercial operations.
* **Infrastructure Support:** Capital support for establishing storage godowns, cold chains, and administrative hubs for PACS and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs).

### Key Modernization and Support Initiatives

* **Computerization of PACS:** Integration of functional PACS onto a unified Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) platform with central funding, ensuring transparent Common Accounting Systems (CAS).
* **FPO Registration:** Organizing agriculturalists and horticulturalists under the State Cooperative Act to streamline market linkages.
* **Sector Diversification:** Extending cooperative frameworks to non-traditional areas including adventure tourism, film production, herbal products, and generic medicine retail.
* **Apex Institutional Integration:** Strengthening financial flow through the **Sikkim State Co-operative Bank Ltd. (SISCO/SIBCO)**, the lead agency for rural credit and Kisan Credit Card (KCC) disbursals in the state.

## 2. A Deep Dive into Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS)

PACS represent the foundational tier of India's three-tier Short-Term Cooperative Credit (STCC) structure:

1. **State Cooperative Bank (SISCO/SIBCO):** Apex tier at the state level.
2. **District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs) / Regional Hubs:** Intermediate financing tier.
3. **Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS):** Village or Gram Panchayat-level grassroots units directly serving individual farmers.

### Evolution into Multi-Service Centres (MSCs)

Rather than acting solely as short-term lenders, PACS in Sikkim are expanding into multi-service hubs. Today, PACS operate as:

* **Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Kendras** for affordable generic medicines.
* **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendras (PMKSK)** for certified seeds, fertilizers, and farm equipment.
* **Common Service Centres (CSCs)** delivering digital utility payments, Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) processing, and e-governance services to villagers.

## 3. Loan Products, Limits, and Conditions

PACS disburse credit financed directly or refinanced through SISCO/SIBCO and NABARD.

### Primary Loan Products

* **Short-Term Agricultural Loans (Crop Loans / KCC):** Disbursed for seasonal agricultural operations (seeds, labor, inputs). Sanctioned based on the **Scale of Finance** set by the District Level Technical Committee (DLTC) multiplied by the cultivated acreage. Period: up to 12 months.
* **Medium-Term Loans:** Issued for purchasing machinery (power tillers, pumpsets), land development, or establishing livestock units (dairy, poultry, fisheries). Repayment tenure: 3 to 7 years.
* **Pledge & Diversified Loans:**
* *Jewel/Gold Loans:* Up to ₹2,00,000 against pledged gold ornaments.
* *Produce Marketing Loans:* Up to ₹10,00,000 (capped at 75% of the market value of pledged produce) to prevent distress selling.



### Eligibility Conditions

1. **Mandatory Membership:** The applicant must be a registered member of the local PACS holding required share capital.
2. **Land Tenure Verification:** Proof of land ownership or recognized tenant cultivation (Parcha Khatiyan / Record of Rights).
3. **Creditworthiness:** A clear credit record free from defaults with other cooperative or commercial banks.
4. **Security Charge:** Creation of a statutory first charge on standing crops or land under Section 49 of the Sikkim Co-operative Societies Act, 1978.

## 4. Debunking the Myth: Are Political Recommendations Required?

**No.** No provision within the Sikkim Co-operative Societies Act, 1978, the RBI guidelines, or NABARD operational manuals requires a recommendation from a Minister, Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA), or political representative to secure a loan.

Loan decisions are purely administrative and financial, determined by:

* Verification of land records and scale of finance.
* Approval by the elected **Managing Committee** of the PACS.
* Compliance with CIBIL/credit checks and collateral norms.

Political intervention is not part of the statutory appraisal process.

1. **Membership Enrollment:** Register as a member of your local PACS by completing the membership form and purchasing required share capital.
2. **Form Filing & Document Collection:** Obtain the appropriate loan application form. Attach identity proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID), land records (RoR, Parcha Khatiyan), and passport photos.
3. **Submission & Verification:** Submit the application to the PACS Manager/Secretary. The PACS conducts field verification of land, crop choice, or proposed asset purchase.
4. **Board Approval:** The application is reviewed during the Managing Committee meeting against eligibility criteria and Bye-laws.
5. **Execution & Disbursement:** Sign the loan agreement, register the statutory charge on land/crop, and receive direct loan disbursement into your bank account.

## 6. The Auditor's Lens: An Exhaustive Audit Checklist for PACS

Under **Section 64 of the Sikkim Co-operative Societies Act, 1978**, every PACS must undergo a statutory annual audit conducted by authorized auditors or the Registrar of Co-operative Societies (RCS).

Here is an exhaustive 27-point checklist covering financial verification, legal compliance, and internal governance:

### Exhaustive Audit Checklist

#### I. Financial & Accounting Checks

1. **Books Completeness:** Verify that Cash Book, General Ledger, Loan Ledgers, and Vouchers are up-to-date and reconciled.
2. **Trial Balance Accuracy:** Confirm opening balances against the previous audited report and verify mathematical accuracy.
3. **ERP System Verification:** Reconcile manual vouchers against system-generated ledgers in the computerized ERP portal.
4. **Physical Cash Count:** Conduct an unannounced physical cash verification on the audit date and reconcile with the Cash Book balance.
5. **Bank Reconciliation (BRS):** Check Bank Reconciliation Statements for all accounts held with SISCO/SIBCO or commercial banks.
6. **Sanction Authority:** Verify whether loan sanctions adhere to Bye-law limits and carry explicit Managing Committee approval.
7. **Scale of Finance Adherence:** Match crop loan disbursement values against official DLTC Scale of Finance notifications.
8. **Statutory First Charge:** Verify that statutory charges under Section 49 on crops/land are properly executed and annotated in records.
9. **Asset Classification (NPA):** Audit loan ageing schedules and classify default accounts into Sub-standard, Doubtful, or Loss categories per IRAC norms.
10. **Provisioning Adequacy:** Check if mandatory bad-debt provisions are created against classified NPA accounts.
11. **Interest Recognition:** Verify interest calculations on loans and ensure uncollected interest on NPAs is credited to Overdue Interest Reserve rather than Profit & Loss.
12. **Subsidy Accounting:** Match received Interest Subvention and computerization grants against government sanction letters and utilization certificates.
13. **Fixed Asset Verification & Depreciation:** Inspect fixed asset registers (godowns, equipment) and verify depreciation rates applied.

#### II. Compliance & Statutory Checks

14. **Borrowing & Investment Limits:** Verify compliance with **Section 47** (Maximum Borrowing Limit) and **Section 48** (Investment of Funds) of the Sikkim Act.
15. **Bye-law Compliance:** Ensure all operational transactions conform strictly to the registered Bye-laws of the society.
16. **Legal Documentation:** Verify proper execution of loan agreements, mortgage deeds, and guarantee bonds.
17. **Annual General Meeting (AGM):** Confirm whether the AGM was conducted within the statutory timeframe with proper notice and quorum.
18. **Statutory Profit Appropriation:** Check if net profits were correctly allocated under **Section 62** (Reserve Fund allocation, Cooperative Education Fund, Dividend caps).
19. **Audit Defect Rectification:** Review the Audit Rectification Report (ATR) submitted to the RCS for issues raised in the previous year.

#### III. Operational & Internal Control Checks

20. **Member Register Integrity:** Ensure the Member Register reflects complete share details, admissions, and terminations.
21. **Cash Control & Segregation:** Evaluate dual-custody controls for physical cash, vault access, and chequebook issuance.
22. **Inventory Audit:** Perform physical stock verification and valuation of agri-inputs (seeds, fertilizers) held in PACS godowns.
23. **End-Use Verification:** Sample-check invoices and field reports to confirm term loans were utilized for intended assets.
24. **Staff Capacity & ERP Security:** Check staff training logs and user-access controls on the common ERP software.

#### IV. Audit Finalization

25. **Statutory Schedules:** Ensure all standard audit schedules and financial statements are attached per Cooperative Rules.
26. **Audit Classification (Rating):** Award the final audit score/class (**A, B, C, D, or E**) based on overdues, profitability, and compliance indices.
27. **Report Submission:** Sign and officially submit the final Audit Report and Management Letter to the Registrar of Co-operative Societies (RCS).
