# Using Artificial Intelligence to Combat GST Fraud

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is one of the most significant tax reforms in India. It replaced multiple indirect taxes with a unified taxation system, simplifying compliance and improving transparency. However, the scale and complexity of GST have also created opportunities for sophisticated tax fraud.

Fake invoices, shell companies, fraudulent Input Tax Credit (ITC) claims, refund fraud, circular trading, and tax evasion continue to challenge tax authorities. Traditional audit methods are no longer sufficient to detect these frauds efficiently.

Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and advanced data analytics offer a powerful solution. By processing millions of transactions in real time, AI can identify suspicious patterns that human auditors might miss.

This article explores how AI can transform GST administration and significantly reduce tax fraud.

---

# Understanding GST Fraud

GST fraud generally falls into several categories.

## Fake Invoice Fraud

Businesses generate invoices without supplying goods or services to claim Input Tax Credit.

### Example

Company A issues fake invoices worth ₹10 crore to Company B.

Company B claims ITC without purchasing any goods.

The government loses tax revenue.

---

## Shell Companies

Fraudsters establish multiple companies that exist only on paper.

These companies:

- Issue fake invoices
- Claim fraudulent refunds
- Disappear after investigation begins

Many such companies share:

- Directors
- Phone numbers
- Email addresses
- Bank accounts
- Internet IP addresses

---

## Circular Trading

Goods or invoices circulate among several businesses without any real commercial activity.

Example:

```
Company A
     ↓
Company B
     ↓
Company C
     ↓
Company D
     ↓
Company A
```

Every company claims Input Tax Credit while no real value is created.

---

## Refund Fraud

Exporters may inflate exports or fabricate purchases to obtain illegal GST refunds.

---

## Underreporting Sales

Businesses report lower turnover to reduce GST liability.

Example

Actual Sales

₹12 Crore

Reported Sales

₹7 Crore

GST is paid only on the reported amount.

---

# Why Traditional Audits Are No Longer Enough

Manual GST audits face several limitations.

- Millions of invoices are generated every day.
- Human auditors cannot examine every transaction.
- Fraudsters use complex networks across multiple states.
- Paper-based verification is slow.
- Fraud is often detected years after it occurs.

Modern tax systems require intelligent automation.

---

# Role of Artificial Intelligence in GST Administration

Artificial Intelligence can continuously monitor GST transactions, detect anomalies, calculate fraud risk, and prioritize investigations.

Instead of randomly selecting taxpayers for audit, AI helps tax officers focus on the highest-risk cases.

---

# AI-Based Invoice Matching

## The Problem

A supplier uploads invoices.

The recipient claims Input Tax Credit.

If supplier data does not match recipient claims, fraud may occur.

Checking millions of invoices manually is impossible.

---

## AI Solution

Machine Learning models automatically compare

- Invoice numbers
- GSTIN
- Invoice dates
- Tax amounts
- HSN codes
- E-way bills
- Payment history

The AI immediately identifies inconsistencies.

### Example

Supplier reports:

```
Taxable Value
₹2,50,000
```

Recipient claims

```
₹8,50,000
```

AI detects the mismatch instantly.

# Fraud Risk Scoring

Every taxpayer can receive an AI-generated fraud risk score.

Example:

| Risk Score | Interpretation |
|------------|---------------|
| 0–20 | Very Low Risk |
| 21–40 | Low Risk |
| 41–60 | Moderate Risk |
| 61–80 | High Risk |
| 81–100 | Critical Risk |

Risk scores may consider

- Late filing frequency
- ITC anomalies
- Turnover fluctuations
- Refund history
- Supplier quality
- Previous audit findings
- Industry benchmarks

Tax officers can prioritize investigations efficiently.

---

# Machine Learning for Fraud Detection

Machine Learning improves over time.

Instead of relying on fixed rules, it learns from historical fraud cases.

For example,

Past fraud data may reveal:

- unusually high ITC
- newly registered suppliers
- multiple amendments
- repeated refunds
- no e-way bills

The model identifies similar behaviour in future taxpayers.

---

# Network Analysis for Shell Companies

One of AI's strongest capabilities is relationship analysis.

Instead of examining companies individually, AI maps connections among them.

```
Company A
   │
   ├────────Company B
   │
Company C──────Company D
   │
   └────────Company E
```

AI detects

- Common directors
- Shared addresses
- Shared bank accounts
- Common mobile numbers
- Common IP addresses
- Circular invoice chains

These hidden networks often reveal organized fraud.

---

# Detecting Circular Trading

Graph analytics is particularly effective against circular trading.

Example

```
A → B → C → D → A
```

Invoices circulate continuously.

No genuine value addition occurs.

AI recognizes repeated cycles automatically.

---

# Intelligent ITC Verification

Input Tax Credit fraud remains one of the largest GST challenges.

AI verifies ITC using multiple data sources.

- Supplier returns
- Recipient returns
- E-way bills
- Banking transactions
- Customs data
- Income Tax records

Only genuine claims proceed automatically.

Suspicious claims receive additional scrutiny.

---

# AI for GST Refund Verification

Refund fraud can be significantly reduced through intelligent verification.

Before approving refunds, AI validates

- Shipping bills
- ICEGATE export records
- Banking transactions
- GST returns
- Previous refund patterns

### Example

Exporter claims

```
Refund:
₹3 Crore
```

AI discovers

- No shipping records
- No customs clearance
- No foreign remittance

Refund is automatically held for verification.

---

# E-Way Bill Analytics

Fake invoices often have no corresponding movement of goods.

AI compares

- Vehicle GPS
- E-way bill
- Toll data
- Transport invoices

Example

Invoice states

```
Delhi → Bengaluru
```

GPS indicates

```
Vehicle never left Delhi.
```

AI flags the transaction.

- Industry risk
- Filing behaviour
- Refund claims
- Turnover consistency
- Supplier profile
- Payment history

Audit resources become more efficient.

Many GST errors occur unintentionally.

AI assistants can help taxpayers before returns are filed.

Example

> Your ITC claim exceeds industry averages by 300%. Please verify invoices before submission.

This prevents genuine mistakes and improves voluntary compliance.

# Computer Vision for Invoice Verification

Businesses frequently upload scanned invoices.

Computer Vision combined with OCR extracts

- Invoice number
- GSTIN
- Tax amount
- HSN codes
- Dates

AI checks whether

- the invoice has been altered,
- duplicate invoices exist, or
- required fields are missing.

---

# Facial Recognition During Registration

AI can reduce fake GST registrations by verifying applicant identity.

Checks may include

- Aadhaar verification
- PAN validation
- Facial recognition
- Liveness detection
- Duplicate identity detection

This helps prevent fraudulent registrations using stolen identities.

# AI-Powered Early Warning System

Instead of detecting fraud years later, AI can generate real-time alerts.

Examples include

- Sudden spike in ITC
- Massive refund claim

## Cybersecurity

# Case Study

A newly registered company reports

- Turnover: ₹25 crore
- Employees: 2
- Office: Residential apartment
- Refund claimed: ₹4 crore
- No transport records
- Same director linked to 14 companies

AI evaluates multiple indicators.

Risk Score:

```
96 / 100
```

Actions generated

- Suspend refund
- Initiate audit
- Verify physical premises
- Investigate related companies
- Freeze suspicious ITC claims

Without AI, such fraud might remain undetected for months.

# Recommendations

Governments should prioritize the following initiatives:

1. Develop a centralized AI-powered GST fraud detection platform.
2. Integrate GST data with Income Tax, Customs, MCA, banking, and transport databases.
3. Deploy machine learning models for continuous fraud risk assessment.
4. Use graph analytics to uncover shell companies and circular trading networks.
5. Automate invoice matching and refund verification.
6. Introduce AI-powered compliance assistants for taxpayers.
7. Build explainable AI systems to ensure transparency and fairness.
8. Invest in cybersecurity and data governance frameworks.
9. Train tax officers in AI-assisted auditing and data analytics.
10. Regularly update AI models using confirmed fraud cases and evolving fraud patterns.

---

# Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence has the potential to fundamentally transform GST administration. Rather than relying solely on manual audits and reactive investigations, tax authorities can shift toward a proactive, intelligence-driven approach.

AI enables real-time monitoring, automated invoice reconciliation, predictive risk scoring, network analysis, and intelligent audit selection. These capabilities not only improve fraud detection but also reduce compliance burdens for honest taxpayers, accelerate refund processing, and strengthen public confidence in the tax system.

While successful implementation requires high-quality data, strong cybersecurity, transparent governance, and skilled personnel, the long-term benefits far outweigh the challenges. As GST ecosystems continue to generate massive volumes of transactional data, AI will become an indispensable tool for creating a more efficient, fair, and resilient tax administration.

The future of GST compliance lies not merely in digital taxation but in intelligent taxation, where data-driven insights help governments detect fraud early, improve voluntary compliance, and safeguard public revenue.
