# Understanding Rule 12 of the Sikkim Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 2016

Promotion is one of the most significant milestones in a government servant's career. Besides higher responsibilities and designation, it also affects pay. To ensure uniformity in pay fixation after the implementation of the revised pay structure, the Government of Sikkim introduced **Rule 12** under the **Sikkim Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 2016**.

This article explains the rule in simple language, discusses the available options, provides illustrations, and demonstrates how to calculate pay after promotion.

> **Note:** This article is intended for educational purposes and is based on the discussion in this chat. Always refer to the latest Government Orders and official notifications for administrative decisions.

---

# What is Rule 12?

Rule 12 governs the **fixation of pay on promotion or appointment to a post carrying higher responsibilities** after the implementation of the revised pay structure.

It provides government servants with the option to choose how their pay should be fixed when promoted.

The objective of the rule is to ensure that an employee receives the financial benefit of promotion while also allowing them to choose the option that results in greater long-term financial advantage.

---

# Who is Covered?

Rule 12 generally applies to employees who are:

- Promoted on a regular basis.
- Appointed to a higher post carrying greater responsibilities.
- Covered under the revised pay matrix.

It generally does **not** apply to:

- Ad-hoc promotions.
- Direct recruitment.
- Deputation to ex-cadre posts (unless specifically provided).

---

# The Two Pay Fixation Options

One of the most important features of Rule 12 is that the employee may choose between two methods of pay fixation.

---

## Option 1 – Fixation from the Date of Promotion

Under this option:

1. One increment is granted in the existing Pay Level.
2. The employee is then placed in the promoted Pay Level.
3. Pay is fixed at the **equal or next higher cell** in the higher Pay Level.

### Advantages

- Immediate increase in salary.
- Higher allowances immediately.
- Useful if retirement is approaching.

### Disadvantages

- In some situations, lifetime earnings may be lower than Option 2.

---

## Option 2 – Fixation from the Date of Next Increment (DNI)

This option delays the financial fixation until the employee's next increment date.

During the intervening period:

- The employee works in the promoted post.
- Pay is drawn in the higher level provisionally.

On the Date of Next Increment:

1. One normal annual increment is granted.
2. One promotional increment is granted.
3. Pay is then fixed in the higher level at the equal or next higher cell.

### Advantages

- Frequently results in higher basic pay.
- Better long-term financial benefit.
- Higher pension benefits in many cases.

### Disadvantages

- Immediate salary increase may be smaller.

---

# Time Limit for Exercising the Option

The employee should exercise the option within the prescribed period, generally **within one month** from the date of promotion.

If no option is exercised, the default fixation is generally from the date of promotion.

# Step-by-Step Pay Fixation Process

Assume:

| Particular | Value |
|------------|-------|
| Existing Level | Level 4 |
| Existing Basic Pay | ₹29,600 |
| Promotion Level | Level 5 |

---

## Option A Calculation

### Step 1

Current Pay

```
₹29,600
```

### Step 2

Grant one increment in Level 4

```
₹30,500
```

### Step 3

Locate equal or next higher cell in Level 5.

Suppose the next higher cell is

```
₹31,000
```

### Final Pay

```
₹31,000
```

---

## Option B Calculation

### Step 1

Continue until the next increment date.

### Step 2

Grant:

- One annual increment
- One promotional increment

Pay becomes

```
₹31,400
```

### Step 3

Locate equal or next higher cell in Level 5.

Suppose that value is

```
₹31,900
```

### Final Pay

```
₹31,900
```

# Comparison

| Option | Fixed Pay |
|---------|-----------:|
| Promotion Date | ₹31,000 |
| Next Increment Date | ₹31,900 |

Difference:

```
₹900
```

This difference continues throughout the employee's career and also affects:

- Dearness Allowance
- HRA
- NPS contribution
- Retirement benefits
- Pension (where applicable)

---

# Which Option is Better?

There is no universal answer.

Generally,

Choose **Option A** if:

- Immediate higher salary is important.
- Retirement is near.
- Immediate financial commitments exist.

Choose **Option B** if:

- You have many years of service remaining.
- You wish to maximize future increments.
- Long-term financial benefit is preferred.

# Common Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes during pay fixation.

- Not exercising the option within time.
- Choosing Option A without comparing Option B.
- Selecting the wrong Pay Matrix Level.
- Ignoring the Date of Next Increment.
- Using outdated Pay Matrix tables.

---

# Conclusion

Rule 12 of the Sikkim Services (Revised Pay) Rules, 2016 gives employees flexibility in choosing how their promotional pay is fixed. While immediate fixation provides instant financial relief, opting for fixation from the Date of Next Increment often results in higher long-term financial benefits.

Every promotion should therefore be accompanied by a careful comparison of both options before submitting the pay fixation form.

Understanding the rule thoroughly can significantly improve an employee's lifetime earnings and retirement benefits.
