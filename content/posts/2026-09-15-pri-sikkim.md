# Panchayati Raj in Sikkim: History, Structure and Current Situation

Panchayati Raj means democratic local self-government in rural areas. It allows village and district-level representatives to participate in planning, development, public services and management of local funds.

Sikkim follows a **two-tier Panchayati Raj system**:

1. **Gram Panchayat** at the village level.
2. **Zilla Panchayat** at the district level.

The modern system is based mainly on the **Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993**, enacted after the 73rd Constitutional Amendment. The State’s Panchayati Raj system also has older roots in traditional village administration, the Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1965 and the decentralisation measures of the 1980s.

As of the current post-2022 reorganisation, Sikkim has:

- **6 districts** for Panchayati Raj administration.
- **124 Zilla Panchayat Territorial Constituencies**.
- **199 Gram Panchayat Units**.
- **1,149 Gram Panchayat wards**.

The increase from 185 to 199 Gram Panchayat Units and from 113 to 124 Territorial Constituencies followed reorganisation of rural local bodies. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

***

# Part I: Meaning and Importance

## 1. What Is Panchayati Raj?

Panchayati Raj is a system in which rural residents elect representatives to manage local affairs.

The word can be understood as:

- **Panchayat:** A local council.
- **Raj:** Governance or administration.

In modern India, Panchayati Raj is not merely an informal village council. It is a constitutional system of local self-government.

### Simple explanation

Panchayati Raj means:

> People in villages choose representatives who help plan and implement development work in their own areas.

A Panchayat may be involved in:

- Rural roads.
- Drinking water.
- Sanitation.
- Local development.
- Poverty alleviation.
- Community assets.
- Social welfare.
- Local revenue.
- Scheme implementation.
- Identification of beneficiaries.

***

## 2. Why Is Panchayati Raj Important?

A State Government cannot personally understand every village-level problem.

Local representatives are closer to:

- Households.
- Farmers.
- Women’s groups.
- Youth.
- Senior citizens.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Local markets.
- Schools.
- Health centres.
- Roads and water sources.

Therefore, local self-government can improve:

- Participation.
- Responsiveness.
- Accountability.
- Social justice.
- Service delivery.
- Local planning.
- Monitoring of development schemes.

### Example

A State department may know that a village requires water infrastructure. The Gram Panchayat may know the more specific problem:

- Which households lack water.
- Which spring has dried up.
- Which road is needed for pipeline access.
- Which families are most vulnerable.
- Which land is available for a tank.

# Part II: Historical Development

## 3. Traditional Local Governance in Sikkim

The roots of local self-government in Sikkim are older than the modern Panchayat Acts.

During the period of the Namgyal dynasty:

- Local administration operated through traditional officials.
- Landlords were known as **Kazis**.
- **Mandals** and **Karbaris** assisted in local administration.
- Agricultural and revenue matters were managed locally.
- Taxes included the traditional **Dhuri Khazana**.

This was not democratic Panchayati Raj in the modern constitutional sense. However, it provided a local administrative structure through which village-level matters were handled. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Before elected Panchayats existed, Sikkim already had local institutions and officials who helped administer villages and collect revenue.

The important difference is that these institutions were based on traditional authority, not modern universal elections.

***

## 4. Panchayats After the Abolition of Zamindari

After the abolition of zamindari in **1948**, Panchayats were created in Sikkim.

The early Panchayats included:

- The former landlord or his representative.
- Four other members from the block.
- Members selected by local people in a village meeting.

These early institutions helped fill the administrative gap created after the zamindari system ended.

However, they were initially closer to **Nyaya Panchayats** or quasi-judicial bodies than to fully developed democratic local governments.

### What does quasi-judicial mean?

A quasi-judicial body performs limited functions similar to a court, such as helping settle local disputes or deciding certain local matters.

***

## 5. The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1965

The **Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1965** came into force in **December 1965**.

Its purpose was to consolidate and amend laws relating to Panchayats in Sikkim.

The main objectives were:

- Rural development.
- Local participation.
- Village-level administration.
- Community involvement.
- Resource mobilisation.
- Development of local institutions.

Under the 1965 system:

- The Panchayat term was **three years**.
- Panchayats had **16 duties and functions**.
- They received funds from taxes, cesses, land revenue and grants.

### Sources of funds

The resources included:

- House tax.
- Part of land revenue.
- **10 per cent of the land revenue of the block**.
- Sanitation cess.
- Water cess.
- Matching grants for original works.
- Public contributions.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The 1965 Act made Panchayats more organised. It also gave them functions and financial resources, which created the need for proper accounting and audit.

***

## 6. Decentralisation in the 1980s

A new decentralisation law was introduced in **1982**.

It envisaged the constitution of **Zilla Panchayats at the district level**. This was an important development because it extended local government beyond the village level.

### Why district-level Panchayats were needed

A Gram Panchayat could manage local village matters, but some activities required coordination across several villages:

- Major roads.
- Larger water schemes.
- Health facilities.
- Agricultural development.
- District-level planning.
- Block and inter-village projects.

### Simple explanation

The 1982 development recognised that rural planning needed both:

- Village-level institutions.
- District-level institutions.

***

## 7. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 gave Panchayati Raj Institutions constitutional status.

It introduced or strengthened:

- Part IX of the Constitution.
- Gram Sabha.
- Regular elections.
- Five-year terms.
- Reservation for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
- Reservation for women.
- State Election Commissions.
- State Finance Commissions.
- Panchayat planning.
- Constitutional recognition of local self-government.

The Eleventh Schedule contains **29 subjects** that may be entrusted to Panchayats.

These subjects include:

- Agriculture.
- Minor irrigation.
- Animal husbandry.
- Rural housing.
- Drinking water.
- Roads.
- Rural electrification.
- Poverty alleviation.
- Education.
- Health and sanitation.
- Women and child development.
- Public distribution.
- Maintenance of community assets.

### Simple explanation

The 73rd Amendment transformed Panchayats from ordinary administrative bodies into constitutionally recognised institutions of self-government.

***

## 8. The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993

The Government of Sikkim enacted the **Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993** to implement the constitutional framework.

It was:

- Act No. 6 of 1993.
- Assented to on **11 October 1993**.
- Published through notification dated **18 October 1993**.

The Act was intended to:

- Consolidate laws relating to Panchayats.
- Organise Panchayats as units of self-government.
- Establish Gram Panchayats.
- Establish Zilla Panchayats.
- Provide for elections.
- Define powers and functions.
- Create funds and budgets.
- Provide for accounts and audit.
- Establish a State Finance Commission.
- Establish a State Election Commission.

 [indiacode.nic](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/17087/1/the_sikkim_panchayat_act_1993.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The 1993 Act is the main legal foundation of the present Panchayati Raj system in Sikkim.

# Part III: Structure of Panchayati Raj in Sikkim

## 9. Two-Tier System

Sikkim has a two-tier Panchayati Raj structure.

| Tier | Institution | Area covered |
|---|---|---|
| First tier | Gram Panchayat | Village or group of villages |
| Second tier | Zilla Panchayat | District |

Unlike some States, Sikkim does not have a separate intermediate or block-level elected Panchayat tier.

There are administrative Block Development Offices, but the elected Panchayati Raj structure consists mainly of Gram Panchayats and Zilla Panchayats.

### Simple explanation

The Gram Panchayat works closest to the people. The Zilla Panchayat coordinates larger district-level development.

***

## 10. Gram Panchayat

A Gram Panchayat is the village-level institution of self-government.

The Sikkim Panchayat Act provides that each Panchayat area should have an institution called the Gram Panchayat.

A Gram Panchayat may consist of:

- Elected members from wards.
- A Sabhapati.
- An Up-Sabhapati.
- A Sachiva.
- Other functionaries and supporting staff.

The Act provides for between **five and nine elected members**, depending on the population and government determination. [indiacode.nic](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/17087/1/the_sikkim_panchayat_act_1993.pdf)

### Main responsibilities

A Gram Panchayat may be involved in:

- Village roads.
- Drainage.
- Drinking water.
- Sanitation.
- Community assets.
- Local development schemes.
- Beneficiary identification.
- Local taxation and fees.
- Maintenance of records.
- Implementation of government programmes.

***

## 11. Zilla Panchayat

The Zilla Panchayat operates at the district level.

It coordinates development among Gram Panchayats and may perform functions requiring a wider geographical approach.

A Zilla Panchayat may be involved in:

- District development planning.
- Inter-village roads.
- Larger public works.
- District-level schemes.
- Coordination among Gram Panchayats.
- Supervision and support.
- Consolidation of plans.
- Management of district-level funds.
- Monitoring of programme implementation.

The elected head of a Zilla Panchayat is called the **Adhakshya**, while the deputy head is the **Up-Adhakshya**.

***

## 12. Gram Sabha

The Gram Sabha is the assembly of voters in a Gram Panchayat area.

It is one of the most important institutions in local democracy because it provides direct public participation.

Under the Sikkim Panchayat Act:

- The Gram Sabha should meet at least **twice a year**.
- The quorum is **one-tenth of the total membership**.
- It may consider annual accounts.
- It may examine the previous year’s administration report.
- It may consider the latest audit note and replies.
- It may discuss development programmes.
- It may help identify beneficiaries.
- It may assist in development schemes.

 [indiacode.nic](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/17087/1/the_sikkim_panchayat_act_1993.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The Gram Sabha is not the same as the Gram Panchayat.

- **Gram Sabha:** All eligible voters of the area.
- **Gram Panchayat:** The elected body that manages local administration.

### Why Gram Sabha matters

It can improve:

- Transparency.
- Social audit.
- Beneficiary selection.
- Public participation.
- Local monitoring.
- Accountability of elected representatives.

***

## 13. Meetings of the Gram Panchayat

The Sikkim Panchayat Act provides that a Gram Panchayat should ordinarily hold a meeting at least **twice every month**.

Important meeting controls include:

- Notice to members.
- Agenda.
- Quorum.
- Recording of minutes.
- Decision by majority.
- Disclosure of conflict of interest.
- Circulation of minutes.
- Follow-up of decisions.

### Simple explanation

Regular meetings ensure that the Panchayat does not function informally or through undocumented decisions.

### Internal-control importance

The minutes should clearly show:

- What issue was discussed.
- Who attended.
- What decision was taken.
- Who was responsible.
- What amount was approved.
- What action is required.
- When the matter will be reviewed.

***

## 14. Sabhapati and Up-Sabhapati

The **Sabhapati** is the elected head of the Gram Panchayat.

The Sabhapati generally:

- Presides over meetings.
- Supervises Panchayat activities.
- Oversees records and registers.
- Supervises officials and employees.
- Operates the Panchayat fund jointly with the Sachiva.
- Authorises payments jointly.
- Issues receipts for money received.
- Ensures preparation of reports and statements.

The **Up-Sabhapati** assists the Sabhapati and performs duties during the Sabhapati’s absence.

### Important financial control

The Gram Panchayat fund is operated jointly by the Sabhapati and Sachiva.

This is an example of **segregation of duties** and **joint control**.

***

## 15. Sachiva

The Sachiva performs an important administrative and accounting role.

The Sachiva may be responsible for:

- Maintaining records.
- Preparing minutes.
- Preparing statements.
- Keeping accounts.
- Preparing receipts and payment documents.
- Supporting meetings.
- Maintaining registers.
- Corresponding with higher authorities.
- Preparing reports.
- Assisting in scheme implementation.

### Why the Sachiva is important

The elected members make policy and local decisions, while the Sachiva helps maintain administrative continuity and records.

A strong system requires cooperation between:

- Elected representatives.
- Sachiva.
- Accounts staff.
- Technical staff.
- Block officials.
- District officials.

***

# Part IV: Functions and Powers

## 16. Functions of the Gram Sabha

The Gram Sabha may:

- Consider the annual statement of accounts.
- Review the administration report.
- Consider the latest audit note.
- Discuss development programmes.
- Recommend beneficiaries.
- Mobilise voluntary labour.
- Mobilise contributions.
- Assist in implementing village development schemes.
- Promote community participation.

### Simple explanation

The Gram Sabha helps citizens examine how money was used and how future development work should be selected.

### Audit connection

A local fund audit should examine whether:

- Gram Sabha meetings occurred.
- Accounts were placed before the Gram Sabha.
- Audit observations were disclosed.
- Beneficiary selection was documented.
- Minutes were genuine and complete.
- Resolutions were followed.

***

## 17. Duties of Gram Panchayat

The Sikkim Panchayat Act divides Gram Panchayat responsibilities into:

- Obligatory duties.
- Other duties.

The exact functions may be changed or supplemented through rules, notifications and government orders.

Typical responsibilities may include:

- Public health.
- Sanitation.
- Village roads.
- Water supply.
- Drainage.
- Maintenance of community assets.
- Rural development.
- Scheme implementation.
- Local records.
- Public convenience.
- Social welfare support.

### Important point

Not every function is automatically transferred in the same way. The practical extent of Panchayat responsibility depends on:

- State notifications.
- Departmental orders.
- Funds transferred.
- Staff transferred.
- Technical capacity.
- Scheme guidelines.

***

## 18. Zilla Panchayat Functions

A Zilla Panchayat may:

- Prepare district-level development plans.
- Coordinate Gram Panchayat plans.
- Implement district schemes.
- Supervise or support Gram Panchayats.
- Execute larger works.
- Manage district-level funds.
- Promote economic development.
- Support social justice programmes.
- Coordinate line departments.

The Act allows a Zilla Panchayat to supervise Gram Panchayats and to jointly execute schemes where more than one Panchayat is involved.

### Simple explanation

A Zilla Panchayat provides the district-level coordination that individual Gram Panchayats cannot provide alone.

***

## 19. Devolution of the Three Fs

Effective local self-government requires devolution of the three Fs:

1. **Functions** — What the Panchayat is allowed to do.
2. **Functionaries** — Staff required to perform the work.
3. **Funds** — Money required to perform the function.

The Government of Sikkim reports that powers and functions have been devolved to Panchayats and that administrative support includes:

- **8 Offices of Additional District Collector (Development)** in sub-divisions.
- **33 Block Development Offices** across the State.

The activities transferred to Zilla and Gram Panchayats were notified through a 2008 notification. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Giving a Panchayat a responsibility without money or staff does not create real decentralisation.

For example:

- If a Panchayat is responsible for water supply but has no funds, it cannot perform the function.
- If it has funds but no technical staff, execution may be weak.
- If it has staff but no decision-making authority, it remains dependent on higher departments.

# Part V: Elections and Representation

## 20. First Panchayat Election Under the 1993 Act

The first Panchayat elections under the Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993 were held in **1997**.

The Government of Sikkim described the 2022–2027 body as the **sixth succession** under the 1993 Act. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

### Electoral cycle

The historical structure has changed through delimitation and reorganisation.

| Period | Districts | Zilla Territorial Constituencies | Gram Panchayat Units | Gram Panchayat wards |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1997–2002 | 4 | 92 | 159 | 873 |
| 2002–2007 | 4 | 100 | 164 | 905 |
| 2007–2012 | 4 | 95 | 163 | 891 |
| 2012–2017 | 4 | 110 | 176 | 986 |
| 2017–2022 | 4 | 113 | 185 | 1,040 |
| 2022–2027 | 6 | 124 | 199 | 1,149 |

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/SikkimHerald/January_23_0_20240123.pdf)

### Observation

Between 1997 and 2022:

- Gram Panchayat Units increased from 159 to 199.
- Zilla Territorial Constituencies increased from 92 to 124.
- Gram Panchayat wards increased from 873 to 1,149.
- The number of districts increased from 4 to 6.

This reflects the expansion and reorganisation of rural local governance.

***

## 21. The 2022 Panchayat Election

The 2022 Panchayat election was significant because it was conducted under a **party-less system**.

The State amended Section 129 of the Sikkim Panchayat Act to provide for non-party-based Panchayat elections.

The election results included:

- **102 Zilla Panchayat Territorial Constituencies** contested or declared through the reported process.
- **695 Gram Panchayat wards** with declared results.
- **19 Zilla seats** declared uncontested.
- **448 Gram Panchayat wards** with uncontested winners.
- Four seats reportedly decided by coin toss after tied votes.

 [sikkimexpress](https://www.sikkimexpress.com/news-details/sikkim-panchayat-polls-results-declared)

### Important caution

The figures above relate to the reported election process and should be read with the final official election records for complete constituency-wise details.

### Meaning of party-less election

Candidates contest without formal political-party symbols.

The intended advantages may include:

- Greater focus on local issues.
- Reduced party polarisation.
- More opportunities for independent candidates.
- Greater emphasis on personal reputation.
- Local rather than state-level campaign issues.

Possible challenges include:

- Informal political affiliations may still exist.
- Voters may have less information about policy positions.
- Accountability may be difficult if collective responsibility is unclear.

# Part VI: Special Traditional Institutions

## 23. The Dzumsas of Lachung and Lachen

Sikkim has two traditional institutions of self-governance in North Sikkim:

- Lachung Dzumsa.
- Lachen Dzumsa.

The head of a Dzumsa is known as the **Pippon**, selected by the public.

The Dzumsas were treated as Gram Panchayat Units for the purposes of the Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993. They were allowed to continue exercising traditional powers along with Gram Panchayat functions. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/ag/sikkim/en/page-ag-sikkim-local-bodies-introduction)

### Simple explanation

Sikkim’s Panchayati Raj system recognises that some areas have traditional local institutions with deep social legitimacy.

The Dzumsas are an example of combining:

- Constitutional local government.
- Local tradition.
- Community-based administration.

### Audit and accountability issue

Where traditional procedures operate alongside statutory Panchayat functions, the following should be clear:

- Which law applies.
- Who controls public funds.
- Who maintains accounts.
- Which decisions require formal records.
- How audit is conducted.
- How public assets are protected.
- How beneficiaries are selected.

Tradition should be respected, but public money must still be accounted for.

# Part VII: Panchayat Finance

## 24. Sources of Panchayat Funds

Panchayat funds may include:

- Own-source revenue.
- Government grants.
- Finance Commission grants.
- Scheme funds.
- Taxes.
- Fees.
- Cesses.
- Public contributions.
- Loans or deposits where permitted.
- Income from local assets.

Under the historical 1965 framework, Sikkim Panchayats received house tax, land-revenue shares, cesses, grants and public contributions.

Under the modern system, a large portion of Panchayat resources comes through:

- State Government grants.
- Central Government schemes.
- Finance Commission grants.
- Departmental transfers.

### Simple explanation

Most rural local bodies do not have enough own revenue to finance all responsibilities. They depend significantly on grants and transfers.

***

## 25. Finance Commission Grants

The Finance Commission system provides financial support to local bodies.

The **Fifteenth Finance Commission grants** generally include:

- Untied grants.
- Tied grants.

### Untied grants

Untied grants allow local bodies to address local priorities, subject to applicable rules.

### Tied grants

Tied grants are generally directed toward basic services such as:

- Drinking water.
- Sanitation.
- Waste management.
- ODF status.
- Rainwater harvesting.
- Water recycling.
- Fecal-sludge management.

A January 2026 report stated that the Union Government released the first instalment of Fifteenth Finance Commission untied grants amounting to approximately **₹6.43 crore** for Sikkim’s rural local bodies for 2025–26, covering five eligible District Panchayats and all 199 Gram Panchayats. [psuconnect](https://www.psuconnect.in/ministry-of-india-news/centre-releases-6-43-crore-in-first-instalment-of-xv-finance-commission-grants-to-rural-bodies-in-sikkim)

Another report stated that the second instalment for 2025–26 was approximately **₹6.6 crore**, covering six district panchayats and 199 Gram Panchayats, along with an additional eligible district panchayat allocation. [english.mathrubhumi](https://english.mathrubhumi.com/news/india/centre-releases-rs-137-crore-rural-local-bodies-4-states-v5mqmsrg)

### Caution on figures

Finance Commission releases may differ depending on:

- Instalment.
- Eligibility.
- Revised allocation.
- Additional district Panchayat coverage.
- Official release date.
- Reporting source.

The latest sanction order and official Ministry of Panchayati Raj or PIB release should be used for an official financial statement.

# Part VIII: Panchayati Raj and Audit

## 28. Audit of Panchayat Accounts

Audit is necessary because Panchayats manage public money.

The Sikkim Panchayat Act includes an audit chapter covering:

- Audit of funds.
- Submission of accounts.
- Powers of auditors.
- Penalty.
- Audit reports.
- Action on audit reports.
- Surcharge.
- Appeal.
- Payment of certified sums.

The Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012 further created a comprehensive statutory framework for auditing local funds.

### Simple explanation

Panchayats must:

1. Prepare accounts.
2. Submit accounts for audit.
3. Produce supporting records.
4. Respond to audit findings.
5. Correct irregularities.
6. Recover losses where required.
7. Face escalation for unresolved matters.

***

## 29. Role of the Director of Local Fund Audit

The Director of Local Fund Audit, or DLFA, is the primary State-level auditor of local funds.

The Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act gives the audit authority power to:

- Audit local-body accounts.
- Conduct annual audit.
- Conduct concurrent or special audit.
- Examine stock and stores.
- Verify cash.
- Demand documents.
- Require attendance of responsible officials.
- Issue audit reports.
- Follow up on objections.
- Report defalcation or loss.
- Submit consolidated reports to Government.
- Support legislative accountability.

The Act provides that accounts should generally be presented within **three months** after the end of the financial year and audit should normally be completed within **six months** from presentation. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

***

## 30. Relationship With the CAG

Sikkim uses a model involving both:

- The Directorate of Local Fund Audit.
- The Comptroller and Auditor General of India.

The State Government entrusted audit of Panchayati Raj Institutions to the CAG under Section 20(1) of the CAG’s Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service Act, 1971 in **June 2011**, under a Technical Guidance and Supervision arrangement. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/ag/sikkim/en/page-ag-sikkim-local-bodies-introduction)

### Simple explanation

The DLFA is the main State-level local-body auditor, while the CAG may provide:

- Technical guidance.
- Supervision.
- Quality review.

# Part XII: Panchayati Raj and the 3Es

## 48. Economy in Sikkim Panchayats

Economy asks whether Panchayats obtained inputs at reasonable cost.

### Audit questions

- Were materials purchased at reasonable rates?
- Were quotations or tenders obtained?
- Were specifications suitable?
- Was the purchase necessary?
- Was there overstocking?
- Were funds spent before year-end without actual need?
- Were similar purchases split?
- Were life-cycle costs considered?

### Example

A Panchayat purchases a water pump for ₹1.2 lakh. Similar pumps cost ₹90,000, but the Panchayat selected the higher-cost pump without a technical justification.

The possible economy concern is:

\[
₹1,20,000 - ₹90,000 = ₹30,000
\]

The auditor must still examine whether the higher-priced pump had superior quality or lower maintenance cost.

***

## 49. Efficiency in Sikkim Panchayats

Efficiency asks whether Panchayats converted resources into outputs with reasonable waste and delay.

### Audit questions

- Was the project completed on time?
- Were materials used efficiently?
- Were assets idle?
- Were staff and machinery utilised?
- Was the service delivered within the prescribed time?
- Did poor planning increase costs?
- Were works repeatedly extended?

### Example

A Gram Panchayat receives ₹20 lakh for a community hall.

- Planned completion: 8 months.
- Actual completion: 22 months.
- Planned cost: ₹20 lakh.
- Revised cost: ₹27 lakh.

The audit should examine:

- Cause of delay.
- Price escalation.
- Contractor performance.
- Availability of funds.
- Technical changes.
- Land issues.
- Supervision.
- Whether the hall is being used.

***

## 50. Effectiveness in Sikkim Panchayats

Effectiveness asks whether the Panchayat achieved the intended public result.

### Audit questions

- Did the project serve the intended community?
- Are the assets functional?
- Are beneficiaries receiving the intended benefit?
- Did service quality improve?
- Was the target achieved?
- Is the result sustainable?

### Example

A Panchayat constructs a footbridge for a remote settlement.

The output is the bridge.

The outcome is safe and regular access.

The impact may be:

- Better access to school.
- Easier access to health services.
- Reduced travel time.
- Improved market access.

If the bridge is constructed but damaged before use, the project may not be effective.

# Part XIII: Current Governance Cycle

## 51. How a Panchayat Should Work

A sound local-governance cycle may be represented as follows:

```text
Citizens identify needs
        ↓
Gram Sabha discusses priorities
        ↓
Gram Panchayat prepares plan
        ↓
Zilla Panchayat coordinates district priorities
        ↓
Funds and staff are assigned
        ↓
Work or scheme is implemented
        ↓
Records and accounts are maintained
        ↓
Citizens monitor progress
        ↓
Audit examines legality, finance and performance
        ↓
Panchayat corrects defects
        ↓
Results are reviewed
```

This cycle connects democracy, planning, finance, implementation and audit.

# Part XV: Simple Explanation of the Current Situation

## 56. What Is the Present Position?

In simple terms, Panchayati Raj in Sikkim is constitutionally established and institutionally active.

Its important features are:

- A two-tier rural local-government system.
- Gram Panchayats at village level.
- Zilla Panchayats at district level.
- Six current districts.
- 199 Gram Panchayat Units.
- 124 Zilla Territorial Constituencies.
- 1,149 Gram Panchayat wards.
- Party-less Panchayat elections in 2022.
- 50 per cent reservation for women in the 2022–2027 cycle.
- Traditional Dzumsas recognised as local institutions.
- Devolution of functions through the Rural Development Department.
- Support from Block Development Offices and district authorities.
- Panchayat planning and capacity building through SIRD&PR.
- Audit through the State local fund audit framework and CAG-related arrangements.
- Continued dependence on government and Finance Commission grants.
- Ongoing challenges in finance, capacity, participation, audit follow-up and service outcomes.

The major strengths include:

- Constitutional recognition.

# Part XVI: Conclusion

Panchayati Raj in Sikkim has evolved through several stages:

1. Traditional village administration under the Namgyal system.
2. Panchayat development after the abolition of zamindari in 1948.
3. The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1965.
4. District-level decentralisation in the 1980s.
5. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment.
6. The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993.
7. Regular Panchayat elections from 1997.
8. Reorganisation and expansion of local bodies in 2022.
9. Continued devolution of functions, funds and functionaries.

The current system is significant because it combines:

- Constitutional local democracy.
- Village-level participation.
- District-level coordination.
- Traditional institutions.
- State-supported development.
- Finance Commission funding.
- Local fund audit.
- Citizen accountability.

The future success of Panchayati Raj in Sikkim will depend not only on the number of Panchayats or elected representatives. It will depend on whether the institutions can:

- Prepare realistic plans.
- Receive adequate funds.
- Exercise real authority.
- Maintain reliable accounts.
- Deliver quality services.
- Protect public assets.
- Include women and vulnerable groups.
- Respond to citizens.
- Correct audit findings.
- Achieve measurable development results.

The core principle is:

> Panchayati Raj becomes meaningful when local people have not only elected representatives, but also real decision-making power, adequate resources and effective accountability.
