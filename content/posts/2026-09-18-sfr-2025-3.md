# Sikkim Financial Rules 2025 and the Extraordinary Government Vehicle Rules

Sikkim introduced the **Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025** with effect from **1 April 2025**. The Rules provide the general framework for Government receipts, expenditure, sanctions, budget control, procurement, DDO responsibilities, audit, accounts and financial propriety.

A separate **Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025** was published in an Extraordinary Gazette dated **23 July 2025** and published on **24 July 2025** as Gazette No. 303. These rules regulate the procurement, repair, maintenance, use, user charges, condemnation and disposal of Government vehicles. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

A related **Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025** was published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 57 on **25 March 2025**. It revised additional tax rates for registration and certain transfers of vehicles. [archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.gazette.sikkim.2025-03-25.57_20250801/57_20250801_djvu.txt)

This article explains the three instruments separately:

1. Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
2. Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025.
3. Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025.

> **Important:** This is an explanatory guide. Before approving expenditure, purchasing a vehicle, passing a repair bill or calculating vehicle tax, verify the latest Gazette, Finance Department circular, Transport Department instruction and applicable amendment.

***

# 1. Three Different 2025 Instruments

These instruments are connected with Government finance and vehicles, but they are not the same law.

| Instrument | Main subject | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 | Government financial administration | Controls receipts, expenditure, sanctions, bills, DDOs, procurement and accounts |
| Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025 | Government vehicles | Controls purchase, repair, maintenance, user charges and disposal |
| Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025 | Motor-vehicle taxation | Revises additional tax on vehicle registration and certain transfers |

### Simple explanation

- The **Financial Rules** explain how Government money should be managed.
- The **Vehicle Rules** explain how Government vehicles should be purchased and maintained.
- The **Motor Vehicles Taxation Act** explains additional vehicle tax payable during registration or certain transfers.

# Part I: Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025

## 2. Notification and Commencement

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 were notified by the Finance Department through Notification No. 1/FIN/ADM dated **1 April 2025**.

The Rules:

- Are called the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
- Extend to the whole of Sikkim.
- Apply from 1 April 2025.
- Apply to State Government departments and offices.
- Apply to subordinate authorities.
- Apply to autonomous bodies under State administrative control unless approved separate financial rules exist.

 [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Simple explanation

From 1 April 2025, these Rules became the main general financial framework for the Sikkim Government.

***

## 3. Applicability

The Rules apply to:

- State Government departments.
- Government offices.
- Subordinate authorities.
- Autonomous bodies under State administrative control.
- Other institutions covered by Government orders.

An autonomous body may follow separate financial rules only when:

- Its bye-laws provide for separate rules.
- The separate rules are approved by the State Government.
- The Finance Department gives prior concurrence.

### Practical implication

A DDO should not assume that an autonomous body can use its own procedure without checking whether that procedure was formally approved.

***

## 4. Definitions

The Rules define important terms.

### Accounts Officer

An officer of the Sikkim Finance and Accounts Service performing accounts and finance functions.

### Administrative approval

Formal acceptance by the competent authority of a proposal for expenditure, project, scheme, supply or work.

Administrative approval:

- Accepts the need for the proposal.
- Approves the broad proposal.
- States the estimated amount.
- Does not by itself authorise payment.

### Financial sanction

Approval required before actual drawal of funds, subject to budget provision.

### DDO

The Rules define the DDO as an officer from the Sikkim Finance and Accounts Service declared by the Finance Department, on the recommendation of the Head of Department, to draw bills and make payments on behalf of the State Government.

Where no such officer is available:

1. Another officer may discharge the function.
2. If no suitable officer is available, the Head of Office may act as DDO.
3. The Head of Department may also be the DDO where authorised.

 [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Local body

An authority legally entitled or specifically empowered by Government to administer a local fund.

### Local fund audit

Audit carried out by the Directorate of Local Fund Audit on funds managed by local bodies.

### Public works

Civil, electrical or mechanical works, including:

- Public buildings.
- Public services.
- Transport infrastructure.
- Original works.
- Repair works.
- Infrastructure projects.

### Simple explanation

The definitions section tells officers what the important words mean before the rest of the Rules are applied.

***

## 5. Interdepartmental Consultation

When a financial proposal concerns more than one department:

- All concerned departments should be consulted.
- No final order should normally be issued until the departments agree.
- If agreement is not reached, the matter should be decided by the competent authority or Cabinet process.

### Example

A vehicle proposal may involve:

- Administrative department.
- Transport Department.
- Finance Department.
- Home Department.
- Chief Secretary or other approving authority.

The proposal should not be processed as if it concerns only one office.

***

## 6. Finance Department Concurrence

Departmental instructions having financial implications must be issued:

- By the Finance Department; or
- With the prior approval and concurrence of the Finance Department.

### Simple explanation

A department should not independently create a new financial practice that affects Government expenditure without Finance Department involvement.

***

## 7. Removal of Doubts and Modification

Where an officer is unsure how to interpret the Rules:

- The matter should be referred to the Finance Department.
- The Finance Department’s decision is authoritative for the interpretation.

The Finance Department may issue:

- General instructions.
- Special instructions.
- Clarifications.
- Amendments.
- Procedural orders.

### DDO control

Maintain a file of:

- Clarification orders.
- Finance Department circulars.
- Amendments.
- Departmental instructions.
- Important legal opinions.

***

# Part II: Government Receipts and Revenue

## 8. Government Money Must Be Credited Promptly

All money received on behalf of Government should be:

- Collected lawfully.
- Recorded.
- Deposited without delay.
- Credited to the correct Government account.
- Reconciled with bank records.

This includes:

- Revenue.
- Fees.
- Fines.
- Deposits.
- Recoveries.
- User charges.
- Reimbursements.
- Other Government receipts.

## 9. Assessment and Collection

The Head of Department or Head of Office must ensure that money due to Government is:

- Assessed.
- Collected.
- Recorded.
- Credited under the correct head.
- Monitored for arrears.

Monthly returns should show:

- Amount due.
- Amount collected.
- Arrears.
- Reasons for delay.
- Recovery action.

### Simple explanation

Government should not merely record money that arrives. It should also monitor money that is due but has not yet been collected.

***

## 10. Receipt Books

Where officers receive money:

- Receipt books should be controlled.
- Issue of receipt books should be recorded.
- Used receipt books should be returned.
- Receipt numbers should be checked.
- The receipt-book register should be reconciled with the officer’s accounts.

### Audit risk

- Missing receipt.
- Duplicate receipt.
- Cancelled receipt not accounted for.
- Collection not deposited.
- Receipt book issued without authorisation.

***

## 11. Depositing Government Money

The Rules prescribe procedures for depositing money into Government accounts.

A challan should identify:

- Nature of payment.
- Name of payer.
- Department or office.
- Correct account classification.
- Amount.
- Date.
- Bank details.

Separate challans should generally be used for different account heads.

For cash deposits:

- Up to ₹5,000 may be accepted under the specified procedure without insisting on a departmental challan.
- Deposits above ₹5,000 and up to ₹50,000 require the prescribed challan process.
- Cash above ₹50,000 at a time should generally be deposited through banking instruments or electronic means.

The exact procedure should be read with the current Finance Department instructions and bank arrangements. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Correct classification is as important as depositing the money. A receipt credited under the wrong head creates accounting and reconciliation problems.

***

# Part III: Government Expenditure

## 12. Essential Conditions for Expenditure

The Sikkim Financial Rules provide that:

- Expenditure must be sanctioned.
- The authority must have power to sanction it.
- Budget provision must be available.
- The payment must be for a legitimate public purpose.
- The expenditure should not exceed the available appropriation.
- The payment should not be split to avoid approval limits.

The Rules specifically state that budget provision alone does not authorise expenditure. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Simple explanation

A valid expenditure needs both:

1. Money in the budget.
2. Approval from the competent authority.

***

## 13. No Splitting of Expenditure

Suppose a complete purchase is worth ₹12 lakh, but the officer’s approval limit is ₹5 lakh.

The office should not divide the purchase into:

- ₹4 lakh.
- ₹4 lakh.
- ₹4 lakh.

to avoid referring the full proposal to a higher authority.

The Rules prohibit splitting a single item or scheme merely to bring each part within a lower delegated power. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

***

## 14. Financial Propriety

Every officer authorising expenditure should ask:

- Is the expenditure necessary?
- Is the amount reasonable?
- Is the quality suitable?
- Is the price fair?
- Was the procurement process fair?
- Is there any personal benefit?
- Is the expenditure for public interest?
- Has the reasoning been recorded?

The Rules state that choosing the cheapest offer alone does not discharge financial responsibility. The selected offer must also meet the requirement and provide reasonable quality at a reasonable price. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Simple example

Supplier A quotes ₹90,000 but offers poor-quality equipment without warranty.

Supplier B quotes ₹1,00,000 and offers compliant equipment with a five-year warranty.

The DDO may select Supplier B if the decision is properly justified and approved.

***

# Part IV: Responsibilities

## 15. Accounts Officer

The Accounts Officer is responsible for:

- Applying financial rules.
- Advising the department.
- Vetted proposals involving financial implications.
- Identifying irregularities.
- Advising the Head of Department.
- Referring unresolved differences to the Finance Department.

The Rules state that Accounts Officer advice is obligatory before expenditure is sanctioned. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The Accounts Officer is the financial-control point before the proposal reaches final approval.

***

## 16. Drawing Officer or DDO

The DDO is responsible for:

- Correct bill preparation.
- Immediate need for funds.
- Expenditure within appropriation.
- Correct deductions.
- Supporting vouchers.
- Proper accounting.
- Timely submission of accounts.
- Avoiding duplicate payment.
- Preserving records.
- Reporting irregularities.

The DDO should not sign a bill merely because someone else has recommended it.

***

## 17. Controlling Authority

The Controlling Authority should verify:

- Necessity of expenditure.
- Fair and reasonable rates.
- Previous sanction.
- Supporting vouchers.
- Correct calculations.
- Grant availability.
- Speed of expenditure.
- Risk of excess.

The Rules specifically assign responsibility for checking vouchers, calculations and whether grants have been exceeded. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

***

## 18. Head of Department and Head of Office

The Head of Department or Head of Office must ensure that:

- Expenditure does not exceed allocation.
- Expenditure is used for the sanctioned purpose.
- Expenditure is in public interest.
- Internal controls are functioning.
- Liabilities are monitored.
- Finance Department is informed about expenditure and commitments.
- Audit findings can be explained.

The Rules require Heads of Department and controlling officers to maintain liability information and review expenditure periodically. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

***

# Part V: Sanction and Delegation

## 19. Delegated Financial Powers

A subordinate authority can exercise only the powers delegated to it.

Every sanction should show:

- Amount.
- Purpose.
- Budget head.
- Grant or appropriation.
- Competent authority.
- Financial year.
- Conditions.
- Source of funds.

## 20. No Unauthorised Re-Delegation

A delegated power should not be passed down further unless Finance Department concurrence or another authorised procedure permits it.

### Simple explanation

An officer cannot automatically give another officer the power that was delegated to the first officer.

## 21. Finance Department Powers

Financial powers not delegated to subordinate authorities remain with the Finance Department.

A proposal outside delegated powers should include:

- Budget provision.
- Earlier liabilities.
- Current-year liabilities.
- Total liabilities.
- Expenditure incurred.
- Balance available.
- Pending proposals.
- Amount of the current proposal.

***

# Part VI: Projects and Works

## 22. Scheme and Project Approval

New schemes or projects outside delegated powers require Finance Department concurrence.

A project proposal should include:

- Background.
- Objective.
- Estimated cost.
- Funding source.
- Site details.
- Drawings.
- Photographs.
- Implementation plan.
- Procurement method.
- Expected completion date.
- Recurring expenditure.
- Maintenance plan.
- Expected public benefit.

The Rules specifically require detailed project information for construction, renovation, upgradation and proposals involving substantial financial implications. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Sikkim%20Financial%20Rules%202025%20Gazette%20No.%2066.pdf)

## 23. Works Sanction Chain

```text
Need identification
        ↓
Administrative approval
        ↓
Financial sanction
        ↓
Technical sanction
        ↓
Budget and resource release
        ↓
Procurement
        ↓
Agreement
        ↓
Work order
        ↓
Measurement
        ↓
Running bill
        ↓
Final bill
        ↓
Completion and maintenance
```

### Important distinction

Administrative approval does not itself authorise payment.

Financial sanction allows expenditure within budget.

Technical sanction approves the technical design, estimate and specifications.

# Part VII: Sikkim Government Vehicle Rules, 2025

## 24. Gazette and Commencement

The **Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025** were notified by the Finance Department as Gazette No. 303.

The notification was dated **23 July 2025** and published on **24 July 2025**.

The Rules came into force on publication in the Official Gazette. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

## 25. Purpose

The Vehicle Rules regulate:

- Procurement of new vehicles.
- Allotment and entitlement.
- Repair.
- Maintenance.
- Servicing.
- Tyre replacement.
- Battery and accessories.
- Vehicle history records.
- User charges.
- Accident repairs.
- Condemnation.
- Disposal.
- Public auction.

### Simple explanation

The Rules attempt to prevent uncontrolled vehicle purchases, excessive repair bills, unauthorised use and premature disposal.

***

## 26. Important definitions

The Rules define:

- Government vehicle.
- DDO.
- Head of Department.
- Head of Office.
- Departmental auto-workshop.
- Cumulative expenditure.
- History sheet.
- Year.

### Cumulative expenditure

Cumulative expenditure means the total progressive expenditure incurred on repair and maintenance of a vehicle.

### Simple explanation

The Government does not look only at the current repair bill. It also looks at how much has already been spent on that vehicle.

***

## 27. Vehicle Repair Ceilings

The Vehicle Rules specify ceiling limits for repair expenditure through a Schedule.

The Rules require Heads of Department, Heads of Office and DDOs to:

- Maintain a repair-expenditure register for each vehicle.
- Track cumulative expenditure.
- Ensure that the prescribed ceiling is not exceeded.
- Carry forward permitted savings where the Rules allow.
- Obtain technical certification where required.

### Schedule categories

The Schedule provides categories including:

- Two-wheelers.
- Light motor vehicles.
- SUVs.
- Utility vehicles.
- Pickup vans.
- Trucks.
- Buses.
- Heavy vehicles.
- Pool vehicles.
- Ambulances.
- Special categories.

The exact ceiling depends on:

- Vehicle type.
- Vehicle age.
- Distance run.
- Functional or non-functional status.
- Health or emergency use.
- Applicable schedule.

 [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Every vehicle has an approved repair-cost limit. The DDO must calculate both current and cumulative expenditure.

***

## 28. Repair Sanction Authority

Heads of Department and Heads of Office may exercise discretion to repair vehicles in authorised private garages where the repair cost does not exceed the limits prescribed in the Rules.

The Rules also require:

- Technical inspection.
- Authorised workshop.
- Proper sanction.
- History-sheet entry.
- Verification of bill.
- Compliance with the ceiling.

For repairs above the authorised limit, technical certification and higher approval may be required.

***

## 29. Mandatory Sanction Details for Repair Bills

The sanction order accompanying a vehicle-repair bill should mention:

- Date of vehicle purchase.
- Vehicle number.
- Amount already spent on repair and maintenance.
- Current repair amount.
- Cumulative distance covered.
- Applicable ceiling.
- Certification that expenditure is within the prescribed limit.

The Vehicle Rules state that the paying authority should not make payment if these details and the required ceiling certification are absent. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

### Simple explanation

A repair bill is incomplete if it shows only the garage invoice. It must also show the vehicle’s age, history, mileage and cumulative expenditure.

***

## 30. Vehicle History Sheet

Each Government vehicle should have a history sheet or history register.

It should record:

- Vehicle number.
- Make and model.
- Engine number.
- Chassis number.
- Date of purchase.
- Cost.
- Expected life.
- Repairs.
- Parts replaced.
- Servicing.
- Tyres.
- Battery.
- Seat covers.
- Cumulative expenditure.
- Mileage.
- DDO or Head of Office authentication.

The Rules require vehicle repair and maintenance records to be authenticated before payment is released. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

## 31. Servicing and Tyres

The Vehicle Rules prescribe limits or conditions for:

- Servicing.
- Lubricants.
- Furnishing.
- Tyres.
- Tubes.
- Batteries.
- Seat covers.
- Other accessories.

They also prescribe intervals or kilometre limits for certain servicing and tyre replacement categories.

### DDO checks

- [ ] Manufacturer’s free service exhausted.
- [ ] Service interval reached.
- [ ] Mileage verified.
- [ ] Tyre replacement interval reached.
- [ ] Number of tyres replaced justified.
- [ ] Warranty checked.
- [ ] History sheet updated.
- [ ] Bill within the applicable limit.

***

## 32. New Vehicle Procurement

The Vehicle Rules require concurrence of the Finance Department for procurement of new or replacement vehicles.

In specific cases, approval of the Home Department and sanction through the Chief Secretary or competent Government authority may also be required.

The department must also provide:

- Budget provision.
- Need justification.
- Entitlement.
- Vehicle category.
- Existing fleet position.
- Replacement reason.
- Maintenance implication.

### Simple explanation

A department cannot purchase a vehicle simply because it has money available. It must prove that the vehicle is needed and that the purchase is authorised.

***

## 33. Vehicle Entitlement

The Rules prescribe vehicle categories or models according to the level of officers and approved entitlement.

The relevant classifications include:

- Senior officers.
- Other Group A officers.
- Touring officers.
- Programme vehicles.
- Utility vehicles.
- Pool vehicles.
- Emergency vehicles.

The DDO should not approve a vehicle purchase outside the entitlement category without the required approval.

***

## 34. Vehicle User Charges

Where a Government vehicle is attached to an officer or public functionary and used for official and private purposes, user charges may be recovered from salary.

The Rules indicate monthly user charges by vehicle-cost category:

| Vehicle cost category | Monthly user charge |
|---|---:|
| Up to ₹10 lakh | ₹1,000 |
| More than ₹10 lakh up to ₹15 lakh | ₹1,500 |
| More than ₹15 lakh | ₹2,000 |

The Rules also state that shared users may pay charges individually and that officers using programme vehicles may be required to pay 50% of the applicable charge.

Certain vehicles are exempt, including:

- Vehicles attached to the Governor.
- Vehicles attached to the Chief Minister.
- Vehicles attached to the Chief Justice and Judges.
- Ambulances.
- Hearses.
- Buses.
- Trucks.
- Fire tenders.
- Vehicles attached to fire stations.
- Vehicles attached to police stations.
- Pilot and escort vehicles.

 [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

### DDO check

- [ ] Vehicle category identified.
- [ ] User charge calculated.
- [ ] Salary deduction made.
- [ ] Shared-use deduction handled.
- [ ] Exemption verified.
- [ ] Deduction credited under the correct head.

***

## 35. Accident Repairs

For accident-related repair expenditure exceeding **₹60,000**, the Rules require special examination and concurrence procedures.

The proposal should include:

- Technical report from Sikkim Nationalised Transport or competent authority.
- Police First Information Report.
- Police report.
- Date and time of accident.
- Name of driver.
- Inquiry report.
- Head of Department observations.
- Home Department concurrence.
- Finance Department concurrence.

The Vehicle Rules state that Home and Finance Department concurrence is required even where accident repair expenditure is within the normal vehicle ceiling. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

### Important restriction

Repair costs may not be accepted where:

- The accident occurred during unauthorised personal use.
- The vehicle was driven by an unauthorised driver.
- Required reports are missing.
- The cause of accident is not established.

***

## 36. Vehicle Condemnation

A Government vehicle should be condemned only after:

- Technical inspection.
- Certification that it is no longer economically usable.
- Valuation.
- Approval of the competent authority.
- Preparation of condemnation documents.
- Decision on disposal method.

The Rules require a technical certificate and prescribed procedure before condemnation.

## 37. Disposal of Vehicles

Vehicles ordered for disposal should generally be disposed of by public auction.

The procedure includes:

- Condemnation report.
- Valuation.
- Wide publicity.
- At least one month for participation, as specified.
- Attendance of an authorised officer.
- Recording of the final bid.
- Sale account.
- Credit of sale proceeds to Government account.

The Vehicle Rules prescribe forms for:

- Condemnation and valuation.
- Sale account.
- Auction and disposal records.

 [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/Gazette/Documents/Extraordinary%20Gazette%2001%20Aug%202025.pdf)

### Simple explanation

A condemned vehicle cannot simply be sold informally or handed over to a preferred person.

# Part VIII: Motor Vehicles Taxation Amendment Act, 2025

## 38. Gazette No. 57

The Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025:

- Was published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 57.
- Was dated 25 March 2025.
- Received Governor’s assent on 26 February 2025.
- Came into force on publication in the Official Gazette.
- Amended the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982.

 [archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.gazette.sikkim.2025-03-25.57_20250801/57_20250801_djvu.txt)

## 39. Additional tax on new vehicles

The Act introduced revised additional-tax rates based on invoice cost for:

- Private vehicles.
- Government vehicles.
- Public-sector undertaking vehicles.
- Non-transport two-wheelers.

| Invoice cost | Additional tax |
|---|---:|
| Up to ₹2 lakh | 1% |
| Above ₹2 lakh up to ₹4 lakh | 2% |
| Above ₹4 lakh up to ₹10 lakh | 3% |
| Above ₹10 lakh up to ₹25 lakh | 4% |
| Above ₹25 lakh up to ₹50 lakh | 5% |
| Above ₹50 lakh | 6% |

 [archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.gazette.sikkim.2025-03-25.57_20250801/57_20250801_djvu.txt)

### Example

Government vehicle invoice cost: ₹12,00,000

Applicable additional tax: 4%

$$Additional Tax = ₹12,00,000 \times 4\% = ₹48,000$$

## 40. Additional tax on commercial vehicles

The amendment provides categories including:

- Taxis and goods vehicles below ₹10 lakh: 1%.
- Taxis and goods vehicles above ₹10 lakh: 2%.
- Stage carriages or buses: 2%.
- Educational-institution buses and buses owned by companies or private organisations: 3%.
- Private service vehicles: 3%.
- Tractors, trailers, cranes and other equipment: 3%.
- Ambulances, fire tenders and public-utility vehicles: 2%.

The exact classification should be checked with the Transport Department or registering authority.

## 41. Transfer and new registration mark

For Sikkim-registered vehicles brought for transfer or replacement and allotment of a new registration mark, the Act provides fixed additional-tax amounts by engine capacity.

For example, motor-car categories include:

| Engine capacity | Additional tax |
|---|---:|
| Up to 900 cc | ₹10,000 |
| 901–1490 cc | ₹15,000 |
| 1491–2000 cc | ₹20,000 |
| Above 2000 cc | ₹25,000 |

For vehicles registered in other States and brought to Sikkim with an NOC, the Act provides for additional tax at 50% of the specified transfer rates.

## 42. Electric and hybrid vehicles

The Act provides:

- No additional tax on commercial electric, hybrid or battery-operated vehicles.
- 1% of invoice cost for non-transport electric, hybrid or battery-operated vehicles.

 [archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.gazette.sikkim.2025-03-25.57_20250801/57_20250801_djvu.txt)

### Important distinction

This is vehicle registration or motor-vehicle tax. It is separate from:

- GST.
- GST-TDS.
- Income-tax TDS.
- Vehicle user charge.
- Repair expenditure.
- Fuel cost.
- Road tax or other applicable charges.

# Part XII: Simple Explanation of the Main Changes

## 54. What changed from the DDO’s perspective?

### Earlier general system

The earlier financial system relied on older Financial Rules, amended instructions, departmental orders and separate manuals.

### 2025 Financial Rules

The 2025 Rules provide a consolidated and updated framework covering:

- Departments.
- Offices.
- DDOs.
- Accounts Officers.
- Local Fund Audit.
- SIFMS.
- Budget.
- Sanction.
- Financial propriety.
- Procurement.
- Liabilities.
- Projects.
- Public works.

### 2025 Vehicle Rules

The Vehicle Rules introduce a dedicated framework for:

- Vehicle procurement.
- Officer entitlement.
- Repair ceilings.
- History sheets.
- User charges.
- Accident repairs.
- Condemnation.
- Auction.

### 2025 Motor Vehicles Taxation amendment

The amendment changes additional tax for:

- New vehicle registration.
- Vehicle categories.
- Invoice-cost slabs.
- Transfer or replacement registration.
- Electric and hybrid vehicles.

# 55. Final Takeaway

A Sikkim Government officer should use the following sequence:

```text
Identify the transaction
        ↓
Select the correct rule
        ↓
Check current amendments
        ↓
Obtain administrative approval
        ↓
Obtain financial sanction
        ↓
Confirm budget and resources
        ↓
Follow procurement procedure
        ↓
Verify goods, work or vehicle
        ↓
Calculate taxes and recoveries
        ↓
Pass payment through the DDO
        ↓
Record in SIFMS and registers
        ↓
Retain evidence for audit
```

The most important controls are:

- No payment on administrative approval alone.
- No expenditure without financial sanction.
- No spending beyond appropriation.
- No splitting of expenditure.
- No vehicle repair beyond the prescribed ceiling without required certification.
- No vehicle purchase without required Finance Department concurrence.
- No vehicle disposal without technical certification and approved auction procedure.
- No user charge omission where applicable.
- No rule interpretation without referring doubts to the Finance Department.

> **Practical conclusion:** The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 control the broader financial system, while the Vehicle Rules, 2025 control Government-vehicle procurement and maintenance. The Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025 separately controls additional vehicle tax. DDOs must apply all three according to the nature of the transaction.
