# Sikkim Financial Rules 2025 and the Extraordinary Government Vehicle Rules

Sikkim introduced the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 with effect from 1 April 2025. The Rules establish the statutory framework for Government receipts, expenditure, sanctions, budget control, procurement, Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO) responsibilities, audit, accounts and financial propriety.

A separate regulatory instrument, the Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025, was published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 303 dated 23 July 2025 and issued on 24 July 2025. These rules govern the procurement, repair, maintenance, official use, user charges, condemnation and disposal of Government vehicles.

A related legislative enactment, the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025, was published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 57 on 25 March 2025. It revised additional tax rates for registration and specified transfers of motor vehicles across the State.

This guide examines these three distinct statutory instruments:

* The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
* The Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025.
* The Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025.

> **Important:** This guide serves an explanatory purpose. Before approving expenditure, acquiring a vehicle, passing an automotive repair bill or assessing motor vehicle tax, verify the latest Gazette notifications, Finance Department circulars, Transport Department instructions and applicable statutory amendments.

---

## 1. Overview of the Three 2025 Instruments

These statutory instruments govern State financial management and motor transport administration through distinct legal mechanisms.

| Statutory Instrument | Primary Subject | Statutory Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 | General public financial administration | Regulates receipts, expenditure, sanctions, billing, DDO responsibilities, procurement and accounting |
| Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025 | State Government vehicular fleet | Governs vehicle procurement, maintenance, repair ceilings, user charges, condemnation and disposal |
| Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025 | Motor vehicle revenue and taxation | Revises statutory additional tax on vehicle registration and ownership transfers |

The Financial Rules define the management of State funds; the Vehicle Rules govern public fleet procurement and maintenance; and the Motor Vehicles Taxation Act determines additional tax payable during vehicle registration or transfer.

## Part I: Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025

## 2. Notification and Commencement

The Finance Department notified the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 through Notification No. 1/FIN/ADM dated 1 April 2025 (published in Gazette No. 66).

The Rules:

* Are cited as the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
* Extend across the entire State of Sikkim.
* Came into force on 1 April 2025.
* Apply to State Government departments, offices and subordinate authorities.
* Apply to autonomous bodies under State administrative control, unless separate approved financial rules govern.

From 1 April 2025, these Rules replaced previous circulars and manuals as the primary administrative framework for State public finance.

---

## 3. Scope and Applicability

The Rules apply to:

* State Government departments.
* Subordinate and field offices.
* District collectorates and administrative subdivisions.
* Autonomous bodies under the administrative control of the State.
* Public institutions covered under executive orders of the State Government.

An autonomous body may operate under separate financial rules only when:

* Its registered bye-laws formally authorise separate financial regulations.
* The separate regulations are approved by the State Government.
* The Finance Department provides written prior concurrence.

A DDO cannot assume that an autonomous agency may adopt non-standard financial procedures without formal Government approval and Finance Department concurrence.

---

## 4. Statutory Definitions

The Rules define fundamental administrative and accounting terminology:

* **Accounts Officer:** An officer of the Sikkim Finance and Accounts Service performing designated accounting, financial advisory and control functions.
* **Administrative Approval:** Formal acceptance by the competent administrative authority of a proposal for expenditure, project execution, scheme implementation, supply procurement or civil work. It accepts the necessity and estimated outlay, but does not authorise the disbursement of funds.
* **Financial Sanction:** The statutory authorization required before drawing funds or incurring liability against an approved budget appropriation.
* **Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO):** An officer of the Sikkim Finance and Accounts Service declared by the Finance Department, on the recommendation of the Head of Department, to draw bills and disburse payments on behalf of the State Government. Where no such officer is available, another gazetted officer may be designated, or the Head of Office or Head of Department may act as DDO where authorised.
* **Local Body:** A statutory authority legally entitled or specifically empowered by the Government to administer a local fund (such as a Gram Panchayat Unit, Zilla Panchayat or Municipality).
* **Local Fund Audit:** The statutory audit conducted by the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) over accounts administered by local authorities and designated institutions.
* **Public Works:** Civil, electrical or mechanical works relating to public infrastructure, administrative buildings, transport networks, utilities, original construction and structural maintenance.

---

## 5. Interdepartmental Consultation

When a financial proposal directly affects more than one Government department:

* All affected departments must be formally consulted.
* No final executive order may be issued until the consulted departments reach concurrence.
* If an agreement cannot be reached, the proposal must be submitted to the Chief Secretary, the Minister-in-Charge or the State Cabinet for decision.

For example, acquiring an official vehicle requires coordination among the administrative department, the Transport Department, the Home Department and the Finance Department.

---

## 6. Finance Department Concurrence

Executive orders or departmental guidelines carrying financial implications must be issued:

* Directly by the Finance Department; or
* With the documented prior concurrence of the Finance Department.

Administrative departments may not independently alter expenditure norms, financial thresholds or payment procedures without Finance Department approval.

---

## 7. Interpretation, Doubt and Amendments

When an ambiguity arises regarding the interpretation of the Rules:

* The matter must be referred to the Finance Department.
* The decision of the Finance Department is final and binding.
* The Finance Department may issue general or special instructions, procedural orders and amendments.

DDOs must maintain a permanent departmental reference dossier containing:

* Official clarification orders.
* Finance Department notifications and circulars.
* Amendments to financial delegations.
* Related legal opinions.

## Part II: Government Receipts and Revenue

## 8. Realisation and Crediting of Public Moneys

All public moneys received on behalf of the Government must be:

* Realised under lawful authority.
* Entered immediately into official cashbooks and subsidiary ledgers.
* Deposited without delay into an authorised treasury or bank.
* Classified under the correct budget receipt head.
* Reconciled monthly against treasury schedules and bank scrolls.

This requirement applies to statutory taxes, administrative fees, fines, security deposits, recoveries, user charges and departmental reimbursements.

---

## 9. Assessment, Collection and Monitoring

The Head of Department and Head of Office must ensure that all Government revenues are:

* Assessed accurately under the governing statutes.
* Collected within established timelines.
* Recorded under the proper major, minor and detailed accounting heads.
* Tracked to prevent accumulation of arrears.

Monthly revenue returns must document:

* Total demand due.
* Aggregate collections realized.
* Outstanding arrears.
* Justification for collection delays.
* Recovery actions initiated.

---

## 10. Custody of Receipt Books

Where departmental officers accept public revenue in cash or instruments:

* Machine-numbered receipt books must be kept under secure custody.
* A register of receipt books must record every book issued and returned.
* Used receipt books with counterfoils must be returned before new books are issued.
* Cancelled receipt leaves must remain attached to the counterfoil with recorded reasons.
* Receipt book balances must reconcile with the departmental cashbook.

Auditors examine missing receipt leaves, duplicate receipts, uncredited collections and unrecorded receipt books.

---

## 11. Procedures for Depositing Revenue

Moneys paid into the Government account must be supported by a treasury challan detailing:

* The specific nature and description of the payment.
* The full legal name and address of the remitter.
* The depositing department and DDO code.
* The full 15-digit budget receipt classification head.
* The exact amount deposited in figures and words.
* Date, bank branch details and validation seal.

Separate challans must be prepared for distinct receipt heads.

For cash receipts:

* Cash deposits up to ₹5,000 may be processed under standard counter procedures without an advance departmental challan.
* Cash deposits exceeding ₹5,000 and up to ₹50,000 require the standard verified challan process.
* Payments exceeding ₹50,000 should be remitted through banking instruments, NEFT/RTGS or official electronic payment gateways.

---

## Part III: Control of Public Expenditure

## 12. Essential Conditions for Incurring Expenditure

Under the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025:

* Every item of expenditure must be supported by a formal sanction issued by an authority holding delegated powers.
* The sanction must be covered by available budget provision under the relevant primary unit of appropriation.
* The expenditure must serve an authorised, legitimate public purpose.
* Commitments must not exceed the sanctioned appropriation limit.
* Sanction limits must not be evaded by artificial contract splitting.

Budget provision alone does not authorise expenditure; a separate expenditure sanction from the competent authority is mandatory.

---

## 13. Prohibition Against Splitting Sanctions

A financial proposal must not be subdivided to bring individual components within the delegated sanction powers of a lower authority.

For example, if an equipment procurement requires an aggregate outlay of ₹12 lakh, an officer with delegated purchasing powers of ₹5 lakh cannot divide the purchase into three separate orders of ₹4 lakh each. The Sikkim Financial Rules prohibit splitting a single project or supply requirement to circumvent higher sanction requirements.

---

## 14. Standards of Financial Propriety

Every officer sanctioning or disbursing public funds must observe the following principles:

* The expenditure must be necessary and appropriate to the public occasion.
* Rates must be competitive and reasonable compared to prevailing market standards.
* Specifications must remain neutral and competitive.
* Financial powers must not be exercised to obtain direct or indirect personal benefit.
* No particular supplier or individual may receive arbitrary preference.
* Comprehensive reasons must be documented for major procurement decisions.

Accepting the lowest financial quote alone does not fulfill financial responsibility. The selected tender must satisfy mandatory technical specifications, deliver required quality and provide value for public money.

---

## Part IV: Institutional Responsibilities

## 15. The Accounts Officer

The designated Accounts Officer:

* Enforces financial rules, accounting standards and treasury instructions.
* Provides independent financial vetting of departmental proposals carrying financial implications.
* Identifies procedural irregularities, budgetary deficits and delegation breaches.
* Advises the Head of Department on financial management.
* Refers unresolved financial disagreements to the Finance Department.

The formal advice of the Accounts Officer must be obtained prior to the sanction of expenditure proposals.

---

## 16. The Drawing and Disbursing Officer

The DDO is personally responsible for:

* Ensuring that payment bills comply with statutory formats and financial rules.
* Confirming that funds drawn are required for immediate disbursement.
* Verifying that expenditures remain within the sanctioned appropriation.
* Executing statutory tax deductions (GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS, Labour Cess).
* Attaching certified primary vouchers, measurement entries and receipt records.
* Maintaining the cashbook, bill register and subsidiary ledgers.
* Reconciling departmental expenditure monthly against treasury accounts.
* Preserving financial records for statutory audit.

---

## 17. The Controlling Authority

The Controlling Authority (such as a Director, Chief Engineer or Joint Secretary) must verify:

* The necessity and propriety of incurred expenditure.
* The reasonableness of contract rates against approved schedules.
* The existence of prior administrative and technical sanctions.
* Arithmetic accuracy and supporting documentation on vouchers.
* The availability of uncommitted grant balances.
* Expenditure progression to prevent year-end rushes.

---

## 18. Head of Department and Head of Office

The Head of Department (HoD) and Head of Office (HoO) must ensure that:

* Departmental expenditure does not exceed the legislative grant.
* Funds are applied exclusively to sanctioned public purposes.
* Internal control and maker-checker systems function effectively.
* Departmental liabilities and multi-year contractual commitments are recorded.
* Periodic expenditure returns are submitted to the Finance Department.
* Internal and statutory audit observations are addressed and resolved.

---

## Part V: Financial Delegations and Sanctions

## 19. Exercise of Delegated Powers

A subordinate authority may exercise only those financial powers expressly delegated to it by the State Government under the Delegation of Financial Powers Rules.

Every formal financial sanction must state:

* The exact amount sanctioned in figures and words.
* The specific public purpose of the expenditure.
* The 15-digit budget classification head.
* The relevant grant number and primary unit of appropriation.
* The statutory rule or delegation order authorizing the sanction.
* The financial year of currency.
* Conditions or milestone requirements attached to the release.
* The funding source (State Budget, CSS or external financing).

---

## 20. Prohibition on Re-Delegation

Powers delegated to a specific authority cannot be re-delegated to a subordinate officer unless the original Government notification or the Finance Department explicitly authorises such transfer.

---

## 21. Powers Reserved by the Finance Department

Financial powers not explicitly delegated to administrative departments remain reserved by the Finance Department.

A proposal submitted for Finance Department concurrence must detail:

* The existing budget provision and scheme outlay.
* Cumulative liabilities incurred in prior years.
* Fresh liabilities incurred during the current financial year.
* Progressive expenditure booked to date.
* The uncommitted net budget balance.
* Total monetary liability of the current proposal.

---

## Part VI: Capital Projects and Public Works

## 22. Scheme and Project Clearances

New capital projects, public infrastructure works or schemes exceeding delegated departmental powers require Finance Department concurrence.

Project proposals must include:

* A comprehensive project report with socioeconomic justifications.
* Engineering designs, architectural drawings and structural calculations.
* Detailed estimates based on the prevailing Schedule of Rates (SOR).
* Land title verification, site availability and environmental clearances.
* The proposed procurement and contracting strategy.
* Milestone schedules and expected completion dates.
* Long-term operational costs and maintenance arrangements.

---

## 23. Public Works Sanction Hierarchy

A capital works project must follow a sequential sanction hierarchy:

```text
Need Identification & Feasibility Study
                  ↓
       Administrative Approval
                  ↓
          Technical Sanction
                  ↓
          Financial Sanction
                  ↓
    Resource Allocation & Budget
                  ↓
    Competitive Tendering Process
                  ↓
Contract Agreement & Formal Work Order
                  ↓
Measurement Book (MB) Entry & RA Bills
                  ↓
     Final Bill & Completion Cert.
                  ↓
     Asset Register & Maintenance

```

* **Administrative Approval** accepts the public need and the broad cost estimate.
* **Technical Sanction** approves the detailed engineering drawings, structural designs and bill of quantities (BOQ).
* **Financial Sanction** authorises the commitment of public funds within the approved budget.

## Part VII: Sikkim Government Vehicle Rules, 2025

## 24. Gazette and Commencement

The Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025 were notified by the Finance Department in Extraordinary Gazette No. 303 dated 23 July 2025, published on 24 July 2025.

The Rules came into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette.

---

## 25. Regulatory Scope

The Vehicle Rules regulate:

* Procurement criteria for fresh and replacement Government vehicles.
* Vehicle allotment standards and officer entitlements.
* Authorized servicing and routine maintenance.
* Expenditure ceilings for mechanical repairs.
* Periodic replacement of tyres, tubes, batteries and accessories.
* Mandatory vehicle history sheets and logbook registers.
* Recovery of monthly user charges for dual official/private use.
* Concurrence procedures for accident repairs.
* Technical condemnation criteria.
* Public auction and disposal procedures.

---

## 26. Key Definitions

* **Government Vehicle:** Any motor vehicle owned, leased or operated by a State Government department, office, local body or state-controlled institution.
* **Departmental Auto-Workshop:** The mechanical workshop operated by the Sikkim Nationalised Transport (SNT) or an authorized departmental engineering facility.
* **Cumulative Expenditure:** The total progressive expenditure incurred on the repair, overhaul, servicing and maintenance of an individual vehicle over its operating life.
* **History Sheet:** The permanent service ledger maintained for each Government vehicle recording purchases, specifications, mechanical repairs, replaced parts, tyre changes, cumulative expenditure and distance run.

---

## 27. Vehicle Repair Ceilings

The Vehicle Rules prescribe financial ceilings for repair and maintenance expenditure through an official Schedule.

Heads of Department, Heads of Office and DDOs must:

* Maintain an individual vehicle repair expenditure register for each vehicle.
* Track progressive cumulative expenditure against mileage and vehicle age.
* Ensure that annual repair costs do not exceed prescribed ceilings.
* Obtain technical certification from the SNT workshop or authorized technical authorities before undertaking major repairs.

The Schedule establishes repair ceilings across distinct vehicle categories:

* Two-wheelers.
* Light Motor Vehicles (LMVs) and passenger sedans.
* Sports Utility Vehicles (SUVs) and four-wheel-drive vehicles.
* Utility vehicles and light commercial pickups.
* Medium and heavy goods trucks.
* Passenger buses and school buses.
* Specialized pool and protocol vehicles.
* Emergency ambulances and specialized rescue vehicles.

Financial ceilings are determined by vehicle category, chronological age, cumulative distance run and functional classification.

---

## 28. Repair Sanction Powers

Heads of Department and Heads of Office may sanction repairs through authorized workshops or private garages up to the financial limits defined in the Rules.

Requirements include:

* Preliminary technical inspection by an authorized mechanical engineer.
* Work execution through an authorized or contracted workshop.
* Verification of garage bills against approved part prices and labour schedules.
* Entry of replaced components in the vehicle history sheet.
* Verification that cumulative repair costs remain within the prescribed ceiling.

Repairs exceeding authorized departmental limits require higher technical certification and prior Finance Department approval.

---

## 29. Mandatory Data in Vehicle Repair Sanctions

Every sanction order and bill scrutiny sheet for a vehicle repair must specify:

* Date of original vehicle purchase.
* Official registration number.
* Cumulative expenditure incurred on repairs to date.
* Gross amount of the current repair bill.
* Total distance covered (odometer reading).
* Prescribed financial ceiling for that vehicle class and age.
* A certificate confirming that total expenditure remains within the ceiling.

Paying authorities (Treasury Officers and DDOs) must withhold payment if these particulars or the ceiling compliance certificate are omitted from the voucher docket.

---

## 30. The Vehicle History Sheet

Every Government vehicle must maintain a permanent history sheet.

The register records:

* Official vehicle registration number.
* Make, model and variant.
* Chassis and engine numbers.
* Date of purchase and capital cost.
* Departmental allotment and assigned driver.
* Progressive odometer mileage.
* Chronological repair details, replaced parts and garage names.
* Replacement dates for tyres, tubes, batteries and upholstery.
* Progressive cumulative repair expenditure.
* Dated authentication by the DDO or Head of Office.

## 31. Servicing, Tyres and Component Replacements

The Vehicle Rules define conditions for routine maintenance:

* **Servicing and Lubricants:** Manufacturer free services must be utilized before claiming paid servicing. Periodic servicing must follow prescribed distance intervals.
* **Tyre Replacements:** Tyres and tubes may be replaced only after the vehicle completes the minimum prescribed distance or chronological duration, supported by a tread-wear inspection report.
* **Batteries and Accessories:** Battery replacements require verification of the warranty card and a test report confirming the old unit cannot hold charge.

The DDO must confirm that replaced parts were entered in the vehicle history sheet and that the old parts were surrendered to departmental stores for auction or disposal.

---

## 32. New Vehicle Procurement

Procuring fresh or replacement vehicles requires the prior written concurrence of the Finance Department. Where required by executive instructions, clearances from the Home Department and sanction through the Chief Secretary or State Cabinet must be obtained.

The proposal must establish:

* Approved budget appropriation under the capital vehicle head.
* Operational justification for the acquisition.
* Entitlement of the designated post or department.
* Approved vehicle category and specification.
* Current vehicle fleet status within the department.
* Justification for replacement, including condemnation records of older vehicles.
* Projected operating, fuel and maintenance costs.

---

## 33. Vehicle Entitlement Classifications

The Rules align vehicle models and engine capacities with official entitlement levels:

* Senior Government functionaries and Apex-scale officers.
* Senior Administrative Grade and Group 'A' officers.
* Field, inspection and touring officers.
* Dedicated departmental programme vehicles.
* Utility transport and operational trucks.
* Central administrative pool vehicles.
* Emergency services, ambulances and law enforcement vehicles.

DDOs must not pass procurement or leasing claims for vehicles exceeding an officer's approved entitlement scale without explicit Government sanction.

---

## 34. Recovery of Monthly User Charges

Where an official vehicle is attached to an officer and used for combined official and private purposes, monthly user charges must be deducted from salary.

The monthly user charges follow vehicle capital cost tiers:

| Vehicle Capital Cost Category | Monthly Salary Deduction |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹10 lakh | ₹1,000 |
| Above ₹10 lakh and up to ₹15 lakh | ₹1,500 |
| Above ₹15 lakh | ₹2,000 |

Additional conditions:

* Shared vehicle users pay user charges on an individual basis.
* Officers utilising dedicated programme or project vehicles for dual purposes pay 50% of the applicable scale.

### Exempted Vehicle Categories

User charges are not deducted for:

* Vehicles assigned to the Governor.
* Vehicles assigned to the Chief Minister.
* Vehicles assigned to the Chief Justice and Judges of the High Court.
* Ambulances, mobile clinics and hearses.
* Fire tenders and emergency rescue vehicles.
* Heavy transport trucks and public service buses.
* Operational vehicles assigned to police stations.
* Dedicated pilot and security escort vehicles.

The DDO must ensure that monthly user charges are deducted from pay bills and credited to the proper State revenue receipt head.

---

## 35. Procedures for Accident Repairs

When a Government vehicle incurs accident damage and repair estimates exceed ₹60,000, specialized clearance procedures apply:

* An immediate technical damage assessment must be prepared by the Sikkim Nationalised Transport (SNT) workshop or authorized Government automobile engineer.
* A copy of the Police First Information Report (FIR) or formal police investigation report must be secured.
* A departmental inquiry report must establish the date, time, operating route, authorized journey and driver identity.
* The Head of Department must record observations regarding responsibility and insurance recovery.
* Concurrence of the Home Department and Finance Department must be obtained, even if total repair costs fall within the normal annual repair ceiling.

Repair expenditures cannot be admitted against public funds if:

* The accident occurred during unauthorized private use.
* The vehicle was operated by an unauthorized driver.
* The departmental inquiry indicates gross negligence without recovery proceedings.
* Police and technical inspection reports are absent.

---

## 36. Vehicle Condemnation Criteria

A Government vehicle may be formally condemned only when:

* It has completed its prescribed statutory lifespan in years and kilometres.
* An inspection committee, including an SNT technical representative, certifies that further operation is unviable.
* A formal valuation report establishes the reserve scrap value.
* Administrative approval for condemnation is granted by the competent authority.

---

## 37. Public Auction and Disposal

Condemned vehicles must be disposed of through public auction:

* The condemnation report, mechanical survey and valuation certificate must be on file.
* The auction notice must receive wide public notice with at least one month allowed for participation.
* An authorized administrative officer must preside over the auction.
* The highest responsive bid must be accepted and recorded in the formal sale account.
* The successful bidder must pay the full purchase amount before taking possession.
* Net sale proceeds must be credited to the State revenue receipt head within 24 hours.

## Part VIII: Motor Vehicles Taxation Amendment Act, 2025

## 38. Gazette Notification and Enactment

The Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025:

* Was published in Extraordinary Gazette No. 57 on 25 March 2025.
* Received the assent of the Governor on 26 February 2025.
* Came into force on the date of its publication in the Official Gazette.
* Amended the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982.

---

## 39. Additional Tax on New Vehicles

The Amendment Act established revised additional tax rates based on invoice cost for new private vehicles, Government vehicles, public-sector undertaking (PSU) vehicles and non-transport two-wheelers:

| Vehicle Invoice Cost Category | Statutory Additional Tax Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹2 lakh | 1% of invoice cost |
| Above ₹2 lakh and up to ₹4 lakh | 2% of invoice cost |
| Above ₹4 lakh and up to ₹10 lakh | 3% of invoice cost |
| Above ₹10 lakh and up to ₹25 lakh | 4% of invoice cost |
| Above ₹25 lakh and up to ₹50 lakh | 5% of invoice cost |
| Above ₹50 lakh | 6% of invoice cost |

### Calculation Example

A Government department procures an official vehicle with an invoice cost of ₹12,00,000 (excluding GST):

$$Additional\text{ }Tax = \text{Invoice Cost} \times \text{Applicable Rate}$$

$$Additional\text{ }Tax = ₹12,00,000 \times 4\% = ₹48,000$$

The DDO must draw and disburse ₹48,000 to the Motor Vehicles Division, Transport Department, at the time of registration.

---

## 40. Additional Tax on Commercial and Transport Vehicles

The amendment establishes the following additional tax rates for commercial vehicles:

* Taxis and light commercial goods vehicles with an invoice cost below ₹10 lakh: 1%.
* Taxis and commercial goods vehicles with an invoice cost above ₹10 lakh: 2%.
* Stage carriages and public transport buses: 2%.
* Educational-institution buses and buses owned by private companies: 3%.
* Private service transport vehicles: 3%.
* Commercial tractors, recovery trailers, mobile cranes and earth-moving machinery: 3%.
* Ambulances, firefighting tenders and public-utility vehicles: 2%.

---

## 41. Tax on Vehicle Transfers and Reregistration

For Sikkim-registered motor vehicles submitted for ownership transfer, engine replacement or allocation of a new registration mark, the Act prescribes fixed tax rates based on engine displacement:

| Engine Displacement Category | Fixed Additional Tax Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 900 cc | ₹10,000 |
| 901 cc to 1490 cc | ₹15,000 |
| 1491 cc to 2000 cc | ₹20,000 |
| Above 2000 cc | ₹25,000 |

For vehicles previously registered in other States and brought into Sikkim under a No Objection Certificate (NOC), the Act prescribes additional tax at 50% of the standard transfer rates.

---

## 42. Electric and Hybrid Vehicles

The Amendment Act establishes concessional tax rates for alternative fuel vehicles:

* **Commercial electric, hybrid or battery-operated vehicles:** Nil (exempt from additional tax).
* **Non-transport private electric, hybrid or battery-operated vehicles:** 1% of invoice cost.

Motor vehicle registration tax is a State motor vehicle levy administered by the Transport Department. It is distinct from GST, GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS, monthly user charges and routine maintenance expenses.

## 47. Vehicle Procurement Checklist

## Part XI: Audit Perspectives and Oversight

## 51. General Expenditure Audit Documentation

Statutory auditors from the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) and the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) examine:

* Original administrative and financial sanctions.
* SIFMS budget allocation and commitment registers.
* Tenders, comparative statements and contract agreements.
* Supplier invoices, delivery challans and stock entries.
* Measurement Books with engineering inspection records.
* Tax deduction calculations and treasury deposit challans.
* SIFMS payment vouchers and bank scrolls.
* Permanent asset ledgers and liability registers.

---

## 52. Vehicle Audit Documentation

During fleet audits, examiners review:

* Permanent vehicle history sheets and repair registers.
* Cumulative repair calculations compared against statutory ceilings.
* SNT technical inspection reports and garage invoices.
* Logbooks showing official routes, timings and fuel consumption.
* Salary schedules confirming deduction of monthly user charges.
* Treasury challans for vehicle auction proceeds.
* Concurrence orders for accident repairs exceeding ₹60,000.

---

## 53. Audit Findings in State Administration

State Finances Audit Reports for Sikkim by the Comptroller and Auditor General have identified recurring control weaknesses, including:

* Mechanical repairs sanctioned without checking cumulative repair ceilings.
* Vehicles retained in operational fleets without mandatory history sheets.
* Non-deduction of monthly user charges from officers utilising pool vehicles for personal travel.
* Interest-free mobilization advances disbursed without verified site readiness.
* Capital advances outstanding across projects without recovery tracking.

A financial sanction alone does not demonstrate regulatory compliance; the DDO must ensure the entire administrative and operational trail is documented.

## Part XII: Summary of Regulatory Requirements

## 54. Comparative Summary for DDOs

### Previous Administrative Framework

Financial management relied on earlier financial rules, scattered manual instructions, departmental circulars and separate public works manuals.

### Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025

Establishes a unified, consolidated public financial management framework governing:

* Departments, field offices and subordinate authorities.
* Dedicated DDO appointments and Accounts Officer advisory roles.
* Mandatory prior administrative, technical and financial sanctions.
* Modernised SIFMS treasury architecture and budget appropriation.
* Strict standards of financial propriety and competitive procurement.
* Systematic recording of multi-year financial commitments.

### Sikkim Government Vehicle Rules, 2025

Establishes an integrated vehicular regulatory code governing:

* Fleet procurement and entitlement scales.
* Prescribed annual repair ceilings by vehicle category and age.
* Mandatory vehicle history sheets and cumulative expenditure tracking.
* Standard monthly user-charge recoveries for private vehicle use.
* Dual-department (Home and Finance) concurrence for major accident repairs.
* Structured technical condemnation and public auction procedures.

### Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025

Revises statutory motor vehicle revenue structures:

* Tiered additional tax rates (1% to 6%) on new vehicle registrations based on invoice cost.
* Engine-displacement-based tax rates on vehicle transfers and reregistrations.
* Dedicated tax concessions for electric and hybrid transport.

## 55. Operational Summary

Administrative departments and DDOs in Sikkim should apply a sequential review process:

```text
Identify Transaction Nature (General Supply / Works / Motor Vehicle)
                        ↓
          Determine the Governing Statutory Rule
                        ↓
          Verify Latest Amendments & Circulars
                        ↓
    Obtain Administrative Approval & Technical Sanction
                        ↓
     Obtain Prior Financial Sanction Within Delegations
                        ↓
      Verify SIFMS Budget Appropriation & Resource Code
                        ↓
      Execute Competitive, Authorised Procurement
                        ↓
Verify Physical Delivery, MB Entry or Technical Inspection
                        ↓
    Calculate Statutory Taxes, Deductions and Ceilings
                        ↓
    DDO Scrutiny & Authorisation on SIFMS Voucher
                        ↓
      Post Entries to SIFMS, Cashbooks & History Sheets
                        ↓
    Retain Complete Voucher Dossier for Audit Review

```

Core compliance rules:

1. Never disburse funds on the basis of administrative approval alone.
2. Never incur public expenditure without prior financial sanction.
3. Never exceed the sanctioned budget appropriation.
4. Never split purchase orders or schemes to evade financial thresholds.
5. Never pass a vehicle repair bill without verifying cumulative expenditure against the statutory ceiling.
6. Never procure a Government vehicle without prior Finance Department concurrence.
7. Never condemn or dispose of a vehicle without technical survey certificates and public auction procedures.
8. Never omit the recovery of monthly user charges where an official vehicle is used for dual purposes.
9. Refer ambiguities in rule interpretation to the Finance Department for decision.

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 govern general State public finance; the Sikkim Government Vehicle Procurement and Maintenance Rules, 2025 regulate public fleet administration; and the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Act, 2025 determines statutory additional vehicle tax. DDOs and public administrators must apply each instrument in accordance with the transaction.
