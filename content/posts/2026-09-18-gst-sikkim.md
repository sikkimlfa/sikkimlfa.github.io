# GST for Sikkim Government Offices: Supplies, Works Contracts, Rates and Deductions

Sikkim Government offices, Panchayats, Zilla Panchayats and Municipalities regularly purchase goods and services and award contracts for roads, buildings, drainage, water supply, sanitation, maintenance and office operations. When processing these bills, the main questions are:

* Is GST applicable?
* What GST rate applies?
* Is the supplier registered?
* Is the work a works contract?
* Is the supply exempt?
* Should GST-TDS be deducted?
* Should income-tax TDS also be deducted?
* Does the office need a GSTIN as a tax deductor?
* Does the office need a TAN?
* How should a bill be processed when the contractor is unregistered?
* What records should be retained for audit?

Government status alone does not automatically make a purchase GST-free. Taxability depends on the nature of the supply, the supplier, the recipient, the applicable exemption and the relevant statutory notification. This guide outlines the operational and audit requirements needed for bill processing and local fund audit.

> **Important:** GST rates, exemptions, reverse-charge rules, TDS requirements and filing deadlines can change. Verify the latest CBIC notifications, GST Council decisions, GST portal instructions, Income Tax Department guidance and Sikkim Government orders before processing an official bill.

## 1. Government Purchase and GST

### 1.1 Government office as a recipient

A Government office may receive:

* Goods.
* Services.
* Works contract services.
* Pure labour services.
* Composite supplies.
* Consultancy.
* Maintenance.
* Transport.
* Security.
* Housekeeping.
* Printing.
* Software and IT services.
* Construction and repair work.

The supplier's GST treatment depends entirely on the statutory nature and classification of the supply.

### 1.2 Government office as a supplier

A Government department or local authority may also provide:

* Licences.
* Permits.
* Renting of premises.
* User charges.
* Testing services.
* Registration services.
* Advertisements.
* Parking services.
* Other taxable or exempt services.

The tax treatment must be examined separately whenever the Government office acts as the supplier.

### 1.3 Government status does not automatically remove GST

A contractor supplying taxable goods or services to a State Government office is generally required to charge GST. For example:

* Construction services may be taxable.
* Office stationery may be taxable.
* Security services may be taxable.
* Vehicle hiring may be taxable.
* Repair and maintenance may be taxable.

Specific supplies are exempt or outside the scope of GST only if designated statutory criteria and notifications are satisfied.

## 2. Important Terms

### 2.1 Government department

A department or establishment of the Central Government or State Government, such as:

* Public Works Department.
* Rural Development Department.
* Health Department.
* Education Department.
* Finance Department.
* District administration.
* Subordinate Government offices.

### 2.2 Local authority

For GST purposes, a local authority includes:

* Panchayat (Gram Panchayat Unit).
* Zilla Panchayat or Zilla Parishad.
* Municipality.
* Municipal Corporation.
* District Planning Committee.
* Other statutory local bodies established under law.

The precise classification must be verified against Section 2(69) of the CGST Act and applicable notifications.

### 2.3 Government entity

The term applies to an entity meeting the statutory ownership or control conditions prescribed under GST law. A Government entity is legally distinct from:

* A Government department.
* A local authority.
* A Government company.
* A statutory agency.

This classification directly influences the availability of specific tax exemptions.

### 2.4 Supplier

The supplier executing the contract may be:

* A registered taxpayer under GST.
* An unregistered vendor under GST.
* An individual contractor or sole proprietorship.
* A partnership firm or Limited Liability Partnership.
* A private or public limited company.
* A cooperative society or registered trust.
* A Government undertaking or public-sector enterprise.

### 2.5 Recipient

The recipient is the statutory office or institution receiving the goods or services:

* A Sikkim Government department or office.
* A Gram Panchayat Unit (GPU).
* A Zilla Panchayat (ZP).
* A Municipality or Municipal Corporation.
* A State Government agency or society.

## 3. GST Registration Status

### 3.1 Registered supplier

A registered supplier holds a valid Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN) and must issue a GST tax invoice containing:

* Supplier legal name and registered address.
* Supplier GSTIN.
* Consecutive serial invoice number and invoice date.
* Description of goods or services.
* HSN code for goods or SAC code for services.
* Taxable value of the supply.
* Applicable GST rate.
* Itemised GST amounts (CGST and SGST, or IGST).
* Place of supply, along with State name and code.
* Recipient official name and details.

### 3.2 Unregistered supplier

An unregistered supplier does not hold a GSTIN. An unregistered vendor must not charge GST on a bill. When evaluating an unregistered supplier's bill, the office must examine:

* Whether the supplier was legally required to register based on threshold limits.
* Whether the supply is exempt.
* Whether the value falls within registration exemptions.
* Whether reverse-charge provisions apply.
* Whether State procurement rules permit purchase from an unregistered supplier.
* Whether a Permanent Account Number (PAN) is furnished.
* Whether income-tax TDS applies.

### 3.3 Supplier verification

Before admitting a registered supplier's bill:

* Verify the GSTIN on the official GST common portal.
* Confirm that the legal and trade names match departmental sanctions.
* Check that the registration status is active.
* Verify the registered address and principal place of business.
* Confirm the taxpayer constitution and type (regular, composition, etc.).
* Ensure the invoice details match the purchase order and verified bank account.

## 5. GST Rates: General Principles

### 5.1 Determinants of GST rates

GST rates are determined by statutory classification rather than buyer identity:

* HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature) for goods.
* SAC (Services Accounting Code) for services.
* Statutory classification of the supply.
* Taxability and scope under Section 7 and Section 9 of the CGST Act.
* Specific exemption notifications.
* Statutory supplier and recipient conditions.
* Place of supply determinations.
* Reverse-charge notifications.
* Applicable composite or mixed supply rules.

Rates must be verified against current CBIC notifications and schedules.

### 5.2 Common GST rate tiers

Standard GST rate tiers include:

* Nil or exempt (0%).
* Concessional rates (5%).
* Standard lower tier (12%).
* Standard regular tier (18%).
* Higher rate tier (28%).
* Special statutory rates for notified commodities.

The applicable rate is determined by the statutory entry for the item or service, not by the buyer's status as a public office.

### 5.3 Goods commonly purchased by Government offices

* Office stationery, paper and registers.
* Office furniture, fixtures and storage units.
* Desktop computers, laptops, servers and peripherals.
* Multifunction printers, scanners and consumables.
* Electrical wiring, luminaires and appliances.
* Construction materials: cement, structural steel, aggregates and pipes.
* Hardware, fittings and maintenance tools.
* Motor vehicles and utility transport.
* Disinfectants, sanitation and cleaning supplies.
* Pharmaceuticals, diagnostic equipment and medical consumables.

Each item must be classified under its specific HSN code to identify the applicable rate.

## 6. GST on Office Supplies

### 6.1 Verification process for office supplies

When processing supply bills for office goods, verify:

* Administrative approval and purchase order reference.
* Supplier GSTIN and registration validity.
* HSN classification and item description.
* Billed quantities against the Goods Receipt Note (GRN).
* Unit rates against contract or approved rate agreements.
* Accurate calculation of taxable value.
* Correct rate and arithmetic calculation of GST.
* Physical delivery receipt and entry in the central stock register.
* Formal tax invoice compliance.
* Final passing order by the Competent Financial Authority.

### 6.2 Billing calculation example

An office purchases wooden furniture with an 18% GST rate:

* Taxable value: ₹2,00,000
* Applicable GST rate: 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST)
* CGST: ₹18,000
* SGST: ₹18,000
* Total invoice value: ₹2,36,000

$$GST = \text{Taxable Value} \times \text{GST Rate}$$

$$GST = ₹2,00,000 \times 18\% = ₹36,000$$

$$\text{Gross Invoice Value} = ₹2,00,000 + ₹36,000 = ₹2,36,000$$

Whether GST-TDS applies depends on the taxable contract value and Section 51 statutory conditions.

### 6.3 Audit verification checklist

* Were the purchased goods physically delivered and verified?
* Does the invoice match the purchase order and tender terms?
* Was GST computed strictly on the taxable base value?
* Was the supplier registered and active during the transaction date?
* Is the item properly recorded in the consumable or dead-stock register?
* Were standard procurement rules and delegation of financial powers observed?
* Were required tax deductions made before disbursement?

## 7. GST on Government Works Contracts

### 7.1 Scope of works contracts

Under Section 2(119) of the CGST Act, a works contract involves:

* Construction, erection, installation and completion.
* Fitting out, structural repairs, maintenance and renovation.
* Alteration and structural improvement.

A works contract must relate to immovable property wherein transfer of property in goods is involved during contract execution. Examples include:

* Roads, bridges, culverts and causeways.
* Government administrative complexes, hospitals and schools.
* Surface drainage, retaining walls and slope-stabilisation structures.
* Potable water distribution and sewerage pipelines.
* Internal electrification and permanent architectural fittings.

### 7.2 Classification as a service

Under Schedule II, paragraph 6(a) of the CGST Act, a works contract is treated as a composite supply of services. The contractor issues a tax invoice for services, charging GST at the rate applicable to works contracts.

### 7.3 Standard tax rates for works contracts

Ordinary works-contract services awarded by public authorities generally attract 18% GST:

* **Intra-State supply:** 9% CGST plus 9% SGST.
* **Inter-State supply:** 18% IGST.

> **Note:** Concessional 12% rates previously applicable to certain public works contracts were amended to 18% effective 18 July 2022 under CBIC Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) as amended. Do not apply 18% automatically; verify the specific notification entry, recipient entity criteria, work nature and contract date.

### 7.4 Works contract calculation example

A contractor submits a running account bill for building renovation:

* Taxable value of civil work: ₹10,00,000
* Applicable GST rate: 18% (Intra-State)
* CGST (9%): ₹90,000
* SGST (9%): ₹90,000
* Gross bill value: ₹11,80,000

$$CGST = ₹10,00,000 \times 9\% = ₹90,000$$

$$SGST = ₹10,00,000 \times 9\% = ₹90,000$$

$$\text{Gross Bill Amount} = ₹10,00,000 + ₹90,000 + ₹90,000 = ₹11,80,000$$

## 8. Pure Services and Composite Supplies to Government

### 8.1 Exemption parameters

Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) provides specific exemptions for certain pure services and composite supplies rendered to Government departments or local authorities. CBIC Circular No. 177/09/2022 outlines criteria regarding:

* Pure services without any supply of goods.
* Composite supplies where goods constitute not more than 25% of the total value.
* Services relating to functions entrusted to Panchayats under Article 243G of the Constitution.
* Services relating to functions entrusted to Municipalities under Article 243W of the Constitution.

### 8.2 Pure services

Pure services involve no transfer of materials:

* Advisory and engineering consultancy.
* Security and facility management.
* Contractual manpower services.
* Software and system maintenance.

Exemption eligibility requires verification of:

* The legal status of the recipient (Government department vs. statutory agency).
* Direct alignment with constitutional functions under Article 243G or 243W.
* Complete absence of supply of goods within the contractual scope.

### 8.3 Composite supplies

Where a contract involves both goods and services, the exemption applies only if the contract is predominantly for services and the value of goods does not exceed 25% of total contract value, subject to notification terms.

### 8.4 Sanitation and conservancy services

Sanitation and waste-management services connected with statutory local authority duties may be exempt in specific circumstances. Exemption does not apply merely because the recipient is a public entity; the activity must directly fulfill an entrusted constitutional function.

## 9. Government-to-Government Transactions

### 9.1 Evaluation of inter-governmental supplies

Transactions where both supplier and recipient are public bodies are not automatically outside GST. The department must examine:

* Whether the transaction constitutes a supply under Section 7 of the CGST Act.
* Whether valuable consideration is exchanged.
* Whether a specific statutory exemption covers the activity.
* Whether the supplying agency acts as a public authority or provides commercial services.
* Whether the services relate to statutory functions under Article 243G or 243W.

### 9.2 Renting of Government premises

The lease or rental of Government property may be taxable or exempt depending on:

* The classification and use of the property (commercial vs. residential).
* The statutory profile of the lessor and lessee.
* Notified threshold limits and reverse-charge mandates.

### 9.3 Transfer of public funds

Transfers of public money do not constitute a supply when they represent:

* Budgetary allocations and departmental releases.
* Statutory grants-in-aid.
* Security deposits and performance bank deposits.
* Non-taxable inter-departmental book adjustments.
* Pure cost reimbursements under principal-agent relationships.

## 10. GST-TDS Under Section 51

### 10.1 Obligated deductors

Section 51 of the CGST Act mandates tax deduction at source by:

* Central Government departments.
* State Government departments.
* Local authorities (Panchayats and Municipalities).
* Governmental agencies.
* Notified public-sector undertakings and statutory boards.

### 10.2 Monetary threshold

GST-TDS applies where the total taxable value of supply under a single contract exceeds ₹2,50,000, excluding CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess itemised on the invoice.

### 10.3 Statutory deduction rates

* **Intra-State supply:** 1% CGST + 1% SGST (Total: 2%).
* **Inter-State supply:** 2% IGST.

### 10.4 Formula and calculation

GST-TDS is calculated strictly on the taxable value excluding taxes:

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable GST-TDS Rate}$$

#### Example

* Taxable contract value: ₹5,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹90,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹5,90,000

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹10,000$$

Disbursement net of GST-TDS before other statutory deductions:

$$\text{Net Payment} = ₹5,90,000 - ₹10,000 = ₹5,80,000$$

### 10.5 Deductor registration

Every obligated deductor must obtain registration as a tax deductor under Section 24(vi) of the CGST Act, receiving a dedicated GSTIN. This tax-deductor registration is separate from any normal supplier GSTIN held by the entity.

### 10.6 Compliance procedures

The drawing and disbursing officer must:

* Deduct statutory tax at the time of credit or payment.
* Remit withheld tax to the Government within 10 days following the end of the deduction month.
* File monthly returns in Form GSTR-7 within 10 days of the subsequent month.
* Issue TDS certificates in Form GSTR-7A to deductees.
* Maintain a dedicated GST-TDS deduction and remittance register.

## 11. Income-Tax TDS on Government Contracts

### 11.1 Scope of Section 194C

Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, mandates tax deduction from payments to resident contractors for carrying out works, including supply of labour for:

* Civil infrastructure and construction works.
* Facility repair and maintenance.
* Waste collection and conservancy.
* Vehicle and equipment hiring.
* Catering and hospitality contracts.
* Transport and carriage of goods.

### 11.2 Applicable rates

* **1%:** Where payment is made to an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF).
* **2%:** Where payment is made to other legal entities (firms, companies, societies).
* **20%:** Mandatory rate under Section 206AA if the contractor fails to furnish a valid PAN.

### 11.3 Statutory thresholds

* No deduction if a single payment does not exceed ₹30,000.
* Deduction applies once aggregate payments to a contractor exceed ₹1,00,000 within a financial year.

### 11.4 TAN requirement

Public offices deducting income-tax TDS must hold a Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN). A TAN cannot be substituted with a PAN, a supplier's GSTIN or a GST-TDS deductor GSTIN.

## 12. Combined Deductions: GST-TDS and Income-Tax TDS

### 12.1 Concurrent application

A taxable works bill may require simultaneous deduction of both GST-TDS and income-tax TDS. Each deduction is computed independently on the taxable base value.

#### Worked calculation

* Taxable works bill value: ₹4,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹72,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹4,72,000
* Contractor: Individual (furnishing valid PAN)

#### Deductions

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$Income\text{-}tax\text{ }TDS\text{ }(1\%) = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$Total\text{ }Statutory\text{ }Deductions = ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹12,000$$

$$\text{Net Disbursement} = ₹4,72,000 - ₹12,000 = ₹4,60,000$$

### 12.2 Structural comparison

| Feature | GST-TDS | Income-tax TDS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statutory legislation | CGST, SGST and IGST Acts, 2017 | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Primary section | Section 51 | Section 194C (Works Contracts) |
| Deductor identifier | GSTIN (Tax Deductor profile) | TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) |
| Contractor identifier | GSTIN (if registered) | PAN (Mandatory) |
| Monetary threshold | Taxable contract value above ₹2,50,000 | Single bill: > ₹30,000; Annual: > ₹1,00,000 |
| Standard rates | Intra-State: 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST); Inter-State: 2% IGST | 1% (Individual/HUF); 2% (Other business entities) |
| Statutory return | Form GSTR-7 (Monthly) | Form 26Q (Quarterly) |
| Statutory certificate | Form GSTR-7A | Form 16A |
| Payee tax credit | Electronic Cash Ledger on GST portal | Annual Tax Statement (Form 26AS / AIS) |
| Independence of levy | Calculated independently of Income-tax TDS | Calculated independently of GST-TDS |

## 13. Managing TAN and GSTIN Compliance Deficiencies

### 13.1 Handling a missing deductor TAN

If income-tax TDS is required but the office lacks a TAN:

* Do not ignore the statutory withholding requirement.
* Search office records to verify whether a TAN was previously assigned.
* Submit an application in Form 49B immediately.
* Escalate the matter to the controlling administrative department.
* Never use a contractor's TAN, an officer's personal PAN or a GSTIN as a substitute.
* Record an official file note documenting the regularisation process.

### 13.2 Handling a missing deductor GSTIN

If GST-TDS is required but the office lacks a tax-deductor GSTIN:

* Verify whether the office is a notified deductor under Section 51.
* Apply for registration in Form GST REG-07 on the GST portal.
* Designate the Drawing and Disbursing Officer as the authorised signatory.
* Establish monthly Form GSTR-7 return compliance procedures.
* Maintain a manual GST-TDS deduction register while the application is processed.

### 13.3 Decision matrix for registration deficiencies

| Scenario | Statutory Obligation | Immediate Operational Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No TAN; income-tax TDS not applicable | None | Record non-applicability reasons; process payment normally. |
| No TAN; income-tax TDS is applicable | Mandatory under Income-tax Act | Apply for TAN via Form 49B; regularise arrears with interest. |
| No GSTIN; GST-TDS not applicable | None | Document contract value below threshold; process payment. |
| No GSTIN; GST-TDS is applicable | Mandatory under Section 51 | Apply for deductor GSTIN via Form GST REG-07 immediately. |
| Supplier GSTIN missing; GST charged | Defective tax invoice | Return bill; do not disburse unverified GST charges. |
| Deductor GSTIN omitted on bill | Clerical omission | Insert deductor GSTIN on the bill scrutiny sheet. |
| Unregistered supplier; no GST charged | Statutory compliance | Verify PAN, check registration limits, evaluate Section 194C. |
| Unregistered supplier charges GST | Statutory violation | Reject bill; an unregistered entity cannot collect GST. |

## 14. Processing Bills from Suppliers Without GSTIN

### 14.1 Evaluating payment admissibility

Payment to an unregistered supplier is permissible if:

* The supplier's aggregate turnover is below statutory registration thresholds.
* The goods or services are exempt from GST.
* The procurement complies with Sikkim Financial Rules and purchase procedures.
* The contractor's PAN is verified.
* Applicable income-tax TDS under Section 194C is deducted.
* The transaction does not trigger unaddressed reverse-charge liabilities.

### 14.2 Prohibited practices

* Permitting an unregistered vendor to charge or collect GST.
* Creating or recording an invented GSTIN.
* Using the department's GSTIN as the supplier's GSTIN.
* Passing a tax claim supported only by a non-statutory commercial bill.
* Failing to deduct income-tax TDS because the supplier lacks a GSTIN.

## 17. Sample Works Bill Scrutiny

### 17.1 Contract facts

A Sikkim Government department executes an office renovation contract through a registered partnership firm:

* Total taxable contract value: ₹8,00,000
* Current running account bill taxable value: ₹3,00,000
* Statutory GST rate: 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST)
* Contractor status: Partnership firm (furnishing verified PAN and GSTIN)
* Department maintains active deductor GSTIN and TAN.
* Section 194C applies at 2% for non-individual entities.

### 17.2 Gross tax invoice calculation

$$CGST = ₹3,00,000 \times 9\% = ₹27,000$$

$$SGST = ₹3,00,000 \times 9\% = ₹27,000$$

$$\text{Gross Invoice Amount} = ₹3,00,000 + ₹27,000 + ₹27,000 = ₹3,54,000$$

### 17.3 GST-TDS calculation

Because the underlying contract value (₹8,00,000) exceeds ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS applies on the current taxable base of ₹3,00,000:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹3,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹3,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹3,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹3,000$$

$$\text{Total GST-TDS Deducted} = ₹3,000 + ₹3,000 = ₹6,000$$

### 17.4 Income-tax TDS calculation

For a partnership firm, tax is deducted at 2% under Section 194C:

$$\text{Income-tax TDS} = ₹3,00,000 \times 2\% = ₹6,000$$

### 17.5 Net payment voucher passing

$$\text{Net Payable} = ₹3,54,000 - ₹6,000 - ₹6,000 = ₹3,42,000$$

> **Note:** This calculation illustrates tax deductions only. Contractual retention money, labour welfare cess, advance adjustments and liquidated damages must be calculated and deducted separately where applicable.

## 18. Special GST Procurement Situations

### 18.1 Pure labour contracts

Pure labour contracts are exempt only when they meet specific notification criteria. For example, Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) exempts pure labour contracts for construction under designated beneficiary-led individual house-construction schemes. Ordinary departmental labour contracts remain taxable.

### 18.2 Sanitation and conservancy works

Sanitation and solid waste management contracts performed for local authorities may be exempt under Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), provided the activity directly discharges functions listed under the Eleventh Schedule (Article 243G) or Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W) of the Constitution. Internal office housekeeping contracts awarded by regular administrative departments remain taxable.

### 18.3 Vehicle hiring versus passenger transport

Tax treatment differs based on contractual control:

* **Renting of a motor vehicle:** The department controls routes, schedules and vehicle deployment. This constitutes a rental service (SAC 9966) and may involve reverse-charge liabilities.
* **Passenger transport service:** The contractor operates transport over pre-determined routes and schedules (SAC 9964).

### 18.4 Maintenance contracts versus works contracts

Civil repairs to permanent structures qualify as works contracts (SAC 9954). In contrast, maintenance or servicing of movable machinery, air conditioners and office computers constitutes maintenance and repair services (SAC 9987), subject to distinct tax classifications.

### 18.5 Capital projects and housing infrastructure

Public infrastructure works executed under specific Central or State flagship schemes require detailed review of funding sources, ownership models and operational notifications before applying concessional rates or exemptions.

## 19. Reverse-Charge Mechanism (RCM)

### 19.1 Statutory principle

Under Section 9(3) of the CGST Act, statutory liability to pay GST shifts from the supplier to the recipient for notified categories of supplies.

### 19.2 Departmental verification for RCM

For notified services, the department must verify:

* Whether the procurement falls under Section 9(3) notifications (e.g., Goods Transport Agency services, legal services by advocates, security services).
* Whether the supplier's legal constitution triggers RCM.
* Whether the department must deposit GST directly via its regular or deductor registration.
* That reverse-charge tax is deposited via cash challan, as input tax credits cannot be used to pay RCM liabilities.

### 19.3 Invalid operational assumptions

* Public offices are never automatically exempt from reverse-charge liabilities.
* Procurement from an unregistered vendor does not automatically trigger reverse charge unless covered by a specific notification.
* The absence of a vendor GSTIN does not waive statutory tax liability.
* Contracts under ₹2,50,000 are not exempt from reverse-charge obligations; the ₹2,50,000 threshold applies solely to GST-TDS under Section 51.

## 20. Input Tax Credit (ITC) for Public Authorities

Government departments and local bodies functioning as administrative authorities cannot claim Input Tax Credit under Section 16 of the CGST Act unless:

* The office maintains regular taxpayer registration under Section 22 or Section 24.
* The inward procurements directly support outward taxable commercial supplies.
* The claims comply with statutory tax invoice and reporting requirements.
* The procurements do not fall under blocked credit categories defined in Section 17(5) of the CGST Act.

Taxes paid by non-commercial administrative offices represent a final component of procurement expenditure.

## 22. Audit Approach for GST and TDS

### 22.1 Audit objectives

Determine whether the public office accurately identified, deducted, deposited, reported and reconciled GST, GST-TDS and income-tax TDS in compliance with statutory provisions.

### 22.2 Key audit questions

* Was the supplier's registration status verified on the GST portal?
* Was GST levied at the correct rate on a valid tax invoice?
* Was the taxable supply properly distinguished from exempt activities?
* Was GST-TDS calculated on the base value excluding taxes?
* Was GST-TDS deducted on all eligible contracts exceeding ₹2,50,000?
* Was income-tax TDS deducted in accordance with Section 194C thresholds?
* Were contractor PAN and office TAN validated on vouchers?
* Were statutory deductions deposited within established timeframes?
* Were Form GSTR-7 and Form 26Q returns filed on time?
* Do register balances agree with cashbook disbursements?

### 22.3 Primary audit documentation

* Tender notices, comparative statements and contract agreements.
* Sanctions, work orders and approved purchase orders.
* Original vendor tax invoices and debit notes.
* Measurement Books (MB) with engineer certifications.
* Stock registers and asset registers.
* Bank statements, treasury schedules and payment vouchers.
* GST-TDS and income-tax TDS registers.
* Challans confirming tax remittances to Government accounts.
* Filed Form GSTR-7 and Form 26Q returns.
* Issued Form GSTR-7A and Form 16A certificates.
* Periodic bank and ledger reconciliation statements.

## 23. Standard Audit Observation Templates

### 23.1 Audit observation: Non-deduction of GST-TDS

**Criteria:** Under Section 51 of the CGST Act, 2017, and corresponding State GST law, a public authority must deduct 2% GST-TDS from taxable supply payments made under a contract whose value exceeds ₹2,50,000 (excluding taxes).

**Condition:** The office awarded a civil works contract with a taxable value of ₹[Amount]. Payments totaling ₹[Amount] were released without deducting statutory GST-TDS.

**Effect:** Non-deduction and non-remittance of GST-TDS amounting to ₹[Amount], resulting in statutory non-compliance and exposure to penal interest.

**Recommendation:** The office must assess the liability, obtain registration if unassigned, remit outstanding amounts to the Government account, submit Form GSTR-7 and institute pre-payment verification controls.

### 23.2 Audit observation: Non-deduction of income-tax TDS under Section 194C

**Criteria:** Under Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, tax must be deducted from contractor payments exceeding ₹30,000 for a single bill or ₹1,00,000 in aggregate within a financial year.

**Condition:** The office disbursed ₹[Amount] to a works contractor under an eligible contract without deducting income-tax TDS.

**Effect:** Non-compliance with Section 194C, exposing the office to recovery proceedings, interest under Section 201 and penalties under Section 271C.

**Recommendation:** The office should determine the tax liability, remit the outstanding amount using its TAN, file corrected statements and implement a contractor threshold monitoring system.

### 23.3 Audit observation: GST billed without a valid GSTIN

**Criteria:** Under Section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017, a tax invoice charging CGST and SGST can be issued only by a registered supplier holding an active GSTIN.

**Condition:** A contractor billed CGST and SGST totaling ₹[Amount], but the invoice lacked an active GSTIN and no verification records were on file.

**Effect:** Public funds were disbursed against unverified tax charges, creating a risk of irregular payments.

**Recommendation:** The office should recover the unauthorized tax payment of ₹[Amount], verify the contractor's registration status on the GST portal and reject any invoice charging GST without an active GSTIN.

### 23.4 Audit observation: TDS deducted without a valid TAN

**Criteria:** Under Section 203A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, every person deducting tax at source must obtain and quote a valid TAN on all challans, statements and certificates.

**Condition:** The office deducted ₹[Amount] as income-tax TDS from contractor bills, but lacked an allotted TAN and could not provide proof of statutory remittance or return filing.

**Effect:** Deducted funds remained uncredited to the Central Government and contractor tax accounts (Form 26AS), leaving the office exposed to interest and reporting penalties.

**Recommendation:** The office must apply for a TAN in Form 49B immediately, regularize outstanding remittances and ensure no deductions occur without an active TAN.

## 24. Internal Control Framework

### 24.1 Pre-payment verification checklist

Before approving any works or supply bill, the accounts section must verify:

* Payee legal identity and PAN documentation.
* Validated supplier GSTIN (for invoices claiming GST).
* Active deductor GSTIN and TAN recorded on the voucher.
* Contract value to test the ₹2,50,000 GST-TDS threshold.
* Accurate separation of taxable base from GST amounts.
* Independent calculations for GST-TDS and income-tax TDS.
* Attached Measurement Book entries and engineer certifications.

### 24.2 Maker-checker workflow

Voucher processing must remain divided:

* **The Preparer (Accountant):** Verifies invoices, checks rates against sanctions, tests threshold limits and prepares the scrutiny sheet.
* **The Checker (Drawing and Disbursing Officer):** Re-verifies calculations, reviews portal registrations, confirms TAN/GSTIN details and authorizes disbursement.

### 24.3 Segregation of administrative duties

To maintain financial control, separate the following duties:

* Bill preparation and scrutiny.
* Technical inspection and measurement.
* Tax calculation and ledger entry.
* Financial sanction and voucher passing.
* Fund disbursement and banking operations.
* Tax remittance to Government accounts.
* Return filing and certificate generation.
* Periodic ledger reconciliation.

### 24.4 Statutory tax calendar

* **GST-TDS remittance:** Remit to the Government within 10 days after the deduction month.
* **Form GSTR-7 return:** File within 10 days after the deduction month.
* **Form GSTR-7A certificate:** Generate and issue to the deductee after filing Form GSTR-7.
* **Income-tax TDS remittance:** Remit challans within 7 days of the following month (or by April 30 for March deductions).
* **Form 26Q return:** File quarterly by July 31, October 31, January 31 and May 31.
* **Form 16A certificate:** Issue to deductees within 15 days of filing quarterly statements.

### 24.5 Monthly supervisory review

The Accounts Officer must review:

* All contracts and supply orders exceeding ₹2,50,000.
* All works vouchers and running account bills.
* All vouchers charging GST.
* All vouchers submitted by unregistered vendors.
* All payments subject to income-tax TDS.
* Pending tax remittances and challan receipts.
* Unfiled returns or portal filing rejections.

## 28. Conclusion

Sikkim Government offices and local bodies must manage GST and TDS as distinct statutory compliance responsibilities.

* **Supplier GSTIN** identifies the registered vendor or contractor.
* **Deductor GSTIN** identifies the public office as a GST-TDS deductor under Section 51.
* **TAN** identifies the public office responsible for income-tax TDS.
* **Contractor PAN** identifies the payee for income-tax deductions.
* **Government status** does not provide an automatic exemption from GST.
* **Ordinary works contracts** are generally taxed at 18%, subject to statutory classification.
* **GST-TDS** applies at 2% on taxable contract values exceeding ₹2,50,000, computed strictly on the base value excluding taxes.
* **Income-tax TDS** applies independently under Section 194C based on payment thresholds.
* **Unregistered vendors** may be paid if turnover limits permit, but must never charge GST.
* **Missing identifiers** require immediate regularisation; offices must never invent numbers or omit statutory deductions.

Accounts officers must follow a structured review: classify the supply, verify vendor status, confirm rates and exemptions, test contract thresholds, calculate GST-TDS and income-tax TDS independently, verify all identifying numbers, approve the voucher, remit taxes to the Government and reconcile all registers.
