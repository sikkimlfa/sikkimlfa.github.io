# GST and TDS in Local-Body Bills in Sikkim

When a Gram Panchayat, Zilla Panchayat or Municipality pays a contractor or supplier, the bill may involve several separate compliance questions:

* Is the supply taxable under GST?
* Is the supplier registered or unregistered?
* Is the local body required to deduct GST-TDS?
* Is income-tax TDS under Section 194C also applicable?
* Does the deductor have a GSTIN as a tax deductor?
* Does the deductor have a TAN?
* What should happen if the bill does not show the deductor’s GSTIN or TAN?
* Should payment be released, returned or processed with a compliance note?

These questions are often confused because GST, GST-TDS and income-tax TDS operate as distinct statutory regimes. A local body must examine them separately before passing a works or supply bill.

> **Important:** This guide serves as a practical audit and bill-processing manual, not formal legal or tax counsel. GST and income-tax provisions undergo frequent amendments, notifications, circulars and judicial interpretations. Verify current CBIC, GSTN, Income Tax Department and Government of Sikkim instructions before executing deductions or returning bills.

## 1. Statutory Concepts

### 1.1 GST charged by the supplier

GST is charged by a registered supplier on a taxable supply. The supplier levies:

* CGST and SGST for intra-State supplies.
* IGST for inter-State supplies.
* Applicable cess, where statutory provisions mandate.

A registered supplier must issue a formal tax invoice containing:

* Supplier legal name and address.
* Supplier GSTIN.
* Consecutive invoice number and date.
* Description of goods or services.
* Taxable value.
* Statutory tax rates and levied tax amounts.
* Place of supply, with State name and code.
* Recipient details.

### 1.2 GST-TDS

GST-TDS is deducted under Section 51 of the CGST Act and corresponding State GST legislation. It applies to designated deductors, including:

* Central Government departments.
* State Government departments.
* Local authorities.
* Governmental agencies.
* Notified public-sector undertakings and statutory boards.

GST-TDS is deducted at source from gross disbursements (excluding taxes) when statutory criteria are satisfied.

### 1.3 Income-tax TDS

Income-tax TDS is deducted under the Income-tax Act, 1961. For works contracts and labour arrangements, Section 194C governs payments to resident contractors for:

* Works contracts.
* Labour supply.
* Advertising.
* Carriage of goods and passengers.
* Catering.
* Manufacturing according to client specifications using client-provided materials.

Entities responsible for deducting specified tax at source must obtain a Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN).

A supplier charges GST on a taxable bill, whereas the local body separately evaluates deductions for GST-TDS under GST law and income-tax TDS under the Income-tax Act, 1961. One deduction does not substitute for the other.

## 2. Core Terminology

### 2.1 Deductor

The statutory authority deducting tax from disbursements:

* Gram Panchayat Unit (GPU).
* Zilla Panchayat (ZP).
* Municipality or Municipal Corporation.
* Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO).
* Designated treasury or financial administrator.

### 2.2 Deductee

The payee, contractor, merchant or service agency from whose gross claim statutory deductions are withheld.

### 2.3 Supplier

The party executing or supplying:

* Physical goods and machinery.
* Professional, technical or manual services.
* Works contracts and composite supplies.
* Construction, structural renovations and periodic maintenance.

### 2.4 Registered supplier

A supplier holding a verified GSTIN under the GST regime. Registered suppliers must issue tax invoices showing prescribed particulars and charge statutory taxes on taxable procurements.

### 2.5 Unregistered supplier

A supplier without a GST registration. Procurement from an unregistered entity does not indicate illegality or demand immediate voucher rejection. Bill acceptance depends on:

* Nature and value of the procurement.
* Statutory threshold requirements for compulsory supplier registration.
* Applicability of product or service exemptions.
* Place of supply and reverse-charge mandates.
* General Financial Rules and State procurement provisions.
* Income-tax PAN status.

### 2.6 GSTIN

The Goods and Services Tax Identification Number. While a registered merchant holds an operational supplier GSTIN, a local body requiring TDS deduction holds a dedicated tax-deductor GSTIN.

### 2.7 TAN

The Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number assigned by the Income Tax Department to authorities responsible for withholding tax at source under the Income-tax Act, 1961. A TAN operates completely independently from:

* Supplier GSTIN.
* Deductor GST-TDS GSTIN.
* Contractor PAN.
* Local body PAN.

## 3. Legal Framework

### 3.1 GST statutory provisions

* **Section 7, CGST Act:** Scope of supply.
* **Section 9, CGST Act:** Levy and collection of tax.
* **Section 10, CGST Act:** Composition levy parameters.
* **Section 24(vi), CGST Act:** Compulsory registration for TDS deductors.
* **Section 31, CGST Act:** Tax invoice mandates.
* **Section 51, CGST Act:** Tax deduction at source parameters.
* **Section 73, 74 or 74A, CGST Act:** Demand and recovery mechanisms.
* **Rules 12 and 66, CGST Rules, 2017:** Deductor registration, returns and certificates.
* **Statutory Forms:** Form GST REG-07 (Registration Application), Form GSTR-7 (TDS Return) and Form GSTR-7A (TDS Certificate).

Statutory authorities under Section 51 include local authorities and governmental agencies. The monetary threshold excludes CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess shown on the invoice.

### 3.2 Income-tax statutory provisions

* **Section 194C:** Payments to resident contractors and sub-contractors.
* **Section 194J:** Fees for technical or professional engagements.
* **Section 194I:** Rent disbursements.
* **Section 197:** Certificates for lower or nil tax withholding.
* **Section 206AA:** Higher deduction rates for unverified or missing PAN.
* **Section 206AB:** Higher withholding rates for specified non-filers.
* **Sections 200 and 203:** Payment remittances, return statements and Form 16A certificates.
* **Sections 201, 234E and 271C:** Penal interest, late fees and non-deduction penalties.

## 4. GST-TDS Deductor Registration

### 4.1 Mandatory deductor registration

Every public authority falling within the scope of Section 51 must obtain registration as a tax deductor under Section 24(vi) of the CGST Act, 2017, irrespective of whether its aggregate turnover falls below normal registration limits. Deductors register via Form GST REG-07 using either an official PAN or TAN.

### 4.2 Local authorities in Sikkim

Panchayats and Municipalities fall within the statutory meaning of "local authority" under Section 2(69) of the CGST Act, 2017. A GPU, ZP or Municipality processing qualifying contracts must maintain:

* An active tax-deductor GSTIN.
* A designated Drawing and Disbursing Officer or authorised signatory.
* Portal access for monthly Form GSTR-7 filings.
* An established tax deduction and ledger reconciliation procedure.

### 4.3 Deductor GSTIN absent on bill vouchers

If a local body qualifies as an obligated deductor but its tax-deductor GSTIN is omitted from the bill records:

* Do not ignore the statutory omission.
* Return the voucher to rectify required documentation details.
* Record an official bill audit query.
* Never write or adopt the supplier's GSTIN as the deductor identifier.
* Escalate immediately to the Finance and Accounts Department to obtain a registration if unassigned.

## 5. GST-TDS Contract Threshold

### 5.1 Threshold parameters

Under Section 51, GST-TDS applies where the total value of taxable supply under a single contract exceeds ₹2,50,000, excluding taxes (CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess) itemised on the invoice.

### 5.2 Contract value versus interim running bills

Threshold eligibility depends on the overall taxable value stipulated in the formal contract or work order, rather than the net claim of an individual progressive bill.

#### Example

A Municipality signs a taxable sanitation and drainage contract:

* Total contract value excluding GST: ₹3,00,000.
* Bill 1 value: ₹1,00,000.
* Bill 2 value: ₹1,00,000.
* Final Bill value: ₹1,00,000.

Because the underlying taxable contract value exceeds ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS applies to every progressive payment made under the contract, even though each bill falls below ₹2,50,000.

### 5.3 Artificially divided contracts

When distinct work orders are legitimately executed below ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS does not apply solely because aggregate billing across several unrelated awards exceeds that amount. However, dividing single operational assignments into smaller sub-contracts to bypass statutory thresholds violates procurement discipline and triggers audit objections.

Audit warning signs for artificial splitting include:

* Identical contractor engaged under concurrent contracts.
* Identical scope, nature or timeline of work.
* Same procurement approval reference or fund sanction.
* Multiple bills prepared just below ₹2,50,000.
* Execution at the same site, facility or municipal ward.

## 6. GST-TDS Rates and Calculations

### 6.1 Statutory tax rates

The deduction rate depends on the character of the supply:

* **Intra-State supply:** 1% CGST plus 1% SGST (Total: 2%).
* **Inter-State supply:** 2% IGST.

### 6.2 Formula and base calculation

GST-TDS is calculated exclusively on taxable supply value, omitting all itemised GST charges and cesses:

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable GST-TDS Rate}$$

#### Example

* Taxable value of civil repairs: ₹5,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹90,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹5,90,000

For an intra-State works supply:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,000 + ₹5,00,00 = ₹10,000$$

Disbursement net of GST-TDS:

$$Net\text{ }Disbursement = ₹5,90,000 - ₹10,000 = ₹5,80,000$$

Income-tax TDS under Section 194C and other statutory recoveries must be calculated separately from this balance.

## 7. Exceptions to GST-TDS Deduction

GST-TDS is not deducted when:

* Taxable value under the contract does not exceed ₹2,50,000.
* The procurement consists of wholly exempt or non-taxable goods or services.
* The transaction falls under mandatory reverse-charge mechanisms.
* The supplier is unregistered, as outlined in official CBIC operational procedures.
* Location of the supplier and place of supply reside in a State different from the registration State of the deductor.

Exemption from GST-TDS does not exempt the voucher from scrutiny. The local body must confirm whether the unregistered vendor should have registered under statutory limits, whether reverse-charge obligations arise and whether income-tax TDS applies.

## 8. Managing Registered and Unregistered Contractors

### 8.1 Registered contractors

A registered contractor must submit a tax invoice detailing:

* Active GSTIN and permanent PAN.
* Harmonised System of Nomenclature (HSN) or Service Accounting Code (SAC).
* Itemised taxable values and statutory tax rates.
* Place of supply and corresponding State jurisdiction.
* Direct work-order reference and banking details.

The local body must authenticate the vendor's registration status on the official GST portal prior to passing the bill.

### 8.2 Unregistered contractors

An unregistered contractor typically provides an ordinary bill or commercial statement showing:

* Full legal name, residential address and verified PAN.
* Scope of work, technical references and verified measurements.
* Bank account details for direct transfer.
* Explicit declaration confirming non-registration under GST.

> **Warning:** A contractor without a valid GSTIN must never itemise or levy CGST, SGST or IGST. If an unregistered contractor includes GST charges on an invoice, the accounts branch must reject the bill for immediate correction.

### 8.3 Payment criteria for unregistered vendors

Payment can be released to an unregistered vendor provided that:

* Aggregate contractor turnover remains below the statutory GST registration threshold.
* The procurement adheres to State Financial Rules and local purchase orders.
* No reverse-charge liability remains unresolved.
* Applicable income-tax TDS deductions under Section 194C are executed.

## 9. Works Contract Processing

### 9.1 Works contract defined

Under Section 2(119) of the CGST Act, 2017, a works contract covers building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance or renovation of immovable property wherein transfer of property in goods is involved.

For GST purposes, works contracts are classified as composite supplies of services.

### 9.2 Vetting procedures for works claims

Accounts branches must assemble the following records before admitting a works claim:

* Sanctioned estimate, administrative approval and technical sanction.
* Contract agreement and detailed work order.
* Standard Measurement Book (MB) with entries attested by the responsible site engineer.
* Current running account (RA) bill or completion statement.
* Certified tax invoice displaying contractor GSTIN and PAN.
* Verified physical completion certificate and quality clearance.
* Itemised calculations for GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS, labour cess and contractual retention amounts.

## 10. Comparative Framework: GST-TDS versus Income-Tax TDS

| Parameter | GST-TDS | Income-tax TDS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governing law | CGST, SGST and IGST Acts, 2017 | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Relevant section | Section 51, CGST Act | Section 194C (Works Contracts) |
| Deductor identification | GSTIN (Tax Deductor profile) | TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) |
| Threshold trigger | Taxable contract value above ₹2,50,000 | Single bill above ₹30,000 or aggregate annual payments above ₹1,00,000 |
| Assessment base | Taxable value excluding itemised GST and cess | Gross amount credited or paid |
| Standard rates | Intra-State: 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST); Inter-State: 2% IGST | 1% (Individual/HUF); 2% (Other corporate/firm entities) |
| Statutory return | Form GSTR-7 | Form 26Q |
| Compliance certificate | Form GSTR-7A | Form 16A |
| Payee credit mechanism | Electronic Cash Ledger on GST portal | Annual Tax Statement (Form 26AS / AIS) |
| Registration process | Form GST REG-07 | Form 49B |

Both deductions are mandatory where applicable. GST-TDS and income-tax TDS are calculated independently; neither deduction reduces the base of the other.

## 11. Income-Tax TDS Under Section 194C

### 11.1 Scope and application

Section 194C applies to payments made to resident contractors for carrying out works, including supply of labour. Qualifying activities include:

* Road and civil infrastructure development.
* Public utility and drainage clearances.
* Waste collection, transport and facility maintenance.
* Vehicle and equipment hiring.
* Dedicated catering and events.

### 11.2 Standard statutory rates

* **1%:** Payable to an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF).
* **2%:** Payable to persons other than individuals or HUFs (firms, companies, societies).
* **20%:** Mandatory minimum deduction rate under Section 206AA if the contractor fails to provide a verified PAN.

### 11.3 Statutory payment thresholds

Under current provisions of Section 194C:

* No deduction is required if a single contract payment does not exceed ₹30,000.
* Deduction applies once aggregate disbursements to the same contractor exceed ₹1,00,000 within a financial year.

## 12. Processing Deficiencies: Missing TAN

A local authority cannot make lawful income-tax deductions without holding an active TAN. If deductions are made without a TAN, tax credits cannot be deposited through the tax accounting system or reported via Form 26Q.

If a local body lacks a valid TAN:

1. Confirm whether an existing TAN was previously allotted to the administrative department.
2. Apply for allotment of TAN in Form 49B immediately.
3. Never use an individual officer's personal PAN or an external vendor's identifier as a substitute.
4. Record an administrative compliance delay note on the file.
5. Deposit accumulated deductions with statutory interest as soon as the registration is activated.

---

## 13. Processing Deficiencies: Missing Deductor GSTIN

A local body deducting GST-TDS must obtain registration as a tax deductor under Section 24(vi) of the CGST Act, 2017:

| Scenario | Audit and Operational Action |
| --- | --- |
| Deductor is not legally obligated | Record operational exemption reasons and process voucher without GST-TDS. |
| Deductor is liable but unassigned | Obtain Form GST REG-07 registration; do not pass vouchers without statutory deductions. |
| Application pending at department | Note formal acknowledgment reference and follow up for allocation. |
| Deductor GSTIN active but not printed | Return voucher for clerical inclusion or amend through payment scrutiny sheet. |
| Contractor's GSTIN missing on claim | Refuse GST disbursement; do not treat unverified claimants as registered vendors. |
| GST charged by unregistered vendor | Return bill; reject any unauthorised tax levies. |

## 17. Practical Billing Calculations

### 17.1 Registered local contractor in Sikkim

A Municipal Council in Sikkim engages a local registered contractor (sole proprietor) for town road repairs.

* Total contract value (excluding GST): ₹10,00,000
* Statutory GST rate: 18%
* Current RA bill taxable value: ₹4,00,000
* CGST (9%): ₹36,000
* SGST (9%): ₹36,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹4,72,000
* Valid PAN supplied; Deductor GSTIN and TAN active.

#### 1. GST-TDS calculation

Because the taxable contract value exceeds ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS applies on the taxable base of ₹4,00,000:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹8,000$$

#### 2. Income-tax TDS calculation

Under Section 194C, an individual contractor providing a valid PAN is subject to a 1% deduction on the taxable amount:

$$Income\text{-}tax\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

#### 3. Net disbursement

$$Net\text{ }Payment = ₹4,72,000 - ₹8,000 - ₹4,000 = ₹4,60,000$$

### 17.2 Unregistered local labour contractor

A Gram Panchayat engages an unregistered local contractor for drain desilting.

* Total contract value: ₹1,80,000
* Contractor has provided PAN, but holds no GSTIN.
* The contractor does not itemise GST on the bill.

#### Tax evaluation

* **GST-TDS:** Because total contract value (₹1,80,000) does not exceed ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS does not apply.
* **Income-tax TDS:** Because the total contract exceeds the ₹1,00,000 annual threshold under Section 194C, income-tax TDS applies at 1% on the bill amount (assuming a valid PAN is on record).

### 17.3 Calculating taxable base from GST-inclusive bills

A contractor submits a gross bill of ₹2,60,000 stating that it includes 18% GST, without separating the taxable base. The local body must calculate the base amount to determine threshold applicability:

$$\text{Taxable Value} = \text{Gross Inclusive Amount} \times \frac{100}{100 + \text{GST Rate}}$$

$$\text{Taxable Value} = ₹2,60,000 \times \frac{100}{118} \approx ₹2,20,339$$

Because the base value (₹2,20,339) is below ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS does not apply.

## 18. Common Administrative and Audit Errors

1. **Conflating GST-TDS with income-tax TDS:** Treating GST-TDS and Section 194C as interchangeable deductions, using identical rates, forms or ledgers.
2. **Deducting GST-TDS on gross value:** Calculating GST-TDS on the invoice total including GST, rather than the taxable supply base.
3. **Using supplier GSTIN as deductor GSTIN:** Entering the contractor's GSTIN as the deductor identifier on payment vouchers and returns.
4. **Substituting GSTIN for TAN:** Attempting to report income-tax TDS using the local body's GSTIN rather than a valid TAN.
5. **Treating absence of GSTIN as fraud:** Automatically refusing payment to small, unregistered suppliers whose turnover legitimately falls below statutory registration thresholds.
6. **Permitting unregistered vendors to collect GST:** Releasing payments where an unregistered supplier itemsises or charges GST on the invoice.
7. **Testing thresholds on single bills:** Failing to deduct GST-TDS on running bills because each bill is under ₹2,50,000, even when the underlying contract value exceeds that threshold.
8. **Artificial contract splitting:** Splitting a single works project into multiple purchase orders under ₹2,50,000 to bypass GST-TDS.
9. **Failing to obtain PAN:** Processing contractor claims without obtaining a PAN, resulting in under-deduction under Section 206AA.
10. **Neglecting reverse-charge liabilities:** Failing to identify procurements where the local authority must pay GST directly under reverse-charge rules.
11. **Poor ledger reconciliation:** Failing to reconcile cashbook entries with Form GSTR-7, Form 26Q and Treasury balances.

## 21. Audit Procedures for Sikkim Local Bodies

### 21.1 Audit focus across administrative tiers

* **Gram Panchayat Units (GPUs):** Examine local works vouchers, spot-purchase claims, small labour contracts, contractor PAN verification, unauthorized GST levies by unregistered suppliers, contract splitting and the presence of dedicated TAN or GSTIN registrations.
* **Zilla Panchayats (ZPs):** Review district-level works, multi-GPU program funds, high-value infrastructure contracts, tax deduction consistency across engineering branches and reconciliation between district books and treasury records.
* **Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities):** Audit waste collection and disposal contracts, property maintenance agreements, machinery rentals, parking and market collections, professional consulting payments under Section 194J and compliance across municipal divisions.

### 21.2 Audit evidence to assemble

* Certified contract agreements, estimates and technical sanctions.
* Original vendor tax invoices and measurement records.
* Portal printouts confirming supplier GSTIN and PAN status.
* Deductor registration certificates (Form GST REG-06 for GSTIN; Form 49B for TAN).
* Bill scrutiny notes and formal payment vouchers.
* Treasury challans, bank statements and remittance records.
* Filed Form GSTR-7 and Form 26Q returns.
* Issued Form GSTR-7A and Form 16A certificates.
* Contractor running ledgers and year-end reconciliation statements.

## 22. Standard Audit Observation Templates

### 22.1 Audit observation: Failure to deduct GST-TDS

**Criteria:** Under Section 51 of the CGST Act, 2017, and corresponding State GST law, a local authority must deduct 2% GST-TDS from taxable supply payments made under a contract whose value exceeds ₹2,50,000 (excluding itemised taxes).

**Condition:** The local body awarded a civil works contract with a taxable value of ₹[Amount]. Progressive disbursements totaling ₹[Amount] were released without deducting GST-TDS.

**Effect:** Non-deduction and non-remittance of statutory GST-TDS of ₹[Amount], resulting in a violation of Section 51, loss of timely revenue to the Government and potential exposure to penal interest.

**Recommendation:** The local body should calculate the undeducted liability, secure deductor registration if unassigned, remit the outstanding balance to the Government account, submit Form GSTR-7 and institute pre-payment verification controls.

### 22.2 Audit observation: Failure to deduct income-tax TDS under Section 194C

**Criteria:** Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961, requires local authorities to deduct tax at source from payments to resident contractors exceeding ₹30,000 for a single bill or ₹1,00,000 in aggregate within a financial year.

**Condition:** The local authority disbursed ₹[Amount] to a works contractor under a contract exceeding the statutory threshold without deducting income-tax TDS.

**Effect:** Non-compliance with Section 194C, exposing the local body to recovery proceedings, interest under Section 201 and penalty provisions under Section 271C.

**Recommendation:** The local body should determine liability under the applicable tax rates, remit arrears through its TAN, file corrected quarterly statements and implement a bill checklist to track contractor thresholds.

### 22.3 Audit observation: GST charged without verified supplier GSTIN

**Criteria:** Under Section 31 of the CGST Act, 2017, a tax invoice charging CGST and SGST can be issued only by a registered supplier holding a valid GSTIN.

**Condition:** A contractor billed CGST and SGST totaling ₹[Amount] on a works bill, but the invoice omitted a valid GSTIN and no verification records were found on file.

**Effect:** Disbursement of public funds against unverified tax levies, creating risk of irregular payment and ineligible tax claims.

**Recommendation:** The local body should recover the unauthorized tax payment of ₹[Amount], verify the contractor's registration status on the GST portal and reject any invoice charging GST without a verified GSTIN.

### 22.4 Audit observation: Deductions executed without a valid TAN

**Criteria:** Under Section 203A of the Income-tax Act, 1961, every person deducting tax under Chapter XVII-B must hold and cite a valid TAN on all challans, returns and certificates.

**Condition:** The local authority deducted ₹[Amount] as income-tax TDS from contractor bills, but lacked an allotted TAN and could not provide proof of statutory remittance or quarterly statement filing.

**Effect:** Deducted funds remained uncredited to the Central Government and contractor tax accounts (Form 26AS), leaving the local body vulnerable to penal interest and reporting penalties.

**Recommendation:** The local authority must apply for a TAN in Form 49B immediately, regularize outstanding remittances and ensure that no tax deductions occur without an active TAN.

## 23. Internal Financial Controls

### 23.1 Bill passing checklist

No works or supply voucher should be approved for disbursement without documenting:

* Verified identity and PAN of the payee.
* Supplier registration status (authenticated on the GST portal if claiming GST).
* Active deductor GSTIN and TAN recorded on the voucher.
* Value of the underlying contract to test the ₹2,50,000 GST-TDS threshold.
* Correct separation of taxable base from GST and other taxes.
* Independent calculations for GST-TDS and income-tax TDS.
* Attached Measurement Book records and engineer completion certificates.

### 23.2 Maker-checker review protocol

The preparation and review of vouchers must remain divided:

* **The Preparer (Accounts Assistant / Accountant):** Verifies invoices, validates rates against sanctions, tests threshold limits and prepares the scrutiny sheet.
* **The Checker (DDO / Head of Accounts):** Re-verifies calculations, reviews portal registrations, confirms TAN/GSTIN details and authorizes net disbursement.

### 23.3 Statutory tax calendar

* **GST-TDS deposit:** Remit to the Government within 10 days after the end of the deduction month.
* **Form GSTR-7 return:** File within 10 days after the end of the deduction month.
* **Form GSTR-7A certificate:** Make available to the deductee after the return is accepted and funds are credited.
* **Income-tax TDS deposit:** Remit challans within 7 days of the following month (or by April 30 for March deductions).
* **Form 26Q return:** File quarterly by July 31, October 31, January 31 and May 31.
* **Form 16A certificate:** Issue to deductees within 15 days of filing quarterly statements.

### 23.4 Monthly ledger reconciliation

Every month, the accounts branch must reconcile:

$$\text{Bill Register} \longleftrightarrow \text{TDS Registers} \longleftrightarrow \text{Cashbook} \longleftrightarrow \text{Bank Statements} \longleftrightarrow \text{Filed Returns (GSTR-7 26Q)}$$

## 25. Final Bill Processing Checklist

### 1. Supplier details

* [ ] Legal business name confirmed against work order.
* [ ] Registered business address recorded.
* [ ] Verified PAN documentation attached.
* [ ] Active GSTIN authenticated on portal (if registered).
* [ ] Direct bank mandate and IFSC verified.

### 2. Contract and procurement

* [ ] Work-order number and sanction date recorded.
* [ ] Total contract value excluding taxes confirmed.
* [ ] Taxable components separated from exempt goods/services.
* [ ] Contract verified against artificial splitting risks.

### 3. GST verification

* [ ] Taxable supply confirmed.
* [ ] Applicable GST rate verified against current schedules.
* [ ] Correct tax heads identified (CGST/SGST or IGST).
* [ ] Compliant tax invoice verified.
* [ ] GST-TDS eligibility confirmed (contract > ₹2,50,000).
* [ ] Deductor GSTIN recorded.
* [ ] Return and certificate schedule noted.

### 4. Income-tax verification

* [ ] Applicable deduction section verified (e.g., Section 194C).
* [ ] Single-payment (₹30,000) and aggregate (₹1,00,000) limits checked.
* [ ] Valid contractor PAN confirmed.
* [ ] Deductor TAN confirmed.
* [ ] Applicable deduction rate verified.
* [ ] Deposit and Form 26Q filing schedule noted.

### 5. Works measurements and passing

* [ ] Original work order attached.
* [ ] Standard Measurement Book verified and signed by site engineer.
* [ ] Works completion / stage certification confirmed.
* [ ] Stock register entries completed (for materials/goods).
* [ ] Statutory withholdings and contractual retention amounts calculated.
* [ ] Net payment voucher approved by Competent Financial Authority.

---

GST, GST-TDS and income-tax TDS operate as independent statutory obligations. A Sikkim local body processing works and supply vouchers must examine each mechanism separately.

* **Supplier GSTIN** identifies the registered contractor.
* **Deductor GSTIN** identifies the local authority as a tax deductor under the GST law.
* **TAN** identifies the local authority responsible for income-tax TDS.
* **Contractor PAN** identifies the payee for income-tax deductions.
* **GST-TDS** applies to taxable contract values exceeding ₹2,50,000, excluding taxes.
* **Income-tax TDS** applies independently under Section 194C based on payment thresholds.
* **Unregistered contractors** may be paid if turnover criteria are met, but must never charge GST.
* **Missing identifiers** require immediate regularisation; a local body must never invent numbers or ignore statutory deductions.

Accounts officers must follow a consistent review order: verify the contractor, classify the supply, examine registration, test contract value, calculate GST, deduct GST-TDS and income-tax TDS independently, verify all identifying numbers, approve the voucher, remit taxes to the Government and reconcile all registers.
