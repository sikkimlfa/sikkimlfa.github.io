# Audit Report Templates for Sikkim Local Bodies

An audit report for a Gram Panchayat, Zilla Panchayat or Municipality should do more than list mistakes. It should show what was examined, which law or rule applied, what actually happened, how much money or public service was affected, who was responsible, what action is required and whether the issue has been corrected.

This guide provides a common audit-report structure for:

* Gram Panchayat Units.
* Zilla Panchayats.
* Municipal Corporations.
* Municipal Councils.
* Nagar Panchayats.

The templates are designed for financial, compliance, internal-control, transaction and performance audits. They should be adapted to the latest Sikkim Acts, Financial Rules, audit instructions, DLFA procedures, CAG requirements and scheme guidelines.

## 1. Legal and Audit Context

### 1.1 Panchayat Audit Framework

The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993 contains a separate chapter on the audit of Gram Panchayat and Zilla Panchayat funds.

The relevant provisions cover:

* Audit of funds.
* Submission of accounts.
* Powers of auditors.
* Audit reports.
* Action on audit reports.
* Surcharge.
* Appeal.
* Payment of certified sums.

The CAG’s audit reports identify the following provisions:

* Section 48(2): Appointment of an auditor for Gram Panchayat accounts.
* Section 48(3): Audit of Gram Panchayat accounts by the CAG.
* Section 86: Examination and audit of Gram Panchayat and Zilla Panchayat funds.

### 1.2 Municipal Audit Framework

The Sikkim Municipalities Act, 2007 contains the main municipal accounting and audit provisions.

Important sections include:

* Section 55: Maintenance of accounts.
* Section 56: Municipal Accounting Manual.
* Section 57: Financial statement.
* Section 58: Balance sheet.
* Section 59: Submission of financial statement and balance sheet to Auditor.
* Section 60: Powers and appointment of auditor.
* Section 61: Audit report.
* Section 62: Placing audited accounts before the Municipality.
* Section 63: Submission of audited accounts.
* Section 64: Enforcement of orders on audit report.
* Section 65: Special audit.
* Section 66: Internal audit.
* Section 67: Municipal Accounts Committee.

### 1.3 DLFA and CAG

The Director of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) is the primary State-level auditor for local bodies in Sikkim. The State Government established the DLFA in June 2012 under the Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012.

The State also entrusted the audit of Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies to the CAG under Section 20(1) of the CAG’s Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service Act, 1971, through Technical Guidance and Support arrangements.

> **Note:** There can be more than one audit layer operating within local bodies:
> * Regular statutory audit by the DLFA or another authorised auditor.
> * CAG audit, supplementary audit, test check or technical guidance.
> * Internal audit.
> * Special audit.
> * Social audit for eligible schemes.
> * Legislative or departmental review.
> 
> 
> The exact arrangement depends on the current law, notification and audit order.

## 2. Comparison of Templates Across Tiers

While the basic reporting structure remains common, the operational focus differs significantly across tiers:

| Area | Gram Panchayat | Zilla Panchayat | Municipality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main level | Village or group of villages | District | Urban area |
| Main records | Cashbook, receipts, grants, works, assets | Consolidated accounts, district schemes, works, grants | Financial statements, balance sheet, revenue, municipal services |
| Main revenue | Taxes, fees, user charges and grants | Assigned revenues, grants and schemes | Property tax, licences, parking, user charges and grants |
| Main service focus | Village roads, water, sanitation and community assets | District planning, coordination and larger works | Waste, drainage, roads, streetlights and urban services |
| Key audit risk | Weak records and unsupported payments | Poor consolidation and district-level planning | Revenue, grants, service delivery and municipal accounting |
| Performance audit | Local outputs and beneficiaries | District outcomes and coordination | Urban service levels and citizen outcomes |

## 12. Internal Control Assessment

### Control Environment

* Are responsibilities clearly assigned?
* Are financial powers delegated in writing?
* Are meetings and decisions documented?
* Are subordinate officials adequately supervised?

### Revenue Controls

* Are demand registers properly maintained?
* Are receipt books machine-numbered or pre-numbered?
* Are collections deposited promptly into bank accounts?
* Are revenue arrears reviewed systematically?

### Expenditure Controls

* Are all payments supported by original vouchers?
* Are approvals obtained prior to incurring expenditure?
* Are budget limits strictly observed?
* Are financial duties adequately segregated?

### Bank and Cash Controls

* Is the cashbook written and closed daily?
* Is bank reconciliation prepared on a monthly basis?
* Is physical cash verified periodically by the head of office?
* Are cheque books kept under secure custody?

### Asset Controls

* Is an asset register updated regularly?
* Is annual physical verification conducted?
* Are specific custodians designated for all assets?
* Are losses and damages reported promptly?

### Audit Follow-Up

* Are previous audit objections tracked systematically?
* Are recoveries effected in accordance with orders?
* Are repeat control deficiencies progressively reduced?

## 25. Core Elements of an Observation

* **Criteria:** What standard or rule should have been followed?
* **Condition:** What situation was actually observed in audit?
* **Evidence:** Which documents, measurements or records substantiate the finding?
* **Cause:** Why did the deviation occur?
* **Effect:** What was the financial, legal or service consequence?
* **Management Response:** What explanation was provided by the audited entity?
* **Audit Assessment:** Is the response acceptable based on documentary proof?
* **Recommendation:** What specific remedy must management implement?
* **Status:** What is the current status of recovery, correction or compliance?

## 26. Sample Audit Observations

### Audit Observation No. 1: Long-Outstanding Advance of ₹80,000

#### Criteria

Advances must be adjusted within the prescribed statutory period through verified adjustment bills with supporting vouchers. Unspent balances must be promptly refunded to the Panchayat Fund.

#### Condition

An advance of ₹80,000 sanctioned in June 2024 remained unadjusted at the time of audit. No adjustment bill, expenditure vouchers or refund challans were produced.

#### Evidence

* Advance register entries.
* Voucher No. 14 dated 12 June 2024.
* Cashbook debit entry.
* Absence of settlement bills in the adjustment file.

#### Cause

The local body failed to periodically review the advance register and did not enforce accountability on the advance holder.

#### Effect

Public funds amounting to ₹80,000 remained blocked outside the fund account, and the legitimacy of the expenditure could not be verified.

#### Recommendation

The Panchayat should obtain the detailed adjustment bill with supporting vouchers or recover the full advance of ₹80,000 with applicable interest, update the advance register and conduct monthly reconciliation.

#### Status

* Amount recoverable: ₹80,000
* Amount recovered: ₹0
* Further evidence required: Vouchers or treasury receipt
* Responsible authority: Executive Authority / Sachiva
* Due date: 30 days
* Current status: Open

### Audit Observation No. 2: Utilisation Certificate Submitted Without Verified Expenditure

#### Criteria

Utilisation certificates submitted to funding authorities must represent actual, verified expenditure incurred for approved scheme components, supported by measurement and payment vouchers.

#### Condition

The Panchayat submitted a final utilisation certificate for the full sanctioned grant of ₹5,00,000. Examination of the cashbook, bank statements and bills revealed actual verified expenditure of only ₹3,80,000.

#### Cause

The certificate was issued routinely upon receipt or withdrawal of funds rather than following actual work execution and final accounting.

#### Effect

Scheme expenditure was misreported and overstated by ₹1,20,000, creating a risk of grant misuse.

#### Recommendation

The Panchayat should submit a revised utilisation certificate reflecting the true expenditure of ₹3,80,000, credit the unspent balance of ₹1,20,000 along with accrued bank interest to the scheme account, and institute mandatory verification prior to issuing certificates.

#### Status

* Discrepancy amount: ₹1,20,000
* Current status: Open

### Audit Observation No. 3: Payment for Incomplete Community Water Supply Work

#### Criteria

Payments to contractors should be released only for work actually executed, measured in the Measurement Book by the technical officer, certified by the supervising authority and taken over.

#### Condition

The Panchayat disbursed final payment of ₹12,00,000 against a sanctioned work order of ₹15,00,000. Physical inspection revealed that although the RCC storage tank had been constructed, the pipeline distribution network was incomplete and disconnected from the intake source.

#### Evidence

* Work Order No. 42.
* Measurement Book No. 08, pages 14–22.
* Final payment voucher dated 18 March 2025.
* Joint physical inspection report and site photographs.

#### Cause

Supervisory engineers and executive authorities cleared final payments without verifying functional completion at the project site.

#### Effect

An investment of ₹12,00,000 remained unfruitful, and the beneficiary community was deprived of intended drinking water facilities.

#### Recommendation

The Panchayat should direct the contractor to complete the pipeline works without extra cost, carry out fresh technical measurement, and withhold further contracts until physical functionality is certified.

#### Status

* Value of unfruitful expenditure: ₹12,00,000
* Current status: Open

### Audit Observation No. 4: Low Utilisation of Waste-Collection Vehicles

#### Criteria

Capital assets procured through municipal funds must be deployed promptly according to an approved service delivery plan to achieve planned civic outcomes.

#### Condition

The Municipality purchased five waste-collection vehicles at a cost of ₹75,00,000. Two vehicles remained parked and unutilised for 14 months following delivery due to the non-availability of drivers and operational route schedules.

#### Economy

The procurement was initiated without budgeting for driver honoraria or operating costs.

#### Efficiency

With only three vehicles deployed, the fleet operated at a utilisation rate of 60%:

$$Vehicle Utilisation = \frac{Operational Vehicles}{Total Vehicles} \times 100$$

Where $Operational Vehicles = 3$ and $Total Vehicles = 5$, resulting in:

$$Vehicle Utilisation = \frac{3}{5} \times 100 = 60\%$$

#### Effectiveness

Solid waste collection coverage across outer municipal wards remained at 52% against the mandated municipal target of 90%.

#### Recommendation

The Municipality should deploy the idle vehicles by rationalising existing operational staff or contracting drivers, establish route plans, and maintain logbooks to monitor vehicle performance.

#### Status

* Capital cost of idle assets: ₹30,00,000
* Current status: Open

## 27. Categorisation of Audit Findings

### Financial Audit Observations

* Discrepancies in opening or closing cash and bank balances.
* Omission of receipts from accounts or short-crediting.
* Payments unsupported by primary documentation or vouchers.
* Misclassification of capital expenditure as revenue expenditure.
* Unreconciled bank balances and stale cheques.
* Inaccurate recording of physical assets and stores.
* Unrecorded short-term or long-term liabilities.
* Long-pending unadjusted advances.

### Compliance Audit Observations

* Violations of the Sikkim Panchayat Act or Municipalities Act.
* Expenditure incurred without administrative approval or budget sanction.
* Violations of delegated financial powers.
* Splitting of tenders to evade higher sanction thresholds.
* Diversion of tied scheme grants to general administrative expenses.
* Execution of works without formal contract agreements.
* Failure to deduct or deposit statutory taxes (TDS, GST, cess).

### Performance Audit Observations

* **Economy:** Incurring higher prices due to restrictive specifications or non-competitive procurement.
* **Efficiency:** Delays in project completion, idle equipment, cost overruns and administrative bottlenecks.
* **Effectiveness:** Inability to achieve planned scheme outcomes, failure to deliver services to beneficiaries, and unutilised community infrastructure.

## 28. Reporting Financial Impact

### Classification of Identified Amounts

Auditors must accurately categorise financial values based on evidence:

| Classification | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Irregular expenditure | Expenditure incurred contrary to prescribed rules, statutory provisions or administrative approvals |
| Excess payment | Payment made in excess of admissible rates, measured quantities or contract terms |
| Possible loss | Questioned transactions requiring investigation, verification or recovery proceeding |
| Confirmed loss | Financial loss conclusively established through irretrievable loss, fraud or default |
| Unspent balance | Sanctioned funds remaining idle in bank accounts beyond the project completion period |
| Blocked funds | Public money locked in incomplete assets, idle advances or non-functional schemes |
| Unsupported expenditure | Payments lacking primary vouchers, muster rolls, receipts or delivery proof |
| Avoidable expenditure | Expenditure that could have been prevented through proper planning and supervision |
| Misappropriation | Unauthorised conversion, personal retention or fraudulent use of public money |
| Wasteful expenditure | Spending that yielded no tangible public utility or civic benefit |

> **Important:** The term "loss" must be reserved exclusively for instances where definitive documentary proof demonstrates that funds cannot be recovered or that public property has been irretrievably depleted.

## 32. Audit Quality Checklist

Before signing and issuing the audit report, verify all quality indicators:

### Legal Accuracy

* [ ] Is the cited Act, Rule or Notification current and applicable?
* [ ] Are section and rule numbers verified against official gazettes?
* [ ] Is the statutory audit authority correctly designated?

### Factual Accuracy and Evidence

* [ ] Are all dates, vouchers and register references verified?
* [ ] Have all calculations and additions been cross-checked?
* [ ] Are physical inspection reports and photographs indexed?
* [ ] Have management responses been considered and evaluated?

### Financial Impact

* [ ] Are identified amounts correctly classified and totalled?
* [ ] Are recoverable amounts separated from systemic procedural errors?
* [ ] Have double counts between advances and final payments been eliminated?

### Reporting Quality

* [ ] Does each observation clearly present criteria, condition, cause, effect and recommendation?
* [ ] Are recommendations addressed to specific designations with deadlines?
* [ ] Is the tone objective, constructive and free of unsupported assumptions?

## 33. Historical Audit Context in Sikkim

### Panchayat Audit Backlog

The CAG’s Annual Technical Inspection Report for the year ended March 2017 documented significant audit pendency across Panchayati Raj Institutions in Sikkim:

* 294 outstanding Inspection Reports.
* 1,144 outstanding audit paragraphs.
* An aggregate financial exposure of approximately ₹253.71 lakh.

This highlights that reporting is incomplete without documented follow-up mechanisms, recovery tracking and institutional remediation.

### Municipal Reporting Timelines

Under Section 61(1) of the Sikkim Municipalities Act, 2007, the auditor must prepare the audit report as soon as practicable following audit completion, and not later than 30 September each year. The report must be submitted to the Chief Municipal Officer, along with CAG test-check findings where applicable.

### Urban Local Body Audit Settlement

The CAG’s technical review as of March 2017 recorded substantial accumulation in municipal audit objections:

* 13 total Inspection Reports issued.
* 123 total audit paragraphs.
* 10 outstanding Inspection Reports.
* 76 outstanding paragraphs awaiting settlement.
* An aggregate financial value of approximately ₹99.15 lakh.

Audit reports should therefore incorporate a dedicated objection-tracking section to systematically clear legacy findings rather than treating prior reports as closed archives.

## 35. Key Audit Principles

An effective audit report for Sikkim local bodies relies on clear logical structure:

```
Criteria (What rule applied?)
  ↓
Condition (What happened?)
  ↓
Evidence (What records prove it?)
  ↓
Cause (Why did it happen?)
  ↓
Effect (What financial or service impact resulted?)
  ↓
Management Response (What did the local body state?)
  ↓
Audit Assessment (Is the reply valid?)
  ↓
Recommendation (What action is required?)
  ↓
Follow-Up (Was the issue corrected?)

```

> **Important:** These templates provide general working formats. They do not supersede statutory schedules, departmental codes or official reporting forms prescribed by the Government of Sikkim or the Director of Local Fund Audit. Always verify current legislative amendments, rules and audit instructions before issuing an official report.
