# GST-TDS Deduction Rules for DDOs in Sikkim

Drawing and Disbursing Officers (DDOs) in Sikkim must treat Goods and Services Tax–Tax Deducted at Source (GST-TDS) as a distinct compliance system, separate from income-tax TDS under the Income-tax Act, 1961. Where Section 51 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 and corresponding provisions of the Sikkim Goods and Services Tax (SGST) Act, 2017 apply, the DDO must deduct GST-TDS from payments to suppliers, deposit the tax within the statutory deadline, file Form GSTR-7 electronically, and ensure that the deductee receives credit through Form GSTR-7A.

> **Important:** Verify the latest Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) notifications, GST portal instructions, Commercial Taxes Division (Finance Department, Government of Sikkim) directions, and State treasury accounting procedures before applying these rules to a live bill.

---

## 1. When GST-TDS Applies

GST-TDS applies when all of the following statutory conditions are satisfied:

1. **Notified deductor:** The deductor is a designated entity, such as a State Government department, local authority, or governmental agency.
2. **Taxable supply:** The disbursement relates to a taxable supply of goods, services, or both.
3. **Contract value threshold:** The total taxable value under the individual contract exceeds ₹2,50,000.
4. **No statutory exclusion:** The transaction is not excluded by a specific exemption, reverse-charge notification, or territorial condition.

The ₹2,50,000 threshold is tested on the contract value excluding CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST, and cess shown separately in the invoice. The threshold applies to the total contract or work order value, not merely to an individual interim bill.

### Example: Contract Value versus Individual Bills

A Zilla Panchayat in Sikkim awards a taxable civil maintenance contract for ₹4,00,000 (excluding GST). The contractor submits two running bills of ₹2,00,000 each:

- Total taxable contract value: ₹4,00,000 (exceeds ₹2,50,000 threshold).
- Running bill 1: ₹2,00,000.
- Running bill 2: ₹2,00,000.

Even though each running bill is below ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS applies to every progressive payment made under the contract because the aggregate taxable contract value exceeds the statutory threshold.

## 2. Who Acts as the Deductor

Under Section 51(1) of the CGST Act and the Sikkim GST Act, notified deductors include:

- Departments and establishments of the Central or State Government.
- Local authorities (Gram Panchayat Units, Zilla Panchayats, Municipalities, and Municipal Corporations).
- Governmental agencies.
- Other public authorities or public-sector undertakings notified by the Government on the recommendations of the GST Council.

The DDO performs the operational role of:

- Identifying taxable contracts that exceed the ₹2,50,000 threshold.
- Computing the exact deduction excluding the GST component.
- Recording deductions in the bill-passing registers and cashbook.
- Remitting withheld amounts to the Government account.
- Maintaining the statutory GST-TDS register.
- Filing monthly returns in Form GSTR-7.
- Generating and issuing certificates in Form GSTR-7A.

The DDO must confirm that their office or local body is properly registered on the GST common portal as a tax deductor.

## 3. GSTIN of the Deductor

A DDO or Government office required to deduct GST-TDS must obtain a dedicated GST registration as a tax deductor under Section 24(vi) of the CGST Act and use the Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN) issued for that purpose.

The deductor's GSTIN is distinct from the supplier's GSTIN and other statutory identifiers:

| Identifier | Assigned To | Primary Operational Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier GSTIN | Registered contractor or vendor | Billed tax invoices and vendor return filings |
| Deductor GSTIN | Government office or local body | Deductor registration, challans, and Form GSTR-7 returns |
| Deductee PAN | Supplier or contractor | Permanent income-tax identification and Section 194C TDS |
| Deductor TAN | Drawing and Disbursing Officer | Income-tax TDS deposits and Form 26Q returns |
| DDO Code | Government accounts or treasury office | State treasury operations and budget accounting |

The Government of India guidelines instruct Government DDOs acting as GST deductors to register on the GST common portal and obtain a dedicated GSTIN.

### Procedure When the DDO Has No GSTIN

If an office is liable to deduct GST-TDS but lacks a deductor GSTIN:

1. Confirm whether the office is a notified deductor under Section 51.
2. Verify whether a deductor GSTIN already exists under a parent administrative department or head of office.
3. Submit an application in Form GST REG-07 on the GST common portal.
4. Never substitute or enter the supplier's GSTIN as the deductor's identifier.
5. Never fabricate or invent a temporary GSTIN.
6. Record the compliance issue on the payment voucher and notify the controlling administrative authority.
7. Do not ignore the statutory withholding obligation; deduct the amount and hold it in an authorized departmental suspense account until the GSTIN is activated and remittance can occur.

## 4. GST-TDS Rates and Formulas

The statutory deduction rate depends on the location of the supplier and the place of supply:

- **Intra-State supply (Supplier and place of supply within Sikkim):** 1% CGST plus 1% SGST (Total: 2%).
- **Inter-State supply (Place of supply outside Sikkim or supplier from another State):** 2% IGST.

Deductions are calculated strictly on the taxable value of the supply, excluding CGST, SGST, IGST, and cess shown separately on the invoice.

### Formula

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable Rate}$$

### Intra-State Calculation Example

A registered local contractor submits a bill for building repairs in Gangtok:

- Taxable value of work: ₹5,00,000
- CGST (9%): ₹45,000
- SGST (9%): ₹45,000
- Gross billed invoice: ₹5,90,000

The calculations for statutory GST-TDS are:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹10,000$$

The DDO must deduct ₹5,00,00 under CGST-TDS and ₹5,00,0 under SGST-TDS.

## 5. Determining the Correct Deduction Base

GST-TDS must be deducted from the taxable value and never from the gross invoice value containing GST components.

| Bill Component | Amount | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Taxable value of works | ₹5,00,000 | Base for GST-TDS calculation |
| CGST (9%) | ₹45,000 | Excluded from TDS base |
| SGST (9%) | ₹45,000 | Excluded from TDS base |
| Gross invoice value | ₹5,90,000 | Total claimed on invoice |
| CGST-TDS (1%) | ₹5,000 | Deducted from payment |
| SGST-TDS (1%) | ₹5,000 | Deducted from payment |
| Net payment before other recoveries | ₹5,80,000 | Balance payable before income-tax TDS and cess |

> **Warning:** A DDO must not calculate 2% on the gross amount of ₹5,90,000 when tax is shown separately. Calculating TDS on tax charges violates Section 51 of the CGST Act.

## 6. Scope of Contracts Requiring GST-TDS Review

The DDO must review all procurement contracts exceeding ₹2,50,000, including:

- Civil infrastructure, road construction, bridges, and culverts.
- Construction of Government buildings, schools, and health centres.
- Building repair, renovation, and structural maintenance.
- Drainage, flood-control, and slope-stabilisation works.
- Water-supply pipeline installation and sanitisation infrastructure.
- Solid-waste management and conservancy services.
- Security and facility management services.
- Housekeeping and cleaning arrangements.
- Technical, legal, and financial consultancy.
- Supply of office equipment, IT hardware, and furniture.
- Rental or hiring of motor vehicles.
- Annual maintenance contracts (AMC).
- Composite supplies involving goods and services.

Taxability depends on whether the supply is taxable, exempt, governed by reverse charge, or otherwise excluded by law.

## 7. Situations Where GST-TDS Is Not Applicable

GST-TDS is not deducted in the following circumstances:

- The total taxable value under the contract does not exceed ₹2,50,000.
- The goods or services supplied are wholly exempt under a statutory notification.
- The goods or services are non-taxable under GST law.
- The transaction is subject to the reverse-charge mechanism (RCM) under Section 9(3) or 9(4) of the CGST Act.
- The supplier is unregistered under the applicable GST-TDS framework.
- The location of the supplier and the place of supply are in a State or Union Territory different from the State of registration of the recipient (proviso to Section 51(1)).
- The procurement is excluded by a specific CBIC notification.

The DDO must document the reason on the bill scrutiny sheet whenever GST-TDS is omitted.

### Sample File Note for Non-Deduction

> **Compliance Note:** GST-TDS was not deducted because the total taxable contract value was ₹1,80,000, which does not exceed the statutory threshold of ₹2,50,000 under Section 51 of the CGST Act. The bill was reviewed separately for income-tax TDS under Section 194C and other statutory recoveries.

## 8. Managing Registered and Unregistered Suppliers

### Registered Suppliers

A registered supplier must furnish a valid tax invoice containing:

- Active supplier GSTIN.
- Consecutive invoice serial number and date.
- Applicable HSN (for goods) or SAC (for services).
- Itemised taxable values.
- Applicable GST rate and calculated tax amounts.
- Place of supply with State code.
- Contract or purchase order reference.

### Unregistered Suppliers

An unregistered vendor cannot charge GST on a bill. When processing an unregistered supplier's bill, the DDO must verify:

- Whether the vendor's aggregate turnover legally required GST registration.
- Whether the item or service supplied is exempt.
- Whether the transaction triggers reverse-charge liability for the department.
- Whether the supplier furnished a valid PAN.
- Whether income-tax TDS under Section 194C applies.
- Whether procurement rules and the Sikkim Financial Rules permit engagement of an unregistered supplier.
- Whether physical delivery of goods or execution of work was certified.

The absence of a GSTIN does not eliminate income-tax TDS requirements.

## 9. Scrutiny of Works Contract Bills

Before passing a works contract bill, the DDO must verify the complete administrative and technical audit trail:

- Administrative approval and expenditure sanction.
- Technical sanction from the designated engineering authority.
- Tender notice, comparative statement, and contract agreement.
- Formal work order.
- Original Measurement Book (MB) with dated engineer certifications.
- Certified running account (RA) bill or completion statement.
- Quality control test reports and stage-completion certificates.
- Contractor's GSTIN, PAN, and bank mandate.
- Calculation of taxable value excluding taxes.
- Applied GST rate (generally 18% for works contracts).
- Statutory deductions: GST-TDS (2%), income-tax TDS (1% or 2%), and labour welfare cess (1%).
- Contractual recoveries: security deposit, advance adjustment, or penalties.
- Fixed asset register or stock entry.

> **Principle:** Never approve a works bill solely because a technical officer recommended it or budget provision exists. The DDO is personally responsible for confirming that the work was executed, measured, billed, and taxed in accordance with financial rules.

## 10. Distinct Treatment of GST-TDS and Income-Tax TDS

A contractor's works bill frequently requires simultaneous deductions under both GST law and direct tax law. Each deduction must be calculated and accounted for separately.

| Attribute | GST-TDS | Income-tax TDS |
|---|---|---|
| Governing statute | CGST Act, SGST Act, and IGST Act, 2017 | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Primary section | Section 51 | Section 194C (Works Contracts) |
| Deductor identifier | Deductor GSTIN | Deductor TAN |
| Payee identifier | Supplier GSTIN (if registered) | Contractor PAN |
| Calculation base | Taxable value excluding GST and cess | Gross amount credited or paid |
| Common statutory rates | Intra-State: 2% (1% CGST + 1% SGST) | 1% (Individual/HUF); 2% (Firms/Companies) |
| Statutory return | Form GSTR-7 (Monthly) | Form 26Q (Quarterly) |
| Deduction certificate | Form GSTR-7A | Form 16A |
| Payee tax credit | Electronic Cash Ledger on GST common portal | Form 26AS / AIS statement |

### Comprehensive Billing Example

A registered individual contractor submits an RA bill for civil works:

- Taxable value of civil work: ₹4,00,000
- GST (18%): ₹72,000
- Gross billed invoice: ₹4,72,000

#### Step 1: Compute GST-TDS (Intra-State: 2%)

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$\text{Subtotal GST-TDS} = ₹8,000$$

#### Step 2: Compute Income-Tax TDS (Section 194C: 1%)

$$\text{Income-tax TDS} = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

#### Step 3: Compute Total Statutory Withholdings

$$\text{Total Deductions} = ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹12,000$$

#### Step 4: Determine Net Payment

$$\text{Net Payable to Contractor} = ₹4,72,000 - ₹12,000 = ₹4,60,000$$

## 11. Payment and Remittance Procedures

Government DDOs can adopt one of two administrative remittance models under Government of India and Sikkim Finance Department instructions:

### Option 1: Bill-Wise Deduction and Deposit

- Calculate GST-TDS for the individual bill.
- Deduct the amount from the gross disbursement.
- Generate a Common Portal Identification Number (CPIN) challan for the exact amount on the GST portal.
- Quote the CPIN reference on the treasury payment voucher.
- Remit the amount to the designated Government account.
- Include transaction details in the monthly Form GSTR-7 return.
- Facilitate generation of Form GSTR-7A for the vendor.

### Option 2: Periodical Bunched Deduction and Deposit

For offices processing large volumes of vouchers:

- Deduct GST-TDS from each individual bill passed during the period.
- Credit the withheld amounts to the designated functional accounting head: `8658.00.139-GST-TDS`.
- Maintain a bill-wise deduction tracking register.
- Total the accumulated deductions at periodic intervals (weekly or monthly).
- Generate a consolidated CPIN challan on the GST common portal.
- Authorize the treasury or bank payment by debiting the suspense head.
- File Form GSTR-7 with invoice-wise deductee breakdowns.
- Reconcile the zero-balance status of the suspense head.

Accounting entries and suspense classifications must comply with instructions issued by the Controller General of Accounts (CGA) and the Sikkim Finance Department.

Under Section 51(2) of the CGST Act, the amount deducted as GST-TDS must be remitted to the Government account within 10 days after the end of the month in which the deduction is made.

|---|---|---|
| April | 10 May | 10 May |
| May | 10 June | 10 June |
| June | 10 July | 10 July |
| July | 10 August | 10 August |
| August | 10 September | 10 September |
| September | 10 October | 10 October |
| October | 10 November | 10 November |
| November | 10 December | 10 December |
| December | 10 January | 10 January |
| January | 10 February | 10 February |
| February | 10 March | 10 March |

> **Audit Warning:** Do not defer GST-TDS remittance until the final bill is passed. Deductions made on interim running account bills must be deposited within 10 days of the close of that deduction month. Late remittances attract mandatory interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act.

## 13. Filing Monthly Return in Form GSTR-7

Under Rule 66(1) of the CGST Rules, 2017, every registered tax deductor must furnish a return in Form GSTR-7 electronically through the common portal on or before the 10th day of the month succeeding the calendar month of deduction.

Form GSTR-7 captures:

- Deductor GSTIN and legal trade name.
- Deductee GSTIN.
- Total amount paid or credited to the deductee.
- Taxable value on which deduction was executed.
- Separate tax heads: CGST-TDS, SGST-TDS, or IGST-TDS.
- Amendments or corrections to past period deductions.

Before submitting Form GSTR-7, the DDO must cross-reconcile the return data against the bill register, treasury payment schedules, bank scrolls, and the physical GST-TDS register.

---

## 14. Issuance of Form GSTR-7A Certificates

Following the electronic submission of Form GSTR-7 and credit of the tax to the Government account, the deduction details become available to the deductee on the GST common portal in Form GSTR-7A.

- The certificate allows the contractor to accept the deduction and claim direct credit into their Electronic Cash Ledger.
- The DDO must verify on the portal that Form GSTR-7A is made available for every deductee included in the monthly return.
- If a contractor reports missing tax credits, the DDO must verify whether the supplier's GSTIN was entered accurately in Form GSTR-7 and file a correction return if necessary.

## 17. Protocol for Unregistered Suppliers

When dealing with a vendor who does not hold a GSTIN, the DDO must:

1. Obtain a signed declaration confirming non-registration under GST.
2. Verify the vendor's PAN.
3. Confirm that the bill does not contain unauthorized GST levies.
4. Verify that aggregate annual turnover is below the statutory GST registration threshold.
5. Examine whether the transaction triggers reverse-charge liability.
6. Deduct income-tax TDS under Section 194C if applicable.
7. Verify delivery challans, inspection notes, and goods receipt notes.

### Prohibited Actions

- Do not invent or assign a temporary GSTIN.
- Do not use the DDO's deductor GSTIN as the supplier's identifier.
- Do not pass an unregistered vendor's bill that includes GST charges.
- Do not assume that the lack of a GSTIN eliminates income-tax TDS requirements.

## 18. Interaction with Exemptions and Reverse Charge

GST-TDS must be evaluated alongside specific tax treatments:

- **Wholly exempt supplies:** No GST is levied, and GST-TDS does not apply.
- **Pure services:** Certain pure services provided to local authorities for functions under Article 243G or 243W of the Constitution may be exempt under Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate).
- **Reverse-charge mechanism (RCM):** When a supply is subject to RCM (e.g., Goods Transport Agency services or legal services), the recipient pays tax directly. The recipient does not deduct GST-TDS from payments made under reverse charge.

> **Operational Rule:** The DDO must place a written compliance note on the voucher file whenever a bill is passed without GST or GST-TDS, citing the specific statutory notification or threshold provision.

## 20. Common DDO Operational Errors

1. **Deducting TDS from the gross invoice total:** Calculating 2% on the total invoice amount, including GST charges, rather than on the base taxable value.
2. **Ignoring contract value:** Failing to deduct GST-TDS on a running bill under ₹2,50,000, even when the overall contract exceeds that amount.
3. **Using supplier GSTIN as deductor GSTIN:** Entering the contractor's GSTIN in deductor fields on challans and vouchers.
4. **Conflating GST-TDS and income-tax TDS:** Treating the two systems as interchangeable or attempting to use a GSTIN instead of a TAN.
5. **Approving GST charges from unregistered vendors:** Disbursing taxes to vendors who do not hold a valid GSTIN.
6. **Missing monthly return deadlines:** Failing to file Form GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month, which delays tax credits to deductees.
7. **Neglecting Form GSTR-7A verification:** Failing to confirm that certificates are generated and available to contractors.
8. **Delaying tax remittances:** Holding withheld tax until the final bill is prepared, exposing the office to mandatory interest liabilities under Section 50.
9. **Artificial contract splitting:** Splitting single works contracts into smaller orders to avoid the ₹2,50,000 threshold.
10. **Omitting monthly reconciliations:** Failing to match bill-passing records with filed returns and treasury accounts.

## 21. Standard Audit Observation Templates

### Observation 1: Failure to Deduct GST-TDS on Taxable Contract

**Criteria:** Under Section 51 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Section 51 of the Sikkim GST Act, 2017, a notified deductor must deduct 2% GST-TDS on taxable supplies where the total contract value exceeds ₹2,50,000 (excluding taxes).

**Condition:** A test check of works files revealed that the DDO awarded a civil works contract with a taxable value of ₹[Amount] excluding GST. Progressive disbursements totaling ₹[Amount] were released without deducting GST-TDS.

**Effect:** Statutory non-compliance with Section 51, resulting in non-deduction of GST-TDS of ₹[Amount], delayed revenue collection, and potential liability for penal interest under Section 50.

**Recommendation:** The DDO must compute the outstanding liability, regularize remittances through the GST common portal, file Form GSTR-7, and implement a mandatory pre-payment checklist.

### Observation 2: Delay in Remittance of Withheld GST-TDS

**Criteria:** Under Section 51(2) of the CGST Act, 2017, taxes deducted at source must be paid to the Government account within 10 days after the end of the deduction month.

**Condition:** GST-TDS totaling ₹[Amount] deducted during [Month] was deposited on [Date], representing an unauthorized delay of [Number] days beyond the statutory due date.

**Effect:** Exposure of the public authority to mandatory interest under Section 50(1) and potential administrative penalties.

**Recommendation:** The DDO should maintain a monthly tax compliance calendar, designate an officer responsible for timely remittances, and reconcile all deductions before the 10th of each month.

### Observation 3: Non-Filing of Monthly Return in Form GSTR-7

**Criteria:** Under Rule 66 of the CGST Rules, 2017, every registered tax deductor must file Form GSTR-7 electronically by the 10th of the month following deduction.

**Condition:** The office deducted GST-TDS of ₹[Amount] from contractor bills, but failed to produce acknowledgements of Form GSTR-7 filings for [Month/Period].

**Effect:** Contractors were unable to claim credit in their Electronic Cash Ledgers via Form GSTR-7A, and the department remains liable for late filing fees.

**Recommendation:** The DDO must submit overdue returns, generate Form GSTR-7A certificates, reconcile the GST-TDS register against treasury accounts, and ensure future returns are filed by the 10th of each month.

## 23. Summary of DDO Compliance Responsibilities

Drawing and Disbursing Officers in Sikkim should review these primary controls before releasing payments on public contracts:

1. **Notified deductors:** State Government departments, local authorities, and governmental agencies must deduct GST-TDS on eligible contracts.
2. **Contract threshold:** Deductions apply when the taxable value of a contract exceeds ₹2,50,000 (excluding taxes).
3. **Calculation base:** GST-TDS is calculated strictly on the taxable base value, excluding all itemised GST and cess charges.
4. **Statutory rates:** 2% total (1% CGST + 1% SGST for intra-State supplies; 2% IGST for inter-State supplies).
5. **Separate identifiers:** Maintain a deductor GSTIN for GST-TDS and a TAN for income-tax TDS. Do not confuse them with contractor identifiers.
6. **Independent tax regimes:** GST-TDS under Section 51 and income-tax TDS under Section 194C operate independently and must be computed separately.
7. **Statutory deadlines:** Remit withheld taxes and file Form GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month.
8. **Credit generation:** Ensure Form GSTR-7A is made available to contractors through the GST common portal.
9. **Unregistered suppliers:** Never permit an unregistered vendor to charge GST on a bill.
10. **Written audit trail:** Document the legal basis on file whenever GST or GST-TDS is not deducted.
11. **Monthly reconciliations:** Cross-reconcile bill registers, tax registers, cashbooks, challans, and portal filings monthly.

### Operational Workflow

```text
Classify Supply Nature
        ↓
Verify Supplier Registration and Contract Value
        ↓
Compute Taxable Base (Excluding GST)
        ↓
Verify GST Rate and Invoice Particulars
        ↓
Determine GST-TDS and Income-tax TDS
        ↓
Deduct Withholdings and Record in Voucher
        ↓
Disburse Net Payment to Contractor
        ↓
Remit Withheld Taxes by the 10th of Next Month
        ↓
File Form GSTR-7 Electronically
        ↓
Generate Form GSTR-7A Certificate
        ↓
Reconcile Accounts and Retain Audit Dossier
```
