# Sikkim Local Fund Audit Report Template for GPUs, ZPs and Municipalities

A Sikkim Local Fund Audit report should clearly connect law, records, transactions, public money, service delivery and corrective action. It should not merely list irregularities. It should explain what was examined, what should have happened, what actually happened, what evidence supports the finding, how much money or public service was affected and what the local body must do next.

This guide provides reusable audit-report formats for:

* Gram Panchayat Units (GPUs).
* Zilla Panchayats (ZPs).
* Municipal Corporations.
* Municipal Councils.
* Nagar Panchayats.

The templates are designed for audits conducted by or under the framework of the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA), with suitable adaptation for Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) audits, Technical Guidance and Support, internal audit, special audit and departmental inspection.

> **Important:** These are working templates, not prescribed statutory forms. Before issuing an official report, verify the latest Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, rules, departmental instructions, DLFA manual, CAG arrangements and applicable scheme guidelines.

## 1. Legal Basis of the Audit

### 1.1 Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012

The Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012 regulates the audit of local funds under the control or management of local authorities in Sikkim.

The Act covers:

* Municipal Corporations.
* Municipalities.
* Nagar Panchayats.
* Gram Panchayat Units.
* Zilla Panchayats.
* Other authorities or institutions administering a local fund when included or authorised.

The Act was enacted as Act No. 22 of 2012 and was published in the Sikkim Gazette on 30 June 2012.

### 1.2 Statutory Provisions Under the Act

| Section | Subject | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 3 | Appointment of Director | Creates the authority of the Director of Local Fund Audit. |
| Section 4 | Audit of accounts | Gives the Director responsibility to audit scheduled local authorities. |
| Section 5 | Additional authorities | Allows audit of other bodies with Government sanction. |
| Section 6 | Stock, stores and cash | Allows physical checking of stores, assets and cash. |
| Section 7 | Manner of audit | Provides for annual, concurrent or other audit. |
| Section 9 | Preparation of accounts | Requires the local authority to prepare and submit accounts. |
| Section 10 | Completion of audit | Provides a normal six-month audit-completion period after presentation. |
| Section 11 | Production of records | Allows the auditor to demand documents and explanations. |
| Section 12 | Audit report | Requires the report to be sent to the local authority and other authorities. |
| Section 13 | Contents of report | Specifies important matters that must be reported. |
| Section 14 | Action after report | Requires correction or explanation by the local authority. |
| Section 15 | Director’s powers | Provides supervision and control over auditors. |
| Section 17 | Limited audit | Allows limited checks instead of detailed audit where justified. |
| Section 18 | Loss and defalcation | Requires prompt reporting of loss or shortage. |
| Section 19 | Legislative reporting | Provides for an annual consolidated report to Government and Legislature. |

### 1.3 Panchayat Legal Framework

For Panchayats, the report should also refer to:

* The Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993.
* The Sikkim Gram Panchayat Financial Rules, 2003, as amended.
* The Sikkim Zilla Panchayat Financial Rules, 2001, as amended.
* Government orders and financial instructions.
* Scheme guidelines.
* Applicable CAG audit standards.

The Sikkim Panchayat Act contains provisions relating to the audit of Gram Panchayat and Zilla Panchayat funds, including audit reports, action on audit findings, surcharge and appeal.

## 2. Audit Authority and Reporting Relationships

### 2.1 Primary Local Fund Auditor

The Director of Local Fund Audit is the primary State-level auditor for local bodies in Sikkim. The Directorate was established in June 2012 under the Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012.

### 2.2 CAG Technical Guidance and Support

The Government of Sikkim entrusted the audit of Panchayati Raj Institutions and Urban Local Bodies to the CAG under Section 20(1) of the Comptroller and Auditor General’s (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971.

This arrangement provides Technical Guidance and Support (TGS). The CAG may:

* Conduct audit or test checks.
* Provide technical guidance.
* Review audit quality.
* Examine internal controls.
* Conduct performance or compliance audits.
* Issue Inspection Reports.
* Include significant findings in broader audit reports.

### 2.3 Distribution of the Audit Report

Copies of the final audit report are issued to:

1. The Executive Authority of the local body.
2. The Director of Local Fund Audit.
3. The concerned District or departmental authority.
4. The Finance, Revenue and Expenditure Department, where required.
5. The Rural Management and Development Department, for Panchayat matters.
6. The Urban Development and Housing Department, for municipal matters.
7. The Accountant General or CAG office, where required under the audit arrangement.
8. Any other authority specified by Government or applicable law.

## 6. Common Audit Examination Areas

### 6.1 Internal Control Assessment

* **Control environment:** Are duties clearly assigned? Are financial powers formally delegated? Are meetings held regularly and decisions recorded? Are subordinate officials adequately supervised?
* **Revenue controls:** Are demand registers maintained? Are receipt books pre-numbered? Are collections deposited into bank accounts promptly? Are arrears monitored?
* **Expenditure controls:** Are all payments supported by original vouchers? Is sanction obtained prior to payment? Is budget provision available? Are duties segregated?
* **Cash and bank controls:** Is the cashbook balanced and updated daily? Is monthly bank reconciliation prepared? Is physical cash verified periodically? Are cheque books kept under secure lock and key?
* **Asset controls:** Is an updated asset register maintained? Are physical assets verified annually? Are custodians designated? Are losses or damages reported?
* **Audit follow-up:** Are previous audit objections tracked in an objection register? Are recoveries enforced? Are recurring systemic defects eliminated?

## 9. Sample Audit Observations

### Audit Observation No. 1: Bank Reconciliation Not Prepared

#### Criteria

Under the applicable financial rules, cashbook balances must be reconciled with bank statements at the end of each month to detect clerical errors, unrecorded transactions, unauthorized withdrawals and dishonoured cheques promptly.

#### Condition

The Gram Panchayat did not prepare monthly bank reconciliation statements for six months during the audited financial year.

#### Evidence

* Cashbook entries.
* Bank account passbooks and monthly statements.
* Absence of reconciliation sheets in the records produced.
* Written statement of the Panchayat Secretary (Sachiva).

#### Effect

The accuracy of the reported closing cash and bank balance could not be verified. Uncredited remittances, direct bank debits, uncashed cheques and possible unauthorized transactions remained undetected.

#### Recommendation

The Panchayat must prepare monthly bank reconciliation statements for the pending period immediately, secure supervisory certification from the Executive Authority, and investigate all outstanding unreconciled transactions.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Evidence required: Certified bank reconciliation statements

### Audit Observation No. 2: Long-Outstanding Advance of ₹80,000

#### Criteria

Advances sanctioned for official purposes must be adjusted within the prescribed period through verified adjustment bills with original supporting vouchers. Any unspent balance must be refunded immediately to the local fund.

#### Condition

An advance of ₹80,000 sanctioned in June 2024 remained unadjusted at the time of audit. No adjustment bill, expenditure vouchers or refund challans were produced.

#### Cause

The advance register was not monitored periodically, and supervisory authorities failed to enforce timely settlement.

#### Effect

Public money amounting to ₹80,000 remained blocked without account, creating a risk of loss or personal use.

#### Recommendation

The Executive Authority should ensure immediate submission of valid adjustment vouchers or recover the entire advance of ₹80,000 with applicable interest, update the advance register and conduct monthly supervisory reviews.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Responsible authority: Executive Authority

### Audit Observation No. 3: Utilisation Certificate Not Supported by Actual Expenditure

#### Criteria

Utilisation certificates must reflect actual, verified expenditure incurred on approved scheme components, supported by vouchers, Measurement Books and physical assets.

#### Condition

The local body submitted a final utilisation certificate for the full sanctioned grant of ₹5,00,000, whereas verified expenditure in the accounts was only ₹3,80,000.

#### Financial Effect

The reported expenditure exceeded verified expenditure by:

$$Difference = Reported Expenditure - Verified Expenditure$$

Where $Reported Expenditure = ₹5,00,000$ and $Verified Expenditure = ₹3,80,000$:

$$Difference = ₹5,00,000 - ₹3,80,000 = ₹1,20,000$$

The reported expenditure was overstated by ₹1,20,000.

#### Recommendation

The local body should submit a revised utilisation certificate reflecting the true expenditure of ₹3,80,000, refund or regularise the unspent balance of ₹1,20,000 along with accrued interest, and establish an independent check before submitting future certificates.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Evidence required: Revised utilisation certificate and bank deposit challan

### Audit Observation No. 4: Payment Made for Incomplete Water-Supply Work

#### Criteria

Payments to contractors must be released only for work actually executed, verified in the Measurement Book, and certified by the competent technical authority.

#### Condition

The Panchayat disbursed final payment of ₹12,00,000 against a sanctioned water-supply work. Physical inspection revealed that while the storage reservoir was constructed, the distribution pipeline network was incomplete and disconnected from the water source.

#### Evidence

* Work Order No. [ ]
* Measurement Book entries.
* Final payment voucher dated [ ].
* Physical inspection report and site photographs.

#### Effect

The expenditure of ₹12,00,000 failed to achieve the project objective, and the intended community was deprived of safe drinking water facilities.

#### Recommendation

The Panchayat must direct the executing contractor to complete the pipeline works without additional cost, carry out joint technical measurements, record the functional asset, and prepare an operation and maintenance plan.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Responsible authority: Block Technical Officer and Panchayat President

### Audit Observation No. 5: Diversion of Funds from Approved Purpose

#### Criteria

Grants and scheme funds must be utilised exclusively for the specific objectives sanctioned by the competent authority in accordance with approved guidelines.

#### Condition

An amount of ₹2,50,000 sanctioned for community infrastructure under [Scheme Name] was diverted to meet general administrative running expenses without competent Government approval.

#### Effect

The expenditure was irregular, and approved infrastructure development under the scheme remained unfulfilled.

#### Recommendation

The local body must recoup the diverted sum of ₹2,50,000 to the dedicated scheme bank account from its own-source revenues and submit proof of rectification.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Amount recoverable: ₹2,50,000

### Audit Observation No. 6: Under-utilisation of Waste-Collection Vehicles

#### Criteria

Capital equipment procured from public funds must be deployed according to an approved service delivery plan to achieve target civic outcomes.

#### Condition

Five waste-collection vehicles costing ₹75,00,000 were purchased. Two vehicles remained unutilised for 14 months due to an absence of drivers, fuel allocations and operational route schedules.

#### Efficiency Analysis

$$Vehicle Utilisation = \frac{Operational Vehicles}{Total Vehicles} \times 100$$

Where $Operational Vehicles = 3$ and $Total Vehicles = 5$:

$$Vehicle Utilisation = \frac{3}{5} \times 100 = 60\%$$

Vehicle utilisation stood at 60%.

#### Effectiveness Analysis

Solid waste collection coverage across the municipal area remained below the mandated benchmark of 90%.

#### Recommendation

The Municipality should rationalise personnel to deploy the idle vehicles, formulate route-specific operation plans, and maintain daily vehicle logbooks to track operational performance.

#### Follow-Up Status

* Current status: Open
* Capital value of idle assets: ₹30,00,000

## 10. Financial Impact Classification

Auditors must apply precise definitions when classifying questioned transactions:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Irregular expenditure | Expenditure incurred contrary to prescribed rules, statutory provisions or administrative approvals. |
| Unsupported expenditure | Payments lacking primary documentation, bills, vouchers or receipts. |
| Excess payment | Amounts paid above verified quantities, contract rates or admissible entitlements. |
| Possible loss | Questioned transactions requiring administrative investigation or recovery proceedings. |
| Confirmed loss | Financial losses conclusively established through default, fraud or damage. |
| Unspent balance | Sanctioned funds remaining idle in bank accounts beyond project deadlines. |
| Blocked funds | Public money tied up in non-functional assets, unadjusted advances or stalled schemes. |
| Avoidable expenditure | Expenditure that could have been prevented through reasonable administrative foresight. |
| Misappropriation | Unauthorised conversion, personal retention or fraudulent use of public money. |
| Wasteful expenditure | Spending that yielded no useful public utility or civic outcome. |

> **Note:** Reserve the classification "loss" exclusively for cases where evidence demonstrates that money cannot be recovered or that public assets have been irreversibly depleted.

## 14. Audit Quality Checklist

Before final signature and transmission, complete the quality verification:

* **Legal review:** Are the cited Acts, Rules and Notifications current? Are section numbers verified against gazette copies? Is the statutory audit authority correctly designated?
* **Financial review:** Do receipts match primary accounts? Do payment totals agree with vouchers? Are bank and grant balances reconciled? Have duplicate amounts been eliminated from the financial summary?
* **Evidence review:** Are all major findings supported by documentary proof? Are voucher numbers, Measurement Book folios and dates referenced? Are physical inspection reports indexed?
* **Report quality:** Are criteria, condition, cause, effect and recommendation distinctly articulated? Are recommendations assigned to specific authorities with realistic deadlines? Has supervisory review been signed off?

The CAG’s Annual Technical Inspection Report for 2016–17 documented the following administrative context:

* 4 Zilla Panchayats and 88 Gram Panchayats were audited during 2016–17.
* 92 PRIs in total were audited by the CAG audit office between April 2016 and March 2017.
* Full annual audit coverage by the DLFA was constrained by staffing limitations.
* As of 31 March 2017, 294 Inspection Reports containing 1,144 paragraphs remained outstanding.
* The aggregate money value of outstanding PRI paragraphs stood at approximately ₹253.71 lakh.

These figures underscore the necessity for structured audit planning, capacity building, systematic response tracking and prompt compliance monitoring.

## 16. Universal Audit Observation Workflow

Every finding follows this logical progression:

```
Criteria (What standard or rule should have been followed?)
  ↓
Condition (What actual state was observed during audit?)
  ↓
Evidence (What documents, measurements or records prove it?)
  ↓
Cause (Why did the non-compliance or failure happen?)
  ↓
Effect (What financial exposure, loss or service impact occurred?)
  ↓
Management Response (What explanation did the audited entity provide?)
  ↓
Auditor's Assessment (Is the response tenable and supported by proof?)
  ↓
Recommendation (What specific action is required to rectify the issue?)
  ↓
Follow-Up and Closure (Has verified compliance been achieved?)

```
