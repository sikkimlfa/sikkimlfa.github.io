# Local Fund Audit and Internal Audit in Sikkim
## A Complete Beginner's Guide for Newly Appointed Accounts Officers

> *"Every rupee of public money belongs to the people. Audit ensures that every rupee is accounted for."*

---

## Introduction

Financial accountability is one of the cornerstones of good governance. Every government department, municipality, panchayat, autonomous body, and public institution receives public funds to implement schemes and deliver services. Citizens expect these funds to be spent legally, efficiently, economically, and for the intended purpose.

Audit is the mechanism that provides assurance that this has actually happened.

In Sikkim, the **Finance, Revenue & Expenditure Department (FRED)** performs a major role in maintaining financial discipline through two important audit functions:

* Internal Audit
* Local Fund Audit

Although both examine financial records, they differ in purpose, scope, reporting structure, and legal authority.

This guide explains these concepts from the perspective of a newly appointed Accounts Officer.

---

# Why is Audit Necessary?

Without audit:

* Financial irregularities may remain undetected.
* Government money may be misused.
* Fraud may continue unnoticed.
* Projects may remain incomplete despite expenditure.
* Public confidence decreases.

Audit protects public money by ensuring that:

* Expenditure follows laws and rules.
* Records are complete.
* Funds are used for approved purposes.
* Assets actually exist.
* Financial statements are reliable.

---

# What is Audit?

Audit is an independent and systematic examination of:

* financial records,
* transactions,
* accounts,
* documents,
* assets,
* internal controls,

to determine whether public money has been managed according to applicable laws, rules and government orders.

An audit is **not** conducted merely to find mistakes. Its primary purpose is to improve governance.

---

# Objectives of Audit

An audit seeks to answer several important questions.

## Was the money spent legally?

Every payment should comply with:

* Acts
* Rules
* Government Orders
* Budget provisions
* Delegation of Financial Powers

---

## Was the expenditure properly authorized?

Every expenditure should have:

* Administrative Approval
* Financial Sanction
* Technical Sanction (where applicable)
* Budget provision

---

## Was the expenditure actually incurred?

Supporting documents should exist. Examples include:

* Bills
* Vouchers
* Cash Book
* Measurement Book
* Stock Register
* Completion Certificate

---

## Was public money spent economically?

Government should receive value for money. For example, buying an item at ₹5 lakh when the market price is ₹3 lakh indicates poor financial management.

---

## Was the intended objective achieved?

Audit also evaluates outcomes.

**Example:** A school building was sanctioned. Questions include:

* Was it constructed?
* Was construction completed?
* Is the building being used?

---

# Types of Audit

Government organizations are subjected to different kinds of audits.

## Internal Audit

Conducted by the Government itself.
* **Purpose:** Improve internal controls and prevent errors before external audit.

---

## Local Fund Audit

Conducted under the Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act for Local Authorities.
* **Purpose:** Ensure proper utilization of Local Funds.

---

## Statutory Audit

* Required by law.
* Mandatory for organizations specified under Acts.

---

## Financial Statement Audit

Examines:
* Balance Sheet
* Income & Expenditure Account
* Receipts & Payments Account

* **Objective:** Express an opinion whether financial statements present a true and fair view.

---

## Compliance Audit

Examines whether rules were followed. Examples include:

* GFR
* Financial Rules
* Treasury Rules
* Procurement Rules

---

## Performance Audit

Evaluates:
* Economy
* Efficiency
* Effectiveness

---

## Social Audit

Citizens participate in reviewing government schemes. Commonly used in:

* MGNREGA
* Panchayat Works

---

# Finance, Revenue & Expenditure Department (FRED)

The Finance, Revenue & Expenditure Department is responsible for maintaining financial discipline throughout the State Government.

Its audit responsibilities are mainly discharged through:

* Internal Audit Wing
* Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA)

These two organizations complement each other.

---

# Internal Audit in Sikkim

## What is Internal Audit?

Internal Audit is an independent appraisal function established within Government to examine financial operations and strengthen internal controls. Its objective is not punishment, but institutional improvement.

---

## Functions of Internal Audit

The Internal Audit Wing performs:

* Audit of departmental expenditure
* Audit of government receipts
* Audit of Public Sector Undertakings
* Physical verification of stores
* Inspection of completed works
* Audit of stock and inventories
* Review of internal controls
* Special investigations assigned by Government

---

## Objectives of Internal Audit

Internal Audit helps Government by:

* Preventing financial irregularities
* Detecting errors early
* Improving financial management
* Ensuring compliance
* Advising departments
* Strengthening internal control

---

## Benefits

A strong Internal Audit system:

* reduces fraud
* improves efficiency
* minimizes audit objections
* protects government assets
* improves decision-making

---

# Local Fund Audit

## Meaning

Local Fund Audit is the audit of funds managed by Local Authorities. Examples include:

* Gram Panchayats
* Zilla Panchayats
* Municipalities
* Urban Local Bodies
* Development Authorities
* Boards
* Autonomous Bodies
* Institutions receiving Government grants

---

# Legal Basis

In Sikkim, Local Fund Audit is governed primarily by:

* Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012
* Relevant Rules framed thereunder
* Sikkim Municipalities Act, 2007
* Panchayati Raj laws
* Government Financial Rules

---

# Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA)

The Directorate of Local Fund Audit functions under the Finance Department. Its primary responsibility is auditing Local Authorities.

---

## Major Functions

DLFA:

* Audits accounts of Local Authorities
* Examines financial statements
* Detects irregularities
* Issues Audit Reports
* Raises Audit Objections
* Recommends corrective action
* Ensures compliance with financial rules

---

# Objectives of Local Fund Audit

Local Fund Audit ensures:

* accountability
* transparency
* financial discipline
* legal compliance
* proper maintenance of accounts
* safeguarding of public money

---

# Powers of Auditor

Auditors generally possess powers to:

* inspect records
* verify cash
* inspect stock
* examine vouchers
* inspect works
* call for explanations
* verify contracts
* examine bank records
* obtain supporting documents

Failure to produce records may itself become an audit objection.

---

# Audit Process

A typical Local Fund Audit follows these stages.

## Stage 1 – Audit Planning

* Selection of auditee
* Collection of previous reports
* Risk assessment
* Preparation of audit programme

---

## Stage 2 – Entry Conference

The Audit Team meets departmental officers to:

* Explain audit scope
* Discuss records required
* Finalize schedule

---

## Stage 3 – Examination of Records

Auditors verify:

* Cash Book
* Ledgers
* Bills
* Vouchers
* Budget
* Sanctions
* Work files
* Measurement Books
* Stock Registers
* Bank reconciliation
* Asset Registers

---

## Stage 4 – Field Inspection

Auditors physically verify:

* Buildings
* Roads
* Water supply schemes
* Equipment
* Vehicles
* Inventories

---

## Stage 5 – Audit Findings

Irregularities are identified, such as:

* excess expenditure
* unauthorized payment
* missing vouchers
* idle assets
* incomplete works
* non-recovery of dues

---

## Stage 6 – Audit Objection

Audit communicates findings, and the department provides replies.

---

## Stage 7 – Audit Report

Final report contains:

* observations
* recommendations
* financial implications
* compliance status

---

## Stage 8 – Follow-up

The department must:

* rectify defects
* recover losses
* strengthen controls
* submit compliance reports

---

# Role of CAG

Although Local Fund Audit is conducted by the State Government, the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) provides:

* Technical Guidance
* Audit Standards
* Capacity Building
* Test Audit
* Inspection Reports
* Professional Oversight

---

# Internal Audit vs Local Fund Audit

| Aspect | Internal Audit | Local Fund Audit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Execution** | Conducted within Government | Conducted by Directorate of Local Fund Audit |
| **Frequency** | Continuous | Periodic |
| **Nature** | Preventive | Independent examination |
| **Focus** | Improves controls | Ensures accountability |
| **Reporting** | Reports to management | Reports to Government |

# Responsibilities of an Accounts Officer

Newly appointed Accounts Officers play a critical role in successful audits. Their responsibilities include:

* Maintaining proper books of accounts
* Ensuring budgetary control
* Preserving vouchers
* Maintaining registers
* Following financial rules
* Reconciling bank accounts
* Assisting auditors
* Replying to audit observations
* Implementing corrective actions

---

# Common Audit Observations

Frequently observed issues include:

* Missing vouchers
* Cash book not updated
* Bank reconciliation pending
* Stock register incomplete
* Unauthorized expenditure
* Excess payment
* Delay in utilization certificates
* Non-recovery of advances
* Idle assets
* Incomplete projects

---

# Best Practices for Accounts Officers

To minimize audit objections:

* Maintain records daily.
* Follow financial rules.
* Verify every payment before processing.
* Conduct periodic internal checks.
* Maintain updated registers.
* Reconcile accounts regularly.
* Preserve supporting documents.
* Respond promptly to audit queries.
* Implement audit recommendations.

# Conclusion

A strong audit system is essential for transparent and accountable governance. Internal Audit and Local Fund Audit are complementary mechanisms that help the Government of Sikkim safeguard public resources, improve financial management, and strengthen public confidence.

For newly appointed Accounts Officers, understanding audit principles is not merely a professional requirement—it is a responsibility toward good governance. Maintaining accurate records, ensuring compliance with financial rules, and responding effectively to audit observations contribute directly to better administration and more efficient use of public funds.

As public financial managers, Accounts Officers are not only record keepers but also guardians of accountability, transparency, and integrity in government.
