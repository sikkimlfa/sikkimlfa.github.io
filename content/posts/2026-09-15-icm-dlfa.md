# Internal Control Mechanism in Local Fund Audit and the Role of the DLFA

Internal control is the system of policies, procedures, approvals, registers, checks and supervision used to protect public money and ensure that government work is performed correctly.

In local fund audit, internal control operates at two levels:

1. **Internal control within the local authority**, such as a Gram Panchayat, Zilla Panchayat or Municipality.
2. **Internal control within the Local Fund Audit Directorate**, which ensures that audits are properly planned, conducted, reviewed, reported and followed up.

The Director of Local Fund Audit, commonly called the **DLFA**, is responsible for supervising the audit of local funds and for ensuring that the Directorate itself maintains professional and administrative control.

The Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012 gives the DLFA authority to audit local authorities, inspect cash and stores, demand records, issue audit reports, supervise auditors and arrange special audits. The Act applies to Municipal Corporations, Municipalities, Gram Panchayat Units and Zilla Panchayats. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

***

## 1. Meaning of Internal Control

Internal control is not merely an inspection conducted by an auditor.

It is a continuous system operated by management and staff to provide reasonable assurance that the organisation will:

- Protect its assets.
- Record all receipts and payments correctly.
- Comply with laws and rules.
- Prevent and detect fraud.
- Prepare reliable financial information.
- Achieve its objectives economically and efficiently.
- Correct errors promptly.

The CAG’s Local Bodies Manual describes internal control as an integral process carried out by management and personnel. Its objectives include fulfilling accountability obligations and complying with applicable laws and regulations. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/LOCAL-AUDIT-MANUAL-20200723130806.pdf)

### Simple example

A municipality collects property tax.

An internal control system should ensure that:

1. The taxpayer receives an assessment.
2. The demand is recorded.
3. The collection is supported by a receipt.
4. The money is deposited into the bank promptly.
5. The collection register agrees with the cashbook.
6. The bank statement agrees with the cashbook.
7. Supervisors review outstanding demands.
8. The auditor can trace the transaction from demand to deposit.

If any of these steps is missing, the municipality faces a risk of leakage or fraud.

***

## 2. Internal Control Is Not the Same as Internal Audit

The terms are related but different.

| Point | Internal control | Internal audit |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | The complete system of checks and procedures | An independent review of the system |
| Responsibility | Management and staff | Internal audit personnel or an authorised reviewer |
| Timing | Continuous | Periodic or risk-based |
| Objective | Prevent and detect errors | Evaluate whether controls are adequate and working |
| Example | Two officers approve and record a payment separately | An auditor tests whether approvals and recording were actually done |

### Simple explanation

Internal control is the **system of protection**.

Internal audit is the **review of that system**.

External or local fund audit then provides an independent examination of accounts, compliance and financial management.

***

## 3. Why Internal Control Is Important in Local Bodies

Local bodies manage public money at the point closest to citizens.

They may handle:

- Local taxes.
- User charges.
- Government grants.
- Finance Commission funds.
- Scheme funds.
- Loans.
- Deposits.
- Public contributions.
- Income from markets and municipal assets.

The CAG’s Local Bodies Manual notes that grants from the State and Union Governments constitute a major source of Panchayat finance. It also records an estimate that about **40 per cent of combined State and Union plan funds** were allocated to district-level schemes at the time of the cited study. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/LOCAL-AUDIT-MANUAL-20200723130806.pdf)

This creates significant financial risk.

### Common risks

- Collection money is not deposited.
- Expenditure is incurred without budget provision.
- Payments are made without sanction.
- Work is paid for without measurement.
- Grants are used for an unauthorised purpose.
- Advances remain outstanding.
- Assets are purchased but not recorded.
- Stores are missing.
- Bank accounts are not reconciled.
- Cashbooks are written late.
- Vouchers are incomplete.
- Tender procedures are bypassed.
- Officials approve and pay their own claims.
- Audit objections remain unresolved.

A strong internal control mechanism reduces these risks before they become audit findings.

## 4. Internal Control Objectives

The control system in a local authority should provide reasonable assurance regarding the following objectives.

### Legality

Transactions must comply with:

- Acts.
- Rules.
- Regulations.
- Government orders.
- Budget provisions.
- Delegation of financial powers.
- Sanction conditions.

### Regularity

Transactions must be properly authorised, recorded and supported by documents.

### Financial accuracy

The accounts must correctly show:

- Receipts.
- Payments.
- Assets.
- Liabilities.
- Advances.
- Grants.
- Outstanding balances.

### Asset protection

Cash, stores, equipment, vehicles, land, buildings and other assets must be safeguarded.

### Revenue protection

All amounts due to the local body should be:

- Assessed.
- Collected.
- Recorded.
- Deposited.
- Reconciled.
- Followed up when outstanding.

### Fraud prevention

The system should reduce opportunities for:

- Misappropriation.
- Fictitious payments.
- Duplicate payments.
- Manipulation of records.
- Unauthorised procurement.
- Diversion of grants.

### Economy, efficiency and effectiveness

The local body should obtain reasonable value from public expenditure and achieve the intended result.

## 5. The Five Components of Internal Control

The CAG Local Bodies Manual identifies five interrelated components of internal control:

1. Control environment.
2. Risk management or risk assessment.
3. Control activities.
4. Information and communication.
5. Monitoring. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/LOCAL-AUDIT-MANUAL-20200723130806.pdf)

These five components can be explained in simple language.

# Part I: Components of Internal Control

## 6. Control Environment

The control environment is the general atmosphere in which financial administration takes place.

It reflects the attitude of:

- The elected body.
- The Executive Officer.
- The Panchayat Secretary.
- The Accounts Officer.
- The Drawing and Disbursing Officer.
- The Storekeeper.
- Supervisors.
- Internal auditors.
- Other employees.

### A strong control environment includes

- Clear assignment of duties.
- Ethical behaviour.
- Proper supervision.
- Accountability for mistakes.
- Compliance with rules.
- No interference with audit.
- Regular meetings.
- Timely preparation of accounts.
- Action on audit objections.
- Protection for persons reporting irregularities.

### A weak control environment looks like

- Officials treating rules as optional.
- One person controlling the full transaction.
- Delayed accounts being accepted as normal.
- Unreconciled advances being ignored.
- Missing records being tolerated.
- Audit objections being closed without evidence.
- Repeated irregularities without responsibility being fixed.

### How to improve the control environment

1. Issue a written delegation of financial powers.
2. Prepare a responsibility chart for every major function.
3. Conduct monthly financial review meetings.
4. Record decisions in minutes.
5. Require officers to certify important registers.
6. Maintain an audit-objection monitoring register.
7. Fix responsibility for material losses.
8. Include internal control performance in supervisory review.
9. Provide protection and a reporting channel for suspected fraud.
10. Enforce disciplinary action in serious cases.

***

## 7. Risk Assessment

Risk assessment means identifying what can go wrong, how likely it is to happen and how serious the effect may be.

### Examples of local-body risks

| Area | Risk | Possible consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Property tax | Demand register incomplete | Revenue loss |
| Cash collection | Money retained by collector | Misappropriation |
| Procurement | Purchase made without competition | Overpayment or favouritism |
| Works | Measurement manipulated | Payment for incomplete work |
| Grants | Funds diverted | Recovery and liability |
| Payroll | Ghost or unauthorised employees | Excess expenditure |
| Stores | Stock not physically verified | Shortage |
| Bank | Reconciliation not prepared | Undetected errors |
| Advances | Old advances not recovered | Loss of public money |
| Digital system | Weak passwords | Unauthorised alteration of records |

### Risk rating

A simple risk rating can be used:

\[
\text{Risk Score} = \text{Likelihood} \times \text{Impact}
\]

Use a scale of 1 to 5 for each factor.

| Score | Risk level | Suggested response |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Routine control |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Periodic supervisory review |
| 10–16 | High | Detailed audit and frequent monitoring |
| 17–25 | Very high | Immediate management action and special review |

### Example

If the likelihood of cash misappropriation is 4 and the impact is 5:

\[
4 \times 5 = 20
\]

The risk score is 20, which is very high. The local body should introduce:

- Daily deposit.
- Dual verification.
- Surprise cash checks.
- Independent reconciliation.
- Rotation of collection duties.

***

## 8. Control Activities

Control activities are the actual procedures used to prevent or detect errors.

These include:

- Authorisation.
- Segregation of duties.
- Verification.
- Reconciliation.
- Physical control.
- Supervision.
- Review.
- Documentation.
- Access control.
- Exception reporting.

### Preventive controls

These try to stop an error before it happens.

Examples:

- Budget approval before spending.
- Technical sanction before works.
- Purchase approval before ordering.
- Maker-checker approval in software.
- Spending limits.
- Password controls.
- Pre-numbered receipt books.
- Tender requirements.

### Detective controls

These identify an error after it happens.

Examples:

- Bank reconciliation.
- Surprise cash verification.
- Stock verification.
- Review of unusual payments.
- Audit of vouchers.
- Comparison of budget and actual expenditure.
- Review of outstanding advances.

### Corrective controls

These correct an identified problem.

Examples:

- Recovery of excess payment.
- Reversal of an incorrect entry.
- Reconciliation of accounts.
- Disciplinary action.
- Replacement of missing assets.
- Revision of defective procedures.

***

## 9. Information and Communication

Controls cannot work if information is incomplete, late or unreliable.

A local authority must communicate:

- Sanction orders.
- Budget approvals.
- Grant conditions.
- Revised rules.
- Delegation orders.
- Procurement instructions.
- Audit findings.
- Recovery directions.
- Financial review results.

### Internal communication

Information should flow between:

- Elected representatives.
- Executive authority.
- Accounts section.
- Engineering section.
- Revenue section.
- Stores section.
- Scheme implementation staff.
- Audit and monitoring officers.

### External communication

Relevant information may also need to be shared with:

- State Government.
- Finance Department.
- District authorities.
- CAG or Accountant-General.
- Granting agencies.
- Legislative committees.
- Citizens.

### Practical improvement

Create a **financial control dashboard** containing:

- Total receipts.
- Total expenditure.
- Grant-wise balances.
- Unspent funds.
- Pending utilisation certificates.
- Outstanding advances.
- Unresolved audit objections.
- Revenue arrears.
- Bank reconciliation status.
- Physical verification status.

***

## 10. Monitoring

Monitoring means checking whether controls are continuing to work.

Monitoring should operate at three levels.

### Daily monitoring

- Cash receipts.
- Cashbook entries.
- Bank deposits.
- Payment authorisation.
- Attendance.
- Receipt books.

### Monthly monitoring

- Bank reconciliation.
- Budget versus actual expenditure.
- Grant utilisation.
- Revenue arrears.
- Advances.
- Works progress.
- Stock movement.
- Payroll changes.

### Annual monitoring

- Financial statements.
- Asset verification.
- Stock verification.
- Internal control assessment.
- Audit objections.
- Annual administration report.
- Follow-up of previous audit findings.

### Monitoring question

The main question is not:

> “Do we have a control?”

The better question is:

> “Did the control operate effectively throughout the period?”

For example, a local body may have a bank-reconciliation rule, but if no reconciliation was prepared for eight months, the control exists only on paper.

# Part II: Internal Controls in the Local Fund Audit Directorate

## 11. Meaning of Internal Control in DLFA

The DLFA must not only audit the internal controls of local bodies. The Directorate itself must maintain internal controls over its audit work.

The internal control system of a DLFA should ensure that:

- The right institutions are selected for audit.
- Audit planning is risk-based.
- Audit teams are properly authorised.
- Records requested are sufficient.
- Evidence is documented.
- Audit work is reviewed.
- Reports are issued on time.
- Objections are followed up.
- Sensitive matters are escalated.
- Staff performance is monitored.
- Confidential information is protected.

The CAG’s Audit Regulations require auditors to understand and evaluate internal control when determining the nature, timing and extent of audit procedures. The Regulations also require supervision, review, competent evidence and reporting of significant internal-control deficiencies. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

***

## 12. Main DLFA Internal Control Mechanisms

### Annual audit planning

The Directorate should prepare an annual audit plan based on:

- Number of local bodies.
- Size of funds handled.
- Previous audit findings.
- History of fraud or loss.
- Delays in accounts.
- Revenue importance.
- Works expenditure.
- Grant dependence.
- Quality of internal controls.
- Complaints and media reports.
- Changes in management.

### Audit universe register

The DLFA should maintain a complete register of all auditable institutions.

The register should include:

- Name of local body.
- Type of institution.
- Location.
- Population served.
- Annual receipts.
- Annual expenditure.
- Major grants.
- Last audit date.
- Period audited.
- Date of report.
- Outstanding objections.
- Risk rating.
- Next proposed audit date.

### Audit scheduling control

The Directorate should ensure that:

- No local body is unintentionally omitted.
- High-risk bodies are audited more frequently.
- Audit teams are not repeatedly assigned to the same institution without review.
- Travel is planned economically.
- Special audits are separately approved.
- Backlog is monitored monthly.

### Audit authorisation

Each audit should have:

- Written authority.
- Defined audit period.
- Defined audit objectives.
- Assigned audit team.
- Approved programme.
- Expected completion date.
- Required records list.

### Entry conference

An entry conference should explain:

- Scope of audit.
- Period covered.
- Records required.
- Responsibilities of the local body.
- Expected dates.
- Communication procedure.
- Key risk areas.

Minutes should be recorded and signed.

### Audit requisition control

All requisitions should be:

- Written.
- Numbered.
- Dated.
- Clear.
- Linked to an audit objective.
- Given a compliance date.
- Tracked until response.

The Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act permits an auditor to require vouchers, statements, returns, correspondence, notes and other records. It also allows the auditor to require responsible persons to appear and answer questions. A reasonable compliance period of not less than three days may be fixed. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Working-paper control

Every audit conclusion should be supported by documented evidence.

Working papers should show:

- Objective.
- Population examined.
- Sample selected.
- Documents checked.
- Result of checking.
- Exceptions found.
- Explanation obtained.
- Auditor’s conclusion.
- Supervisor’s review.

### Supervisory review

A senior officer should review:

- Audit planning.
- Risk assessment.
- Sampling.
- Major findings.
- Evidence.
- Draft report.
- Proposed recommendations.
- Classification of serious irregularities.

The CAG Audit Regulations state that audit work at each level and phase should be supervised and that documented work should be reviewed by a senior member of the audit staff. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

### Exit conference

The exit conference should:

- Present major findings.
- Confirm factual accuracy.
- Obtain management explanations.
- Identify recoverable amounts.
- Discuss corrective action.
- Record unresolved matters.

The local body’s explanation should not automatically be accepted. It should be evaluated against documentary evidence.

### Report control

The DLFA should maintain a report register containing:

- Audit institution.
- Audit period.
- Date of completion.
- Date of draft report.
- Date of approval.
- Date of issue.
- Major observations.
- Amount involved.
- Responsible authority.
- Reply due date.
- Follow-up status.

### Follow-up control

Every audit objection should have a status:

- Pending reply.
- Reply received.
- Evidence pending.
- Recovery in progress.
- Accepted and closed.
- Partly settled.
- Referred for departmental action.
- Referred for criminal investigation.
- Recommended for legislative attention.

### Quality assurance

Quality assurance should include:

- Periodic review of completed audit files.
- Re-performance of selected audit procedures.
- Review of high-value findings.
- Analysis of recurring defects.
- Staff training.
- Standard checklists.
- Review of report language.
- Monitoring of report delays.

# Part III: Section-by-Section Explanation of the Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act

## 13. Section 1: Short Title, Extent and Commencement

### Legal purpose

This section names the law, extends it to the whole of Sikkim and provides that it will commence on a date notified by the State Government.

### Simple explanation

The Act is applicable throughout Sikkim, but the Government must formally notify its operational date.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should maintain:

- Gazette notification.
- Commencement record.
- Current copy of the Act.
- Amendments and rules.
- Distribution list for audit officers.

***

## 14. Section 2: Definitions

### Legal purpose

This section defines terms such as:

- Auditor.
- Concurrent audit.
- Detailed audit.
- Director.
- Executive Authority.
- Local Authority.
- Local Fund.
- Special Audit.
- Schedule.

### Simple explanation

This section tells auditors what the important words in the Act mean.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should maintain a legal reference file explaining:

- Which bodies are local authorities.
- Which funds are local funds.
- Which audits are regular.
- Which audits are special.
- Which officers are authorised auditors.

This prevents jurisdictional confusion.

***

## 15. Section 3: Appointment of Director

### Legal purpose

The State Government appoints the Director of Local Fund Audit and may appoint supporting officers and staff.

All staff are subordinate to the Director.

### Simple explanation

The Director is the head of the Directorate and controls the officers who perform local fund audits.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should establish:

- Organisational chart.
- Delegation orders.
- Reporting lines.
- Job descriptions.
- Approval powers.
- Conflict-of-interest declarations.
- Staff rotation procedures.

***

## 16. Section 4: Audit of Accounts

### Legal purpose

The Director audits the accounts of local authorities and local funds included in the Schedule, subject to the CAG Act.

### Simple explanation

The DLFA has the legal responsibility to audit the institutions listed in the Act.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should maintain a jurisdiction register showing:

- Institution covered.
- Legal basis.
- Audit period.
- Audit authority.
- Assigned team.
- Report status.

***

## 17. Section 5: Audit of Additional Authorities

### Legal purpose

The Director may audit bodies not included in the Schedule with prior government sanction and agreed terms.

### Simple explanation

The Government can ask the DLFA to audit other institutions when necessary.

### Internal control connection

Every additional audit should have:

- Government sanction.
- Written terms of reference.
- Scope.
- Period.
- Fee or resource arrangement, if applicable.
- Reporting recipient.
- Confidentiality requirements.

***

## 18. Section 6: Stock, Stores and Cash Verification

### Legal purpose

The auditor may examine stock and stores. The Director may obtain technical assistance. An officer not below the rank of Deputy Director may conduct surprise inspection. Cash must be verified during audit and inspection.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The auditor can physically check goods, equipment, materials and cash, not only written accounts.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should require audit teams to document:

- Date and time of cash verification.
- Cashbook balance.
- Physical cash counted.
- Difference, if any.
- Stock register balance.
- Physical stock.
- Shortage or excess.
- Condition of assets.
- Signatures of custodians and auditors.

### Improvement measure

Introduce a standard **Cash and Stock Verification Sheet** with mandatory fields and photographs where appropriate.

***

## 19. Section 7: Manner of Audit

### Legal purpose

The auditor conducts audit annually. Concurrent or more frequent audit may be conducted where required.

### Simple explanation

Every covered local body should ordinarily receive an annual audit, but high-risk institutions may need more frequent checking.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should use a risk-based frequency model.

| Risk category | Suggested audit approach |
|---|---|
| Low | Annual regular audit |
| Moderate | Annual audit with focused checks |
| High | Annual detailed audit plus targeted review |
| Very high | Concurrent, special or surprise audit |

The exact frequency must follow the Act, rules and approved departmental policy.

***

## 20. Section 8: Auditor as Public Servant

### Legal purpose

An auditor acting under the Act is deemed to be a public servant.

### Simple explanation

The auditor is performing an official public duty and must act lawfully, fairly and honestly.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should implement:

- Code of conduct.
- Confidentiality rules.
- Conflict-of-interest declaration.
- Gifts and hospitality policy.
- Complaint mechanism.
- Disciplinary procedure.
- Protection of audit evidence.

***

## 21. Section 9: Preparation and Presentation of Accounts

### Legal purpose

The executive authority must prepare the accounts in the prescribed form and present them for audit within **three months after the close of the financial year**.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The local authority cannot delay indefinitely. It must complete its accounts and send them for audit within the prescribed period.

### Internal control connection

The local body should maintain an annual closing checklist covering:

- Cashbook closing.
- Bank reconciliation.
- Trial balance.
- Receipts and payments.
- Grants.
- Advances.
- Assets.
- Liabilities.
- Stock.
- Utilisation certificates.
- Supporting schedules.

The DLFA should maintain a delayed-accounts register.

***

## 22. Section 10: Completion of Audit

### Legal purpose

The auditor should complete the audit within **six months from the date on which accounts are presented**.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The audit should also be completed within a defined period.

### Internal control connection

The Directorate should monitor:

- Date of receipt of accounts.
- Planned audit date.
- Actual start date.
- Actual completion date.
- Reasons for delay.
- Pending records.
- Supervisor’s review.
- Report issue date.

A monthly dashboard should show all audits delayed beyond the six-month period.

***

## 23. Section 11: Production of Records and Attendance

### Legal purpose

The auditor may require:

- Vouchers.
- Statements.
- Returns.
- Correspondence.
- Notes.
- Other documents.
- Attendance of employees.
- Explanations from officials and persons connected with contracts.

A reasonable compliance period of not less than **three days** may be fixed. Normal audit notice should be at least **two weeks**, although shorter notice or no notice may be allowed for special reasons or special audit. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The auditor has the right to obtain records and explanations needed to understand the accounts.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should maintain:

- Requisition register.
- Pending information list.
- Record custody receipt.
- Reminder system.
- Non-compliance escalation.
- Record-return acknowledgement.

### Improvement measure

Use a digital requisition tracker with fields for:

- Requisition number.
- Record requested.
- Officer responsible.
- Due date.
- Date received.
- Completeness.
- Follow-up action.
- Final status.

***

## 24. Section 12: Audit Reports

### Legal purpose

After completing the audit, the auditor sends the report to the local authority as soon as practicable and generally not later than **three months after completion**.

Copies may be sent to officers or bodies directed by Government.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The audit report must be issued promptly and sent to all responsible authorities.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should use a report-timeliness register.

### Suggested indicators

\[
\text{Report Timeliness Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Reports issued within prescribed time}}{\text{Total reports due}}
\times 100
\]

\[
\text{Audit Completion Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Audits completed}}{\text{Audits planned}}
\times 100
\]

These indicators should be reviewed monthly.

***

## 25. Section 13: Contents of Audit Report

### Legal purpose

The report must include:

- Payments contrary to law.
- Loss or deficiency caused by negligence or misconduct.
- Misappropriation or misuse.
- Amounts received but not accounted for.
- Other material improprieties or irregularities.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The report must clearly identify the financial and administrative problems found during audit.

### Strong audit observation format

A useful observation should include:

1. Criteria — What rule, law or procedure applies?
2. Condition — What actually happened?
3. Cause — Why did it happen?
4. Effect — What was the financial or administrative consequence?
5. Evidence — Which records prove it?
6. Responsibility — Who was responsible?
7. Recommendation — What should be done?
8. Management response — What does the local body say?
9. Final position — Is the objection closed or continued?

### Example

**Condition:** A Gram Panchayat collected ₹2,40,000 in market fees during the year, but only ₹1,90,000 was deposited into the bank.

**Criteria:** All collections should be recorded and deposited into the local fund promptly.

**Effect:** ₹50,000 remained unaccounted for.

**Required action:** Trace the collection records, fix responsibility, recover the amount and strengthen daily deposit controls.

***

## 26. Section 14: Action After Audit Report

### Legal purpose

The executive authority must either:

- Correct the defect and inform the Director; or
- Submit an explanation.

This should generally occur within **three months** of receiving the report.

The auditor may accept the explanation and drop the objection or decide that the defect remains unresolved.

The local authority must publish specified unresolved matters in its next administration report. The information remains open for public inspection for **one month**. [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The local body must respond to audit findings. It cannot ignore the report.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should classify each objection as:

- Resolved with evidence.
- Resolved after recovery.
- Partially resolved.
- Explanation accepted.
- Explanation rejected.
- Pending further action.
- Referred for disciplinary action.
- Referred for criminal investigation.
- Referred to Government or Legislature.

### Important principle

An objection should not be closed merely because a reply has been received.

Closure should require sufficient evidence, such as:

- Recovery challan.
- Corrected account.
- Revised register.
- Completion certificate.
- Physical verification report.
- Revised sanction.
- Disciplinary order.
- Government decision.

***

## 27. Section 15: Powers and Duties of Director

### Legal purpose

The Director exercises general supervision and control over auditors.

The Director may condone a payment contrary to law if:

- There was no negligence or misconduct.
- The amount does not exceed ₹100 in each case.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The Director supervises the entire audit process and may ignore a very small technical payment where no negligence or misconduct occurred.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should maintain:

- Supervision plan.
- Review notes.
- Inspection of audit parties.
- Training records.
- Performance assessment.
- Register of condoned payments.
- Reasons for every decision.

The power to condone should not be used for:

- Fraud.
- Repeated violations.
- Splitting of payments.
- Misappropriation.
- Deliberate bypass of procedure.
- Material financial loss.

***

## 28. Section 16: Delegation

### Legal purpose

The Director may delegate powers and functions to officers and may later withdraw those powers.

### Simple explanation

The Director can assign specific work to authorised officers.

### Internal control connection

Every delegation order should state:

- Name and designation.
- Exact power delegated.
- Financial or administrative limit.
- Period of delegation.
- Reporting requirement.
- Whether further delegation is prohibited.
- Review and withdrawal procedure.

Delegation should not remove accountability. The delegating authority must continue to supervise the delegated work.

***

## 29. Section 17: Limited or Sample Audit

### Legal purpose

The Director may dispense with detailed audit of an account or class of transactions and apply a limited check when circumstances justify it.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The auditor does not have to check every transaction in every case. The extent of checking may be based on risk.

### Important safeguard

Sample audit is not a shortcut for avoiding difficult work.

A sound sample plan should consider:

- Total number of transactions.
- Total financial value.
- Nature of transactions.
- Previous irregularities.
- Risk of fraud.
- Large and unusual payments.
- Sensitive schemes.
- Weakness of internal controls.

### Mandatory coverage should include

- All suspected fraud.
- All major losses.
- Large-value transactions.
- Unusual payments.
- Related-party transactions.
- Unadjusted advances.
- Major works.
- Significant grant expenditure.
- Cash shortages.
- Long-pending deposits.

***

## 30. Section 18: Defalcation or Loss

### Legal purpose

The executive authority must report loss or defalcation of money or stores to the Director, controlling authority and departmental head.

The Director must conduct or arrange a special audit depending on the magnitude of the loss. Criminal proceedings are not barred.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Losses and shortages must be reported immediately. The local body must not wait for the next annual audit.

### Internal control connection

The local body should use a loss-reporting form containing:

- Date of discovery.
- Nature of loss.
- Amount or quantity.
- Person responsible for custody.
- Circumstances.
- Immediate protective action.
- Police complaint, if applicable.
- Recovery action.
- Disciplinary action.
- Special audit status.

### DLFA control

The DLFA should maintain a **Loss and Defalcation Register** showing:

- Case number.
- Local body.
- Amount involved.
- Date reported.
- Special audit date.
- Responsible persons.
- Recovery status.
- Criminal case status.
- Final disposal.

***

## 31. Section 19: Laying of Audit Report

### Legal purpose

The Director sends an annual consolidated report to the Government. The Government must place it before the Legislative Assembly within **three months of receiving it**.

 [sikkim.gov](https://www.sikkim.gov.in/uploads/Gazette/2012_THE_SIKKIM_LOCAL_FUND_AUDIT_ACT_20210908.pdf)

### Simple explanation

Important audit findings ultimately reach the State Legislature.

### Internal control connection

The DLFA should operate an annual report calendar covering:

- Data collection.
- Verification.
- Consolidation.
- Legal review.
- Approval.
- Submission to Government.
- Tracking of laying before the Assembly.

The consolidated report should identify:

- Total bodies audited.
- Total audit period covered.
- Major irregularities.
- Losses and recoveries.
- Unresolved objections.
- Repeated control failures.
- Delayed accounts.
- Recommendations for system improvement.

***

## 32. Sections 20 to 24

### Section 20: Good-faith protection

Protects authorised officers for lawful actions done in good faith.

**Control requirement:** Maintain evidence that decisions were within authority and properly documented.

### Section 21: Pending audits

Provides for continuation of audits pending when the Act commenced.

**Control requirement:** Maintain a transitional audit register.

### Section 22: Rule-making power

Allows the Government to make detailed rules regarding accounts, audit procedures, publication and other matters.

**Control requirement:** Maintain an updated legal and procedural manual.

### Section 23: Removal of difficulties

Allows the Government to issue orders to resolve implementation difficulties.

**Control requirement:** Maintain a register of clarification orders and instructions.

### Section 24: Savings

Protects pending suits, appeals, revisions and proceedings under the earlier applicable law.

**Control requirement:** Maintain a litigation and pending-case register.

# Part IV: Internal Control by Financial Area

## 33. Cash and Bank Controls

Cash is one of the highest-risk areas in a local authority.

### Required controls

- Use pre-numbered receipt books.
- Issue receipts immediately.
- Record collections daily.
- Deposit collections promptly.
- Restrict cash retention.
- Maintain a daily cashbook.
- Conduct surprise cash verification.
- Separate collection and recording duties.
- Prepare monthly bank reconciliation.
- Investigate old outstanding cheques.
- Restrict access to cheque books.
- Cancel unused receipts properly.
- Maintain a register of cancelled receipts.

### DLFA audit tests

- Compare receipt books with cashbook.
- Trace selected receipts to bank deposits.
- Check receipt number continuity.
- Verify cash physically.
- Examine delayed deposits.
- Review unusual cash withdrawals.
- Compare bank statements with cashbook.
- Check whether the same person collects, records and deposits money.

***

## 34. Revenue Controls

Revenue leakage can seriously affect local-body finances.

### Revenue control cycle

```text

   |

   |

   |
Collection
   |
Receipt
   |
Daily recording
   |
Bank deposit
   |

   |
Bank reconciliation
   |
Arrear monitoring
```

### Required controls

- Maintain updated demand registers.
- Number all assessments.
- Review exemptions and concessions.
- Reconcile demand, collection and balance.
- Issue receipts immediately.
- Review arrears monthly.
- Maintain a recovery register.
- Separate assessment from collection.
- Conduct periodic physical verification of taxable properties.
- Reconcile digital and manual records.

### DLFA audit tests

- Compare demand with collection.
- Verify arrears.
- Check unassessed properties.
- Test receipt books.
- Trace collections to bank deposits.
- Review write-offs.
- Examine unauthorised exemptions.
- Check whether arrears are legally recoverable.

## 36. Procurement Controls

# Part V: Internal Control Mechanisms in DLFA Operations

## 42. Audit Planning Controls

The DLFA should maintain a risk-based annual audit plan.

### Planning inputs

- Previous audit report.
- Unresolved objections.
- Size of local fund.
- Government grants.
- Revenue collection.
- Works expenditure.
- Complaint records.
- Fraud and loss cases.
- Delay in accounts.
- Quality of management response.
- Previous audit rating.

### Suggested audit rating

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Strong controls, timely accounts, few significant objections |
| B | Moderate weaknesses requiring regular follow-up |
| C | Major recurring weaknesses or delayed accounts |
| D | High risk, suspected fraud, major losses or serious non-compliance |

Institutions rated C or D should receive deeper and more frequent attention.

***

## 43. Audit Team Controls

The DLFA should ensure that audit teams:

- Have clear terms of reference.
- Include staff with appropriate skills.
- Declare conflicts of interest.
- Receive current manuals and rules.
- Use standard audit programmes.
- Maintain daily audit diaries.
- Hold entry and exit conferences.
- Obtain sufficient evidence.
- Submit working papers for review.
- Do not alter records of the audited entity.
- Do not perform management functions.

The CAG Regulations state that auditors should ordinarily not become members of management committees. Audit advice should remain clearly identified as advice and should not replace management responsibility. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

***

## 44. Documentation Controls

Audit evidence should be:

- Relevant.
- Reliable.
- Sufficient.
- Properly indexed.
- Traceable to the audit observation.
- Protected from unauthorised alteration.

### Suggested audit-file structure

```text
01. Authority and scope
02. Background information
03. Risk assessment
04. Entry conference
05. Audit requisitions
06. Trial balance and financial statements
07. Cash and bank
08. Revenue
09. Expenditure
10. Procurement
11. Works
12. Grants
13. Assets and stores
14. Payroll
15. Previous audit objections
16. Draft observations
17. Management replies
18. Exit conference
19. Final report
20. Follow-up schedule
```

### Documentation test

A reviewer should be able to understand:

- What was examined.
- How much was examined.
- What evidence was obtained.
- What conclusion was reached.
- Why the conclusion was reasonable.

***

## 45. Quality Review Controls

Quality control should occur before the report is issued.

### Reviewer should check

- Correct legal provision.
- Accurate figures.
- Proper period.
- Complete facts.
- Evidence attached.
- Management explanation considered.
- Financial effect calculated.
- Responsibility correctly described.
- Recommendation is practical.
- Serious matters escalated.
- Confidential information protected.
- Report language is clear and fair.

### Four-eye principle

No important audit report should be issued after review by only one person.

At least two levels should examine:

1. Field audit team.
2. Supervisory or approving officer.

High-risk cases may require:

3. Legal or specialist review.
4. Senior management approval.

***

## 46. Follow-Up and Settlement Controls

Audit has little value if objections remain unresolved.

### Follow-up register

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Objection number | DLFA/GP/2026/014 |
| Local body | Example Gram Panchayat |
| Audit period | 2024–25 |
| Amount involved | ₹1,25,000 |
| Nature | Unadjusted advance |

| Reply received | 15 September 2026 |
| Evidence | Partly submitted |
| Current status | Pending recovery |
| Responsible officer | Executive Authority |
| Next action | Recovery notice |
| Review date | 30 October 2026 |

### Settlement principles

An audit objection should be settled only when:

- The facts are corrected.
- The amount is recovered, where applicable.
- The account is adjusted.
- The responsibility is fixed.
- The system weakness is addressed.
- Evidence is available.

***

## 47. Fraud and Corruption Controls

Audit should distinguish between:

- Error.
- Irregularity.
- Negligence.
- Misconduct.
- Misappropriation.
- Fraud.
- Corruption.

### Red flags

- Missing vouchers.
- Altered records.
- Unusual round amounts.
- Payments to inactive suppliers.
- Duplicate invoices.
- Unauthorised bank accounts.
- Cash shortages.
- Unexplained advances.
- Frequent emergency procurement.
- Related-party transactions.
- False completion certificates.
- Repeated failure to produce records.

### Response procedure

1. Secure records.
2. Preserve electronic evidence.
3. Record the facts.
4. Avoid premature accusations.
5. Inform the competent authority.
6. Conduct a focused review.
7. Quantify the loss.
8. Recommend recovery.
9. Consider disciplinary or criminal action.
10. Improve the control that failed.

CAG Regulations provide that suspected fraud and corruption should be reported and that serious illegal acts may require prompt communication rather than waiting for the final report. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

# Part VI: How to Improve Internal Control in DLFA

## 48. Improvement 1: Establish a Formal Internal Control Policy

The DLFA should issue a written policy covering:

- Audit planning.
- Risk assessment.
- Team assignment.
- Conflict of interest.
- Evidence standards.
- Supervisory review.
- Report approval.
- Follow-up.
- Data security.
- Fraud reporting.
- Record retention.

The policy should be approved by the competent authority and reviewed annually.

***

## 49. Improvement 2: Use Risk-Based Audit Planning

Instead of auditing every institution with the same intensity, classify them according to risk.

### High-risk indicators

- Large grants.
- Large works expenditure.
- Long delays in accounts.
- Serious previous objections.
- Cash shortages.
- Unreconciled bank accounts.
- Weak management response.
- Frequent complaints.
- Rapid staff turnover.
- Missing records.
- Repeated audit findings.

Risk-based audit does not mean abandoning low-risk institutions. It means allocating more resources where the danger to public money is greater.

***

## 50. Improvement 3: Introduce Standard Checklists

The DLFA should prepare separate checklists for:

- General accounts.
- Cash and bank.
- Revenue.
- Grants.
- Procurement.
- Works.
- Payroll.
- Advances.
- Stores.
- Assets.
- Digital systems.
- Previous audit objections.

Checklists should be used as a minimum control, not as a substitute for professional judgement.

***

## 51. Improvement 4: Digitise Audit Workflow

A digital audit-management system can track:

- Audit plans.
- Team assignments.
- Requisitions.
- Documents received.
- Draft observations.
- Management replies.
- Report approval.
- Follow-up.
- Recovery.
- Pending cases.

### Minimum system features

- User-based access.
- Audit trail.
- Document upload.
- Due-date alerts.
- Search.
- Dashboard.
- Report export.
- Data backup.
- Version history.
- Role-based approval.

### Important caution

Digitisation does not automatically create control. A poorly designed digital system can reproduce the same weaknesses electronically.

***

## 52. Improvement 5: Strengthen Training

DLFA staff should receive regular training in:

- Local government laws.
- Accounting systems.
- Financial rules.
- Procurement.
- Works audit.
- Revenue audit.
- Grants.
- Digital evidence.
- Data analysis.
- Fraud indicators.
- Report writing.
- Communication.
- Public-sector ethics.

The CAG’s audit standards emphasise professional competence, continuous development, due care and quality control. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

***

## 53. Improvement 6: Use Data Analytics

Data analysis can help identify unusual transactions.

### Useful tests

- Duplicate invoice numbers.
- Duplicate supplier bank accounts.
- Payments just below approval limits.
- Unusual year-end expenditure.
- Repeated round-number payments.
- Payments on holidays.
- Negative or abnormal balances.
- Unadjusted advances.
- Revenue collections below historical trends.
- Frequent cash withdrawals.
- Suppliers receiving unusually high proportions of expenditure.
- Repeated changes to master data.

### Example SQL-style test

```sql
SELECT supplier_id, invoice_number, COUNT(*) AS duplicate_count
FROM payments
GROUP BY supplier_id, invoice_number
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;
```

This test identifies possible duplicate invoice entries. It does not prove fraud, but it helps auditors select transactions for examination.

***

## 54. Improvement 7: Strengthen Audit Follow-Up

The DLFA should publish or internally monitor:

- Number of objections issued.
- Number settled.
- Amount recovered.
- Amount pending.
- Average age of objections.
- Repeat objections.
- Bodies with delayed replies.
- Bodies with no corrective action.

### Suggested indicators

\[
\text{Objection Settlement Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Objections settled}}{\text{Total outstanding objections}}
\times 100
\]

\[
\text{Recovery Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Amount recovered}}{\text{Amount certified for recovery}}
\times 100
\]

\[
\text{Repeat Defect Rate}
=
\frac{\text{Repeat observations}}{\text{Total observations}}
\times 100
\]

A high repeat-defect rate indicates that the audit system is identifying problems but not producing sufficient corrective action.

***

## 55. Improvement 8: Create an Audit Committee

A Local Fund Audit Committee may review:

- Major objections.
- Fraud and loss cases.
- Delayed accounts.
- Unresolved recoveries.
- Repeated irregularities.
- Serious procurement violations.
- Systemic control weaknesses.
- Requests for special audit.

The committee should not replace the statutory authority of the DLFA. It should strengthen review and follow-up.

***

## 56. Improvement 9: Introduce Internal Quality Reviews

The DLFA should periodically select completed audit files for internal quality review.

### Review questions

- Was the audit properly authorised?
- Was the risk assessment documented?
- Were high-risk areas examined?
- Was sufficient evidence obtained?
- Were figures independently checked?
- Were management replies properly evaluated?
- Was the report issued on time?
- Was the audit conclusion supported?
- Were important issues escalated?
- Was follow-up completed?

The review should result in:

- Corrective instructions.
- Training needs.
- Revised checklists.
- Improved templates.
- Changes in audit planning.

***

## 57. Improvement 10: Protect Audit Independence

A local fund audit organisation should have adequate functional independence.

Important safeguards include:

- Written legal mandate.
- Authority to select audit areas.
- Protection from inappropriate interference.
- Control over audit conclusions.
- Transparent reporting.
- Rotation of sensitive assignments.
- Conflict-of-interest policy.
- Secure communication of findings.

The CAG Regulations state that audit institutions and auditors should remain independent and should not allow executive directions to suppress or modify audit findings within the audit mandate. [cag.gov](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/media/3-7-2-Resources-Acts-Manual-AuditRegulation2007-20201228130115.pdf)

# Part VII: Model Internal Control Framework for DLFA

## 58. Three-Layer Model

A practical DLFA framework can use three layers.

### First layer: Local authority management

Responsible for:

- Daily controls.
- Accounting.
- Budget.
- Cash.
- Procurement.
- Records.
- Asset protection.
- Corrective action.

### Second layer: Supervisory and departmental review

Responsible for:

- Monitoring local authorities.
- Reviewing accounts.
- Checking grant use.
- Monitoring delayed reports.
- Coordinating recovery.
- Escalating serious irregularities.

### Third layer: DLFA and external oversight

Responsible for:

- Independent audit.
- Internal control evaluation.
- Compliance examination.
- Reporting.
- Follow-up.
- Special audit.
- Technical coordination where applicable.

This three-layer model creates accountability without placing all responsibility on the auditor.

## 64. What Is the Internal Control Mechanism in DLFA?

In simple form, the internal control mechanism in the DLFA is the complete system used to ensure that local fund audits are:

- Properly authorised.
- Properly planned.
- Based on risk.
- Conducted by competent staff.
- Supported by evidence.
- Supervised and reviewed.
- Reported accurately.
- Followed up systematically.
- Protected from interference.
- Completed within prescribed time.

It includes both administrative controls and professional audit controls.

### Administrative controls

- Establishment control.
- Attendance and tour control.
- Leave and delegation.
- Record management.
- Budget and expenditure control.
- Procurement.
- Vehicle and travel control.
- Confidentiality.
- Information security.

### Professional audit controls

- Audit planning.
- Risk assessment.
- Audit programme.
- Requisition management.
- Evidence collection.
- Working-paper review.
- Entry and exit conferences.
- Report vetting.
- Quality assurance.
- Follow-up.
- Escalation of serious cases.

***

## 65. How Can the System Be Improved?

The most important improvements are:

1. Adopt risk-based annual audit planning.
2. Maintain a complete audit-universe and backlog register.
3. Use standard audit programmes and control checklists.
4. Introduce digital requisition and follow-up tracking.
5. Strengthen supervisory review and quality assurance.
6. Use data analytics for high-risk transactions.
7. Maintain a loss and defalcation register.
8. Create a formal audit-objection settlement mechanism.
9. Train staff in accounting, procurement, works and digital audit.
10. Protect audit independence and evidence integrity.
11. Measure audit results using timely indicators.
12. Focus on correction of systems, not only reporting of defects.

The ultimate objective is not to produce more audit paragraphs. It is to ensure that local bodies maintain reliable accounts, protect public assets, follow the law and deliver services to citizens.
