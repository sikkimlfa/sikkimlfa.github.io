# Sikkim Financial Rules 2025 and the 2025 Vehicle Tax Notification: Rules, Amendments and Practical Guide

Sikkim introduced a new set of financial rules in 2025, replacing the long-standing Sikkim Financial Rules, 1979. The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 were notified by the Finance Department through Notification No. 1/FIN/ADM dated 1 April 2025 and came into force from 1 April 2025. ([Scribd][1])

The new rules provide a common framework for receiving government money, spending public funds, preparing budgets, maintaining accounts, executing works, purchasing goods and services, managing stores, entering into contracts, releasing grants, handling deposits and dealing with other financial matters.

The rules contain 176 numbered rules arranged across 11 chapters, together with forms and appendices. They also place much greater emphasis on digital financial management through Sikkim Integrated Financial Management System, known as `PRANALI`. ([Scribd][1])

A separate but important 2025 development concerns vehicle taxation. The Transport Department issued Notification No. 19/CCS/GOS/MVD/2025 dated 27 March 2025 under the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982. It substituted Schedule II and introduced an additional tax on registration of new vehicles based on invoice cost, with rates ranging from 1% to 6% for the categories covered by the notification. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

This article explains both developments in simpler language and separates the financial-management rules from the vehicle-tax amendment.

> **Key Takeaway:** The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 are not simply an update of individual financial limits. They create a consolidated financial-management framework covering receipts, expenditure, budgeting, accounts, works, procurement, stores, contracts, grants, deposits and digital financial processing.

## 1. What are the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025?

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 are the State Government's general rules for managing public money.

They were made under Article 166(3) of the Constitution of India by the Governor of Sikkim through the Finance Department. The notification is numbered `1/FIN/ADM` and is dated `01.04.2025`. ([Scribd][1])

The rules apply throughout Sikkim and cover:

* State Government departments
* Government offices
* subordinate authorities
* autonomous bodies under State Government administrative control

An autonomous body may have separate financial rules through its approved bye-laws, but the relevant rules require approval by the State Government with prior concurrence of the Finance Department. ([Scribd][1])

### In simple words

Think of the Financial Rules as the basic financial operating manual of the State Government.

They answer questions such as:

* Who can approve expenditure?
* When can money be drawn?
* How should government revenue be deposited?
* How should purchases be made?
* How should government stores be recorded?
* How should works contracts be managed?
* How should grants be released?
* How should unspent balances be handled?
* How should government accounts be maintained?
* How should financial transactions move through PRANALI?

## 2. What happened to the Sikkim Financial Rules, 1979?

The 2025 Rules expressly repeal the Sikkim Financial Rules, 1979.

Rule 176 provides that the 1979 Rules are repealed. It also contains a saving provision so that actions already taken under the old rules are treated as having been taken under the corresponding provisions of the 2025 Rules. ([Scribd][3])

### In simple words

The State did not merely issue another amendment to the 1979 Rules.

It replaced the old framework with a new set of rules.

This is significant because the 1979 Rules had remained the principal financial framework for decades, although they had been amended from time to time.

For example, amendments in earlier years changed provisions concerning financial powers, budget classification and other financial procedures. A 2017 amendment replaced references to "Plan and Non-Plan" with "Capital and Revenue" in relevant provisions. ([Sikkim Fred][4])

The 2025 Rules therefore represent a much broader restructuring than an ordinary amendment.

## 4. Chapter I: Introduction and basic definitions

Chapter I establishes the foundation of the Rules.

It covers Rules 1 to 6.

### Rule 1: Short title, extent, application and commencement

Rule 1 gives the name of the Rules, their territorial extent, their application and their commencement date.

The Rules apply throughout Sikkim and came into force from 1 April 2025. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

From 1 April 2025, financial transactions covered by these Rules are to be handled under the new framework.

### Rule 2: Definitions

Rule 2 defines important financial terms.

Some particularly important definitions are:

* Accountant General
* Accounts Officer
* Administrative Approval
* Appropriation
* Competent Authority
* Controlling Authority
* Drawing and Disbursing Officer
* Financial Advisor
* Financial Sanction
* Head of Department
* Head of Office
* Internal Audit
* Local Fund
* PRANALI
* Re-appropriation
* Resource Controlling Officer
* SIFMS

The definition of DDO is particularly important. The Rules provide that a DDO is generally an officer from the Sikkim Finance and Accounts Service declared by the Finance Department on the recommendation of the Head of Department. Alternative arrangements are provided where such officers are not available. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

The Rules clearly identify the people responsible for financial transactions.

For example:

`DDO` → draws and disburses money.

`RCO` → monitors departmental financial resources.

`Financial Advisor` → provides financial advice to the department.

`Finance Department` → exercises the central financial-control role.

`PRANALI` → provides the digital financial-management platform.

### Administrative approval is not payment approval

One useful distinction is between administrative approval and financial sanction.

Administrative approval means that the competent authority has accepted a proposal or work.

It does not, by itself, authorize payment.

The Rules specifically state that no payment can be made merely on the basis of administrative approval. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple example

Suppose a department receives administrative approval for a building project costing ₹50lakh.

That does not automatically mean the DDO can draw ₹50lakh.

The required financial sanction, budget provision, procurement or works procedure and other applicable approvals must still be followed.

## 5. Rules 3 to 6: Coordination and Finance Department control

### Rule 3: Interdepartmental consultation

If a matter concerns more than one department, the concerned departments must concur before an order is issued, unless the matter is decided by or under the authority of the Cabinet. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

One department should not take a financial decision that materially affects another department without proper consultation.

### Rule 4: Financial regulations

Departmental orders having financial implications require the prior approval and concurrence of the Finance Department where prescribed by the Rule. ([Scribd][1])

### Rule 5: Doubts

If there is a doubt about the interpretation of the Financial Rules, the matter is to be referred to the Finance Department.

### Rule 6: Modification

The Finance Department may issue general or special instructions modifying or supplementing systems and procedures established by the Rules. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

The 2025 Rules are the main framework, but Finance Department instructions issued under the Rules remain important.

For audit purposes, therefore, an office should not examine only the Rules. Relevant Finance Department notifications, circulars, delegations and instructions should also be checked.

## 6. Chapter II: General system of financial management

Chapter II is one of the most important parts of the Rules.

It deals with the basic handling of government money.

### Rule 7: Government money must enter Government Account

Money received by or on behalf of Government must be brought into Government Account without delay. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Government money should not remain unnecessarily with an officer, office, contractor or intermediary.

If money belongs to Government, it must reach the proper Government Account.

## 7. Rule 8: Assessment and collection of revenue

Heads of departments and offices have responsibility for ensuring that government revenue is:

1. assessed correctly,
2. collected promptly,
3. credited under the correct head of account, and
4. monitored for arrears.

The Rules also require monthly returns and reconciliation of departmental receipts. The monthly reconciled account is to be submitted to the Finance Department in the prescribed form by the 10th of the succeeding month. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

A department should not merely collect money.

It must also prove:

* how much was due,
* how much was collected,
* how much remains outstanding,
* where the money was deposited, and
* whether the accounting records agree with the bank and treasury records.

This is directly relevant to internal audit and Local Fund Audit.

## 8. Rule 9: Refunds and remission

Refunds of government revenue are controlled.

The original demand or receipt should be traced before refund so that the same amount is not refunded twice.

The Rules also provide a 5% establishment-charge deduction for certain wrong and irregular deposits before the refundable amount is processed, subject to the specific conditions of the Rule. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Before returning government money, the department must establish that:

* the money was actually received,
* the refund is legally justified,
* the original transaction can be identified, and
* duplicate or fraudulent refunds are prevented.

## 9. Rule 10: Paying money into Government Account

The Rules provide detailed procedures for challans, bank receipts and digital payments.

Some monetary thresholds specified in Rule 10 include:

| Transaction                                           |                         Rule position |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------: |
| Cash deposit accepted without departmental challan    |                          Up to ₹5,000 |
| Cash deposit requiring prescribed challan procedure   |        Above ₹5,000 and up to ₹50,000 |
| Cash above the prescribed ceiling against one challan |                          Not accepted |
| Amount above ₹50,000                                  | Banking instrument or electronic mode |

The Rules also require computerized bank receipts and provide procedures for distribution of receipt copies to the depositor, department, Treasury, Pay and Accounts Office and bank. Handwritten bank receipts are not to be entertained under the stated procedure. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

The objective is to reduce:

* fake challans,
* incorrect account classification,
* unverified receipts,
* cash-handling risks.

For audit, the trail should therefore connect:

`Demand → Challan/Digital Receipt → Bank Credit → Treasury/PAO → Departmental Accounts`

## 10. Rule 11: Expenditure from public funds

This is one of the most important rules for audit.

A government officer cannot incur expenditure merely because money appears in the budget.

The Rules state that prior specific sanction of the competent authority is necessary before expenditure is incurred or a liability is created. ([Scribd][1])

### The important principle

> **Budget provision does not by itself authorize expenditure.**

This distinction is frequently important in audit.

### Splitting expenditure is not allowed

A single expenditure cannot be artificially divided into smaller parts simply to bring each part within the financial power of a lower authority. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple example

Suppose an item costs ₹12lakh and an officer has delegated authority only up to ₹5lakh.

The purchase should not be divided into:

* ₹4lakh
* ₹4lakh
* ₹4lakh

merely to remain below the officer's delegated limit.

The competence has to be examined with reference to the actual requirement and applicable delegation.

## 11. Rule 12: Financial propriety

Rule 12 establishes standards of financial propriety.

An officer spending public money is expected to exercise the same level of care that a prudent person would exercise with their own money. ([Scribd][1])

The Rules also require consideration of:

* necessity,
* economy,
* fairness,
* reasonableness of price,
* quality,
* applicable procurement procedure,
* public interest.

Importantly, the Rules do not reduce procurement to the simple question of accepting the cheapest offer.

The procurement decision should consider whether the offer meets the requirement, whether the price is reasonable and whether the selected offer is appropriate after considering all relevant factors. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

`Lowest price` does not automatically mean `correct purchase`.

The department must be able to explain why the purchase was necessary, why the specifications were appropriate and why the selected offer was financially reasonable.

## 12. Rule 13: Sanction of expenditure

Rule 13 reinforces three basic principles:

1. Public revenue must be spent only for legitimate public expenditure.
2. A subordinate authority can sanction expenditure only within its delegated authority.
3. Finance Department consent is mandatory for expenditure involving a new principle or practice likely to create increased or recurring expenditure in future. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

An officer cannot create a new recurring financial commitment simply because there is money available for another purpose.

## 13. Rule 14: Contingent expenditure

Contingent expenditure covers incidental expenditure needed for running an office or department where the expenditure does not fall under another specific classification such as works or stores.

The Rules require the prescribed contingent bill generated through PRANALI for drawal of contingent expenditure. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Office expenses still need proper classification, sanction, documentation and digital processing.

## 14. Chapter III: Budget formulation and implementation

Chapter III deals with Rules 61 to 84.

It establishes how the annual budget is prepared, controlled, monitored and adjusted.

### Rule 61: Financial year

The Government financial year runs from:

`1 April → 31 March`

This is the standard financial-year cycle used by the Rules. ([Scribd][3])

### Rule 62: Presentation of budget

The annual budget is the Annual Financial Statement referred to in Article 202 of the Constitution.

The Budget Division of the Finance Department issues instructions for preparation of budget estimates, and departments are required to comply with them. ([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

Every department cannot prepare its budget in any format it chooses.

It must follow the instructions issued by the Budget Division.

## 15. Budget estimates and revenue

The Rules require departments to prepare estimates of:

* revenue receipts,
* expenditure,
* programmes,
* schemes,
* projects,
* interest,
* debt servicing,
* repayments and other relevant items.

For fluctuating receipts and charges, historical performance over the preceding three years may be used as a basis, suitably adjusted for anticipated changes and special circumstances. ([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

Budget figures should have a reasonable basis.

A department should be able to explain why its estimate increased or decreased.

## 16. Non-tax revenue and user charges

The Rules recognize non-tax revenue as an important source of government revenue.

Departments and autonomous bodies collect various forms of non-tax revenue.

The Rules specifically identify user charges as an important component and provide that departments may identify and publish the user charges they levy. ([Scribd][3])

### Audit relevance

An auditor can examine whether:

* the applicable user charge was correctly fixed,
* all eligible users were charged,
* collections were deposited,
* arrears were monitored,
* receipts were correctly classified.

## 17. Budget monitoring and expenditure control

The 2025 Rules provide for regular monitoring of departmental expenditure.

The Resource Controlling Officer plays an important role.

The Rules require departments to obtain expenditure information from DDOs and other offices and monitor expenditure against sanctioned grants or appropriations. ([Scribd][3])

The Rules also require physical and financial progress information for schemes.

### Simple meaning

The Government should know not only:

`How much money was spent?`

but also:

`What work was achieved with that money?`

This connects financial monitoring with physical progress.

## 18. Surrender of savings

One important budget-control principle is that savings should not simply be kept aside for possible future expenditure.

The Rules state that savings and provisions that cannot be profitably used should be surrendered when they are foreseen, subject to the provisions of the Rules. ([Scribd][3])

### Rush of expenditure

The Rules specifically state that rush of expenditure, especially near the end of the financial year, is contrary to financial propriety. ([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

The department should not think:

> "There is money left, so we must spend it before 31 March."

The purpose of government expenditure is to meet an approved requirement, not merely to prevent budget lapse.

## 19. Chapter IV: Government accounts

Chapter IV covers Rules 85 to 97.

The State's annual accounts are prepared to show:

* receipts,
* disbursements,
* surplus or deficit,
* government liabilities,
* government assets.

The accounts are prepared by the Accountant General and certified by the Comptroller and Auditor General of India before being submitted through the constitutional process. The Rules state that they should preferably reach the Governor within six months of the close of the financial year. ([Scribd][1])

### Government account structure

The Rules recognize three principal parts:

1. Consolidated Fund
2. Contingency Fund
3. Public Account

The Consolidated Fund is further divided into Revenue and Capital divisions. ([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

Government money is not one single accounting bucket.

Different types of receipts, expenditure, deposits and other transactions are classified into prescribed funds and heads.

Correct classification matters because it affects:

* budget control,
* financial statements,
* appropriation accounts,
* audit,
* fiscal reporting.

## 20. PRANALI: the digital financial backbone

Rule 90 provides for a digital online system for processing the financial business of the State.

The system is called `PRANALI`, under the Sikkim Integrated Financial Management System. ([Scribd][3])

The Rules provide for PRANALI to support functions including:

* sanction communication,
* bill preparation,
* bill processing,
* payments,
* receipts,
* Direct Benefit Transfer,
* fund-flow management,
* financial reporting.

DDOs are required to register for implementation of the system at the relevant levels for receipts, disbursements and tracking of fund flow and unspent balances. ([Scribd][3])

### Why this matters

This changes the audit trail.

Instead of relying only on paper files, financial transactions increasingly have a digital trail.

A simplified transaction chain can be viewed as:

```text
Administrative proposal
        ↓
Budget provision
        ↓
Financial sanction
        ↓
PRANALI processing
        ↓
Bill
        ↓
Treasury / Pay and Accounts Office
        ↓
Digital payment
        ↓
Bank
        ↓
Accounting and reporting
```

The Finance Department was also conducting capacity-building programmes in 2025 for DDOs and Accounts Officers covering TDS, PFMS, audit procedures and SNA-SPARSH through PRANALI. ([Sikkim Government][5])

## 21. Direct Benefit Transfer and government payments

The Rules provide for digital transfer of government payments to beneficiary bank accounts through mechanisms such as RTGS, NEFT or other approved digital modes.

They also state that money drawn for disbursement should not be encashed or parked in personal accounts. ([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

Government funds should move as directly as possible to the intended recipient.

This reduces unnecessary intermediary handling and creates a clearer audit trail.

## 22. Separate bank accounts

The Rules also regulate government departments maintaining separate bank accounts outside the Government Account.

The listed authorised-bank framework includes:

* State Bank of Sikkim,
* Sikkim State Co-operative Bank,
* scheduled public-sector banks,
* other scheduled banks authorized by Government.

The approval for a separate account is stated to be valid for one financial year and requires revalidation in the following financial year. ([Scribd][3])

Interest earned on such accounts is required to be credited under the prescribed revenue head, subject to the applicable provisions and directions. ([Scribd][3])

### Audit point

A department maintaining an outside bank account should be able to demonstrate:

* authority for opening it,
* current validity,
* bank reconciliation,
* receipts,
* payments,
* closing balance,
* interest earned,
* transfer of interest to Government Account,
* revalidation.

## 23. Chapter V: Works

Chapter V covers Rules 98 to 103.

Works are governed by the Sikkim Public Works Code and Manual 2009, as amended from time to time, together with applicable procedures approved by Government and concurred in by the Finance Department where required. ([Scribd][1])

The Rules distinguish between:

* original works,
* minor works,
* repair works.

### Technical sanction

Technical sanction relates to a properly detailed estimate based on drawings, designs and relevant technical data.

The Rules describe technical sanction as confirmation that the proposal is technically sound and the estimate is properly calculated. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple distinction

`Administrative approval` asks:

> Should Government undertake this work?

`Technical sanction` asks:

> Is the work technically designed and estimated properly?

`Financial sanction` asks:

> Is expenditure authorized from the available budget?

These approvals should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 24. Mobilization and secured advances

For certain specialized and capital-intensive works, mobilization or secured advances may be granted under the applicable works procedures.

The 2025 Rules require a bank guarantee of equivalent amount from a scheduled bank or State Bank of Sikkim, valid for at least one year, before such advance is released. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Government should not release a major advance without adequate security.

If the bank guarantee cannot be revalidated, further payments to the contractor are restricted under the stated rule. ([Scribd][1])

## 25. Chapter VI: Procurement of goods and services

Chapter VI covers Rules 104 to 144.

It establishes general procurement principles for departments.

The procurement rules operate along with special departmental rules such as the Sikkim Public Works Code and Manual and Forest Manual where applicable. ([Scribd][1])

### Basic procurement principle

Purchases should normally be made from the lowest tenderer unless there is a recorded reason to the contrary and the competent authority accepts the procurement in accordance with the applicable procedure. ([Scribd][1])

But Rule 12's financial-propriety principles remain important.

The correct procurement decision is not simply:

`lowest price = automatic purchase`

The department must also examine suitability, quality, specifications, reasonableness and other relevant factors. ([Scribd][1])

## 26. Performance Security

For contracts for goods and services above the stated threshold, the Rules provide for performance security.

The text specifies 5% of the contract value in the relevant provision and states that performance security may be provided through instruments such as TDR, FDR or bank guarantee, subject to the conditions in the Rules. ([Scribd][1])

The stated provision applies to successful bidders awarded supply work having a value above ₹10lakh and requires the performance security to remain valid for 60 days beyond the stipulated completion of contractual obligations, including warranty obligations. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Performance security protects Government if the supplier fails to perform the contract as required.

## 27. Chapter VII: Stores management

Chapter VII covers Rules 145 to 158.

This chapter deals with government materials and stores.

It requires proper:

* inspection,
* receipt,
* measurement,
* counting,
* quality verification,
* stock entry,
* custody,
* accounting,
* physical verification.

Rule 146 requires materials received to be examined, counted, measured or weighed as appropriate and entered in the appropriate stock register. ([Scribd][1])

### Annual physical verification

Dead stock is to be physically verified once every year, with discrepancies investigated and brought to account. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Buying something is only half the process.

Government must also prove:

`What was bought → What was received → Where it was entered → Where it is stored → Who is responsible → Whether it still exists`

## 28. Chapter VIII: Contract management

Chapter VIII contains Rule 159.

It establishes general principles for government contracts.

Contracts made in exercise of the executive power of the State must be entered into by an authority empowered under the applicable constitutional and governmental framework. The Rules also require contract terms to be precise and definite. ([Scribd][1])

### Contract value and documentation

For works contracts or purchase contracts up to ₹1crore, the relevant tender documents and letter of acceptance can constitute the binding contractual arrangement where the stated conditions are met.

For works or purchases of ₹1crore and above, the Rules require execution of a contract document containing the necessary contractual clauses, subject to the detailed provision. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

As contract value increases, documentation and contractual safeguards become more important.

## 29. Contract disputes and compensation

The Rules restrict compensation to contractors outside the strict terms of a contract unless the required Law Department consultation, Finance Department concurrence and Government approval are obtained. ([Scribd][1])

They also state that cost-plus contracts should ordinarily be avoided and require justification where such contracts become unavoidable. ([Scribd][1])

### Audit relevance

An auditor should therefore examine:

* original contract,
* tender documents,
* letter of acceptance,
* work order,
* variation orders,
* extensions,
* additional payments,
* compensation,
* security,
* performance,
* dispute settlement.

## 30. Chapter IX: Grants-in-Aid

Chapter IX covers Rule 160 onwards in the grants section.

It applies to grants-in-aid including assistance to:

* educational institutions,
* local bodies,
* cooperative societies,
* other eligible institutions and organizations.

The Rules identify three categories:

1. Grants-in-Aid General
2. Grants-in-Aid Salaries
3. Grants for creation of Capital Assets ([Scribd][1])

### Conditions for grants

Sanction orders should specify:

* purpose,
* amount,
* whether recurring or non-recurring,
* conditions,
* time limit where applicable.

The Rules also require relevant registration and constitutional documents from grantee institutions where applicable. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

A grant is not simply free money.

The recipient must use it for the approved purpose and comply with the conditions attached to the sanction.

## 31. Utilisation certificates and outcome monitoring

The grants section links financial reporting with physical and outcome performance.

The prescribed certificate framework includes confirmation concerning:

* internal controls,
* compliance with relevant laws and rules,
* assignment of responsibilities,
* intended beneficiaries,
* authorized expenditure proportions,
* physical and financial performance,
* achievement of intended outcomes. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

A utilization certificate should not be treated as a formality.

The question is not only:

`Was the money spent?`

It is also:

`Was it spent for the approved purpose and did the scheme achieve the intended result?`

## 32. Chapter X: Deposit transactions

Chapter X covers deposit transactions.

Deposits are classified under appropriate accounting heads and include categories such as:

* revenue deposits,
* security deposits,
* court deposits,
* personal deposits,
* public works deposits,
* pension-related deposits,
* commercial undertaking deposits,
* election-related deposits,
* other prescribed deposits.

([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Money temporarily held by Government does not automatically become government revenue.

It must be classified correctly and accounted for according to its nature.

## 33. Personal Deposit Accounts

The Rules provide controls over Personal Deposit Accounts.

Withdrawals are subject to the prescribed authorization and the available balance.

The bank cannot allow withdrawals exceeding the credit balance. The prescribed records and payment documentation must support transactions. ([Scribd][1])

### Audit focus

For a Personal Deposit Account, examine:

* authority for opening the account,
* approval validity,
* receipts,
* withdrawals,
* supporting vouchers,
* closing balance,
* reconciliation,
* lapse provisions,
* compliance with the relevant Act or scheme.

## 34. Chapter XI: Miscellaneous financial controls

Chapter XI contains Rules 165 to 176.

It covers matters such as:

* loans and advances,
* establishment changes,
* transfer of officers,
* cash and stores during transfer of charge,
* records,
* conveyance allowance,
* fiscal responsibility,
* government guarantees,
* Chartered Accountant empanelment,
* repeal and saving.

([Scribd][1])

### Establishment changes

Finance Department consultation is required for proposals involving matters such as:

* creation of posts,
* abolition of posts,
* special or personal pay,
* pay-fixation formulas,
* other specified service or post conditions. ([Scribd][1])

### Transfer of charge

When an officer takes over responsibility for cash or stores, the Rules require examination and verification of relevant records, cash and stores. ([Scribd][1])

### Simple meaning

Financial accountability does not stop when an officer is transferred.

The incoming and outgoing officers must establish what was handed over.

## 35. Conveyance allowance

Rule 172 deals with conveyance allowance.

It describes conveyance allowance as an allowance available to a gazetted officer who is entitled to an attached vehicle but does not have one because the vehicle has not been allotted or has been surrendered.

The officer may have the option of an allotted vehicle or conveyance allowance, subject to the applicable instructions issued by the Finance Department. ([Scribd][3])

## 36. Fiscal Responsibility and Government Guarantees

Rules 173 and 174 connect the Financial Rules with the State's fiscal-responsibility and government-guarantee legislation.

The Financial Rules refer to:

* Sikkim Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2010
* Sikkim Ceiling on Government Guarantees Act, 2000

([Scribd][3])

### Simple meaning

Departmental expenditure is not considered in isolation.

It forms part of the wider fiscal position of the State.

## 37. Chartered Accountant empanelment

Rule 175 provides that where Chartered Accountant services are required, firms empanelled with the Government of Sikkim Finance Department are to be engaged. ([Scribd][3])

### Audit implication

A department should verify the firm's eligibility and empanelment before engaging a Chartered Accountant under this provision.

## 38. The 2025 vehicle-tax notification

The Financial Rules and the vehicle-tax notification are separate legal instruments.

The vehicle-tax change was issued by the Transport Department under the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982.

Notification No. 19/CCS/GOS/MVD/2025 is dated 27 March 2025. It exercises the power under sub-section (1-B) of Section 4 and substitutes Schedule II concerning additional tax on registration of new vehicles. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

The official Transport Department notification is available through the department's notification document. [Official Transport Department notification for additional vehicle tax](https://transportdepartment.sikkim.gov.in/notificationdoc/notificationdoc-zEg16bOePoq6hKzICjoQAQQxfqwszUYQqUFxr1JG.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 39. Vehicle additional tax rates in 2025

For registration of private, government and public-sector-undertaking vehicles, including non-transport two-wheelers, the notification establishes additional tax based on the vehicle's invoice cost. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

| Invoice cost of new vehicle | Additional tax |
| --------------------------- | -------------: |
| Up to ₹2lakh                |             1% |
| Above ₹2lakh up to ₹4lakh   |             2% |
| Above ₹4lakh up to ₹10lakh  |             3% |
| Above ₹10lakh up to ₹25lakh |             4% |
| Above ₹25lakh up to ₹50lakh |             5% |
| Above ₹50lakh               |             6% |

These rates are stated in the substituted Schedule II reproduced in the official Transport Department notification. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

## 40. Vehicle-tax calculation examples

The notification uses invoice cost as the basis.

For illustration, if the applicable invoice cost is ₹8lakh and the 3% slab applies:

\(₹8,00,000 \times 3\% = ₹24,000\)

For an invoice cost of ₹20lakh at 4%:

\(₹20,00,000 \times 4\% = ₹80,000\)

For an invoice cost of ₹60lakh at 6%:

\(₹60,00,000 \times 6\% = ₹3,60,000\)

These are mathematical illustrations of the notified percentage rates. They should not be treated as a complete statement of every registration charge, tax, fee or exemption that may apply to a particular vehicle.

## 41. Do not confuse additional tax with every vehicle charge

This distinction matters.

The 2025 notification concerns an **additional tax on registration of new vehicles** under Schedule II of the Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

It should not automatically be described as:

* a universal road tax,
* a fixed ₹25,000 charge,
* a re-registration charge for every old vehicle,
* a transfer-of-ownership fee.

The Transport Department itself published a June 2025 clarification in response to reports about a supposed ₹25,000 vehicle re-registration tax and stated that the circulated information was false. ([The Hills Times][6])

### Practical lesson

For vehicle-related financial claims, always identify:

1. the Act,
2. section,
3. Schedule,
4. notification number,
5. notification date,
6. vehicle category,
7. invoice-cost slab,
8. whether the transaction is new registration, transfer or re-registration.

## 42. The legislative background of the vehicle-tax change

The Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation (Amendment) Bill, 2025 was introduced as Bill No. 01 of 2025.

The Bill proposed amendment of Section 4 and substitution of Schedule II. It provided that the State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, increase the additional tax rates specified in Schedule II for imposing additional tax on registration of vehicles. ([PRS Legislative Research][7])

The notification dated 27 March 2025 subsequently specified the new Schedule II rates. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

### Simple legislative chain

```text
Sikkim Motor Vehicles Taxation Act, 1982
                ↓
2025 Amendment
                ↓
Section 4 amended
                ↓
Schedule II substituted
                ↓
Notification No. 19/CCS/GOS/MVD/2025
                ↓
Additional tax linked to invoice cost
```

## 43. How the Financial Rules and vehicle notification are connected

They are not the same rule.

The Financial Rules govern the financial administration of the State.

The vehicle notification deals with taxation under the Motor Vehicles Taxation Act.

The connection is mainly through government revenue management.

For example:

```text
Vehicle registration
       ↓
Additional tax becomes payable
       ↓
Revenue collected
       ↓
Government Account
       ↓
Correct revenue classification
       ↓
Accounting and reporting
       ↓
Audit
```

The Financial Rules therefore provide the broader financial-control environment within which government receipts are assessed, collected, deposited, classified and monitored.

## 44. What changed from the older financial framework?

The most obvious structural change is that the 2025 Rules replace the 1979 Rules rather than merely adding another isolated amendment. ([Scribd][3])

Several features stand out in the new framework.

| Area                     | Older framework                             | 2025 position                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal rules          | Sikkim Financial Rules, 1979                | Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025                            |
| Status                   | Earlier framework                           | New consolidated rules                                  |
| Commencement             | 1979                                        | 1 April 2025                                            |
| Digital financial system | Earlier procedures                          | PRANALI expressly integrated                            |
| DDO framework            | Earlier provisions                          | SFAS-based definition with fallback arrangements        |
| Revenue control          | Financial rules and departmental procedures | Detailed receipt, reconciliation and digital provisions |
| Procurement              | Earlier rules and amendments                | Consolidated procurement chapter                        |
| Stores                   | Earlier framework                           | Dedicated stores-management chapter                     |
| Contract management      | Earlier framework                           | Dedicated contract-management provisions                |
| Grants                   | Earlier framework                           | Dedicated grants and utilization controls               |
| Deposits                 | Earlier framework                           | Dedicated deposit-transactions chapter                  |
| Repeal                   | 1979 Rules                                  | Expressly repealed by Rule 176                          |

The table describes the structural position of the 2025 Rules and should not be read as saying that every procedure in the 1979 Rules was completely new in 2025. Many principles were carried forward, updated or reorganized.

## 46. What should a DDO check before making a payment?

A practical DDO checklist can be built from the 2025 Rules.

### Before payment

* Is there budget provision?
* Is the expenditure properly sanctioned?
* Is the sanction issued by the competent authority?
* Is the expenditure within delegated financial powers?
* Has procurement procedure been followed?
* Has the supply or work actually been completed to the required extent?
* Are bills and supporting documents available?
* Is the expenditure correctly classified?
* Are statutory deductions applicable?
* Has the transaction been processed through PRANALI where required?
* Is there any advance outstanding?
* Is there any risk of duplicate payment?

### Simple test

A payment should be capable of answering five questions:

```text
Why was the money spent?
Who authorized it?
Was the procedure followed?
Was the item actually received or work performed?
Where is the supporting record?
```

If these questions cannot be answered, the transaction requires closer examination.

## 48. A simple financial-control model

The 2025 Rules can be understood through a six-stage model:

```text
1. PLAN
   ↓
Budget and requirement

2. AUTHORIZE
   ↓
Administrative and financial sanction

3. PROCURE
   ↓
Tender / quotation / contract / purchase

4. EXECUTE
   ↓
Supply / work / service

5. PAY
   ↓
Bill → PRANALI → Treasury/PAO → Bank

6. ACCOUNT AND AUDIT
   ↓
Classification → Reconciliation → Reporting → Audit
```

A weakness at any stage can affect the final transaction.

## 49. Why PRANALI matters for audit

The express integration of PRANALI into the 2025 Financial Rules is one of the important operational features of the new framework. ([Scribd][3])

For auditors, digital processing can potentially make it easier to trace:

* sanction,
* bill,
* DDO,
* payment,
* receipt,
* fund flow,
* unspent balance,
* financial reporting.

But digital processing does not automatically make a transaction valid.

A transaction can still be wrong if:

* the underlying purchase was unnecessary,
* sanction was defective,
* procurement rules were bypassed,
* goods were not received,
* work was not completed,
* classification was incorrect,
* statutory deductions were missed.

### The basic audit principle remains

> **A digitally processed transaction still needs substantive financial justification.**

The Sikkim Legislative Assembly approved the State's General Budget for financial year 2025-26 at ₹16,196crore during the Budget Session in March 2025. The Assembly also considered the Sikkim Appropriation Bill, 2025. ([Sikkim Government][8])

## 51. Common mistakes to avoid

### Mistake 1: "There is budget, so we can spend."

Incorrect.

Budget provision does not itself authorize expenditure. Prior specific sanction is required. ([Scribd][1])

### Mistake 2: "Administrative approval means payment can be made."

Incorrect.

Administrative approval does not itself authorize payment. ([Scribd][1])

### Mistake 3: "Lowest quotation always wins."

Not necessarily.

Financial propriety requires consideration of suitability, quality, price reasonableness and other relevant factors. ([Scribd][1])

### Mistake 4: "We can split the purchase."

Not to circumvent delegated powers.

The Rules prohibit splitting expenditure to bring it within the competence of a lower sanctioning authority. ([Scribd][1])

### Mistake 5: "Unused budget should be spent before 31 March."

The Rules specifically discourage rush of expenditure at the end of the financial year. ([Scribd][3])

### Mistake 6: "A utilization certificate proves everything."

A proper utilization framework also considers physical and financial performance and outcomes. ([Scribd][1])

### Mistake 7: "The 2025 vehicle tax is a flat ₹25,000."

The official Transport Department notification establishes percentage-based additional tax for new vehicle registration according to invoice-cost slabs. The department also rejected misinformation concerning a supposed ₹25,000 re-registration tax. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])

A department implementing the 2025 Financial Rules can maintain the following checklist.

| Sanction         | Is the competent authority's sanction available?                        |
| Procurement      | Was the correct procurement procedure followed?                         |
| Financial powers | Is the sanction within delegated authority?                             |
| PRANALI          | Was the transaction processed through the prescribed digital system?    |
| Receipt          | Was government revenue deposited without delay?                         |
| Reconciliation   | Do departmental, bank and treasury records agree?                       |
| Contract         | Is there a valid agreement or accepted tender arrangement?              |
| Security         | Was required performance/security deposit obtained?                     |
| Stores           | Was the material inspected and entered in the stock register?           |
| Works            | Are administrative, technical and financial approvals available?        |
| Advance          | Has the advance been properly secured and adjusted?                     |
| Grant            | Is the grant used for the sanctioned purpose?                           |
| UC               | Is the utilization certificate supported by actual records and results? |
| Audit            | Are records complete and readily traceable?                             |

## 53. What is genuinely new or significant in 2025?

The most significant point is not one individual percentage or monetary threshold.

It is the consolidation of financial administration into a modern rule framework.

The 2025 Rules expressly bring together:

* traditional treasury controls,
* budget management,
* expenditure authorization,
* procurement,
* works,
* contract management,
* stores,
* grants,
* deposits,
* digital financial processing,
* internal financial accountability.

The Rules also formally recognize PRANALI and SIFMS as part of the State's financial-management architecture. ([Scribd][1])

The vehicle-tax notification is a different development, but it illustrates how a separate departmental tax measure can operate within the broader government-revenue and accounting framework.

## 54. How to read the Rules for audit work

For practical audit work, it is usually better not to read the 176 rules as one continuous document.

Instead, identify the transaction first.

### If it is revenue

Read:

* Rules 7 to 10
* relevant departmental revenue rules
* relevant tax or fee notification

### If it is expenditure

Read:

* Rules 11 to 14
* relevant delegation of financial powers
* budget provisions
* applicable departmental instructions

### If it is a work

Read:

* Rules 98 to 103
* Sikkim Public Works Code and Manual
* tender and contract documents

### If it is procurement

Read:

* Rules 104 onwards
* relevant procurement provisions
* department-specific manuals

### If it is stores

Read:

* Rules 145 to 158
* stock and inventory provisions

### If it is a contract

Read:

* Rule 159
* tender documents
* agreement
* applicable works or procurement rules

### If it is a grant

Read:

* Rule 160 onwards
* sanction order
* scheme guidelines
* utilization certificate
* physical and financial progress

### If it is a deposit

Read:

* Rules 163 and 164
* applicable deposit classification
* bank and treasury records

This transaction-based approach makes the Rules much easier to use.

## 55. Key operational takeaways

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 establish a new financial-management framework effective from 1 April 2025.

The most important practical points are:

1. The 1979 Financial Rules have been repealed and replaced by the 2025 Rules. ([Scribd][3])
2. The Rules contain 176 numbered rules across 11 chapters. ([Scribd][3])
3. Budget provision alone does not authorize expenditure. ([Scribd][1])
4. Administrative approval and financial sanction are different concepts. ([Scribd][1])
5. Expenditure should not be split to bypass delegated financial powers. ([Scribd][1])
6. Financial propriety requires economy, necessity, reasonableness and accountability. ([Scribd][1])
7. Government revenue must be promptly realized, deposited and correctly classified. ([Scribd][1])
8. PRANALI is the designated digital system for major financial processes under the Rules. ([Scribd][3])
9. Works remain linked to the Sikkim Public Works Code and Manual, 2009, as amended. ([Scribd][1])
10. Procurement, stores and contract management have dedicated provisions.
11. Grants require purpose-based control and supporting utilization and performance information. ([Scribd][1])
12. Government deposits must be properly classified and controlled.
13. The 2025 vehicle notification is separate from the Financial Rules and deals with additional tax on registration of new vehicles. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])
14. The notified vehicle additional-tax rates range from 1% to 6% according to invoice-cost slabs for the specified vehicle categories. ([Sikkim Transport Department][2])
15. For audit and compliance, the exact rule, notification, delegation and transaction record should always be read together.
