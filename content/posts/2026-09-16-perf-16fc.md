# 16th Finance Commission Performance Grants in Sikkim

The Sixteenth Finance Commission covers the five-year period from **1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031**. It recommends grants for both rural and urban local bodies, with **80 per cent as basic grants and 20 per cent as performance-based grants**. [indiabudget.gov](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/16fcvol1.pdf)

For Sikkim, the main challenge is not only receiving the grants. The State and its local bodies must also satisfy entry conditions, improve accounting and audit transparency, increase own-source revenue, and establish reliable performance data.

In simple terms:

> The 16th Finance Commission framework will reward local bodies that maintain proper accounts, improve their own revenue and deliver better services.

***

# Part I: What Is the 16th Finance Commission?

## 1. Meaning of the Finance Commission

The Finance Commission is a constitutional body established under **Article 280 of the Constitution of India**.

Its responsibilities include recommending:

- Distribution of tax revenue between the Union and States.
- Allocation of the States’ share among States.
- Grants-in-aid to States.
- Measures to supplement the resources of Panchayats and Municipalities.
- Certain arrangements for disaster-management financing.

The Sixteenth Finance Commission was constituted on **31 December 2023** and covers the award period beginning **1 April 2026** and ending **31 March 2031**. [indiabudget.gov](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/16fcvol1.pdf)

### Simple explanation

The Finance Commission decides how some national resources should be shared with States and local governments.

***

## 2. Why Local-Body Grants Are Important

Panchayats and Municipalities are responsible for services close to citizens.

They need funds for:

- Water.
- Sanitation.
- Solid waste.
- Roads.
- Drainage.
- Streetlighting.
- Public health.
- Local infrastructure.
- Maintenance of community assets.

Local bodies often have limited own-source revenue. Therefore, Finance Commission grants help them perform their responsibilities.

### The problem

A grant may be released, but the local body may still fail to provide good services because of:

- Weak planning.
- Poor accounts.
- Delayed procurement.
- Lack of technical staff.
- Unused assets.
- Weak monitoring.
- Poor audit follow-up.

The performance-grant system tries to connect funding with improvement.

***

# Part II: 16th Finance Commission Grant Structure

## 3. Total Local-Body Grants

The 16th Finance Commission recommended approximately:

- **₹4.4 lakh crore for Rural Local Bodies**, and
- **₹3.6 lakh crore for Urban Local Bodies**

for the five-year award period from 2026–27 to 2030–31. [prsindia](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/report-of-the-16th-finance-commission-for-2026-31)

The combined recommended amount is approximately:

$$₹4.4 \text{ lakh crore} + ₹3.6 \text{ lakh crore}=₹8.0 \text{ lakh crore}$$

The 16th Finance Commission’s detailed tables and implementation documents should be used for final State-wise and year-wise figures.

***

## 4. Basic and Performance Components

The grants are divided as follows:

| Component | Share |
|---|---:|
| Basic grant | 80% |
| Performance-based grant | 20% |
| Total | 100% |

If the total grant is represented by \(G\):

$$\text{Basic Grant} = 0.80G$$

$$\text{Performance Grant} = 0.20G$$

### Example

If an eligible local-government grant is ₹100 crore:

- Basic grant = ₹80 crore.
- Performance grant = ₹20 crore.

### Simple explanation

Most of the money is basic support. A smaller but important part is linked to performance and reforms.

***

## 5. State and Local-Body Performance Components

The 20 per cent performance component is divided equally between:

- State performance grant.
- Local-body performance grant.

| Performance component | Share of total local-body grant | Share within performance component |
|---|---:|---:|
| State performance | 10% | 50% of performance grant |
| Local-body performance | 10% | 50% of performance grant |
| Total performance component | 20% | 100% |

The State component depends on reforms and transfers by the State Government.

The local-body component depends mainly on improvement by individual Panchayats and Municipalities, especially own-source revenue growth. [indiabudget.gov](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/16fcvol2.pdf)

### Simple explanation

- The State must support local bodies.
- Individual local bodies must also improve their own financial performance.

***

# Part III: Entry-Level Conditions

## 6. Why Entry Conditions Exist

The 16th Finance Commission requires certain basic conditions before local-body grants can be accessed.

The three major entry-level conditions are:

1. Proper constitution of local bodies.
2. Public disclosure of provisional and audited accounts.
3. Timely constitution of the State Finance Commission.

 [prsindia](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/report-of-the-16th-finance-commission-for-2026-31)

### Simple explanation

Before asking for performance money, the local-government system must first demonstrate that it is legally constituted, financially transparent and supported by a functioning State Finance Commission.

***

## 7. Condition 1: Duly Constituted Local Bodies

A local body must be legally constituted according to the Constitution and applicable State law.

This means:

- Panchayats and Municipalities must exist under law.
- Elections must be held at the required intervals.
- Terms must be legally valid.
- Wards and constituencies must be properly notified.
- Reserved seats must be determined according to law.
- Elected representatives must be recognised.
- The institution must not be functioning only through an unauthorised or expired arrangement.

### Sikkim application

For Sikkim, this condition requires regular constitution of:

- Gram Panchayat Units.
- Zilla Panchayats.
- Gangtok Municipal Corporation.
- Namchi Municipal Council.
- Nagar Panchayats.
- Other urban local bodies notified under State law.

Sikkim’s current local-government structure includes rural and urban bodies formed under the Sikkim Panchayat Act, 1993 and Sikkim Municipalities Act, 2007.

### Evidence required

- Election notification.
- Gazette notification.
- Constitution order.
- Ward delimitation notification.
- Reservation order.
- List of elected members.
- Meeting records.
- Term-expiry and election calendar.

***

## 8. Condition 2: Public Disclosure of Accounts

Local bodies must publish their financial information in the public domain.

This may include:

- Provisional accounts.
- Audited accounts.
- Balance sheet.
- Income and expenditure statement.
- Cash-flow statement.
- Receipts and payments.
- Grants received.
- Grants utilised.
- Outstanding liabilities.

The 16th Finance Commission links local-body grants to the publication of provisional and audited accounts. [prsindia](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/report-of-the-16th-finance-commission-for-2026-31)

### Simple explanation

Citizens should be able to see how much money the local body received, how much it spent and what assets or liabilities it has.

### Sikkim improvement requirement

Sikkim should maintain a central public portal containing:

- Local-body profile.
- Annual budget.
- Provisional accounts.
- Audited accounts.
- Audit report.
- Action-taken report.
- Grants received.
- Grants utilised.
- Own-source revenue.
- Service-level indicators.

***

## 9. Condition 3: State Finance Commission

The State Finance Commission examines the financial position of local bodies and recommends:

- Sharing of State revenues.
- Assignment of taxes.
- Grants-in-aid.
- Revenue powers.
- Measures to improve financial capacity.

The 16th Finance Commission requires timely constitution of the State Finance Commission and timely action on its report.

### Sikkim requirements

Sikkim should ensure:

- The State Finance Commission is constituted within the required period.
- Its report is submitted.
- The Action Taken Report is prepared.
- The Action Taken Report is placed before the State Legislature within the prescribed time.
- Accepted recommendations are implemented.
- Local-body financial data is available for the next Commission.

### Simple explanation

The State Finance Commission should not be treated as a temporary committee. It is an important institution for local-government finance.

# Part IV: Performance Grants

## 10. Meaning of a Performance Grant

A performance grant is money linked to achievement of specified results or reforms.

It is different from an ordinary grant.

### Ordinary grant

Money is released mainly because the local body is eligible.

### Performance grant

Money is released only when the local body meets specified standards.

### Simple example

A local body may receive a basic grant for sanitation.

To qualify for a performance grant, it may also need to:

- Increase waste-collection coverage.
- Improve its own revenue.
- Publish accounts.
- Complete audit.
- Maintain asset registers.
- Use the funds properly.
- Achieve a defined service target.

***

## 11. Main 16th Finance Commission Performance Metrics

The 16th Finance Commission framework emphasises:

- Own-source revenue growth.
- State transfers to local bodies.
- Public disclosure of accounts.
- Audited accounts.
- Regular constitution of local bodies.
- Timely constitution of State Finance Commissions.
- Basic service improvements.
- Sanitation and water management.
- Financial accountability.

The exact operational requirements should be checked against the final Finance Commission report, Ministry of Finance guidelines and annual release orders.

***

## 12. Local-Body Performance: Rural Criteria

For Rural Local Bodies, including Gram Panchayats and Zilla Panchayats, the performance criterion described in Sikkim’s Medium-Term Fiscal Plan is:

> In year \(T-1\), own-source revenue should be at least 1.025 times the own-source revenue in year \(T-2\), or achieve 2.5 per cent annual compounded growth over the 2025–26 base-year OSR, whichever is lower, subject to a minimum of ₹1,200 per household per year. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/FRBM/Documents/2026-27/MTFP%202026-27.pdf)

### Formula

For a Rural Local Body:

$$\text{Required OSR}_{T-1} = \max \left[ 1.025 \times \text{OSR}_{T-2}, \; \text{Base OSR}_{2025-26} \times (1.025)^n \right]$$

subject to the applicable minimum household amount.

Here:

- \(T\) = year in which the performance grant is received.
- \(T-1\) = immediately preceding year.
- \(T-2\) = year before that.
- \(n\) = number of years after the 2025–26 base year.

### Simple explanation

A Panchayat or Zilla Panchayat must show continuous improvement in its own revenue.

***

## 13. Rural Example

Suppose a Gram Panchayat has:

- OSR in 2024–25 = ₹1,00,000.
- OSR in 2025–26 = ₹1,10,000.

For the next qualifying year, 2.5 per cent growth on the previous OSR would be:

$$₹1,10,000 \times 1.025=₹1,12,750$$

If the alternative base-year calculation produces a higher threshold, the lower applicable threshold may be used, subject to the minimum household requirement.

### Important caution

The local body must verify:

- What qualifies as OSR.
- Whether grants are excluded.
- Whether shared taxes are included.
- Whether user charges are included.
- Whether revenue is actually collected or merely assessed.
- Whether the figures match audited accounts.

***

## 14. Minimum ₹1,200 Per Household Requirement

The Sikkim Medium-Term Fiscal Plan describes a minimum requirement of **₹1,200 per household per year** for Rural Local Bodies under the performance criterion. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/FRBM/Documents/2026-27/MTFP%202026-27.pdf)

This is important because percentage growth alone may produce a very small amount for a Panchayat with a low revenue base.

### Example

If a Panchayat has 500 households:

$$500 \times ₹1,200=₹6,00,000$$

The Panchayat may therefore need to achieve or demonstrate an applicable minimum OSR level of ₹6 lakh per year, subject to the final rules and eligibility calculation.

### Practical question

A Panchayat must determine:

- Number of households recognised for the calculation.
- Whether the figure uses official population or household data.
- Whether the minimum applies uniformly to Gram Panchayats and Zilla Panchayats.
- How shared revenues are treated.

The final implementation instructions should be consulted before applying the formula.

***

## 15. Local-Body Performance: Urban Criteria

For Urban Local Bodies, the Sikkim Medium-Term Fiscal Plan describes the performance criterion as:

> In year \(T-1\), the ULB should raise at least 1.05 times its OSR in year \(T-2\), or achieve 5 per cent annual compounded growth over the 2025–26 OSR base, whichever is lower. [sikkimfred.gov](http://www.sikkimfred.gov.in/FRBM/Documents/2026-27/MTFP%202026-27.pdf)

### Formula

For an Urban Local Body:

$$\text{Required OSR}_{T-1}=\min\left[1.05 \times \text{OSR}_{T-2},\;\text{Base OSR}_{2025-26} \times (1.05)^n\right]$$

### Simple explanation

Municipalities are expected to increase their own revenue by approximately 5 per cent annually, subject to the applicable formula and final guidelines.

***

## 16. Urban Example

Suppose a Municipality has:

- OSR in 2024–25 = ₹20 lakh.
- OSR in 2025–26 = ₹22 lakh.

A 5 per cent increase over ₹22 lakh would be:

$$₹22,00,000 \times 1.05=₹23,10,000$$

The ULB should therefore aim to collect at least the required threshold under the applicable Finance Commission formula.

### Sources of urban OSR

- Property tax.
- Trade licence.
- Parking fees.
- Building-plan fees.
- Waste-management charges.
- Water charges.
- Market fees.
- Rent.
- Advertisement fees.
- User charges.
- Fines and penalties.

# Part V: Basic Grant Conditions

## 17. Basic Grant Is Not Completely Unconditional

Although the basic component is less performance-oriented than the performance component, the State and local bodies must still fulfil entry-level conditions.

The Finance Commission framework requires:

- Legally constituted local bodies.
- Public accounts.
- Audited accounts.
- Timely State Finance Commission.
- Compliance with release conditions.
- Proper use of previous grants.

 [prsindia](https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/report-of-the-16th-finance-commission-for-2026-31)

### Simple explanation

Basic funding is not a reward for high performance, but it still requires minimum accountability.

***

## 18. Tied and Untied Basic Grants

The 16th Finance Commission framework provides for basic grants with tied and untied portions.

The broad structure reported is:

- 50 per cent tied for sanitation, solid waste management and water management.
- 50 per cent untied for local priorities, subject to applicable rules.

 [visionias](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/monthly-magazine/2026-03-31/polity-and-governance/recommendations-of-16th-finance-commission)

### Tied grants

Tied grants must be spent on specified areas such as:

- Water.
- Sanitation.
- Solid waste.
- Wastewater.
- Hygiene.
- Water conservation.

### Untied grants

Untied grants provide more flexibility for local priorities, but they cannot be treated as personal or unrestricted funds.

They must still be:

- Budgeted.
- Approved.
- Recorded.
- Properly procured.
- Audited.
- Reported.

# Part VII: Metrics for Sikkim

## 22. Revenue Metrics

### Rural OSR growth

$$\text{Rural OSR Growth} = \frac{\text{Current OSR} - \text{Previous OSR}}{\text{Previous OSR}} \times 100$$

Target: approximately 2.5 per cent annual growth under the described framework, subject to the final formula.

### Urban OSR growth

$$\text{Urban OSR Growth}=\frac{\text{Current OSR} - \text{Previous OSR}}{\text{Previous OSR}}\times 100$$

Target: approximately 5 per cent annual growth under the described framework.

### Collection efficiency

$$\text{Collection Efficiency}=\frac{\text{Actual Collection}}{\text{Total Demand}}\times 100$$

### Per-household revenue

$$\text{OSR per Household}=\frac{\text{Total OSR}}{\text{Number of Households}}$$

### Arrear recovery rate

$$\text{Arrear Recovery Rate}=\frac{\text{Arrears Recovered}}{\text{Opening Arrears}}\times 100$$

***

|---|---|
| Timely provisional accounts | Accounts published within prescribed period |
| Timely audited accounts | Audited statements published within prescribed period |

# Part VIII: Audit Metrics

## 26. Audit Timeliness

For each local body, track:

- Date accounts closed.
- Date accounts submitted.
- Date audit started.
- Date audit completed.
- Date report issued.
- Date reply received.
- Date objection settled.

### Audit completion rate

$$\text{Audit Completion Rate}=\frac{\text{Audits Completed Within Time}}{\text{Audits Due}}\times 100$$

### Report issue rate

$$\text{Report Timeliness}=\frac{\text{Reports Issued Within Prescribed Period}}{\text{Reports Due}}\times 100$$

***

## 27. Audit Follow-Up Metrics

### Objection settlement

$$\text{Objection Settlement Rate}=\frac{\text{Objections Settled}}{\text{Total Outstanding Objections}}\times 100$$

### Recovery rate

$$\text{Recovery Rate}=\frac{\text{Amount Recovered}}{\text{Amount Certified for Recovery}}\times 100$$

### Repeat-objection rate



------------

$$\text{Repeat Objection Rate}=\frac{\text{Repeat Irregularities}}{\text{Total Irregularities}}\times 100$$

A high repeat-objection rate means that the local body is not correcting the underlying control weakness.

***

A score of 100 per cent means that all required documents have been published, subject to accuracy and accessibility.

# Part XI: Risks and Safeguards

## 41. Risk of Artificial Revenue Growth

A local body may attempt to show higher OSR by:

- Reclassifying grants as revenue.
- Recording arrears as collections.
- Including deposits.
- Raising unrealistic demands.
- Recording receipts without bank deposit.
- Making temporary book adjustments.

### Safeguards

- Reconcile OSR with bank statements.
- Exclude grants and deposits.
- Verify receipts.
- Check actual collection.
- Compare with audited accounts.
- Examine year-end spikes.
- Confirm revenue with source registers.

***

## 42. Risk of Collection Pressure

Revenue growth should not harm vulnerable citizens.

For example:

- Sudden fees may exclude poor households.
- Aggressive recovery may affect essential services.
- Water charges may need lifeline provisions.
- Sanitation charges should be socially appropriate.

### Safeguards

- Transparent fee policy.
- Exemptions for eligible vulnerable groups.
- Public consultation.
- Grievance mechanism.
- Service improvement linked to charges.
- Periodic review of affordability.

***

## 43. Risk of Spending Only to Qualify

A local body may increase spending or collect revenue without improving services.

### Example

A municipality buys equipment to show capital expenditure, but the equipment remains unused.

### Safeguards

- Measure outputs and outcomes.
- Verify asset functionality.
- Link funding to service indicators.
- Review maintenance.
- Conduct field inspection.
- Publish use and benefit information.

***

## 44. Risk of False Certification

False certificates may relate to:

- Audited accounts.
- Utilisation certificates.
- OSR.
- Asset existence.
- Service delivery.
- Beneficiary coverage.

### Safeguards

- Multiple-level certification.
- Digital evidence.
- Independent audit.
- Random verification.
- Geo-tagged photographs.
- Citizen verification.
- Penalties for deliberate misreporting.

To assess whether the State and local bodies of Sikkim complied with the eligibility conditions for 16th Finance Commission performance grants and whether reported own-source revenue and service achievements were accurate, complete and supported by evidence.

# Part XVI: Simple Explanation of the Entire Framework

## 53. What Must Sikkim Do?

In very simple terms, Sikkim must complete five tasks.

### Task 1: Keep local bodies legally valid

Panchayats and Municipalities must be properly constituted and elected.

### Task 2: Publish accounts

Local bodies must prepare and publish provisional and audited accounts.

### Task 3: Maintain a working State Finance Commission

The State must constitute the SFC on time and act on its recommendations.

### Task 4: Increase own revenue

Panchayats, Zilla Panchayats and Municipalities must improve their own-source revenue according to the applicable Finance Commission formula.

### Task 5: Prove results

The State and local bodies must show that grants support real services, functioning assets and better outcomes.

***

## 54. What Does This Mean for a Panchayat?

A Gram Panchayat should:

- Maintain complete accounts.
- Publish financial statements.
- Improve legal own revenue.
- Reconcile cash and bank.
- Track grants.
- Maintain assets.
- Hold Gram Sabha meetings.
- Respond to audit.
- Measure water and sanitation results.
- Keep evidence for every reported achievement.

***

## 55. What Does This Mean for a Municipality?

A Municipality should:

- Update property and trade records.
- Improve tax and fee collection.
- Publish accounts.
- Maintain asset registers.
- Improve waste and water services.
- Track complaints.
- Complete audit.
- Settle objections.
- Demonstrate functional infrastructure.
- Use grant funds according to conditions.

***

# Conclusion

The 16th Finance Commission introduces a stronger connection between local-government finance and institutional performance.

For Sikkim, performance-grant success will depend on three kinds of improvement:

1. **Financial improvement** — increase own-source revenue and reduce leakage.
2. **Institutional improvement** — maintain accounts, audit, disclosure and SFC compliance.
3. **Service improvement** — provide functional water, sanitation, waste, roads and public amenities.

The most important lesson is that performance grant should not be treated as a race to produce figures.

A local body should not merely show:

- Higher revenue.
- More expenditure.
- More certificates.
- More assets.

It should demonstrate:

- Genuine collection.
- Accurate accounts.
- Transparent reporting.
- Functional assets.
- Better services.
- Corrected audit findings.
- Real public benefit.

The practical formula is:

```text
Legal compliance
        +
Transparent accounts
        +
Verified own revenue
        +
Timely audit
        +
Better services
        =
Stronger performance-grant eligibility
```

For Sikkim, the best strategy is to create a unified State-level system in which the Finance Department, Rural Development Department, Urban Development Department, DLFA, State Finance Commission and local bodies use the same definitions, timelines, dashboards and evidence standards.

> A performance grant should reward not only the ability to raise money, but also the ability to manage it honestly and convert it into visible public services.
