# How DDOs in Sikkim Should Manage GST Audit and Record Retention

Drawing and Disbursing Officers (DDOs) are responsible for ensuring that public funds are drawn and disbursed exclusively for valid, authorised and fully documented claims.

In GST-related transactions, a DDO must examine more than the gross invoice amount. The DDO must verify:

- Supplier identity.
- GST registration validity.
- Classification and nature of goods or services.
- Applicable GST rate.
- Taxable value excluding taxes.
- GST-TDS under Section 51 of the CGST Act, where applicable.
- Income-tax TDS under Section 194C or other provisions, where applicable.
- Supporting contract, sanction and measurement records.
- Administrative approval and budget appropriation.
- Verified payment vouchers and banking disbursement trails.
- Statutory retention of records for departmental audit and tax scrutiny.

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 apply to State Government departments, offices, subordinate authorities and autonomous bodies under State administrative control unless separate approved financial rules govern. The Rules define a DDO as the officer authorised to draw bills and make payments on behalf of the State Government.

---

## 1. Role of the Drawing and Disbursing Officer

### 1.1 Financial responsibility

The DDO must ensure that:

- Bills are accurately prepared and categorised.
- Funds drawn are required for immediate disbursement.
- Expenditure remains strictly within available budget appropriation.
- Complete primary supporting documents are attached to the bill.
- Statutory tax deductions are computed and deducted.
- Payments are released exclusively to the rightful payee.
- Core statutory and accounting records are systematically maintained.
- Audit observations and queries are promptly settled.
- Overpayments or disallowed expenditures are recovered.

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 mandate that the drawing officer is responsible for observing rules relating to the preparation of bills, confirming the genuine requirement for funds and verifying that expenditure is covered by valid appropriation.

### 1.2 The complete verification chain

A DDO should not pass a bill merely because:

- The contractor or vendor submitted an invoice.
- A technical engineer recommended payment.
- A budget provision exists under the relevant head.
- A work order is on file.
- The supplier is known to the office.
- The monetary claim is minor.

The DDO must ensure that every link in the public payment chain is fully documented:

```text
Sanction
  ↓
Budget Appropriation
  ↓
Procurement or Contract Agreement
  ↓
Supply Delivery or Work Execution
  ↓
Technical Verification and Measurement
  ↓
Statutory Tax Invoice
  ↓
Tax Deductions (GST-TDS and Income-tax TDS)
  ↓
Bill Scrutiny and Voucher Passing
  ↓
Disbursement
  ↓
Audit Trail and Record Retention

```

### 1.3 Standards of financial propriety

Officers administering public funds must enforce strict standards of financial propriety under the Sikkim Financial Rules:

* Exercise the same vigilance and prudence as a person of ordinary prudence would exercise over personal funds.
* Incur expenditure only to the extent the occasion legitimately demands.
* Exercise financial powers strictly for public service without personal interest.
* Ensure that no section of the community or specific individual receives arbitrary preference.
* Conduct public procurements through open, fair and transparent methods.
* Evaluate comparative quality, technical suitability and commercial price.
* Record clear reasons on file for major procurement choices and variations.

## 2. Statutory GST Responsibilities of a DDO

### 2.1 Transaction classification

Before admitting any bill for payment, determine its exact statutory classification:

* Supply of goods.
* Supply of services.
* Works contract services.
* Pure labour contracts.
* Composite or mixed supplies.
* Professional or technical consultancies.
* Renting of immovable property.
* Hiring of passenger vehicles or goods transport.
* Routine repair and operational maintenance.
* Grants-in-aid or cost reimbursements.
* Inter-governmental transfers of funds.

### 2.2 Supplier verification

Check the statutory status of the payee:

* Regular GST-registered taxpayer.
* Composition taxpayer.
* Unregistered vendor.
* Government entity or local authority.
* Service provider subject to the reverse-charge mechanism (RCM).
* Supplier claiming a statutory exemption.

### 2.3 Portal validation of GSTIN

For every registered supplier:

1. Validate the GSTIN on the official GST portal.
2. Confirm that the legal trade name matches the supplier named in the work order.
3. Verify that the registration status was active on the date of supply.
4. Verify the registered business address.
5. Record screenshot evidence of portal verification in the contract file.

### 2.4 Separation of statutory identifiers

| Identifier | Identifies | Primary statutory purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier GSTIN | Registered supplier or contractor | Billed GST invoice and vendor tax filing |
| Deductor GSTIN | Government office or local body as deductor | GST-TDS deduction and filing Form GSTR-7 |
| Deductee PAN | Supplier or contractor | Direct tax identity and Income-tax TDS |
| Deductor TAN | Drawing and Disbursing Officer | Income-tax TDS deposit and quarterly returns |
| DDO Code | Government accounts office | State Treasury operations and budget accounting |
| Vendor Code | Supplier record in financial system | Electronic payment routing (PFMS / IFMS) |

> **Important:** Never use the contractor's GSTIN as the Government office's identifier. A GSTIN cannot substitute for a TAN when depositing and reporting income-tax deductions.

## 3. Bill-Scrutiny Process

Follow an eight-step bill-scrutiny workflow:

```text
Step 1: Receive and Register the Bill
  ↓
Step 2: Verify Sanction and Budget Authority
  ↓
Step 3: Classify the Supply
  ↓
Step 4: Verify the Tax Invoice
  ↓
Step 5: Verify Measurement and Physical Completion
  ↓
Step 6: Compute Statutory Deductions
  ↓
Step 7: Approve Payment and Issue Voucher
  ↓
Step 8: Deposit Taxes, File Returns and Retain Records

```

### Step 1: Receive and register the bill

Log the incoming bill in the office bill register:

* Bill number and invoice date.
* Supplier or contractor legal name.
* Contract, work order or purchase order reference.
* Claimed gross amount.
* Classification of supply.
* Date of receipt in the DDO section.
* Dealing assistant or accountant assigned.

### Step 2: Verify authority

Confirm statutory approvals:

* Prior administrative approval from the competent department.
* Formal financial sanction.
* Technical sanction from the designated engineering authority for works.
* Delegation of financial powers compliance.
* Available budget balance under the relevant budget head.
* Valid contract or work agreement.

> **Note:** The Sikkim Financial Rules state that mere provision in the approved budget estimates does not authorise expenditure; a prior specific expenditure sanction from the competent authority is mandatory.

### Step 3: Classify the supply

Examine whether the invoice represents goods, services, a works contract, pure labour, a composite supply, an exempt activity or an RCM procurement.

### Step 4: Verify the tax invoice

Check mandatory particulars on the invoice:

* Supplier legal name, address and GSTIN.
* Consecutive invoice serial number and date.
* Correct HSN code for goods or SAC code for services.
* Detailed description of items or scope of work.
* Taxable value excluding tax.
* Applicable GST rate (CGST, SGST or IGST).
* Calculated tax amounts.
* Place of supply with State code.
* Recipient office legal name and address.
* Work order or contract reference number.

### Step 5: Verify physical receipt or work execution

Attach primary field documentation:

* **For goods:** Signed Delivery Challan, Goods Receipt Note (GRN), inspection report and consumable or dead-stock register page numbers.
* **For works contracts:** Measurement Book (MB) pages certified by the site engineer, running account (RA) bill, physical inspection reports, laboratory test certificates and stage-completion certificates.
* **For services:** Attendance records, duty rosters, performance reports and user-department satisfaction certificates.

### Step 6: Determine deductions

Calculate statutory and contractual withholdings:

* GST-TDS under Section 51 of the CGST Act.
* Income-tax TDS under Section 194C or other applicable sections.
* Contractual security deposit or retention money.
* Labour welfare cess (typically 1% for construction works).
* Recovery of mobilisation or equipment advances.
* Liquidated damages or contractual penalties.

### Step 7: Approve and pay

The DDO verifies arithmetic calculations, records the net admissible amount in figures and words, signs the payment order on the bill and issues the treasury cheque or electronic bank advice.

### Step 8: Deposit and report taxes

Following payment release:

1. Remit GST-TDS through the GST portal.
2. File Form GSTR-7 by the 10th of the following month.
3. Issue Form GSTR-7A certificates to contractors.
4. Deposit income-tax TDS through authorized banks.
5. File quarterly TDS statements (Form 26Q).
6. Issue Form 16A certificates.

## 4. GST-TDS for Government Offices

### 4.1 Statutory authority

Section 51 of the CGST Act, 2017 mandates tax deduction at source by:

* Central Government departments.
* State Government departments.
* Local authorities (Gram Panchayat Units, Zilla Panchayats, Municipalities).
* Governmental agencies.
* Other notified public institutions and public-sector undertakings.

### 4.2 Contract threshold

GST-TDS applies when the total taxable value under a contract exceeds ₹2,50,000, excluding taxes (CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess) itemised on the invoice.

Threshold eligibility depends on the total contract value, not the amount of an individual running account bill.

### 4.3 Statutory rates

* **Intra-State supplies:** 1% CGST plus 1% SGST (Total: 2%).
* **Inter-State supplies:** 2% IGST.

### 4.4 Formula and calculation

GST-TDS is calculated on the taxable supply value, excluding all itemised GST charges:

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable GST-TDS Rate}$$

#### Example

A works contractor submits a bill for an intra-State civil work:

* Taxable value: ₹5,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹90,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹5,90,000

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹10,000$$

Disbursement net of GST-TDS before other deductions:

$$\text{Net Amount} = ₹5,90,000 - ₹10,000 = ₹5,80,000$$

### 4.5 Deductor registration requirements

Every public office liable to deduct GST-TDS must obtain registration as a tax deductor under Section 24(vi) of the CGST Act on the GST portal. The DDO must ensure that:

* A dedicated deductor GSTIN is active.
* The authorised signatory is correctly mapped.
* Form GSTR-7 is filed monthly.
* Form GSTR-7A is issued to deductees.
* A dedicated GST-TDS register is updated.

## 5. Income-Tax TDS for DDOs

### 5.1 Contractor payments under Section 194C

Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 applies to disbursements made to resident contractors for carrying out works, including labour supply for:

* Building construction and road works.
* Civil repair and maintenance.
* Waste collection and transport.
* Hiring of plant, equipment or vehicles.
* Dedicated catering contracts.

### 5.2 Standard rates and thresholds

* **1%:** When payment is made to an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) contractor.
* **2%:** When payment is made to other entities (partnership firms, companies, societies).
* **20%:** Under Section 206AA if the contractor fails to provide a verified PAN.

Deductions under Section 194C are required when a single payment exceeds ₹30,000, or when aggregate disbursements to the same contractor exceed ₹1,00,000 in a financial year.

### 5.3 Comparative compliance parameters

| Parameter | GST-TDS | Income-tax TDS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governing law | CGST, SGST and IGST Acts, 2017 | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Relevant section | Section 51 | Section 194C (Works) |
| Deductor identifier | GSTIN (Tax Deductor profile) | TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) |
| Payee identifier | GSTIN (if registered) | PAN (Mandatory) |
| Statutory return | Form GSTR-7 (Monthly) | Form 26Q (Quarterly) |
| Statutory certificate | Form GSTR-7A | Form 16A |
| Payee credit | Electronic Cash Ledger on GST portal | Form 26AS / AIS tax statement |

GST-TDS and income-tax TDS operate independently. One deduction does not substitute for or reduce the base of the other.

## 6. GST Rate Verification

### 6.1 Principles of rate verification

Do not determine GST rates solely from the supplier's billing claim. Verify:

* HSN code for goods against the Customs Tariff Act.
* SAC code for services under Chapter 99.
* Current CBIC rate notifications and amendments.
* Exemption notifications for public authorities.
* Reverse-charge applicability under Section 9(3) or 9(4).
* Official date of supply.

### 6.2 Common departmental procurements

| Supply category | Primary verification focus |
| --- | --- |
| Office stationery | HSN classification and applicable rates |
| Computers and IT hardware | HSN codes; distinguish bundled services from goods |
| Furniture and fixtures | Verified delivery and dead-stock register entries |
| Civil construction works | Works contract composite service classification (SAC 9954) |
| Road and bridge projects | Works contract classification and tender rate terms |
| Building maintenance | Distinguish immovable works contracts from simple repairs |
| Technical consultancy | Professional service classification under SAC 9983 |
| Security services | Service classification and potential reverse-charge liability |
| Office housekeeping | Distinguish pure administrative cleaning from local authority functions |
| Vehicle hiring | Distinguish rental of motor vehicle from passenger transport |
| Pure labour services | Specific scheme exemption conditions (e.g., housing schemes) |
| Conservancy and sanitation | Functions entrusted under Article 243G or 243W of the Constitution |

### 6.3 Standard works contract rates

Ordinary works contracts awarded by Government departments generally attract 18% GST (9% CGST + 9% SGST for intra-State supplies; 18% IGST for inter-State supplies).

Under CBIC Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate) as amended, concessional 12% rates previously applicable to certain public works were revised to 18% effective 18 July 2022. Verify current notifications for project-specific terms.

## 7. Managing Unregistered Suppliers

### 7.1 Legitimate non-registration

Procurement from an unregistered vendor is lawful if:

* The supplier's aggregate annual turnover falls below the statutory registration threshold.
* The goods or services are exempt from GST.
* Procurement complies with the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
* The vendor's PAN is documented.
* Applicable income-tax TDS is deducted.

### 7.2 Prohibition on charging tax

An unregistered vendor cannot charge, itemise or collect CGST, SGST or IGST. If an unregistered supplier includes GST on a bill:

1. Reject the bill and return it for correction.
2. Require an ordinary commercial bill without tax entries.
3. Obtain a formal declaration of unregistered status.
4. Verify the vendor's PAN.
5. Apply income-tax TDS under Section 194C if thresholds are met.

### 7.3 Unregistered vendor verification checklist

* [ ] Legal name, trade style and residential address confirmed.
* [ ] PAN obtained and authenticated.
* [ ] Signed declaration confirming non-registration under GST attached.
* [ ] Written reason for non-registration documented in the file.
* [ ] Confirmation that no GST was charged on the bill.
* [ ] Taxability and reverse-charge obligations verified.
* [ ] Income-tax TDS applied under Section 194C.
* [ ] Procurement procedures under the Sikkim Financial Rules confirmed.
* [ ] Verification of physical receipt or service delivery attached.
* [ ] Passing order recorded by the DDO.

### 8.1 Procurement and contract documentation

Retain:

* Notice Inviting Tender (NIT) and published bid documents.
* Bid opening registers and technical evaluation reports.
* Commercial comparative statements.
* Administrative approval and expenditure sanctions.
* Formal contract agreement.
* Letter of Acceptance (LoA) and detailed work order.

### 8.2 Invoice and delivery documentation

Retain:

* Original statutory tax invoices, revised invoices or bills of supply.

* Electronic Way Bills (E-way bills) where applicable.
* Itemised HSN/SAC classifications and rate notes.

Retain:

* Approved project estimates and bill of quantities (BOQ).

* Site inspection records and quality test certificates.
* Running account (RA) bills and final bills certified by the engineer.

### 8.4 Accounting, deduction and tax remittance records

Retain:

* DDO bill register entries.
* Paid vouchers and payment orders.
* Bank disbursement confirmations and treasury schedules.
* GST-TDS and income-tax TDS calculation sheets.
* GST-TDS challans and payment receipts.

* Generated Form GSTR-7A certificates.

* Quarterly Form 26Q return acknowledgements.
* Issued Form 16A certificates.

### 8.5 Exemption and reverse-charge records

Retain:

* Official CBIC exemption notification citations.
* Technical notes evaluating taxability.
* Signed supplier declarations.
* Analysis notes on reverse-charge obligations.
* Cash challans for RCM tax deposits.
* Opinions or instructions obtained from the Finance Department.

## 10. Statutory Record Retention Under GST Law

### 10.1 Retention period under Section 36

Section 36 of the CGST Act requires registered persons, including tax deductors, to maintain books of accounts and records until the expiry of 72 months from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the relevant financial year.

If records relate to an appeal, revision, investigation, adjudication or litigation, they must be retained until the later of:

* One year after the final disposal of the appeal, revision or proceeding.
* The standard 72-month period from the annual return due date.

### 10.2 Retention timeline calculation

The retention period runs from the statutory due date of the annual return, not the invoice or payment date.

#### Example

* Financial year: 2025–26
* Due date for annual return: 31 December 2026
* Statutory 72-month retention period: 6 years from 31 December 2026
* Minimum preservation deadline: 31 December 2032

### 10.3 Impact of unresolved proceedings

Do not destroy records at the end of the standard 72-month period if the transaction is subject to:

* Statutory audit objections by DLFA or CAG.
* Unsettled Public Accounts Committee (PAC) reviews.
* Ongoing tax department inquiries or notices.
* Arbitration or court proceedings.
* Police or anti-corruption investigations.
* Formal recovery proceedings against a contractor.

## 11. Sikkim Financial Rules and Record Retention

### 11.1 The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2025, governing State Government departments, subordinate offices and autonomous entities under State control.

### 11.2 Retention schedules

| Class of official record | Prescribed retention under Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 |
| --- | --- |
| Acquittance rolls | 3 years, or 1 year after completion of audit, whichever is later |
| Advance registers | Retained until recovery is fully completed and verified |
| Audit inspection reports and files | Preserved until all objections are settled by the Accountant General |
| Bill registers | 5 years, or 1 year after completion of audit, as specified |
| Pay bill registers | 35 years |
| Pay bills | 6 years, or 1 year after completion of audit, as specified |
| Cash receipt registers | Permanently |
| Receipt books (used counterfoils) | 3 years, or 1 year after completion of audit, as specified |
| Muster rolls for labour | 3 years, or 1 year after completion of audit, as specified |

### 11.3 Applying the longer retention period

Where statutory GST provisions and the Sikkim Financial Rules differ, DDOs must preserve the file for the longer applicable period.

#### Retention precedence rule

Retain records until the latest of:

1. 72 months from the due date of the GST annual return.
2. The period specified in the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
3. Settlement of all outstanding audit objections by the Accountant General.
4. Final legal disposal of any dispute, litigation or investigation.

Retain permanently or for extended administrative periods:

* Primary expenditure and sanction registers.

* Monthly bank scroll statements and treasury passbooks.

Retain:

Digital records must preserve:

* Source system of origin.
* Exact timestamp of creation and upload.
* Authenticated identity of the authorising officer.

## 16. Four-Way Reconciliation Framework

Reconcile four accounting records monthly:


## 17. Audit Questions for DDO Records

Auditors from the Directorate of Local Fund Audit and the Comptroller and Auditor General typically review:

### General financial authority

* Was expenditure backed by prior administrative and financial sanction?
* Did budget appropriation cover the expenditure?
* Was payment necessary at the time it was made?
* Were financial calculations arithmetically accurate?
* Was payment made directly to the authorized payee?
* Was the voucher supported by certified sub-vouchers?

### GST compliance

* Was the supply classified correctly as goods, services or a works contract?
* Was GST charged under the correct HSN or SAC code?
* Was the supplier's GSTIN active on the supply date?
* Were exemptions supported by specific statutory notifications?
* Were reverse-charge liabilities identified and paid?
* Was GST-TDS deducted on all contracts exceeding ₹2,50,000?
* Was the deductor GSTIN correctly quoted on all filings?

### Income-tax compliance

* Was Section 194C applied to all qualifying works and labour contracts?
* Were payments checked against single (₹30,000) and aggregate (₹1,00,000) thresholds?
* Was the contractor's PAN verified?
* Was the office TAN quoted on challans and quarterly returns?
* Was tax deducted at the correct rate (1% vs. 2%, or 20% without PAN)?
* Were quarterly Form 26Q statements filed on time?

### Record retention and audit trail

* Are primary vouchers, Measurement Books and delivery challans intact?
* Are electronic records stored with verifiable metadata?
* Have records been preserved for the longer applicable retention period?
* Have files under audit objection been held from destruction?
* Are secure digital backups maintained?

## 18. Common DDO Operational Deficiencies

* **Assuming Government procurements are tax-exempt:** Public status does not exempt transactions from GST without a specific notification.
* **Substituting identifiers:** Quoting the vendor's GSTIN as the office identifier, or using a GSTIN in place of a TAN.
* **Destroying records after an arbitrary five-year period:** GST records require preservation for 72 months from the annual return due date, and open audit queries require indefinite retention.
* **Retaining only summary vouchers:** Preserving payment vouchers without underlying contracts, Measurement Books, invoices and tax challans.
* **Accepting GST on unregistered bills:** Allowing vendors without an active GSTIN to levy and collect taxes.
* **Discarding cancelled vouchers:** Omitting cancelled bills from audit files, which obscures the payment trail.
* **Omitting statutory citations for exemptions:** Marking vouchers "GST Exempt" without recording the governing CBIC notification.
* **Failing to reconcile Form GSTR-7:** Assuming that filing a return confirms accuracy in the cashbook without a formal reconciliation.
* **Relying on verbal assurances:** Accepting oral explanations on technical or tax questions without written file notes.

## 19. Missing Records and Reconstruction Protocol

### 19.1 Immediate operational actions

When a document cannot be located:

1. Record the missing item in the file with date and context.
2. Direct a search across physical archives and digital storage.
3. Obtain certified duplicates of invoices, bank scrolls or treasury orders.
4. Require written explanations from dealing staff for missing records.
5. Notify the Head of Department if major contract records are missing.
6. Tag the voucher for audit follow-up and review related files.

### 19.2 Standard reconstruction declaration

Mark reconstructed files with a formal declaration:

```text
CERTIFICATE OF DOCUMENTARY RECONSTRUCTION

This reconstructed record was prepared on [Date] to replace the missing original.
Reconstruction was completed using the following primary sources:

- Certified bank scroll statements from [Bank Name].
- Certified duplicate invoice issued by M/s [Contractor Name].
- Duplicate Measurement Book entries certified by the Executive Engineer.
- Treasury payment vouchers and computerised token registers.
- Official filing receipts from the GST / Income Tax Portal.

Reason for non-availability of original: [Insert detailed explanation].

Reconstructed by: ________________________ (Dealing Accountant)
Verified and approved: ___________________ (Drawing and Disbursing Officer)
Official Seal: ___________________________

```

Never represent a reconstructed document as an original without this certification.

## 20. Legal and Audit Holds

### 20.1 Criteria for applying a hold

Immediately suspend the scheduled destruction of records upon:

* Receipt of an inspection report from DLFA or CAG with unresolved paras.
* Receipt of a tax inquiry, summons or notice from GST authorities or the Income Tax Department.
* Initiation of departmental proceedings or vigilance inquiries.
* Filing of a petition or appeal before judicial courts or tribunals.
* Commencement of police or anti-corruption investigations.
* Initiation of formal recovery proceedings against a contractor.
* Ongoing review by the Public Accounts Committee (PAC).

## 24. Sample Audit Observation

### Audit Observation: Incomplete GST Documentation and Missing Audit Trail

**Criteria:** Under Section 36 of the CGST Act, 2017, and the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025, the Drawing and Disbursing Officer must maintain complete records establishing the authority, physical receipt, tax treatment, payment and statutory reporting of public expenditure. Records must be preserved for the longer applicable retention period, and matters under audit review must be held until final clearance.

**Condition:** A test check of contractor works files revealed that while original invoices and payment vouchers were on record, the files lacked:

* Verification of contractor GSTIN on the GST common portal.
* Independent GST-TDS calculation worksheets under Section 51.
* Proof of tax remittance and Form GSTR-7 return filing acknowledgements.
* Copies of Form GSTR-7A certificates issued to contractors.
* Treasury challan references for income-tax TDS under Section 194C.
* Monthly reconciliation statements between bill registers and contractor ledgers.

**Effect:** Tax deductions and statutory reporting could not be verified from the vouchers, exposing the department to potential tax demands, interest liabilities and audit disallowances.

**Recommendation:** The DDO should:

1. Reconstruct missing documentation from GST portal records, bank scrolls and treasury schedules.
2. Establish a dedicated contract audit file for every major work.
3. Implement a monthly reconciliation between the bill register, tax registers and portal returns.
4. Maintain records for the longer applicable statutory retention period.

## 25. Practical Implementation Roadmap

### Phase 1: First 30 days

* Audit the office's tax registration status; ensure deductor GSTIN and TAN are active.
* Establish dedicated manual and digital GST-TDS and Income-tax TDS registers.
* Halt the practice of passing unregistered vendor bills that include GST charges.
* Implement the Pre-Payment Tax Scrutiny Sheet for all incoming bills.
* Create standardized contract audit files for all ongoing works.

### Phase 2: Within 90 days

* Verify and document the GSTIN of every recurring contractor on the GST portal.
* Complete monthly reconciliations across the bill register, cashbook and Form GSTR-7 filings.
* Reconcile income-tax TDS deductions with Form 26Q quarterly statements.
* Establish an electronic filing system with standardized naming and directory structures.
* Implement maker-checker segregation of duties for bill passing and voucher approval.
* Set up a statutory tax calendar to track monthly and quarterly filing deadlines.

### Phase 3: Annual controls

* Conduct an annual review of GST and TDS compliance files.
* Verify record-retention dates against the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025.
* Audit a sample of passed vouchers across all procurement categories.
* Review transactions claimed as exempt or subject to reverse-charge rules.
* Review vendor master records for duplicate accounts or outdated information.
* Inspect the Record-Hold Register to ensure no files under audit inquiry are destroyed.
* Report material compliance weaknesses to the Head of Office.

## 26. Summary of DDO Control Responsibilities

A DDO in Sikkim manages GST audit and record retention through five core controls:

1. **Verify before payment:** Authenticate the vendor, contract terms, tax classification, invoice, physical delivery or work completion, and statutory deductions before releasing funds.
2. **Separate tax regimes:** Use the deductor GSTIN for GST-TDS and the office TAN for income-tax TDS. Compute and record both deductions independently.
3. **Preserve the audit trail:** Maintain sanctions, agreements, work orders, tax invoices, Measurement Books, calculation worksheets, vouchers, banking receipts and tax returns together in a structured dossier.
4. **Retain records for the longer period:** Comply with the 72-month requirement under Section 36 of the CGST Act or the schedules in the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025, applying whichever is longer. Suspend destruction for any record under active audit inquiry or legal proceeding.
5. **Reconcile monthly:** Reconcile the bill register, cashbook, departmental tax registers, banking records and portal filings every month.

The complete control chain:

```text
Valid Sanction Authority
          +
Verified Supplier Identity
          +
Correct Tax Classification
          +
Accurate Statutory Deductions
          +
Complete Payment Vouchers
          +
Timely Statutory Reporting
          +
Systematic Record Retention
          =
Audit-Ready DDO Operations

```

DDOs should assume that every major expenditure will be audited by the Directorate of Local Fund Audit, the Accountant General (Audit), the tax authorities or the Public Accounts Committee. The payment file must be self-contained, verifiable and complete.
