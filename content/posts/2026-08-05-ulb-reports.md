# Drafting Professional Local Fund Audit Reports for Urban Local Bodies in Sikkim

Statutory audit is one of the most important mechanisms for ensuring transparency, accountability, and sound financial management in local self-government institutions. In Sikkim, the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA), functioning under the Finance Department, conducts statutory audits of Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs) and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) in accordance with the **Sikkim Local Fund Audit Act, 2012**, the **Sikkim Local Fund Audit Rules, 2014**, and other applicable Government of India and Government of Sikkim financial rules.

This article demonstrates how audit observations can be drafted in a professional government style. It also explains how observations should be converted into actionable recommendations and followed up through an **Action Taken Report (ATR)** mechanism.

---

# Objectives of Local Fund Audit

The primary objectives of a statutory audit are to:

* Verify correctness of accounts.
* Examine compliance with Acts, Rules, Government Orders and financial procedures.
* Assess the adequacy of internal controls.
* Safeguard public money and public assets.
* Identify financial irregularities.
* Recommend corrective measures.
* Ensure accountability of public officials.

Unlike internal audits, statutory audits also examine compliance with legal provisions governing expenditure, revenue collection, procurement, contract management, and utilization of grants.

---

# Essential Structure of an Audit Report

A professional Local Fund Audit Report generally contains the following sections:

1. Executive Summary
2. Scope of Audit
3. Audit Methodology
4. Audit Observations
5. Recommendations
6. Action Taken Report (ATR) Instructions
7. Conclusion
8. Acknowledgement

Each observation should be concise, evidence-based, and objective.

A recommended structure is:

* **Observation:** What was found during verification.
* **Impact:** Why the finding matters and its financial/legal implication.
* **Recommendation:** How the issue should be corrected or mitigated.

# Case Study: Singtam Nagar Panchayat

During the audit of Singtam Nagar Panchayat, several deficiencies were identified relating to financial management, revenue administration, contract management, and utilization of Finance Commission grants.

The following sections explain each observation and why it is important.

---

# Observation 1: Delay in Chartered Accountant Reports

## Audit Observation

The Chartered Accountant reports for FY 2022-23 were found to be substantially delayed beyond the prescribed timeline. Under normal practice, annual accounts should be finalized and audited within one quarter after the close of the financial year.

The audit team also observed that Chartered Accountants were taking original Cash Books outside the office premises during the audit process.

## Why this is a concern

Delayed CA reports result in:

* delayed statutory audit,
* delayed preparation of annual accounts,
* delayed submission of utilization certificates,
* delayed compliance reporting.

Removal of original Cash Books from the office poses a serious risk because these are primary accounting records.

Possible consequences include:

* alteration of records,
* loss of documents,
* unauthorized modifications,
* weakened audit trail.

Original accounting records should remain in official custody at all times.

## Recommended corrective measures

The audit recommended:

* completion of annual accounts within the prescribed timeline,
* improved coordination with Chartered Accountants,
* prohibition on removal of original Cash Books,
* digital scanning of records where off-site examination is unavoidable,
* providing supervised access to records within the office.

---

# Observation 2: Weak Revenue Record Management

## Audit Observation

Revenue registers were not maintained in a systematic manner.

Revenue collections were not classified under individual heads such as:

* trade licence fees,
* parking fees,
* sanitation charges,
* garbage collection fees,
* rent,
* market fees.

No weekly or monthly consolidated statement of revenue collection was available.

Revenue collection staff maintained incomplete records, creating significant difficulties for the Accounts Section.

## Why this matters

Revenue is the primary source of income for Urban Local Bodies.

Poor documentation leads to:

* inaccurate financial statements,
* delayed account preparation,
* weak internal controls,
* difficulty in reconciliation,
* poor financial planning.

The Accounts Section spends considerable time reconstructing revenue data instead of focusing on financial reporting.

## Recommended corrective measures

The audit recommended:

* maintaining head-wise revenue registers,
* preparing monthly revenue statements,
* strengthening supervision over revenue collection,
* periodic reconciliation with the Cash Book,
* staff training in record maintenance,
* introducing computerized revenue management wherever feasible.

---

# Observation 3: Deficiencies in Car Parking and Bazaar Contracts

## Audit Observation

The audit identified two significant deficiencies.

### Absence of Security Deposit

The contract did not contain any clause requiring the contractor to furnish a Security Deposit.

This observation had also been raised in previous audit reports.

### Improper handling of Tender Deposit

The Tender Deposit Receipt (TDR) or Security Deposit was not maintained separately from the contract value.

## Why this matters

Security Deposits protect the municipality against:

* damage to public property,
* breach of contract,
* non-performance,
* unpaid dues.

Without a Security Deposit:

* financial recovery becomes difficult,
* contractors bear reduced financial responsibility,
* municipal interests remain unprotected.

Repeated failure to address earlier audit observations also indicates weak compliance mechanisms.

## Recommended corrective measures

The audit recommended:

* incorporating a mandatory Security Deposit clause,
* maintaining Tender Deposit separately,
* defining procedures for deduction against damages,
* periodic review of standard contract documents,
* fixing responsibility for repeated non-compliance.

---

# Observation 4: Low Reserve Price in Tender

## Audit Observation

The reserve (offset) price fixed for Car Parking and Bazaar contracts was found to be significantly low compared with the revenue potential.

Although marginal increases had been made over previous years, the increase was not commensurate with market conditions.

## Why this matters

A lower reserve price results in:

* reduced municipal revenue,
* loss of earning potential,
* lower return from public assets.

Public assets should generate optimum revenue while ensuring fair competition.

## Recommended corrective measures

The audit recommended:

* periodic market assessment,
* benchmarking against neighbouring municipalities,
* annual revision of reserve price,
* scientific determination of minimum bid values.

---

# Observation 5: Irregular Utilization of 15th Finance Commission Grants

## Audit Observation

The audit observed:

* execution of works without approved plans,
* absence of segregation between Tied and Untied Grants.

## Why this matters

Finance Commission Grants are governed by specific guidelines.

Failure to follow these guidelines may lead to:

* ineligible expenditure,
* non-compliance with Government instructions,
* adverse audit findings,
* recovery proceedings,
* delayed future grant releases.

## Recommended corrective measures

The Panchayat should:

* obtain approval before execution,
* maintain separate records for Tied and Untied Grants,
* preserve all sanctions and supporting documents,
* strengthen internal monitoring.

# Importance of the Action Taken Report (ATR)

An audit report is only effective if its observations are acted upon.

Accordingly, every Local Body should submit an Action Taken Report.

A standard ATR process includes:

1. Discussion of audit observations in the Board meeting.
2. Preparation of corrective action plan.
3. Assignment of responsibility.
4. Implementation.
5. Submission of ATR to the Directorate of Local Fund Audit.

# Principles for Writing Good Audit Observations

A professionally drafted audit observation should be:

* factual,
* supported by evidence,
* objective,
* concise,
* free from personal opinion,
* legally sustainable,
* accompanied by practical recommendations.

Avoid speculative language and ensure that every observation can be substantiated from records examined during audit.

---

# Good Practices for Urban Local Bodies

To improve financial governance, municipalities and Nagar Panchayats should:

* Finalize annual accounts promptly.
* Complete Chartered Accountant audits within prescribed timelines.
* Maintain original accounting records securely.
* Digitize financial records.
* Strengthen revenue monitoring.
* Conduct monthly bank reconciliations.
* Maintain proper asset registers.
* Review contract documents annually.
* Ensure compliance with Finance Commission guidelines.
* Submit ATRs within the prescribed period.

---

# Conclusion

The audit of Singtam Nagar Panchayat demonstrates how routine weaknesses in accounting, revenue administration, procurement, and grant management can affect the efficiency and accountability of a local body.

Many of these deficiencies are procedural rather than structural and can be corrected through stronger internal controls, timely supervision, improved documentation, and strict adherence to statutory provisions.

An effective statutory audit is not merely an exercise in identifying irregularities. Its true purpose is to strengthen governance, promote financial discipline, safeguard public resources, and support local institutions in delivering better public services. The Action Taken Report (ATR) serves as the final and most important stage of the audit cycle, transforming audit observations into measurable improvements in administration and financial management.
