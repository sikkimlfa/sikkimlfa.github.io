# DDOs in Sikkim Government: Checks and Balances for Supply and Works Bills

A Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO) is responsible for more than signing a payment bill. The DDO must ensure that expenditure is authorised, necessary, correctly classified, within budget, supported by evidence, properly taxed and safely recorded.

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 apply to State Government departments, offices, subordinate authorities and autonomous bodies under State administrative control, unless separate approved financial rules apply. The Rules define a DDO as an officer authorised to draw bills and make payments on behalf of the State Government.

A reliable DDO system uses checks and balances:

```text
Need
  ↓
Sanction
  ↓
Budget
  ↓
Procurement
  ↓
Receipt or work completion
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Tax and statutory deductions
  ↓
Bill preparation
  ↓
DDO scrutiny
  ↓
Payment
  ↓
Accounting
  ↓
Audit and follow-up

```

This guide explains each operational stage and provides checklists for supply bills and works bills.

> **Important:** Use these checklists alongside the latest Sikkim Financial Rules, Finance Department orders, Treasury instructions, procurement rules, GST provisions, income-tax requirements, technical manuals and departmental accounting procedures.

---

## 1. Meaning of Checks and Balances

### 1.1 What are checks and balances?

Checks and balances are internal financial controls designed to prevent any single individual from independently:

* Selecting a supplier.
* Approving a purchase.
* Certifying receipt or measurement.
* Preparing the payment bill.
* Authorising payment.
* Reconciling the financial accounts.

Segregation of duties ensures duties are divided among different officers and operational sections.

For example, a storekeeper should never simultaneously select the vendor, receive the consignments, post entries in the stock register, prepare the financial voucher and approve payment.

| Activity | Responsible Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Need identification | Indenting or user section |
| Procurement | Procurement committee or authorised purchasing officer |
| Receipt of goods | Storekeeper or receiving officer |
| Quality verification | Technical or inspection officer |
| Bill preparation | Accounts section |
| Financial approval | Competent authority holding delegated financial powers |
| Payment authorisation | Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO) |
| Reconciliation | Accounts officer or independent review officer |
| Audit review | Internal audit cell, Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) or CAG |

### 1.2 Purpose of internal controls

Segregation of duties protects public expenditure against:

* Fictitious and forged bills.
* Duplicate disbursements.
* Arithmetic and clerical overpayments.
* Unauthorised commitments.
* Inflated quantities and non-existent inventory.
* Substandard supplies.
* Premature disbursements for incomplete civil works.
* Diversion or lapse of statutory grants.
* Omission or under-deduction of statutory taxes.
* Manipulation of official books of account.

## 2. Legal Responsibilities of a DDO

### 2.1 Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025

The Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2025. Under these rules, the DDO is legally responsible for:

* Strict observance of rules governing the preparation and submission of bills.
* Ensuring that funds drawn are required for immediate disbursement or represent recoupment of an authorised permanent advance.
* Ensuring that total expenditure remains strictly within the sanctioned budget appropriation.
* Reporting and regularising anticipated or actual budgetary excesses before releasing funds.

### 2.2 Prior sanction requirements

Under the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025:

* No expenditure or liability may be incurred without a formal sanction from the competent authority.
* The mere existence of budget provision does not constitute authority to incur expenditure.
* A specific administrative and expenditure sanction must be on record prior to entering into contractual commitments.
* Expenditures must never be split into smaller packages to evade the scrutiny of a higher financial authority.

### 2.3 Principles of financial propriety

Every officer administering public funds must enforce strict financial propriety:

* Exercise the same standard of vigilance, prudence and care as an individual of ordinary prudence would exercise over personal funds.
* Incur expenditure only to the extent that the public occasion legitimately requires.
* Ensure that financial powers are not exercised for direct or indirect personal benefit.
* Avoid arbitrarily favouring any particular supplier or individual.
* Enforce fair, competitive and transparent procurement procedures.
* Balance technical quality, suitability and competitive pricing.
* Record formal, reasoned minutes for major procurement selections.

Selecting the lowest price alone is insufficient; the accepted offer must meet technical specifications, delivery terms and performance standards at a fair and reasonable price.

## 3. Three Lines of Responsibility

A robust financial management structure requires at least three independent lines of defense.

### 3.1 First line: User or technical section

The indenting or technical section determines the genuine requirement and verifies physical performance:

* Engineering divisions (roads, buildings, public health engineering).
* Central store sections.
* Health units and hospitals.
* Educational institutions.
* Rural development blocks and Gram Panchayat Units.
* Municipal service departments.

### 3.2 Second line: Accounts and controlling authority

The Accounts Officer and designated controlling authorities verify:

* Formal administrative and expenditure sanctions.
* Budget appropriation balances.
* Chart of accounts classification.
* Statutory tax assessments.
* Arithmetic accuracy.
* Supporting documents.
* Availability of committed resources.

Under the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025, Accounts Officers are responsible for the strict enforcement of financial discipline, and their formal advice must be obtained before significant expenditure is sanctioned.

### 3.3 Third line: DDO and audit

The DDO performs the final pre-payment check before drawing funds or authorising payments through the treasury. Subsequent post-payment reviews are conducted by internal inspection wings, the State Treasury, the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) and the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG).

## 4. Universal Bill-Processing Workflow

The lifecycle of a public expenditure transaction follows a chronological path:

```text
User Requirement & Need Assessment
      ↓
Administrative Approval
      ↓
Budget Verification & Resource Allocation
      ↓
Technical Sanction & Procurement Approval
      ↓
Tender, Quotation or Authorised Procurement Method
      ↓
Contract Agreement or Purchase Order
      ↓
Supply of Goods or Execution of Works
      ↓
Physical Receipt, Measurement Book Entry & Technical Inspection
      ↓
Vendor Tax Invoice Submission & Bill Preparation
      ↓
Accounts Scrutiny & Financial Vetting
      ↓
Statutory Tax Deductions (GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS, Labour Cess)
      ↓
DDO Passing & Payment Authorisation
      ↓
Treasury / Banking Disbursement
      ↓
SIFMS Ledger Posting & Cashbook Entry
      ↓
Monthly Three-Way Reconciliation
      ↓
Dossier Retention & Audit Readiness

```

A bill must never be passed out of sequence. A purchase order must precede delivery, physical measurements must precede bill preparation for works, and statutory tax determinations must precede net disbursement calculations.

## 6. Stage Two: Sanction and Budget

### 6.1 Statutory sanction chain

Depending on the procurement classification and threshold, the file must contain:

* Prior Administrative Approval (AA) from the competent administrative department.
* Formal Financial Sanction (FS) from the designated authority.
* Technical Sanction (TS) from the competent engineering authority for capital works.
* Formal Expenditure Sanction.
* Program or scheme-specific approval.
* Concurrence of the Finance Department, where required by financial delegations.

### 6.2 Budget validation

The DDO must examine:

* Grant number.
* Major head (4 digits).
* Sub-major head (2 digits).
* Minor head (3 digits).
* Detailed head (2 digits).
* Object head (2 digits).
* Scheme code (Central, State or Centrally Sponsored).
* Net available balance under the head.
* Unadjusted commitments and previous payments.

### 6.3 SIFMS and PRANALI integration

The Sikkim Integrated Financial Management System (SIFMS) provides an integrated digital framework linking budget appropriation, revenue receipts, expenditure sanctions, treasury operations, scheme tracking, DDO codes and departmental accounts.

Where a 15-digit expenditure budget head is linked to multiple funding schemes, DDOs must state the specific scheme codes and respective monetary allocations on the bill.

The DDO must ensure:

* The correct DDO code mapped to the appropriate treasury is used.
* The correct expenditure accounting classification is cited.
* Specific funding scheme codes are documented.
* Financial commitments are booked in SIFMS prior to passing vouchers.

---

## 7. Stage Three: Procurement

### 7.1 Procurement control procedures

Public procurement must comply with the following controls:

* Clear, generic technical specifications without proprietary bias.
* Transparent selection of procurement methods (open tender, limited tender or quotation).
* Adequate public notice and competitive bidding periods.
* Preparation and signing of technical and commercial comparative statements.
* Documentation of vendor eligibility and qualification criteria.
* Issue of formal contract agreements or purchase orders.
* Comprehensive delivery inspection, testing and stock entry.

### 7.2 Balanced evaluation

The lowest commercial bid ($L_1$) must not be accepted if it:

* Deviates from mandatory technical specifications.
* Offers deficient quality or inadequate warranties.
* Quotes an unrealistic delivery timeline.
* Excludes essential accessories or operational training.
* Carries unacceptably high lifecycle maintenance costs.

Procurement selections must demonstrate fair competition, reasonable pricing, proven quality and suitability for public service.

### 7.3 Procurement warning signs

* Splitting purchases into small lots to avoid higher administrative sanctions.
* Consecutive procurement awards to the same supplier without competitive justification.
* Quotations displaying matching typographical errors, handwriting or formatting.
* Purchase orders issued after physical delivery has occurred.
* Misuse of emergency procurement clauses for routine purchases.
* Missing comparative statements or unsealed bid files.
* Invoices passed without corresponding stock entries.
* Processing payments before formal quality inspections.
* Purchased equipment left idle or uninstalled in packaging.
* Undisclosed personal or commercial conflicts of interest between suppliers and approving officers.

## 13. Supply Bill: GST and Statutory Deductions

* [ ] Supply classified correctly as goods or services.
* [ ] Billed GST rate verified against statutory tariff schedules.
* [ ] GST calculated on net taxable value.
* [ ] Total contract value examined to test the ₹2,50,000 threshold under Section 51.
* [ ] Deductor GSTIN recorded on the voucher.
* [ ] Income-tax TDS evaluated under Section 194C or other applicable sections.
* [ ] Deductee PAN authenticated.
* [ ] Office TAN recorded on the voucher.
* [ ] Net payable amount calculated and verified.

### Formula and calculation

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable Rate}$$

#### Example

A registered local supplier delivers office computers:

* Taxable supply value: ₹3,00,000
* Applicable GST rate: 18% (Intra-State: 9% CGST + 9% SGST)
* GST charged: ₹54,000
* Gross invoice total: ₹3,54,000

Because the taxable contract value exceeds ₹2,50,000, GST-TDS applies:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹3,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹3,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹3,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹3,000$$

$$\text{Total GST-TDS Withheld} = ₹3,000 + ₹3,000 = ₹6,000$$

## 19. Works Bill: Measurement and Progress

* [ ] Standard Measurement Book (MB) maintained by the site engineer.
* [ ] Measurements recorded on-site and dated.
* [ ] Contractor signature acknowledging measurements obtained.
* [ ] Invoiced quantities match certified MB entries.
* [ ] Cumulative quantities checked against sanctioned BOQ limits.
* [ ] Senior supervisory engineers completed mandatory percentage checks.
* [ ] Laboratory quality test reports (e.g., concrete cube strength, soil compaction) attached.
* [ ] Date-stamped, geo-tagged site photographs filed.
* [ ] Cumulative disbursement ceiling verified.

$$Cumulative\text{ }Payment = Previous\text{ }Disbursements + Current\text{ }Claim$$

The cumulative gross payment must not exceed:

* The sanctioned contract value (plus approved variations).
* The revised administrative and technical sanctions.
* The value of work physically measured and certified on-site.

## 21. Works Bill: Material Control and Reconciliation

* [ ] Sources of structural materials verified.
* [ ] Manufacturer test certificates for cement, structural steel and bitumen attached.
* [ ] Departmental material issues recorded in store issue registers.
* [ ] Material consumption reconciled against theoretical engineering requirements.

## 22. Works Bill: GST and TDS

* [ ] Classification confirmed as a works contract service (SAC 9954).
* [ ] Applicable GST rate verified (standard 18% for works contracts).
* [ ] Taxable base value confirmed from engineer's measurements.
* [ ] Contractor GSTIN and PAN authenticated.
* [ ] GST-TDS applied if contract value exceeds ₹2,50,000.
* [ ] Income-tax TDS applied under Section 194C.
* [ ] Security deposit or retention money deducted per contract terms.
* [ ] Labour welfare cess (typically 1%) deducted where applicable.
* [ ] Deductor GSTIN and TAN recorded on the voucher.

#### Example

A running works bill for a municipal infrastructure contract:

* Taxable civil work executed: ₹5,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹90,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹5,90,000

The statutory deductions are:

$$CGST\text{ }TDS\text{ }(1\%) = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS\text{ }(1\%) = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Income\text{-}tax\text{ }TDS\text{ }(Section\text{ }194C,\text{ }1\%) = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$\text{Total Tax Deductions} = ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹15,000$$

$$\text{Net Payable (Before Retention and Cess)} = ₹5,90,000 - ₹15,000 = ₹5,75,000$$

# Part III: Core DDO Scrutiny Controls

## 25. Arithmetic Verification

The DDO and accounts wing must independently verify:

* Extensions of quantity multiplied by approved unit rates.
* Subtotals, carried-over sums and gross totals.
* Taxable value base computations.
* Independent calculations of GST, GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS and labour cess.
* Total recoveries deducted from gross claims.
* Remaining budget balance under the primary head.

$$\text{Net Payable} = \text{Gross Claim} - \text{GST-TDS} - \text{IT-TDS} - \text{Security Deposit} - \text{Advance Recoveries} - \text{Other Recoveries}$$

---

## 26. Authority Verification

Confirm that every signature on the voucher represents an authorised officer acting within delegated powers:

* Did the sanctioning authority hold powers for this expenditure category?
* Did capital values require higher administrative or Finance Department concurrence?
* Did the engineering authority hold delegated powers for the technical sanction?
* Is the DDO authorised to draw funds under this specific demand and treasury?
* Were countersignatures obtained where required by departmental rules?

A signature does not prove authority; the underlying statutory delegation must be verified.

---

## 27. Budget Appropriation Verification

Verify the following before issuing payment:

* Availability of uncommitted funds under the approved budget estimate.
* Sanctioned supplementary grants or formal re-appropriation orders.
* Specific funding distributions between Central and State shares.
* Prevention of unconstitutional budget excesses under Article 204.
* Departmental liabilities tracking to avoid deferred unpaid bills.

---

## 28. Value-for-Money and Propriety Controls

The DDO must assess financial propriety:

* Did the procurement follow open, competitive bidding?
* Were technical specifications neutral and free of brand bias?
* Was the purchase necessary for immediate public operations?
* Are the unit prices fair, reasonable and in line with prevailing market rates?
* Did the transaction observe the standards expected of an officer managing public funds?

---

## 29. Verification of Physical Receipt and Usability

Confirm physical delivery and operational usability before payment:

* **For supplies:** Confirm items were inspected, accepted and posted to stock registers.
* **For capital assets:** Verify equipment is installed, tested and assigned asset numbers.
* **For civil works:** Confirm measurements were taken on-site, test reports are satisfactory and the structure is functional.
* **For services:** Confirm services were delivered and certified by the user department.

---

## 30. Statutory Tax Checks

Verify:

* Active supplier GSTIN authenticated on the GST portal.
* Deductor GSTIN cited on the passing order.
* Contractor PAN verified against income-tax databases.
* Departmental TAN recorded for direct tax reporting.
* Correct GST rates applied to the invoice.
* GST-TDS calculated on the base value excluding taxes.
* Income-tax TDS deducted at statutory rates (Section 194C or 194J).
* Exemption citations supported by official CBIC notifications.
* Reverse-charge liabilities identified and remitted.
* Statutory remittance deadlines noted (10th of following month for GST-TDS; 7th for IT-TDS).

---

## 31. Document Integrity and Archiving

Ensure:

* All pages are numbered and securely bound in a voucher dossier.
* Sanctions, contracts, invoices, MB extracts and receipts are attached.
* Vouchers are stamped "PAID AND CANCELLED" to prevent reuse.
* Electronic postings are verified in SIFMS.
* Archival periods comply with the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025 and GST regulations.

# Part IV: Statutory Governance Framework

## 32. Responsibilities of the Drawing and Disbursing Officer

Under the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025, the DDO is personally responsible for:

* Ensuring bills are prepared in the prescribed statutory formats.
* Confirming that funds drawn are required for immediate disbursement.
* Maintaining total expenditure within available appropriations.
* Initiating timely requests for supplementary funds or re-appropriation.
* Adhering to treasury, accounting and tax withholding regulations.

## 33. Responsibilities of the Controlling Authority

The Controlling Authority (such as a Joint Secretary, Director or Chief Engineer) must:

* Review expenditure commitments for necessity, economy and propriety.
* Verify that unit rates are fair, reasonable and supported by market standards.
* Confirm that all prior sanctions are valid and documented.
* Monitor expenditure against quarterly budget allocations.
* Prevent rushed year-end spending to exhaust budget balances.

## 34. Responsibilities of the Head of Department

The Head of Department (HoD) must ensure that:

* Expenditure remains within the total departmental grant sanctioned by the State Legislature.
* Public funds are used exclusively for approved purposes.
* Internal control systems prevent financial irregularities, waste and fraud.
* Departmental liabilities are monitored and recorded.
* Audit observations from the DLFA and CAG are reviewed and settled promptly.

## 35. Responsibilities of the Accounts Officer

The designated Accounts Officer must:

* Advise the Head of Office on financial rules and accounting instructions.
* Examine proposals with financial implications prior to sanction.
* Verify accounting classifications, budget heads and SIFMS mapping.
* Identify financial irregularities or breaches of delegated powers.
* Refer unresolved financial disagreements to the Finance Department.

# Part V: SIFMS and Treasury Controls

## 36. SIFMS DDO Architecture

SIFMS assigns structured identifiers to control public transactions:

```text
SIFMS DDO Code Structure: [Demand Number] + [Treasury Code] + [DDO Serial Number]

```

When an administrative department operates across multiple district treasuries (e.g., Gangtok, Namchi, Gyalshing, Mangan, Soreng, Pakyong), the DDO must use the code mapped to the specific treasury processing the bill.

The DDO must ensure:

* The correct DDO code matching the processing treasury is used.
* The correct 15-digit budget expenditure head is cited.
* Specific funding scheme codes are documented.
* Financial commitments are booked in SIFMS prior to passing vouchers.

## 37. Multi-Scheme Accounting Heads

When a single expenditure head receives funding from multiple sources (such as State Budget, Centrally Sponsored Schemes, or North Eastern Council grants), the DDO must prepare a detailed expenditure statement showing:

* Formal scheme name.
* Unique SIFMS scheme code.
* Net expenditure allocated to the State share.
* Net expenditure allocated to the Central share.
* Supporting sanction orders for each funding stream.

## 38. Resource Controlling Officer Oversight

The Resource Controlling Officer (RCO) oversees resource requisitions, fund releases and allocations across salary, supply, capital works and scheme accounts. DDOs must not present bills to the treasury beyond the resources released by the RCO for that expenditure head.

# Part VIII: Red Flags in Financial Administration

## 47. Procurement Red Flags

* Multiple supply orders awarded to the same vendor within short periods.
* Different bidders submitting quotations with matching layouts, typefaces or errors.
* Procurement packages divided to keep values below competitive tender thresholds.
* Purchase orders issued after goods were already delivered.
* Significant procurements executed in late March without operational need.
* Vendor business addresses matching residential properties or non-existent sites.
* Bills passed without signed goods receipt notes or stock register entries.
* Payments released before mandatory technical inspections are completed.

## 48. Works Red Flags

* Measurement Books showing overwriting, erased figures or unauthenticated corrections.
* Measurements entered into the Measurement Book after the payment voucher was prepared.
* Identical site photographs attached to bills for different construction sites.
* Final payments released while structural work remains visibly incomplete.
* Billed quantities exceeding sanctioned technical estimates without approval.
* Large contracts split into small work packages to avoid higher technical sanctions.
* Unrecovered advances carried over across multiple running account bills.
* Structures remaining non-functional or abandoned after final payment.
* Completion certificates lacking signatures from the designated technical authority.

## 49. Tax Compliance Red Flags

* GST billed on invoices lacking a verified supplier GSTIN.
* Supplier GSTIN belonging to a business entity other than the contractor.
* GST-TDS computed on the gross invoice total including GST charges.
* Vouchers passed without citing the deductor GSTIN or office TAN.
* GST-TDS deducted from contractors but omitted from Form GSTR-7 returns.
* Income-tax TDS deducted without deposit challans or Form 26Q filings.
* Multiple separate contracts awarded just below the ₹2,50,000 GST-TDS threshold.
* Unregistered contractors charging and collecting GST on bills.
* Tax exemptions claimed on vouchers without citing statutory notifications.

## 50. Accounting Red Flags

* Paid vouchers lacking "PAID AND CANCELLED" stamps, risking reuse.
* Cashbooks updated weeks after payments were disbursed through the treasury.
* Bank reconciliation statements omitted or showing long-standing unreconciled items.
* Temporary advances carried forward for extended periods without adjustment.
* Unspent scheme balances recorded as final expenditure to prevent fund lapses.
* Abstract Contingent (AC) bills remaining unadjusted by Detailed Countersigned (DC) bills.
* Payments routed through incorrect DDO codes or unrelated budget heads.

The Comptroller and Auditor General's (CAG) State Finances Audit Report for Sikkim for the year ended 31 March 2023 highlighted administrative pendency in financial accountability records, identifying 947 outstanding Utilisation Certificates (UCs) amounting to ₹96.20 crore and 2,286 unadjusted Abstract Contingent (AC) bills amounting to ₹52.42 crore.

# Part IX: Monthly DDO Control Review

Maintain a structured monthly financial review:

## Monthly Financial Review Checklist

### 1. Budget and Appropriation Monitoring

* [ ] Progressive departmental expenditure reconciled against budget allocations.
* [ ] Outstanding contractual commitments and unpaid liabilities reviewed.
* [ ] Scheme-wise funding allocations tracked across State and Central shares.
* [ ] Budget savings and anticipated excesses identified.
* [ ] Re-appropriation or supplementary demand proposals submitted where needed.

### 2. Bill and Voucher Management

* [ ] All passed supply and works vouchers cross-checked against bill registers.
* [ ] Outstanding temporary advances and mobilisation funds reviewed.
* [ ] Pending, disputed or returned bills tracked.
* [ ] Vouchers verified as stamped "PAID AND CANCELLED".

### 3. Statutory Tax Reconciliation

* [ ] GST-TDS deductions reconciled and remitted by the 10th of the following month.
* [ ] Monthly Form GSTR-7 returns filed on the GST common portal.
* [ ] Form GSTR-7A deduction certificates made available to suppliers.
* [ ] Income-tax TDS remitted to the Central Government by the 7th of the following month.
* [ ] Quarterly Form 26Q return filings scheduled and reconciled.
* [ ] Three-way reconciliation completed (Bill Register $\longleftrightarrow$ Tax Registers $\longleftrightarrow$ Filed Returns).

### 4. Cash and Treasury Balances

* [ ] Departmental cashbook closed, totaled and signed by the DDO.
* [ ] Physical cash in the office safe counted and reconciled with the cashbook.
* [ ] Monthly bank reconciliation statements (BRS) completed.
* [ ] Unpresented cheques or delayed treasury scroll clearances investigated.

### 5. Records and Audit Readiness

* [ ] Paid vouchers and sub-vouchers bound and filed in sequential order.
* [ ] Measurement Books, stock registers and asset ledgers updated.
* [ ] Pending audit paras from the DLFA and CAG reviewed for corrective action.

# Part XI: Audit Trail and Record Retention

## 53. Document Retention for Supply Procurements

Retain in the payment voucher docket:

* Original user indent and need-assessment memorandum.
* Administrative approval and expenditure sanction orders.
* Budget availability certificate from SIFMS.
* Tender notice, quotations, bids and commercial comparative statements.
* Departmental purchase order and contract terms.
* Screenshot of portal-verified supplier GSTIN.
* Original vendor tax invoice.
* Delivery challan signed by the storekeeper.
* Goods Receipt Note (GRN) and inspection report.
* Stock register and fixed asset register extracts.
* Tax calculation sheets (GST-TDS and Income-tax TDS).
* Payment voucher, treasury schedule and bank advice.
* Proof of tax remittance and return filing.

## 54. Document Retention for Works Contracts

Retain in the permanent project file:

* Administrative Approval (AA) and financial sanctions.
* Detailed technical estimates, structural drawings and Technical Sanction (TS).
* Tender documents, bid evaluations and contract agreements.
* Formal work orders and site-handover records.
* Standard Measurement Books (MB) with dated entries.
* Laboratory test reports and site inspection logs.
* Running account bills and final bill statements.
* Material reconciliation statements.
* Physical completion certificate and asset handover documentation.
* Statutory tax deduction sheets, treasury challans and return receipts.
* Defect-liability period monitoring records.

## 55. Record Retention Schedules

Apply the longer retention period where rules overlap:

* **Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025:**
* Bill registers: 5 years, or 1 year after audit completion, as specified.
* Pay bill registers: 35 years.
* Cash receipt registers: Permanently.
* Audit inspection reports: Retained until all audit objections are cleared by the Accountant General.


* **GST Regulations (Section 36, CGST Act, 2017):**
* Retain records for at least 72 months from the due date of furnishing the annual return for the relevant year.


* **Ongoing Disputes and Audits:**
* Retain records until final disposal of any appeal, litigation, investigation or audit query, or the standard retention period, whichever is later.

## 57. Summary of DDO Control Responsibilities

The DDO ensures that public funds move through a controlled, transparent and documented process.

For supply bills, the DDO verifies: need, sanction, competitive procurement, vendor identity, tax invoice validity, physical delivery, stock registration, statutory tax withholding, payment authorization and accounting entries.

For works contracts, the DDO additionally verifies: administrative approval, technical sanction, engineering designs, tender selection, contract agreements, Measurement Book entries, quality test reports, physical progress, stage completion, asset registration, advance recoveries and final accounts.

The core financial control chain:

```text
No Sanction, No Contractual Liability
        ↓
No Budget Appropriation, No Expenditure Incurred
        ↓
No Physical Measurement or Delivery Receipt, No Payment Passed
        ↓
No Tax Scrutiny, No Net Payment Calculated
        ↓
No Supporting Vouchers, No Final Passing Order
        ↓
No Reconciliation, No Account Closure

```

A DDO must never rely on a single signature or an isolated certificate. Every payment of public funds requires independent, documented evidence across the administrative, technical, financial, tax and accounting stages.
