# Mandatory Tax Deductions from Government Payment Bills in Sikkim

There is no single universal deduction applicable to every Government payment bill in Sikkim. The correct deductions depend on the nature of the transaction, the contract, the supplier, the tax law, the work department and the applicable tender conditions.

For a typical contractor or supplier bill, the Drawing and Disbursing Officer (DDO) may need to examine:

* GST-TDS.
* Income-tax TDS.
* Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Cess.
* Security deposit or retention money.
* Recovery of advances.
* Cost of departmental materials.
* Royalty or mineral-related recovery.
* Labour or other statutory deductions.
* Penalties, damages or liquidated damages under the contract.

The DDO must distinguish between:

* Tax deductions.
* Welfare cess.
* Contractual recoveries.
* Recovery of advances or materials.
* Penalties and damages.

The Sikkim Public Works Manual states that statutory deductions for taxes and other levies should be made from contractor bills before payment and that the Engineer-in-Charge should verify such deductions before signing first, running, final, supply, advance or ad hoc payment bills.

> **Important:** The actual deduction must be based on the law, contract, tender document, work order and current Government instructions applicable on the payment date.

## 1. Main Categories of Deductions

### 1.1 Tax deductions

These may include:

* GST-TDS under Section 51 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 and corresponding Sikkim Goods and Services Tax (SGST) Act provisions.
* Income-tax TDS under Section 194C or another applicable provision of the Income-tax Act, 1961.
* TDS under provisions applicable to professional services, rent or other designated disbursements.

### 1.2 Welfare cess

For eligible building and other construction work, the Building and Other Construction Workers’ Welfare Cess applies. The commonly prescribed rate is 1% of the cost of construction, subject to statutory limits and rules. Sikkim Government directives specify that 1% welfare cess is collected from construction work and that the department deducts it before clearing bills.

### 1.3 Contractual deductions

These arise from the agreement or tender conditions and may include:

* Security deposit.
* Retention money.
* Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) adjustment.
* Recovery of mobilisation advance.
* Recovery of secured advance.
* Cost of departmental materials.
* Hire charges for plant and machinery.
* Liquidated damages.
* Compensation for damages.
* Penalty for delay.
* Recovery of excess payments.

### 1.4 Other statutory recoveries

Depending on the work, applicable law and Government instructions, these may include:

* Royalty for minor minerals.
* Mineral charges.
* Professional tax, where applicable.
* Labour-related statutory recoveries.
* Insurance or statutory social security contributions.
* Recoveries mandated under specific scheme guidelines.

## 2. Distinction: Tax Versus Contract Deduction

| Deduction | Nature | Main Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GST-TDS | Tax deduction | GST law |
| Income-tax TDS | Tax deduction | Income-tax Act, 1961 |
| Labour welfare cess | Statutory welfare levy | BOCW law and State procedures |
| Security deposit | Contractual retention | Agreement or tender conditions |
| Advance recovery | Recovery of previous payment | Contract and accounting records |
| Departmental material cost | Recovery of materials supplied | Work records and contract terms |
| Royalty | Statutory or mineral-related recovery | Mining rules and site conditions |
| Liquidated damages | Contractual compensation | Agreement and delay assessment |
| Penalty | Contract- or law-based recovery | Specific breach and competent sanction |

A DDO must not categorize every withholding as a tax. Certain amounts represent temporary performance retention, while others recover funds or physical materials previously disbursed by the Government.

## 3. GST-TDS

### 3.1 Who deducts GST-TDS?

GST-TDS applies to notified deductors under Section 51 of the CGST Act, including:

* State Government departments.
* Subordinate Government offices.
* Local authorities (Gram Panchayat Units, Zilla Panchayats, Municipalities and Municipal Corporations).
* Governmental agencies.
* Other notified public entities.

### 3.2 Threshold

GST-TDS applies where the total taxable value of supply under a contract exceeds ₹2,50,000, excluding taxes (CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess) itemized separately on the invoice. Threshold eligibility is tested against the contract value, not against individual running bills.

#### Example

A Municipality awards a taxable contract for ₹4,00,000 excluding GST. It receives three progressive bills:

* First bill: ₹1,50,000.
* Second bill: ₹1,50,000.
* Final bill: ₹1,00,000.

Although each interim bill is below ₹2,50,000, the underlying taxable contract value exceeds the statutory threshold. GST-TDS applies to all disbursements under the contract.

### 3.3 Statutory rates

* **Intra-State supply:** 1% CGST plus 1% SGST (Total: 2%).
* **Inter-State supply:** 2% IGST.

### 3.4 Calculation base

GST-TDS is calculated exclusively on the taxable base value excluding GST and cess shown on the invoice:

$$GST\text{ }TDS = \text{Taxable Value Excluding GST} \times \text{Applicable Rate}$$

#### Example

* Taxable value: ₹5,00,000
* GST (18%): ₹90,000
* Gross billed amount: ₹5,90,000

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹5,000$$

$$Total\text{ }GST\text{ }TDS = ₹5,000 + ₹5,000 = ₹10,000$$

### 3.5 DDO compliance checks

* Confirm that the department is a notified deductor.
* Verify that the deductor GSTIN is active.
* Authenticate the contractor's GSTIN on the portal.
* Verify the total contract value.
* Confirm that GST is excluded from the TDS base.
* Calculate CGST and SGST or IGST separately.
* Post entries in the GST-TDS register.
* Remit deducted taxes within statutory timeframes.
* File monthly returns in Form GSTR-7.
* Ensure generation of Form GSTR-7A certificates.
* Reconcile credits in vendor accounts.

## 4. Income-Tax TDS

### 4.1 Scope of contractor payments

Income-tax TDS commonly arises under Section 194C of the Income-tax Act, 1961 for payments to resident contractors for works and labour, including:

* Civil and infrastructural works.
* Road construction and maintenance.
* Building construction and structural repairs.
* Operational maintenance services.
* Supply of contract labour.
* Solid-waste management and cleaning contracts.
* Catering contracts.
* Transport and carriage contracts.

### 4.2 Statutory rates

* **1%:** When payment is made to an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) contractor.
* **2%:** When payment is made to other resident contractors (partnership firms, companies, societies).
* **20%:** Under Section 206AA if the contractor fails to furnish a verified Permanent Account Number (PAN).

### 4.3 Statutory thresholds

* Deduction is not required if a single bill does not exceed ₹30,000.
* Deduction applies once aggregate disbursements to the contractor exceed ₹1,00,000 within a financial year.

### 4.4 Identifiers and compliance

The DDO must verify:

* Contractor PAN.
* Departmental Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN).
* Applicable section code.
* Single and aggregate thresholds.
* Applied rate.
* Date of deduction and treasury deposit.
* Quarterly return filings in Form 26Q.
* Issuance of Form 16A certificates.

> **Note:** A GSTIN cannot substitute for a TAN in direct tax compliance.

## 5. Concurrent Deduction of GST-TDS and Income-Tax TDS

A single works bill may require simultaneous deductions under both tax regimes.

### Worked Example

* Taxable works value: ₹4,00,000
* Applicable GST (18%): ₹72,000
* Gross billed invoice: ₹4,72,000
* Contractor status: Individual (furnishing valid PAN)

#### Calculation of deductions

$$CGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$SGST\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$Income\text{-}tax\text{ }TDS = ₹4,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹4,000$$

$$Total\text{ }Tax\text{ }Deductions = ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹12,000$$

$$Net\text{ }Disbursement = ₹4,72,000 - ₹12,000 = ₹4,60,000$$

GST-TDS and income-tax TDS operate under separate statutes, identifiers, thresholds, returns and credit systems. Neither deduction reduces the assessment base of the other.

## 6. Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Cess

### 6.1 Statutory objective

The Building and Other Construction Workers’ Welfare Cess Act, 1996 finances social security and welfare measures for construction workers. The levy applies to:

* Public buildings and civil structures.
* Highways, roads, bridges and culverts.
* Flood-control, slope-protection and drainage projects.
* Potable water-supply and sewerage systems.
* Alterations, modifications and capital repairs covered by the statute.

### 6.2 Rate and calculation base

The levy is 1% of the cost of construction incurred by the employer, excluding land costs and specific statutory exemptions.

$$Labour\text{ }Welfare\text{ }Cess = Cost\text{ }of\text{ }Construction \times 1\%$$

#### Example

For an approved civil construction cost of ₹10,00,000:

$$Labour\text{ }Welfare\text{ }Cess = ₹10,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹10,000$$

### 6.3 Compliance considerations

The calculation base must be established in accordance with departmental rules:

* Verify whether the base includes or excludes itemized GST components.
* Confirm the approved contractual cost of construction.
* Exclude costs related to land acquisition or machinery installation where statutory rules permit.
* Account for the treatment of departmental materials.

The DDO must avoid automatically applying 1% to the gross invoice amount without verifying the statutory base.

### 6.4 DDO verification checklist

* [ ] Work falls within the scope of construction welfare legislation.
* [ ] Prescribed 1% cess rate verified.
* [ ] Approved cost-of-construction base confirmed.
* [ ] Deductions posted to departmental registers.
* [ ] Remittance challans and Labour Department receipts filed.
* [ ] Prior cess payments checked to prevent double deductions.

## 7. Security Deposit and Retention Money

### 7.1 Contractual nature

Security deposits represent a contractual withholding rather than a tax, protecting the Government against:

* Structural and quality defects.
* Incomplete work or site abandonment.
* Contractual delays.
* Breaches of contract terms.
* Maintenance failures during the defect-liability period.
* Unadjusted public dues or damages.

### 7.2 Standard Sikkim public works practice

Sikkim public works contracts and standard tender documents generally require security deposit deductions of 5% of the gross value of work done from running account bills. The Sikkim Public Works Manual also specifies security deposit deductions at 5% of the gross bill value.

$$Security\text{ }Deposit = Value\text{ }of\text{ }Work\text{ }Done \times 5\%$$

#### Example

For certified work valued at ₹8,00,000:

$$Security\text{ }Deposit = ₹8,00,000 \times 5\% = ₹40,000$$

### 7.3 DDO control checks

* Verify the specific percentage stipulated in the contract agreement.
* Confirm the correct valuation base.
* Account for initial Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) conversions.
* Check previous cumulative withholdings against contractual ceilings.
* Record deductions in the deposit register.
* Note the defect-liability period (DLP) and maintenance conditions.
* Prevent release of security retention prior to final technical clearance.

A security deposit is a temporary performance retention governed by agreement terms; it is not tax revenue or an administrative penalty.

## 8. Earnest Money Deposit Adjustment

### 8.1 Conversion process

Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) is submitted during bidding to guarantee tender validity. Standard Sikkim tender terms set EMD at 2.5% of the cost put to tender and permit converting the successful bidder's EMD into part of the initial security deposit.

### 8.2 DDO verification checks

* Authenticate original EMD receipts.
* Credit the successful bidder's EMD toward the required contract security deposit.
* Confirm that EMDs from unsuccessful bidders were formally released.
* Record transactions in the security deposit register.
* Prevent double deductions when calculating running bill retentions.

## 9. Recovery of Advances

### 9.1 Categories of advances

Contracts may provide for:

* Mobilisation advances for preliminary site work.
* Secured advances against non-perishable construction materials brought to site.
* Plant, equipment or machinery advances.
* Other contractual advances authorised by financial rules.

### 9.2 Recovery mechanism

The contract agreement must govern:

* The sanctioned advance amount and interest rates, where applicable.
* The approved purpose and required bank guarantees.
* The recovery schedule and percentage deduction per running bill.
* The milestone deadline for complete adjustment.

#### Example

* Outstanding mobilisation advance: ₹2,00,000
* Current running bill value: ₹8,00,000
* Prescribed recovery rate: 20% of gross work done

$$Advance\text{ }Recovery = ₹8,00,000 \times 20\% = ₹1,60,000$$

$$Unadjusted\text{ }Advance\text{ }Balance = ₹2,00,000 - ₹1,60,000 = ₹40,000$$

### 9.3 Audit lessons on advances

The Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) identified instances where interest-free mobilisation advances totaling ₹17.83 crore were disbursed across 34 road projects in Sikkim before sites were available, resulting in unrecovered funds, delays of 14 to 40 months, and an estimated interest loss of ₹4.95 crore.

A DDO must ensure that:

* Advances are disbursed only after site possession is certified.
* Contracts expressly provide for the advance.
* Equivalent bank guarantees remain valid.
* Scheduled recoveries are deducted systematically from each running bill.
* Written approval from the competent authority is on file.

## 10. Recovery of Departmental Materials

### 10.1 Procedural framework

When the Government issues construction materials from departmental stores (such as cement, structural steel, bitumen, pipes or tools), their value must be recovered from the contractor's running account bills in accordance with the Sikkim Public Works Manual.

### 10.2 Recovery calculation

$$Balance\text{ }Material\text{ }Recovery = Total\text{ }Value\text{ }Issued - Prior\text{ }Recoveries$$

#### Example

* Total materials issued: ₹1,20,000
* Amount recovered in previous bills: ₹80,000

$$Current\text{ }Material\text{ }Recovery = ₹1,20,000 - ₹80,000 = ₹40,000$$

### 10.3 DDO control checks

* Examine store issue notes and indents.
* Reconcile quantities with store ledgers and site consumption reports.
* Verify contractor signatures on receipt vouchers.
* Apply approved departmental issue rates.
* Update contractor running ledgers.
* Ensure full recovery before certifying the final bill.

## 11. Royalty and Mineral Charges

### 11.1 Nature of the levy

Royalty is payable on minor minerals extracted and used in public works, including stone, sand, gravel, boulders and ordinary earth. The deduction depends on:

* The source of the minerals (quarried vs. commercially purchased).
* The Sikkim Minor Mineral Concession Rules and prevailing rate schedules.
* Contractual liability clauses.
* Valid mining permits, transit passes and challans produced by the contractor.

### 11.2 DDO verification checks

* Identify the origin of materials used on-site.
* Check mineral transit passes, mining challans or supplier tax invoices.
* Deduct statutory royalty if the contractor cannot produce proof of payment.
* Credit deductions to the Mines and Geology Department revenue head.
* Reconcile deductions against Measurement Book quantities.

Royalty rates vary by mineral type and are subject to revision. The DDO should verify current schedules issued by the Mines and Geology Department, Government of Sikkim.

## 12. Contractual Penalties and Liquidated Damages

### 12.1 Grounds for recovery

Contracts may provide for financial deductions for:

* Project delays beyond scheduled milestone deadlines.
* Defective execution or failure to meet technical specifications.
* Failure to maintain completed works during the contract period.
* Damage to public property.
* Failure to deploy key technical staff or required machinery.

### 12.2 DDO control checks

Before enforcing penalties:

* Identify the specific contract clause governing default.
* Ensure technical inspection reports document the breach.
* Confirm that required formal notices were served on the contractor.
* Obtain approval from the competent administrative authority.
* Retain detailed calculation sheets on file.
* Update contractor ledgers and communicate deductions in writing.

#### Example

* Total liquidated damages assessed by the Superintending Engineer: ₹50,000
* Amount recovered in previous bills: ₹20,000

$$Balance\text{ }Recoverable = ₹50,000 - ₹20,000 = ₹30,000$$

## 13. Labour-Related Statutory Recoveries

Depending on the nature of the project and applicable rules, the DDO may need to examine:

* Labour welfare cess.
* Valid contractor registrations under the Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act and Contract Labour Act.
* Workmen compensation insurance coverage.
* Proof of Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) contributions.
* Proof of Employees' State Insurance (ESI) contributions, where applicable.
* Adherence to statutory minimum wage notifications.
* Verified wage distribution records.

Deductions must have a clear statutory, contractual or administrative basis; arbitrary withholdings are not permitted.

## 14. Professional Tax and Other State Levies

State levies, such as Professional Tax, apply based on:

* The nature of the payment or contract.
* The legal constitution of the vendor (individual, firm or corporate).
* Sikkim professional tax statutes and notifications.
* Departmental administrative circulars.

The DDO must verify:

* Applicability to the specific contractor or transaction type.
* Current tax rate schedules.
* Applicable exemption thresholds.
* Correct treasury accounting receipt heads.
* Statutory filing requirements.

Professional tax does not apply uniformly to all commercial bills; applicability must be evaluated for each transaction.

## 17. Standard Sequence of Deductions

The order of calculation must follow the contract terms and departmental accounting rules. The common sequence is:

```text
Gross Billed Value of Measured Work
        ↓
Add: Statutory GST (CGST/SGST or IGST)
        ↓
Less: GST-TDS (Calculated on taxable value excluding GST)
        ↓
Less: Income-tax TDS (Section 194C)
        ↓
Less: Labour Welfare Cess (1% on construction cost)
        ↓
Less: Contractual Security Deposit (5% on value of work done)
        ↓
Less: Recovery of Mobilisation and Secured Advances
        ↓
Less: Recovery of Departmental Material Costs
        ↓
Less: Mineral Royalty Deductions
        ↓
Less: Liquidated Damages or Other Contractual Penalties
        ↓
Net Payable Disbursement

```

Deduction bases vary: GST-TDS applies to taxable supply value, security deposit applies to the value of work done, labour cess applies to construction cost, and advances follow specific agreement schedules. The DDO must not apply a uniform base across all deductions.

## 18. Comprehensive Works Bill Calculation

### 18.1 Contract parameters

A contractor submits a running account bill for construction work:

* Value of certified work excluding GST: ₹10,00,000
* GST rate: 18%
* Security deposit: 5% of work value
* Labour welfare cess: 1% of construction cost base
* GST-TDS: 2% (Intra-State: 1% CGST + 1% SGST)
* Income-tax TDS: 1% (Sole proprietor with verified PAN)
* Scheduled mobilisation advance recovery: ₹1,00,000
* Departmental material recovery (cement/steel): ₹40,000
* Unpaid minor mineral royalty: ₹10,000

### 18.2 Gross invoice determination

$$GST = ₹10,00,000 \times 18\% = ₹1,80,000$$

$$\text{Gross Invoice Total} = ₹10,00,000 + ₹1,80,000 = ₹11,80,000$$

### 18.3 Deductions

#### GST-TDS (2%)

$$GST\text{ }TDS = ₹10,00,000 \times 2\% = ₹20,000$$

#### Income-tax TDS (1%)

$$Income\text{-}tax\text{ }TDS = ₹10,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹10,000$$

#### Labour welfare cess (1%)

$$Labour\text{ }Cess = ₹10,00,000 \times 1\% = ₹10,000$$

#### Security deposit (5%)

$$Security\text{ }Deposit = ₹10,00,000 \times 5\% = ₹50,000$$

### 18.4 Net payable calculation

$$\text{Total Deductions} = ₹20,000 + ₹10,000 + ₹10,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹40,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹2,40,000$$

$$Net\text{ }Payable = ₹11,80,000 - ₹2,40,000 = ₹9,40,000$$

## 19. Security Deposit: Audit Compliance

A Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) audit of public works in Sikkim identified a short deduction of security deposit where the contract required 5% retention from running bills, but the department applied lower rates. The short deduction totaled approximately ₹2.07 crore in that case.

To prevent short deductions, DDOs should monitor cumulative retention:

$$Security\text{ }Deposit\text{ }Variance = Required\text{ }Cumulative\text{ }Retention - Actual\text{ }Deductions\text{ }to\text{ }Date$$

DDOs must verify that cumulative security withholdings match contractual requirements across all running account bills.

## 20. Distinguishing GST from Labour Welfare Cess

| Feature | Goods and Services Tax (GST) | Labour Welfare Cess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statutory character | Value-added indirect tax | Welfare levy for construction labor |
| Governing law | CGST / SGST / IGST Acts, 2017 | BOCW Welfare Cess Act, 1996 |
| Standard rates | 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% | 1% of construction cost |
| Assessment base | Taxable supply value | Construction cost excluding land/exemptions |
| Billing presentation | Itemized by registered vendor | Recovered from bill by the DDO |
| Remittance channel | GST common portal | State Welfare Board account |
| Input tax credit | Available through GST portal | No input tax credit mechanism |
| Application scope | All taxable commercial transactions | Building and civil construction activities |

GST and labour welfare cess are distinct statutory obligations; one cannot substitute for or offset the other.

## 21. Reconciling Tender Clauses with Statutory Rules

Standard Sikkim tender notices often state that:

* Work values include applicable GST and statutory levies.
* Quoted tender rates cover all taxes, royalties and cesses.
* Contractors must furnish active GSTIN certificates and tax clearances.
* GST-TDS of 2% will be deducted from all payments, including advances.

Tender conditions must align with current statutes:

* A contractual clause cannot override statutory tax rules.
* GST-TDS must be computed on the taxable base value excluding GST, even if tender documents refer to tax-inclusive work values.
* DDOs must verify the tax rates in force on the date of supply.

## 24. Criteria for Returning or Holding Bills

The DDO should hold or return a bill for regularisation if:

* The supplier's GSTIN is invalid or inactive.
* GST is charged by an unregistered vendor.
* The contractor's PAN is missing.
* The underlying contract value cannot be established.
* Base values for GST-TDS cannot be verified.
* The office lacks a TAN for income-tax TDS reporting.
* Measured works lack Measurement Book entries or engineer certifications.
* Physical delivery of goods lacks Goods Receipt Notes or inspection reports.
* Contractual security deposits are omitted.
* Mandatory labour welfare cess is not assessed.
* Scheduled advance recoveries are excluded.
* Mining royalty challans are absent and deductions are omitted.
* The claimed amount exceeds the sanctioned financial estimate.
* A final bill lacks a physical completion certificate.
* The voucher represents a duplicate claim.
* The contract period expired without a formal extension.
* Expenditure sanction from the competent authority is missing.

If a bill is deficient:

1. Record specific deficiencies on a formal return memo.
2. Direct the billing unit or contractor to furnish required documentation.
3. Escalate legal or accounting doubts to the Finance Department.
4. Do not clear the bill until all deficiencies are regularised.

## 25. Common Deductions Deficiencies

* **Treating all deductions as taxes:** Security deposits and advance recoveries are contractual withholdings, not tax revenue.
* **Computing GST-TDS on gross invoice amounts:** Section 51 requires calculating GST-TDS on the base taxable value, excluding itemized taxes.
* **Applying flat income-tax rates without verifying PAN:** Failure to secure a PAN requires a 20% deduction under Section 206AA.
* **Overlooking contract terms:** Omitting mandatory deductions for security deposits, material issues or plant hire specified in the contract.
* **Double-deducting labour cess:** Deducting cess from bills when the contractor has already remitted it directly to the Welfare Board with verified receipts.
* **Releasing security deposits prematurely:** Refunding retention deposits before the defect-liability period ends or without a formal completion certificate.
* **Failing to track advances:** Omitting advance recovery instalments from interim running account bills.
* **Neglecting mineral royalty:** Passing bills for civil works involving stone, sand or earth without verifying mining challans or deducting royalty.
* **Passing final bills without completion records:** Disbursing final payments without certified MB entries, quality clearances and asset handover documents.
* **Omitting monthly ledger reconciliations:** Failing to reconcile bill-passing entries with treasury registers, challans and portal filings.

## 26. Documenting the Audit Trail

Auditors from the Directorate of Local Fund Audit (DLFA) and the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) trace transactions through a documented sequence:

```text
Contract Agreement & Sanction Clauses
        ↓
Governing Statutes & Notifications
        ↓
Verified Calculation Base
        ↓
Arithmetical Deduction Worksheets
        ↓
Voucher Passing Order & DDO Signature
        ↓
Treasury / Banking Disbursement
        ↓
Remittance Challans & Tax Returns
        ↓
Contractor Ledger & Register Reconciliation
        ↓
Archived Payment Dossier

```

To support this audit trail, the DDO must retain:

* Contract agreements, tenders and approved work orders.
* Measurement Books with engineer certifications.
* Vendor tax invoices and delivery challans.
* Deduction calculation worksheets.
* Deduction registers and contractor ledgers.
* Treasury payment vouchers and bank scrolls.
* Remittance challans for GST-TDS, Income-tax TDS, cess and royalties.
* Filed returns (Form GSTR-7 and Form 26Q).
* Generated certificates (Form GSTR-7A and Form 16A).
* Material issue registers and store ledgers.
* Advance registers and bank guarantees.
* Physical completion certificates.

Under Section 36 of the CGST Act and the Sikkim Financial Rules, 2025, records must be retained for at least 72 months from the annual return due date, or until all outstanding audit observations are formally cleared.

### 27.1 Short deduction of security deposit

**Criteria:** Under Clause [ ] of the contract agreement, security deposit was to be deducted at the rate of 5% of the gross value of work done from all running account bills.

**Condition:** For certified work valued at ₹[Amount], required cumulative security retention was ₹[Amount], whereas actual deductions totaled ₹[Amount], resulting in a short deduction of ₹[Amount].

**Effect:** Contractual financial security was reduced, and the contractor received an unearned overpayment pending recovery.

**Recommendation:** The DDO should recover the short deduction of ₹[Amount] from the next running account bill, update the contractor ledger, and ensure that cumulative retentions are reconciled prior to passing future vouchers.

### 27.2 Failure to deduct labour welfare cess

**Criteria:** Under the Building and Other Construction Workers’ Welfare Cess Act, 1996 and State Government instructions, the department must deduct 1% labour welfare cess from civil construction bills.

**Condition:** Running bills for [Project Name] totaling ₹[Amount] were passed and disbursed without deducting the 1% welfare cess, amounting to ₹[Amount].

**Effect:** Statutory non-compliance with the BOCW Act and loss of revenue to the Construction Workers Welfare Board.

**Recommendation:** The department must assess the outstanding liability of ₹[Amount], recover the balance from the contractor, remit it to the Welfare Board, and enforce a statutory deduction checklist for all works bills.

### 27.3 Unrecovered mobilisation advance

**Criteria:** In terms of Clause [ ] of the contract agreement, mobilization advances were to be recovered in installments of [ ]% of the value of work done from each running account bill.

**Condition:** A mobilization advance of ₹[Amount] was disbursed to the contractor, but scheduled recoveries were omitted from Running Account Bill Nos. [ ], leaving an unadjusted advance balance of ₹[Amount].

**Effect:** Public funds remained unrecovered, granting an unauthorised financial benefit to the contractor.

**Recommendation:** The DDO must enforce immediate recovery of the outstanding advance of ₹[Amount] with applicable interest, verify the validity of bank guarantees, and track advance balances in the contractor ledger.

## 28. Summary for Drawing and Disbursing Officers

Before passing a payment bill in Sikkim, the DDO must verify:

### For supply bills

* GST-TDS under Section 51 on taxable contracts exceeding ₹2,50,000.
* Income-tax TDS under Section 194C or other applicable sections.
* Contractual security deposits, if stipulated in the agreement.
* Recovery of prior supply advances.
* Penalties for delayed deliveries or recoveries for defective items.

### For works bills

* GST-TDS (2% on taxable value excluding GST).
* Income-tax TDS (1% for individuals; 2% for other entities).
* 1% Building and Other Construction Workers Welfare Cess.
* Contractual security deposit (typically 5% of work value).
* Earnest Money Deposit conversion and adjustment.
* Recovery of mobilisation and secured advances per contract terms.
* Recovery of departmental materials issued to the site.
* Minor mineral royalties where transit passes or challans are missing.
* Equipment or plant hire charges.
* Liquidated damages or contractual penalties.

Each deduction must be calculated using its own prescribed base:

```text
Gross Billed Value
        ↓
Add: Statutory GST (18%)
        ↓
Deduct: GST-TDS (On taxable value excluding GST)
        ↓
Deduct: Income-tax TDS (On taxable base)
        ↓
Deduct: Labour Welfare Cess (1% on construction cost)
        ↓
Deduct: Security Deposit (5% on value of work done)
        ↓
Deduct: Mobilisation and Secured Advance Recoveries
        ↓
Deduct: Departmental Material and Royalty Recoveries
        ↓
Deduct: Penalties or Other Authorised Recoveries
        ↓
Net Admissible Disbursement

```

A payment voucher should be cleared only after the DDO verifies the legal or contractual basis, calculation base, accounting classification, remittance schedule and ledger reconciliation for every deduction.
